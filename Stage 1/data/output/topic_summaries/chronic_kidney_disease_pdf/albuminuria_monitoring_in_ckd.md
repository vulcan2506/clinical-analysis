# albuminuria monitoring in CKD
*Source: Chronic Kidney Disease.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** albuminuria monitoring in CKD

**Clinical Actions:**
- Small fluctuations in albuminuria or proteinuria may not indicate disease progression; appreciation of impacting factors is important.
- Routine surveillance using ACR or PCR is warranted in higher-risk people with CKD, as changes in urine ACR are associated with kidney failure.
- A doubling of ACR within 2 years is associated with a 50%–100% increase in risk of progression to kidney failure.
- A 50% reduction in proteinuria within the first 2 months of treatment initiation more than halves the risk of kidney disease progression over 5 years.
- ACR >300 mg/g or PCR >630 mg/g is associated with significantly higher risk of disease progression.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- ACR or PCR should be used for routine surveillance in higher-risk CKD patients.
- A doubling in albuminuria or more warrants evaluation if replicated upon repeat testing.
- Reductions in ACR by up to 50% may reflect random fluctuation and do not necessarily indicate improvement.
- Interpretation of ACR in older adults should account for age-related changes in muscle mass and sarcopenia.
- Evaluation of a doubling in albuminuria requires replication upon repeat testing to confirm it exceeds expected variability.
- Interpretation of urine ACR in older adults must account for age-related loss of muscle mass and sarcopenia which can lower urinary creatinine.
