# hyperuricemia treatment efficacy
*Source: Chronic Kidney Disease.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** hyperuricemia treatment efficacy in CKD

**Clinical Actions:**
- No clear benefit of uric acid-lowering therapy on CKD progression in asymptomatic hyperuricemia
- Observational associations between uric acid and CKD progression are not causal
- Pill burden and lack of data do not support routine use of uric acid-lowering agents
- Three large RCTs (CKD-FIX, PERL, FEATHER) showed no significant difference in eGFR/mGFR decline vs. placebo
- Pooled RRs for kidney failure, cutaneous reactions, and hepatotoxicity were unclear (e.g., RR 0.92; 95% CI: 0.43–1.98)
- Pooled RRs for cutaneous reactions/hypersensitivity (RR 1.00; 95% CI: 0.60–1.65) and hepatotoxicity (RR 0.92; 95% CI: 0.37–2.30) were unclear, indicating no significant difference vs. placebo

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Symptomatic hyperuricemia (e.g., gout) may still warrant treatment per standard indications
- Evidence applies specifically to asymptomatic hyperuricemia in CKD
- Trials included CKD G3-G4 or eGFR 40–99.9 mL/min/1.73m² with mean urate ~8.2 mg/dL
