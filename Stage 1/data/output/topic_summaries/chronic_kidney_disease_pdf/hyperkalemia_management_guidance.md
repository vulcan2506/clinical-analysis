# hyperkalemia management guidance
*Source: Chronic Kidney Disease.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** hyperkalemia management in CKD

**Clinical Actions:**
- Implement individualized approach for CKD G3–G5 with emergent hyperkalemia including dietary and pharmacologic interventions, considering comorbidities and QoL
- Provide advice to limit intake of foods rich in bioavailable potassium (e.g., processed foods) for CKD G3–G5 with hyperkalemia history or high-risk periods
- Address correctable factors (e.g., severe metabolic acidosis, non-RASi medications like NSAIDs) before pharmacologic interventions
- Use stepwise approach: 1st line address correctable factors, 2nd line consider diuretics/optimize bicarbonate/licensed potassium exchange agents, 3rd line reduce/discontinue RASi/MRA as last resort
- Educate patients and providers on dietary potassium content, focusing on processed vs. unprocessed foods and cooking methods (e.g., soaking) to reduce potassium

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- CKD G3–G5 with emergent hyperkalemia requires individualized management including dietary and pharmacologic interventions
- Assessment and education through renal dietitian or accredited nutrition provider for CKD G3–G5 with hyperkalemia
- Repeat potassium measurement within 24 hours for unexpected results or moderate hyperkalemia (6.0–6.4 mmol/l)
- Immediate action and ECG monitoring for severe hyperkalemia (≥6.5 mmol/l) or clinically unwell/AKI patients
- Review and restart RASi/MRA at a later date if patient condition allows after discontinuation
- Assessment and treatment of clinically unwell or AKI patients with hyperkalemia must include blood testing and electrocardiogram monitoring
- Clinicians should be aware of local formulary restrictions regarding pharmacologic management of nonemergent hyperkalemia
