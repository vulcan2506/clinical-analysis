# CKD polypharmacy management
*Source: Chronic Kidney Disease.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** CKD polypharmacy management and medication review

**Clinical Actions:**
- Perform thorough medication review periodically and at transitions of care to assess adherence, continued indication, and potential drug interactions.
- Assess medication appropriateness, number, dose, and potential interactions, with drug stewardship promoting safe medication use throughout therapy.
- Discontinue medications when no longer required or when contributing to kidney injury (e.g., PPIs), especially during acute illness or care transitions.
- Implement sick day rules (SADMANS: sulfonylureas, ACEi, diuretics/direct renin inhibitors, metformin, ARBs, NSAIDs, SGLT2i) during acute dehydrating illness, with clear plans for restarting medications.
- Temporarily discontinue medications (e.g., metformin, ACEi, ARBs, SGLT2i) 48–72 hours prior to elective surgery or during acute adverse effects to prevent complications.
- Incorporate pharmacist-led medication reviews as a standard component of CKD polypharmacy management to systematically assess appropriateness, dosing, and interactions, leveraging their expertise in pharmacokinetics and drug-disease interactions.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Medication review must consider patient-specific factors such as GFR decline, volume overload, sarcopenia, and liver dysfunction.
- Clear communication and documentation of medication changes are required, especially between multiple prescribers and care teams.
- Monitoring for efficacy, safety, and adherence is essential during and after medication adjustments.
- Sick day rules should be individualized and clearly communicated to patients, with plans for restarting medications documented.
- Perioperative medication management requires coordination between surgical teams and nephrology to balance risk and benefit.
- Medication review must account for physiologic changes impacting drug volume of distribution, such as volume overload and sarcopenia, in addition to GFR decline.
