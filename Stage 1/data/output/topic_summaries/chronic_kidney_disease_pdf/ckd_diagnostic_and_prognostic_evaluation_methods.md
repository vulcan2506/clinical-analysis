# CKD diagnostic and prognostic evaluation methods
*Source: Chronic Kidney Disease.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** CKD diagnostic and prognostic evaluation methods

**Clinical Actions:**
- Use advanced imaging techniques (e.g., ultrasound-guided, CT-guided) to assess kidney damage and evaluate specific causes of CKD.
- Incorporate genetic testing for CKD cause, prognosis, and treatment selection, with or without kidney biopsy.
- Adopt novel urinary biomarkers (e.g., IL-18, KIM-1, NGAL, MCP-1, TIMP-1, alpha-1-microglobulin, uromodulin, EGF, YKL-40) to identify CKD etiology.
- Standardize and harmonize measured GFR (mGFR) protocols for accuracy and comparability.
- Evaluate kidney functional reserve across demographic characteristics (e.g., birthweight, sex, hormonal stages) and post-AKI.
- Evaluate the utility of total urine protein loss (PCR) in comparison with albumin-to-creatinine ratio (ACR) for evaluating specific kidney diseases in children and adults.
- Assess the non-GFR determinants of endogenous filtration markers such as cystatin C.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Kidney biopsy should be performed with consideration of patient-specific risks (e.g., age, kidney size, comorbidities) and guided by imaging methods.
- Genetic testing should be considered in CKD cases where etiology is unclear or prognosis/treatment decisions are uncertain.
- mGFR protocols must be standardized and validated for accuracy before clinical adoption.
- Novel urinary biomarkers should be validated in diverse populations before routine use.
- Kidney functional reserve assessment requires demographic and clinical context (e.g., GFR, ACR, hormonal status).
- Future studies reporting eGFR performance should report P15 in addition to P30 to approach mGFR accuracy levels.
- Risk scores derived from validated equations must be tested as inclusion criteria for enrichment of study cohorts and as potential surrogate endpoints in clinical trials.
