# cost-effective hyperkalemia management strategy
*Source: Chronic Kidney Disease.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** cost-effective hyperkalemia management strategy in CKD

**Clinical Actions:**
- Balance cost of potassium exchange agents against savings from prevented hyperkalemia-related inpatient/outpatient costs in CKD patients with severe recurrent hyperkalemia (K+ >6 mmol/l)
- Implement affordable hyperkalemia management strategies to maintain other therapies while reducing CKD progression and MACE
- Use plant-based foods for potassium management due to low absorption (50%-60%), net alkalizing effect, and intracellular K+ shifts
- Avoid processed foods (90% absorption) and limit animal-based proteins (70%-90% absorption) to reduce serum K+
- Consider discontinuation of RASi in children with CKD only when necessary, as it may accelerate kidney function decline
- Consider the net cost-benefit of potassium-lowering therapy in CKD management by comparing the price of potassium exchange agents against the reduction in inpatient and outpatient costs from prevented hyperkalemia episodes

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- CKD patients with severe recurrent hyperkalemia (K+ >6 mmol/l)
- Cost of potassium-lowering therapy must be lower than reduction in hyperkalemia-related healthcare costs
- Dietary potassium management must not compromise energy, protein, or micronutrient intake for growth in children
- Specialized low-potassium nutritional formulas may be required for children but may not be widely available
- Monitor serum potassium levels in children with CKD, especially those with glomerular disorders, metabolic acidosis, or on RASi
- For children with CKD, ensure dietary potassium management does not compromise energy, protein, or micronutrient intake required for growth
- Specialized low-potassium nutritional formulas may be required for children with CKD but may not be widely available or palatable
