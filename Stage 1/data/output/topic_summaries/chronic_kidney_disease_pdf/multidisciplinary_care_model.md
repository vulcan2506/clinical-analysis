# multidisciplinary care model
*Source: Chronic Kidney Disease.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** multidisciplinary care model for CKD

**Clinical Actions:**
- Enable access to a patient-centered multidisciplinary care team including dietary counseling, medication management, education, and counseling about KRT modalities, transplant options, dialysis access surgery, and ethical, psychological, and social care
- Implement a navigation system for appropriate and timely referral based on CKD severity and risk of progression
- Provide standardized education programs covering CKD knowledge, treatment to slow progression, and kidney failure management options, tailored to individual needs and literacy levels
- Establish surveillance protocols for laboratory monitoring, clinic visits, cardiovascular and CKD-associated comorbidities (e.g., anemia), and vaccination programs
- Facilitate three-way communication between patients, multidisciplinary specialist care teams, and primary care providers
- Conduct part of each visit without the parent/guardian present to foster autonomy and discuss sensitive topics
- Provide a comprehensive written transfer summary and ideally an oral handover to receiving healthcare providers

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- CKD models of care must align with the chronic disease model principles
- Education programs should involve care partners where indicated to promote informed self-care management
- Telehealth technologies (remote monitoring, education delivery, care delivery) may augment CKD care, particularly for outreach patients
- Transition from pediatric to adult care should start at 11–14 years with readiness assessments and gradual autonomy in visits
- Young adults (<25 years) require more frequent monitoring and may benefit from multidisciplinary young adult clinics
- Transition from pediatric to adult care should start at 11–14 years of age using checklists to assess readiness
- Transfer young people to adult care during times of medical and social stability where possible
