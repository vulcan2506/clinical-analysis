# nephrology leadership profile
*Source: Chronic Kidney Disease.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** nephrology leadership profile

**Clinical Actions:**
- Leads national kidney service oversight (e.g., BC Renal Agency) with responsibility for care planning, budgeting, and service delivery
- Chairs international nephrology organizations (e.g., ISN President 2015–2017) and global advocacy initiatives (e.g., Declaration of Istanbul Custodian Group)
- Develops and implements clinical guidelines (e.g., NICE CKD guidelines, KDIGO 2012 CKD guideline co-chair)
- Conducts high-impact research in CKD epidemiology, risk factors, and models of care with over 600 peer-reviewed publications
- Integrates patient-oriented research (e.g., Can-SOLVE CKD SPOR network) and mentors next-generation researchers
- Leads geriatric nephrology clinics incorporating geriatric assessment into CKD management and dialysis decision-making (e.g., Duke University geriatric nephrology clinic)
- Chairs national/international committees focused on sex and gender differences in kidney disease (e.g., Canadian Institutes of Health Research Institute of Gender and Health Advisory Board)

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Must hold a senior academic or clinical leadership role in nephrology (e.g., Division Head, Executive Director, Professor)
- Must have demonstrated leadership in guideline development or health policy at national/international levels
- Must engage in advocacy for equitable access to kidney care and patient rights
- Must have a track record of securing competitive research funding and publishing in high-impact journals
- Must serve in editorial or advisory roles for major nephrology journals or societies
- Must demonstrate expertise in geriatric nephrology and models of care for aging CKD populations
- Must have leadership experience in integrating sex and gender differences into kidney disease research and clinical practice
