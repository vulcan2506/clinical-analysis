# CKD diagnosis criteria in diabetes
*Source: Chronic Kidney Disease.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** CKD diagnosis criteria in diabetes

**Clinical Actions:**
- CKD is defined as abnormalities of kidney structure or function present for ≥3 months with implications for health.
- CKD classification uses Cause, GFR category (G1–G5), and Albuminuria category (A1–A3) (CGA system).
- GFR estimation should use SCr with an estimating equation rather than SCr alone.
- Markers of kidney damage include albuminuria (ACR ≥30 mg/g), urine sediment abnormalities, or structural abnormalities detected by imaging.
- Decreased GFR is defined as GFR <60 ml/min/1.73 m² (GFR categories G3a–G5).
- Markers of kidney damage include persistent hematuria, electrolyte abnormalities due to tubular disorders, or abnormalities detected by histology as additional criteria beyond albuminuria, urine sediment abnormalities, or structural abnormalities detected by imaging.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Abnormalities must persist for ≥3 months to meet CKD diagnostic criteria.
- Etiology of CKD should be sought to individualize management.
- Initial assessment of GFR requires SCr measurement and an estimating equation.
- Albuminuria must be confirmed with ACR ≥30 mg/g (≥3 mg/mmol) for classification.
- GFR categories G1 and G2 require evidence of kidney damage to fulfill CKD criteria.
- In the absence of evidence of kidney damage, neither GFR categories G1 nor G2 fulfills the criteria for CKD (relative to the young adult level).
