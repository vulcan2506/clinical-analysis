# dialysis initiation criteria
*Source: Chronic Kidney Disease.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** dialysis initiation criteria and preemptive planning

**Clinical Actions:**
- Initiate dialysis based on a composite assessment of symptoms, signs, QoL, preferences, GFR, and laboratory abnormalities rather than arbitrary eGFR thresholds.
- Initiate dialysis if specific clinical situations are evident, such as uremic symptoms, inability to control volume status, or progressive nutritional/cognitive deterioration.
- Avoid early initiation of dialysis at higher GFR levels as data shows no survival or cardiac benefit compared to late start.
- Plan for preemptive kidney transplantation and/or dialysis access when GFR is <15–20 ml/min/1.73 m² or risk of KRT is >40% over 2 years.
- In children, initiate dialysis for poor growth refractory to optimized nutrition and medical management in addition to adult indications.
- Initiate dialysis in the GFR range between 5 and 10 ml/min per 1.73 m² when specific clinical indications are present, though this is not invariably the case.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Presence of one or more specific clinical indications (e.g., neurological signs, pericarditis, medically resistant acid-base/electrolyte abnormalities, intractable pruritus, serositis).
- Inability to control volume status or blood pressure despite management.
- Progressive deterioration in nutritional status refractory to dietary intervention, or cognitive impairment.
- Evidence of progressive and irreversible CKD in children.
- Multidisciplinary discussion involving the patient (if age appropriate), caregivers, and healthcare providers for decision-making.
- Multidisciplinary discussion involving the child (if age appropriate), caregivers, and healthcare providers for decision-making in pediatric cases.
