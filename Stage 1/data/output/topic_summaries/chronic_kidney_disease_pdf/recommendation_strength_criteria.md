# Recommendation Strength Criteria
*Source: Chronic Kidney Disease.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Recommendation Strength Criteria

**Clinical Actions:**
- Balance of benefits and harms determines recommendation strength, with larger differences favoring strong recommendations.
- Higher certainty of evidence generally warrants strong recommendations, though exceptions exist for low-certainty evidence.
- Greater variability or uncertainty in patient values and preferences leads to weak recommendations.
- Values and preferences are derived from literature or Work Group judgment when robust evidence is unavailable.
- Resource implications are considered in recommendation formulation, though formal economic evaluations are not undertaken.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Assessment of the balance between desirable and undesirable effects.
- Evaluation of the certainty of the evidence.
- Determination of patient values and preferences variability.
- Consideration of resource implications.
- Availability of robust evidence or reliance on Work Group judgment for values.
- Inclusion of patient and caregiver perspectives in the Work Group to inform recommendation strength, even without formal qualitative evidence synthesis
