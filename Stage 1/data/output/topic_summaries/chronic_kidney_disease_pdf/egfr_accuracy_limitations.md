# eGFR accuracy limitations
*Source: Chronic Kidney Disease.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** eGFR accuracy limitations and complementary testing

**Clinical Actions:**
- eGFR has inherent error compared to mGFR, with 10% of estimates having ≥30% error even in high-accuracy populations (P50 ≥90%)
- Non-GFR determinants (e.g., diet, muscle mass, tubular secretion) significantly impact creatinine-based eGFR accuracy
- Cystatin C-based eGFR is less affected by non-GFR determinants but has its own confounders (e.g., adiposity, thyroid dysfunction, inflammation)
- Combined eGFRcr-cys improves accuracy over either marker alone due to complementary non-GFR determinants
- Discordance between eGFRcr and eGFRcys (>15 ml/min/1.73m² or ≥20%) warrants further evaluation (e.g., mGFR or cystatin C monitoring)
- Postprandial effects (e.g., cooked meat or fish intake) can increase serum creatinine by ~20 µmol/L (0.23 mg/dL), equivalent to a ~20 ml/min/1.73m² decrease in eGFR, with maximum effects observed at 2–4 hours and best avoided by waiting ≥12 hours
- Non-GFR determinants for cystatin C (e.g., adiposity, smoking, thyroid dysfunction, glucocorticoid excess, inflammation) are less well understood than those for creatinine

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Physicians must understand eGFR/mGFR limitations to select appropriate GFR assessment methods for clinical decisions
- mGFR is advised when accuracy within 15% is required (e.g., critical treatment decisions)
- eGFR is acceptable if accuracy within 30% is sufficient and non-GFR determinants are stable
- For eGFRcr-cys, non-GFR determinants must be considered; eGFRcys alone may be preferable in cases of extreme creatinine variability (e.g., muscle loss)
- Measured urinary creatinine clearance (CrCl) should be supervised if used, as it is prone to collection errors
- For optimal eGFR application, conditions should ideally simulate morning fasting or moderate protein intake, though this may be challenging to implement clinically
- If measured urinary creatinine clearance (CrCl) is used, it should be supervised to minimize collection errors, as unsupervised collections have a mean bias of 25% and high variability (e.g., 25% of participants had CrCl ≥18% lower or ≥23% higher than mGFR)
