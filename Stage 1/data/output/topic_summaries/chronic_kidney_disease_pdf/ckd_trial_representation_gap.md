# CKD trial representation gap
*Source: Chronic Kidney Disease.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** CKD trial representation gap and research recommendations

**Clinical Actions:**
- People with lower GFR remain under-represented in prospective RCTs despite exponential growth in kidney disease literature.
- Decreased GFR should not be an automatic exclusion criterion for research studies.
- Future studies should avoid automatic exclusion of older people, children, pregnant/lactating individuals, and those from low-income countries.
- Studies should measure ACR in all cohorts due to its prognostic value for multiple outcomes.
- People living with CKD should be involved in clinical studies from knowledge gap identification to study design and knowledge mobilization.
- Historical exclusion of low eGFR patients from major trials created a legacy of opinion-based literature, with only 3.3% to 6.5% of published kidney disease articles being RCTs, meta-analyses, or systematic reviews in the 5-year period ending December 2022.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Estimating equations equally applicable to those with and without CKD are required for children and adolescents.
- Benefit-risk ratio assessments of medications should be performed by levels of GFR and ACR or using validated risk equations.
- Pharmacokinetic studies in people with CKD should not automatically exclude GFR categories G4 and G5.
- New risk prediction equations (CVD, mortality, CKD progression) require development, validation across diverse populations, and implementation studies.
- Use of novel study designs (platform, registry-embedded, pragmatic trials) and large administrative datasets should be considered.
- Drug studies evaluating pharmacodynamics should consider using validated eGFR equations or measured GFR (mGFR) for highly toxic drugs with a narrow therapeutic window, especially those frequently used in CKD.
