# SGLT2i and urate-lowering therapy comparative analysis
*Source: Chronic Kidney Disease.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** SGLT2i vs urate-lowering therapy (ULT) in CKD with hyperuricemia

**Clinical Actions:**
- SGLT2 inhibitors are compared against placebo, usual care, or active comparators for CKD outcomes (mortality, progression, complications, adverse events)
- Urate-lowering therapy (ULT) agents (e.g., allopurinol, febuxostat, benzbromarone) are evaluated against placebo, usual care, or active comparators for CKD with hyperuricemia
- PRISMA diagrams summarize evidence synthesis for SGLT2i and ULT in CKD populations
- ULT is assessed separately for symptomatic and asymptomatic hyperuricemia in CKD
- Comparative analyses focus on mortality, CKD progression, complications, and adverse events
- Evaluation of steroidal mineralocorticoid receptor agonists (MRAs; canrenone, eplerenone, spironolactone) versus active comparator, placebo, or usual care in CKD without type 2 diabetes
- Evaluation of glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1) receptor agonists versus placebo, usual care, or an active comparator in CKD without type 2 diabetes

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Adults and children with CKD included in analyses
- Hyperuricemia (symptomatic or asymptomatic) must be present for ULT evaluation
- SGLT2i eligibility includes CKD with or without diabetes
- ULT agents include allopurinol, febuxostat, benzbromarone, and others
- Outcomes include mortality, CKD progression, complications, and adverse events
- CKD with ischemic heart disease requires evaluation of angiography or coronary revascularization versus medical treatment
- CKD at risk for cardiovascular disease requires evaluation of aspirin versus placebo for primary prevention of CVD
