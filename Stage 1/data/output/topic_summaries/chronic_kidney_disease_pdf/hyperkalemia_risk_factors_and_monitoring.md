# hyperkalemia risk factors and monitoring
*Source: Chronic Kidney Disease.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** hyperkalemia risk factors and monitoring in CKD

**Clinical Actions:**
- Be aware of variability in potassium laboratory measurements due to diurnal/seasonal variation, plasma vs. serum samples, and medication effects.
- Monitor serum potassium during treatment with nonsteroidal MRAs (e.g., finerenone) as per KDIGO 2022 guidelines.
- Mitigate hyperkalemia risk to improve potassium control and enable continued use of RASi in indicated patients.
- Recognize pseudohyperkalemia from improper blood draw/clotting (e.g., hemolysis, tourniquet use) and retest appropriately.
- Identify hyperkalemia risk factors: tissue breakdown (rhabdomyolysis, tumor lysis), insulin deficiency, aldosterone blockade, and nonorganic acidosis.
- Recognize hyperkalemia risk from increased plasma osmolarity (e.g., dehydration, hyperglycemia) as a disruption in potassium shifting out of cells.
- Monitor serum potassium in patients with β-adrenergic blockade, particularly during or immediately after exercise, due to increased hyperkalemia risk.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Hyperkalemia defined as serum potassium >5 mmol/L.
- Monitoring protocols for serum potassium during nonsteroidal MRA therapy must be followed.
- Retest potassium if pseudohyperkalemia is suspected (e.g., hemolysis, clotting artifacts).
- Adjust for confounders (e.g., diabetes, CKD, heart failure) when interpreting potassium risk.
- Use plasma potassium if thrombocytosis/leukocytosis is suspected to avoid falsely elevated serum potassium.
- Use plasma potassium measurement if thrombocytosis or leukocytosis is suspected to avoid falsely elevated serum potassium readings.
- Adjust hyperkalemia risk stratification by incorporating eGFR and albuminuria stages with diabetes status as a key confounder.
