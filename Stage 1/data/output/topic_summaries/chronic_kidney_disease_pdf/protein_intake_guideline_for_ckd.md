# protein intake guideline for CKD
*Source: Chronic Kidney Disease.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Protein intake guideline for CKD

**Clinical Actions:**
- Maintain protein intake at 0.8 g/kg body weight/day for adults with CKD G3–G5 (2C).
- Avoid high protein intake (>1.3 g/kg body weight/day) in adults with CKD at risk of progression.
- Consider very low–protein diet (0.3–0.4 g/kg/day) supplemented with essential amino acids or ketoacid analogs (up to 0.6 g/kg/day) in metabolically stable adults with CKD at risk of kidney failure.
- Do not prescribe low- or very low-protein diets in metabolically unstable individuals (e.g., sarcopenia, cachexia, active inflammatory disease).
- Avoid protein restriction in children with CKD due to risk of growth impairment.
- Encourage plant-based protein sources (e.g., legumes, whole grains) over animal-based proteins to potentially slow eGFR decline and reduce uremic toxin generation (Table 21, observational studies and RCT evidence).
- Avoid unsupervised protein restriction without dietary counseling to prevent malnutrition and low dietary diversity.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- CKD stages G3–G5 (adults) or G2–G5 (children) must be confirmed.
- Metabolic stability must be assessed before recommending very low–protein diets.
- Supervision by renal dietitians or accredited nutrition specialists is required for very low–protein diets.
- Patient willingness and ability to adhere to dietary modifications must be evaluated.
- Consider patient preferences, cultural values, and quality of life in dietary planning.
- Assess metabolic stability (e.g., sarcopenia, cachexia, active inflammatory disease, hospitalization) before prescribing any protein restriction.
- Evaluate patient willingness and ability to adhere to dietary modifications, including cultural preferences and quality of life impacts.
