# eGFR equation performance evaluation
*Source: Chronic Kidney Disease.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** eGFR equation performance evaluation + race considerations in eGFR computation

**Clinical Actions:**
- Systematic error (bias) is evaluated as the absolute median difference between eGFR and mGFR, categorized as Small (<5), Moderate (5–10), or Large (>10) ml/min/1.73 m².
- Precision is assessed via the IQR of the difference between eGFR and mGFR, categorized as Small (<10), Moderate (10–20), or Large (>20) ml/min/1.73 m².
- Accuracy is measured by P30 (percentage of estimates within 30% of mGFR), categorized as Optimal (≥90%), Acceptable (80–90%), or Poor (<80%).
- Race is not recommended for inclusion in eGFR computation in the United States due to its social construct nature and variability.
- Region-specific eGFR equations (e.g., Japan, Thailand) are advocated for populations where race-specific adjustments are not applicable.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- eGFR performance must be evaluated against measured GFR (mGFR) for bias, precision, and accuracy.
- Equations with large error (bias), high IQR, or low P30 are considered to have poor performance.
- Race should not be used as a binary variable in eGFR computation due to social and biological diversity concerns.
- Race (and ethnicity) is dynamic and context-dependent, shaped by geographic, cultural, and sociopolitical forces, requiring careful consideration in clinical algorithms.
