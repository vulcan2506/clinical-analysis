# metabolic acidosis in CKD progression
*Source: Chronic Kidney Disease.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** metabolic acidosis management in CKD progression

**Clinical Actions:**
- Serum bicarbonate concentration falls progressively once eGFR falls below 60 ml/min/1.73m², with reductions most evident in CKD stages G4–G5.
- Metabolic acidosis is associated with increased risk of protein catabolism, muscle wasting, inflammation, impaired cardiac function, and mortality.
- Consider pharmacological treatment (with or without dietary intervention) to prevent acidosis when serum bicarbonate <18 mmol/l in adults.
- Monitor treatment to avoid serum bicarbonate exceeding the upper limit of normal and to prevent adverse effects on BP, serum potassium, or fluid status.
- Dietary modifications (e.g., plant-dominant diets) can reduce net endogenous acid production and control metabolic acidosis.
- Metabolic acidosis is associated with increased risk of disease progression in children with CKD (38%–60% of children have serum bicarbonate <22 mmol/l, varying by CKD category).
- Low bicarbonate levels in children are associated with growth retardation, and observational data suggest alkali treatment may improve growth in prepubertal children.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- eGFR <60 ml/min/1.73m² for consideration of treatment.
- Serum bicarbonate <18 mmol/l in adults to consider pharmacological intervention.
- Avoid serum bicarbonate exceeding the upper limit of normal during treatment.
- Monitor BP, serum potassium, and fluid status during treatment.
- For children, normal bicarbonate range may be as low as 17 mmol/l.
- For younger children, the normal serum bicarbonate range may be as low as 17 mmol/l.
