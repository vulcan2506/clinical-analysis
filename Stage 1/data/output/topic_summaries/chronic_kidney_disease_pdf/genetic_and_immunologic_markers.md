# genetic and immunologic markers
*Source: Chronic Kidney Disease.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** genetic and immunologic markers in CKD evaluation

**Clinical Actions:**
- Providers should use genetic and immunologic markers to improve understanding of disease state and extent of active/chronic lesions in CKD.
- Kidney biopsy harms include bleeding risk, pain, non-diagnostic samples, and patient anxiety awaiting results.
- Mortality after native kidney biopsy in CKD is low (3 deaths across 15 studies), with perirenal hematoma rate at 16% (95% CI: 12%-22%).
- Certainty of evidence for biopsy outcomes and harms is very low due to confounding in observational studies.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Conditions amenable to specific disease-modifying therapies include GLA (Fabry), AGXT (primary hyperoxaluria), CoQ10 genes (SRNS), CTNS (cystinosis), and tubulopathies.
- Conditions amenable to nonspecific renoprotective strategies include COL4A3/4/5 (Alport) and RAAS blockade.
- Avoid prolonged immunosuppressive therapies where possible.
- Providers should consider genetic testing in patients with suspected hereditary kidney disease (e.g., APOL1, COL4A, PKD1/2, UMOD, HNF1B) to guide diagnosis and management.
