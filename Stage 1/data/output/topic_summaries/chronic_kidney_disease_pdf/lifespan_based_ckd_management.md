# lifespan-based CKD management
*Source: Chronic Kidney Disease.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** lifespan-based CKD management integrating age, sex, and gender

**Clinical Actions:**
- Adopt a personalized approach considering age, sex, and gender for diagnosis, risk assessment, and treatment across the whole lifespan.
- Tailor diagnostic procedures and treatment aims for extremes of age (very young and very old) due to differences in prognosis and prioritization.
- Optimize care for children and adolescents by addressing unique etiologies like CAKUT, supporting growth/puberty, and ensuring neurodevelopmental gains.
- Adjust treatment goals for older adults by accounting for sarcopenia, frailty, multimorbidity, and prioritizing quality of life over pure survival.
- Integrate sex and gender factors including hormonal status, pregnancy risks, and gender identity into care planning and medication management.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Clinical assessment must include evaluation of age-specific physiological changes (e.g., nephron maturation in infants, sarcopenia in elderly).
- Care planning requires consideration of intersectional factors such as socioeconomic status, race, ethnicity, and geographical location.
- For older adults, laboratory interpretation (eGFRcr, Urine ACR) must account for habitus and potential false readings due to low muscle mass.
- Decision-making for advanced CKD in the elderly must be based on informed shared decision-making regarding benefits, harms, and patient values.
- Management of transgender and gender-diverse individuals requires building cultural safety and addressing specific hormonal therapy impacts.
- Laboratory interpretation for older adults must account for sarcopenia to prevent eGFR overestimation and subsequent drug overdosing.
- Urine ACR results in elderly populations must be interpreted with caution due to falsely elevated values caused by low creatinine in the denominator.
