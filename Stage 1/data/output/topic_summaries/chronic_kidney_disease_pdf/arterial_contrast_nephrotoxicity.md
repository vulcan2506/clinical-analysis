# arterial contrast nephrotoxicity
*Source: Chronic Kidney Disease.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** arterial contrast nephrotoxicity risk and prevention

**Clinical Actions:**
- Arterial administration of contrast carries higher risk of CA-AKI than venous administration due to patient comorbidities or intrinsic nephrotoxicity.
- Withdraw nonessential nephrotoxic medications (e.g., NSAIDs, diuretics, aminoglycosides, amphotericin, platins, zoledronate, methotrexate) 24–48 hours before and 48 hours after radiocontrast exposure in people with AKI or eGFR <30 mL/min/1.73m².
- Withhold RAASi for ≥48 hours before elective contrast-enhanced CT in at-risk patients to avoid hypotension and hyperkalemia if CA-AKI develops; restart if CA-AKI does not occur or after GFR returns to baseline.
- Avoid dehydration in patients with eGFR <30 mL/min/1.73m² or AKI receiving intravenous contrast.
- Use low-osmolality or iso-osmolar contrast media and minimize contrast dose to achieve diagnostic study.
- Known risk factors for CA-AKI include advanced age, heart failure, proteinuria, and hyperglycemia, which should be considered when assessing patient risk before contrast administration.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- eGFR <30 mL/min/1.73m² or AKI for mandatory medication withdrawal and hydration precautions.
- Elective contrast-enhanced CT for RAASi withholding consideration.
- Diagnostic study requiring contrast administration.
- Interventional coronary angiography requires special consideration due to higher risk, particularly in patients with acute myocardial infarction, where hemodynamic instability and larger contrast volumes are common.
