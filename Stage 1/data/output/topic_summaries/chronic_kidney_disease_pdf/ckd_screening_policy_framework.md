# CKD screening policy framework
*Source: Chronic Kidney Disease.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** CKD screening policy framework

**Clinical Actions:**
- Screening for CKD is cost-effective in people with diabetes and hypertension, the two most common causes of CKD worldwide.
- Early identification of CKD in asymptomatic high-risk individuals is beneficial if programs include risk stratification and treatment.
- Screening and treatment programs should prioritize high-risk groups, particularly in resource-limited settings.
- Annual CKD screening is recommended for people with diabetes (starting at T2D diagnosis, 5 years after T1D diagnosis).
- Screening intervals should be guided by CKD risk stratification using available risk equations.
- Screening and treatment programs should prioritize people identified through genetic risk factors or environmental exposures (e.g., pollution, pesticides, nephrotoxic medications, herbal medications) depending on geographical region.
- Screening programs should be integrated with education of both health personnel and at-risk populations to improve awareness and adherence to recommended care.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Screening programs must provide treatment to high-risk patients with newly detected CKD to justify systematic early detection.
- Testing should include eGFR and albuminuria (ACR) for diagnosis and staging of CKD.
- Laboratory assays for SCr and urinary albumin must be standardized with traceability to international reference materials.
- Screening should be integrated with patient education and engagement to improve adherence to lifestyle modifications and medications.
- Primary care providers or allied health workers should lead screening and management in resource-limited settings.
- Screening programs in low- and middle-income countries (LMIC) must account for disparities in access to laboratory diagnostics, kidney biopsy, imaging, and medications, and deploy resources to higher-risk, preventable stages.
- Laboratory assays for cystatin C and urinary albumin should be harmonized via the International Consortium for Harmonization of Clinical Laboratory Results (ICHLR) to ensure comparability of results across settings.
