# CKD diagnostic criteria
*Source: Chronic Kidney Disease.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** CKD diagnostic criteria and early detection framework

**Clinical Actions:**
- CKD is often silent and requires laboratory testing (e.g., GFR, albuminuria) for detection.
- Early detection of CKD enables earlier treatment initiation to reduce morbidity and CKD progression.
- Shared decision-making is recommended to discuss testing, results, and implications with patients.
- Systematic testing of high-risk groups (e.g., hypertension, diabetes, CVD) is prioritized for early detection.
- Testing should be individualized based on patient goals of care and suitability for treatment.
- CKD detection strategies should be aligned with broader public health priorities to avoid compromising other health initiatives.
- CKD disproportionately affects minoritized populations and those with lower socioeconomic status, necessitating equity-focused detection strategies.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Initial testing for CKD includes blood and urine tests to detect abnormalities in creatinine/eGFR or albuminuria.
- Confirmatory testing is required if initial findings indicate abnormalities.
- Annual screening for CKD is recommended for people with diabetes (T2D at diagnosis, T1D after 5 years).
- Testing for CKD should be performed in high-risk groups such as those with hypertension, CVD, or recent AKI/AKD.
- Confirmatory testing for albuminuria requires a repeat first morning void sample when well and not during menstruation.
- CKD detection efforts should be individualized based on patient goals of care and suitability for treatment, particularly in older age groups.
- Confirmatory testing for albuminuria requires a repeat first morning void sample when well and not during menstruation, with confirmation on a second sample if initial results are abnormal.
