# hemodynamic eGFR decline with BP control
*Source: Chronic Kidney Disease.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** hemodynamic eGFR decline with BP control

**Clinical Actions:**
- Acute eGFR decline of 10%-20% within first 3 months of intensive BP control is hemodynamically mediated and transient
- Acute rises in SCr or declines in eGFR <20%-30% do not warrant changes in therapeutic agents
- Initial eGFR decline stabilizes or resolves over time due to resetting of autoregulatory function
- Hemodynamic eGFR decline is common with ACEi/ARBs and SGLT2i due to BP lowering and glomerular arteriolar flow changes
- Acute eGFR 'dip' >30% from baseline warrants review for other causes and close monitoring
- Acute eGFR decline of 10%-20% is typical within the first 3 months of intensive BP control and represents a response to BP falling below the lower threshold of autoregulatory response

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Monitor eGFR closely after initiating antihypertensive agents, RASi, MRA, or SGLT2i
- Avoid stopping kidney-protective agents due to transient eGFR decline unless >30% drop occurs
- Assess for other causes if eGFR decline exceeds 30%
- Avoid stopping kidney-protective agents (e.g., ACEi/ARBs, SGLT2i) due to transient eGFR decline unless the drop exceeds 30% or other causes are suspected
