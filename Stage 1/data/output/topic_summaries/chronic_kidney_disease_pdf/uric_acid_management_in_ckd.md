# uric acid management in CKD
*Source: Chronic Kidney Disease.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** uric acid management in CKD

**Clinical Actions:**
- Offer uric acid–lowering intervention to people with CKD and symptomatic hyperuricemia (1C recommendation)
- Prescribe xanthine oxidase inhibitors (e.g., allopurinol, febuxostat) in preference to uricosuric agents for symptomatic hyperuricemia in CKD
- Initiate uric acid–lowering therapy after first episode of gout in CKD G3–G5 with serum uric acid >9 mg/dl (>535 μmol/l) or urolithiasis
- Use low-dose colchicine as preferred first-line therapy for acute gout in CKD, with dose adjustment for CKD G5
- Counsel patients on increased gout flare risk during first 3 months after uric acid–lowering therapy initiation
- Consider initiating anti-inflammatory prophylaxis (e.g., low-dose colchicine) during the first 3 months after uric acid–lowering therapy initiation in CKD to mitigate expected gout flare risk

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Symptomatic hyperuricemia (e.g., gout flares, tophi) must be present to initiate uric acid–lowering therapy
- HLA-B*5801 testing recommended before allopurinol in populations with higher prevalence (e.g., Han Chinese, Korean, Thai, African descent)
- Serum uric acid >9 mg/dl (>535 μmol/l) or urolithiasis at first gout episode in CKD G3–G5
- Close monitoring at initiation of uric acid–lowering therapy where HLA testing is unavailable
- Avoid initiating uric acid–lowering therapy for asymptomatic hyperuricemia
- Serum uric acid concentration ≥6.8 mg/dl (≥400 μmol/l) defines hyperuricemia per American College of Rheumatology criteria
