# creatinine and cystatin C assay standardization
*Source: Chronic Kidney Disease.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** creatinine and cystatin C assay standardization for CKD

**Clinical Actions:**
- Use assays with required specificity for creatinine/cystatin C and calibrate to reference materials (e.g., JCTLM-listed standards).
- Ensure traceability of results to international reference standards for accuracy and reliability.
- Flag decreased eGFR values to alert providers to potential kidney disease or need for medication adjustments.
- Remove serum from red blood cells within 12 hours for Jaffe assays to prevent SCr increase over time.
- Implement enzymatic assays for creatinine in children due to higher non-creatinine chromogen interference in Jaffe assays.
- Consider measuring both creatinine and cystatin C either in-house or as a referred test when resources allow.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Assays must be traceable to JCTLM-listed reference materials and methods.
- Laboratories should use GFR estimating equations validated for the population and assays in use.
- Quality control must include the lowest expected creatinine range for infants/small children.
- Close communication between labs and clinical users (e.g., primary care, pharmacists) for GFR reporting.
- Regular external quality assessment (EQA) of end-to-end reporting processes, including embedded calculations.
- Ensure end-to-end reporting processes, including embedded calculations in laboratory information systems, are subject to regular external quality assessment (EQA).
- Document the filtration marker (e.g., eGFRcr, eGFRcys, eGFRcr-cys) and the specific equation used in laboratory reports.
