# atrial fibrillation in CKD
*Source: Chronic Kidney Disease.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** atrial fibrillation in CKD: diagnosis, prophylaxis, and management

**Clinical Actions:**
- Use opportunistic pulse-based screening (e.g., during BP measurement) followed by 12-lead ECG if irregularly irregular pulse detected
- Consider NOACs in preference to vitamin K antagonists (e.g., warfarin) for thromboprophylaxis in CKD G1–G4 with atrial fibrillation
- Use CHA₂DS₂-VASc score to guide stroke prophylaxis; oral anticoagulation should be considered even with low scores due to high thromboembolic risk in CKD
- Apply HAS-BLED score to assess bleeding risk and address modifiable factors (e.g., alcohol use, PPI use)
- Control ventricular rate to <90 bpm at rest using beta blockade; consider rhythm control (cardioversion, antiarrhythmics, catheter ablation) for persistent symptoms
- Include laboratory testing (thyroid and kidney function, serum electrolytes, full blood count) as part of the standard diagnostic evaluation for new atrial fibrillation in CKD
- Consider reversible causes of atrial fibrillation (e.g., electrolyte imbalances, thyroid dysfunction) as part of rate/rhythm control management

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- CKD G1–G4 for NOAC preference over warfarin (Recommendation 3.16.1)
- CHA₂DS₂-VASc score assessment for stroke risk stratification
- HAS-BLED score evaluation to identify modifiable bleeding risks
- 12-lead ECG for diagnosis confirmation
- Transthoracic echocardiography for structural heart disease assessment in new atrial fibrillation
- Transthoracic echocardiography to assess left ventricular size and function, left atrial size, valvular disease, and right heart size and function as part of the standard diagnostic package for new atrial fibrillation in CKD
