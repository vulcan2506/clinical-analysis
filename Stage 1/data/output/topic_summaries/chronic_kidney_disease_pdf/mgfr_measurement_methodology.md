# mGFR measurement methodology
*Source: Chronic Kidney Disease.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** mGFR measurement methodology and exogenous filtration markers

**Clinical Actions:**
- mGFR is less influenced by non-GFR determinants compared to eGFR, making it more accurate for clinical decision-making.
- GFR is measured using exogenous filtration markers (e.g., iothalamate, iohexol, EDTA, DTPA) with urinary or plasma clearance methods.
- Precision of mGFR is determined by variability with repeated measures, assessing time-to-time variability as the method to evaluate error.
- For lower GFRs, delayed blood sampling improves accuracy of mGFR.
- Plasma 99mTc-DTPA clearance over 2–4 hours is not recommended due to inaccuracy.
- GFR measurement methods should be selected based on clinical context, with mGFR preferred when non-GFR determinants (e.g., muscle mass, diet) significantly affect eGFR accuracy.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- mGFR is required when accurate GFR assessment is critical for clinical decisions.
- Use of exogenous filtration markers (iothalamate, iohexol, EDTA, DTPA) is recommended for mGFR.
- Individualized protocols are necessary for mGFR to ensure accuracy across the GFR range.
- Individualized protocols for mGFR must account for patient-specific factors (e.g., GFR range, clinical stability) to optimize accuracy and feasibility.
