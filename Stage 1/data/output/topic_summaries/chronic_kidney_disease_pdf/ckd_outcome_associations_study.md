# CKD outcome associations study
*Source: Chronic Kidney Disease.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** CKD outcome associations study and risk stratification

**Clinical Actions:**
- Epidemiological studies evaluate CKD outcomes including all-cause mortality, cardiovascular events (MI, stroke, heart failure), kidney-specific outcomes (progression to kidney failure and AKI), all-cause hospitalization, and incident atrial fibrillation.
- Risk gradients are assessed using eGFR (creatinine- or cystatin C-based equations) and ACR categories to determine population-level risk distributions.
- Updated heatmaps (CKD-PC) incorporate larger population datasets (up to 27.5M) and use the 2021 CKD-EPI equation (race-neutral) for eGFRcr.
- Risk associations increase with worsening eGFR and albuminuria stages across 10 outcomes, with adjustments for age, sex, smoking, BP, lipids, BMI, medications, and comorbidities.
- Heatmaps use color-coded risk stratification (green to red) to depict relative risks (RRs) for each eGFR/ACR combination compared to a reference group.
- Risk gradients are validated using the 2021 CKD-EPI combined eGFRcr-cys equation to evaluate if weaker associations in CKD G3a/A1 are due to creatinine equation limitations.
- Heatmap color schemes are determined separately for each outcome based on the proportion of cells without CKD versus highest risk cells, ensuring consistent green/red cell counts across outcomes.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Both eGFR and ACR parameters must be available for accurate risk assessment in CKD populations.
- Multivariable adjustments require data on age, sex, smoking status, systolic BP, cholesterol (total and HDL), BMI, antihypertensive use, and history of diabetes, CVD, and other comorbidities.
- Use of the 2021 CKD-EPI creatinine-based equation (race-neutral) for eGFR calculations in updated analyses.
- Population-level risk distributions (heatmaps) should not replace individualized risk prediction tools for clinical decisions.
- CKD G3a, A1 (eGFR 45–59, ACR <10 mg/g) may show lower relative risk for most outcomes but remains elevated for AKI and kidney failure.
- Data on peripheral artery disease and chronic obstructive pulmonary disease history are required for multivariable adjustment of relative risks.
- Smoking status must be categorized as current, former, or never for accurate risk adjustment.
