# CKD medication safety guidance
*Source: Chronic Kidney Disease.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** CKD Medication Safety Guidance

**Clinical Actions:**
- Always consider benefits versus potential harms when prescribing nephrotoxic medications to people with CKD.
- Monitor eGFR, electrolytes, and therapeutic medication levels in people with CKD receiving medications with narrow therapeutic windows, potential adverse effects, or nephrotoxicity.
- Review and limit the use of over-the-counter medicines and dietary or herbal remedies that may be harmful for people with CKD.
- Review teratogenicity potential and provide regular reproductive and contraceptive counseling for people with CKD of child-bearing potential.
- Perform thorough medication review periodically and at transitions of care to assess adherence, continued indication, and potential drug interactions.
- Consider planned discontinuation of medications (e.g., metformin, ACEi, ARBs, SGLT2i) 48–72 hours prior to elective surgery or during acute management of adverse effects as a precautionary measure.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Use validated eGFR equations using SCr for drug dosing in most clinical settings.
- Adapt drug dosing when GFR, non-GFR determinants, or volume of distribution are not in a steady state.
- Use equations combining creatinine and cystatin C or measured GFR when more accuracy is required for drug-related decision-making.
- Consider eGFR nonindexed for BSA in people with extremes of body weight, especially for medications with a narrow therapeutic range.
- Ensure clear communication and documentation of medication restart plans after discontinuation during acute illness or surgery, including specific timing for restart.
