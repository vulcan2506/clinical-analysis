# Recommendation Grading Methodology
*Source: Chronic Kidney Disease.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Recommendation Grading Methodology

**Clinical Actions:**
- Strength of recommendations graded as Level 1 ('We recommend') or Level 2 ('We suggest') based on balance of benefits and harms, evidence certainty, patient values, and resource use.
- Overall certainty of evidence graded as high (A), moderate (B), low (C), or very low (D) based on critical outcomes.
- Inclusion of patient values and preferences via lived experience of Work Group members, though no systematic review of qualitative studies was conducted.
- Consideration of healthcare and non-healthcare resources (e.g., direct costs, informal caregiver time, productivity changes) in grading recommendations.
- Inclusion of 'practice points' as consensus statements to supplement graded recommendations when evidence is insufficient.
- Each guideline recommendation includes an explicit assessment of the strength of the recommendation (Level 1 or Level 2) and the overall certainty of the evidence (A, B, C, or D) in a standardized format.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Balance of benefits and harms must be assessed across all critical and important outcomes.
- Overall certainty of evidence must be determined for each recommendation based on critical outcomes.
- Patient values and preferences must be incorporated, including input from individuals with lived experience.
- Resource use and costs must be evaluated, including direct healthcare, non-healthcare, and informal caregiver resources.
- The overall certainty of evidence must be dictated by the critical outcome with the lowest certainty, unless modified based on the relative importance of each outcome to the population of interest.
