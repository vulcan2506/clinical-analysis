# CKD referral risk stratification
*Source: Chronic Kidney Disease.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** CKD referral risk stratification using objective tools

**Clinical Actions:**
- Use objective risk-based tools to triage CKD patients for nephrology referral, prioritizing those with high risk of progression to kidney failure.
- Apply 3%–5% 5-year kidney failure risk threshold for general nephrology referral criteria.
- Use >10% 2-year kidney failure risk threshold to determine eligibility for interdisciplinary care clinics.
- Implement >40% 2-year kidney failure risk threshold for modality education, vascular access planning, and transplantation referral.
- Utilize risk-based thresholds (e.g., >20% 2-year risk) for earlier modality education or presurgical planning in centers with longer wait times.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Risk thresholds must be adjusted based on local healthcare resource availability and wait time standards.
- Consider individual comorbidities and competing risks (e.g., death from other causes) when applying risk thresholds.
- For CKD G4–G5, enroll high-risk patients in interdisciplinary care clinics with nurse, pharmacist, renal dietitian, and allied health support.
- Use externally validated risk prediction models (e.g., CKD-PC, Klinrisk, KidneyIntelx) for accurate risk stratification.
- For specific CKD etiologies (e.g., IgAN, ADPKD), use disease-specific risk models (e.g., MEST score, Mayo Clinic Classification, PROPKD score).
- Risk prediction models for specific etiologies (e.g., IgAN, ADPKD) should be preferentially used over general CKD models in patients with those specific diagnoses.
- Risk thresholds for vascular access planning may be adjusted based on center-specific factors such as access to surgeons and primary failure to maturation rates.
