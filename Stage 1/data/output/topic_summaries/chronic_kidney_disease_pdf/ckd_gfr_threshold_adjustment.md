# CKD GFR threshold adjustment
*Source: Chronic Kidney Disease.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** CKD GFR threshold adjustment by age

**Clinical Actions:**
- Suggested GFR threshold for CKD raised to 75 ml/min/1.73m² for younger adults to account for longer risk horizon and lifetime CKD progression risks.
- Suggested GFR threshold for CKD lowered to 45 ml/min/1.73m² for older adults due to higher absolute risks of CKD complications (e.g., CVD, heart failure, mortality).
- Higher lifetime progression risks in younger adults with GFR 60–89 ml/min/1.73m² can be managed without redefining CKD thresholds.
- Efforts should focus on high-risk individuals with GFR >60 ml/min/1.73m² to prevent CKD incidence or further GFR decline.
- Older adults with CKD G3a, A1 (eGFRcr-cys) have elevated relative risks (RR) for CKD complications, supporting their inclusion in the CKD population.
- Efforts to prevent CKD incidence or further GFR decline should prioritize individuals with GFR >60 ml/min/1.73m² who have additional risk factors (e.g., albuminuria, diabetes, hypertension) rather than applying a uniform threshold.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- GFR thresholds should be adjusted based on age (higher for younger adults, lower for older adults) to reflect lifetime risk and absolute risk of CKD complications.
- Use of eGFRcr-cys (creatinine-cystatin C) is recommended for accurate GFR estimation in older adults with CKD G3a, A1 to confirm elevated relative risks and guide management decisions.
