# albuminuria-associated mortality risk
*Source: Chronic Kidney Disease.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** albuminuria-associated mortality risk stratification

**Clinical Actions:**
- Higher albuminuria (ACR ≥30 mg/g) is associated with increased all-cause mortality risk across all age groups.
- Risk escalates progressively with higher ACR categories (10-29, 30-299, ≥300 mg/g).
- Older age groups (e.g., 60-89, 90-104) show higher mortality risk ratios at equivalent ACR levels compared to younger groups.
- ACR <10 mg/g serves as the reference (lowest risk) category for mortality comparison.
- Mortality risk ratios are highest in the ≥300 mg/g ACR group across all age strata.
- Mortality risk ratios increase non-linearly with age, showing a steeper gradient in younger patients (e.g., <30 years) compared to older patients (e.g., 105+).
- The magnitude of relative risk increase for all-cause mortality is highest in the youngest age strata for equivalent ACR elevations.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- ACR measurement must be performed using spot urine samples standardized to mg/g creatinine.
- Risk stratification requires age-specific ACR thresholds (e.g., <10, 10-29, 30-299, ≥300 mg/g).
- All-cause mortality risk assessment should incorporate both ACR and age group data.
- Clinical decisions (e.g., treatment intensity) should be guided by ACR-based risk tiers.
- Repeat ACR testing is recommended to confirm persistent albuminuria (2 out of 3 samples positive).
- Risk stratification must utilize specific age-stratified hazard ratios rather than applying a single universal risk multiplier across all ages.
- Clinical prioritization for aggressive albuminuria management should be weighted toward younger patients due to their steeper relative risk gradients.
