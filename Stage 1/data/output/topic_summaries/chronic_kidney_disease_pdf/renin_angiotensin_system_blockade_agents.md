# renin-angiotensin system blockade agents
*Source: Chronic Kidney Disease.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** renin-angiotensin system blockade agents in CKD

**Clinical Actions:**
- ACE inhibitors (ACEi) inhibit conversion of angiotensin I to angiotensin II
- Angiotensin II receptor blockers (ARB) inhibit activation of angiotensin I receptor by angiotensin II
- Aldosterone antagonists block aldosterone receptor activation
- Continuation of RAS blockade (RASi) is recommended after hyperkalemia events with appropriate monitoring
- Monitoring of potassium (K+) and sodium (Na+) levels is essential during RAS blockade
- NSAIDs can disrupt the renin-angiotensin system by inhibiting prostaglandin synthesis, reducing renal perfusion and exacerbating kidney injury in susceptible patients

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Use of ACEi, ARB, or aldosterone antagonists as first-line agents for renoprotection in CKD
- Monitoring for hyperkalemia and renal function during therapy
- Avoidance of concurrent use with potassium-sparing diuretics or potassium supplements without monitoring
- Dose adjustment based on eGFR and serum potassium levels
- Consideration of nonsteroidal mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists (ns-MRA) like finerenone in specific cases
- Dose adjustment of ACEi/ARB/aldosterone antagonists based on eGFR and serum potassium levels is mandatory
- Avoidance of concurrent use with potassium-sparing diuretics or potassium supplements without close monitoring
