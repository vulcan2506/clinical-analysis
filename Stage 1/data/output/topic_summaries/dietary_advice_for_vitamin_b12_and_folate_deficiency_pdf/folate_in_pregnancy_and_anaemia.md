# folate in pregnancy and anaemia
*Source: Dietary advice for Vitamin B12 and folate deficiency.pdf*

**Nutrient Deficiency:** folate in pregnancy and anaemia

**Risk Factors and Clinical Manifestations:**
- Folic acid is a B-class vitamin essential for red blood cell formation and fetal neural development.
- Folate deficiency can cause anaemia and may result from poor dietary intake or conditions like coeliac disease, Crohn's disease, or alcoholism.
- Pregnant women should take folic acid supplements in the first 12 weeks of pregnancy to reduce neural tube defect risks.
- Folic acid supplementation is recommended 2–3 months before conception to build protective levels.
- Folate demand increases during lactation due to maternal nutrient transfer to breast milk.

**Patient Preparation / Method:**
- Screen for folate deficiency in high-risk groups (e.g., untreated coeliac disease, Crohn's disease, alcoholism).
- Assess dietary folate intake and absorption capacity before supplementation.
- Assess folate status in women planning pregnancy or with a history of neural tube defects in prior pregnancies.
