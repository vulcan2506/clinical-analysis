# leukocyte differential analysis
*Source: Complete_Blood_Count_CBC_Interpretation_and_Analys.pdf*

**Analytical Parameter or Test Component:** leukocyte differential analysis

**Clinical Findings or Diagnostic Patterns:**
- Provides percentages and absolute numbers of neutrophils, lymphocytes, monocytes, eosinophils, and basophils
- Neutrophils (50-70%) act as primary responders to bacterial infections
- Lymphocytes (20-40%) include T cells, B cells, and NK cells for adaptive immunity
- Monocytes (2-8%) differentiate into tissue macrophages for phagocytosis and antigen presentation
- Eosinophils (1-4%) and basophils (0-2%) mediate allergic and parasitic responses
- Neutrophils include segmented neutrophils (mature) and band neutrophils (immature), with band forms >10% indicating left shift and possible bacterial infection
- Lymphocytes include B cells (humoral immunity), T cells (cell-mediated immunity), and NK cells (innate immunity)

**Pre-analytical Conditions:**
- Total leukocyte count must be within 4.0-11.0 × 10⁹/L in healthy adults
- Sample collection must follow CLSI GP39 standards for venous and capillary blood
- Age, race, and physiological conditions must be considered for accurate reference ranges
- CLSI H20-A2 guidelines must be followed for differential count evaluation
- Quality assurance must adhere to CDC Laboratory Guidelines Series 15(3)
- ICSH recommendations must be followed for analysis of white cell size distribution curves to ensure accurate differential counts
- WHO technical reports must be consulted for hemoglobin concentration thresholds when assessing anemia-related leukocyte changes
