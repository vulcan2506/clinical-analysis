# Bone Metastasis Management
*Source: American Thyroid Association.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Bone Metastasis Management in RAIR DTC

**Key Clinical Findings:**
- Treatment with a bone-modifying agent is recommended to decrease the risk of skeletal-related events in patients with symptomatic and/or multiple bone metastases.
- A dosing schedule of every 3 months may be considered to reduce the risk of adverse events, especially osteonecrosis of the jaw, compared with monthly dosing.
- Denosumab is preferred over zoledronic acid due to reduced nephrotoxicity and subcutaneous administration convenience, though it carries a higher risk of serious hypocalcemia.
- Zoledronic acid must be dose-reduced based on renal function and is contraindicated for individuals with a GFR <30 mL/min.
- Extension of dosing intervals to 12 weeks is supported by data but requires monitoring for potential increased symptomatic skeletal events.
- Bone-directed agents are often administered for several years in patients with bone metastases, though randomized controlled trials generally evaluate treatment durations of 1–2 years.
- A systematic review suggests that the incidence of osteonecrosis of the jaw increases after 2 years of bone-directed therapy, while rates of clinically significant hypocalcemia and nephrotoxicity remain low.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Patient must have advanced RAIR DTC with bone metastases.
- Patient must have symptomatic bone metastases or multiple bone metastases.
- Renal function must be assessed prior to zoledronic acid administration (contraindicated if GFR <30 mL/min).
- Calcium and vitamin D status must be monitored, especially for patients on denosumab or with renal failure.
- Risk of osteonecrosis of the jaw must be weighed against the benefit of reduced dosing frequency.
- Hypocalcemia risk must be particularly monitored in patients with renal failure treated with denosumab.
