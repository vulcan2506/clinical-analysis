# Surgical Management for Low-Risk Thyroid Cancer
*Source: American Thyroid Association.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Surgical Management for Low-Risk Thyroid Cancer

**Key Clinical Findings:**
- Thyroid lobectomy is the initial surgical procedure for thyroid cancer ≤2 cm without gross extra-thyroidal extension (cT1) and without metastases (cN0M0).
- For unilateral thyroid cancer >2 and ≤4 cm (cT2N0M0), thyroid lobectomy may be preferred due to lower risk and side effects, but total thyroidectomy may be adopted based on disease features, suspicious contralateral nodularity, or patient preferences.
- Counsel patients about the possibility of conversion to total thyroidectomy or need for subsequent completion thyroidectomy if higher-risk factors emerge intraoperatively or postoperatively.
- Total thyroidectomy is associated with significantly higher complication rates (e.g., recurrent laryngeal nerve injury, hypocalcemia, hemorrhage) compared to thyroid lobectomy.
- Surgeon experience influences complication rates, with higher-volume surgeons having lower risks for both lobectomy and total thyroidectomy.
- Accurate preoperative ultrasound and targeted use of frozen section can help identify patients best treated with total thyroidectomy, reducing the need for subsequent completion thyroidectomy.
- Total thyroidectomy necessitates lifelong thyroid hormone replacement, while lobectomy is associated with postoperative biochemical hypothyroidism (estimated 22% average) and clinical overt hypothyroidism (4% average).

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Thyroid cancer ≤2 cm without gross extra-thyroidal extension (cT1) and without metastases (cN0M0) for initial thyroid lobectomy recommendation.
- Unilateral thyroid cancer >2 and ≤4 cm (cT2N0M0) for consideration of thyroid lobectomy as preferred initial treatment.
- Preoperative FNA biopsy diagnostic for DTC (typically conventional PTC) to inform surgical planning.
- Multidisciplinary team coordination preoperatively to align surgery with overall treatment strategy and follow-up plan.
- Patient preferences and disease features (e.g., suspicious contralateral nodularity) should guide the choice between lobectomy and total thyroidectomy for cT2N0M0 tumors.
- Preoperative identification of multifocality or suspicious contralateral nodules via ultrasound-guided FNA to inform surgical planning for cT1b-T2N0M0 tumors.
