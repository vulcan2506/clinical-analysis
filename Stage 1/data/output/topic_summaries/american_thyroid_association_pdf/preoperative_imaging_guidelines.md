# Preoperative Imaging Guidelines
*Source: American Thyroid Association.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Preoperative Imaging Guidelines for Differentiated Thyroid Cancer

**Key Clinical Findings:**
- Preoperative cross-sectional imaging (CT/MRI with IV contrast) of the neck and mediastinum is recommended for patients with clinical suspicion for advanced or invasive disease, including gross extrathyroidal extension, bulky/invasive adenopathy, or aerodigestive/thoracic involvement.
- Preoperative cross-sectional imaging of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis is recommended when results will influence the extent of surgery.
- Routine preoperative 18F-FDG-PET/CT is not recommended prior to surgery.
- Ultrasound is operator-dependent and may be insufficient for deep or acoustically shadowed anatomical structures; alternative imaging (CT/MRI) may be preferable in select cases.
- For locally invasive DTC, cross-sectional imaging is useful for surgical planning to delineate laryngeal, tracheal, esophageal, or vascular involvement.
- Use of MRI as an alternative to CT when ionizing radiation exposure is a concern, given comparable performance in detecting cervical nodal disease and lower nephrotoxicity of MRI contrast agents
- Measurement of spot urinary iodine level when there is a concern about iodine clearance after IV contrast administration

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Clinical suspicion for advanced or invasive disease (e.g., gross extrathyroidal extension, bulky/invasive adenopathy, or aerodigestive/thoracic involvement).
- Results of imaging must influence the extent of surgery for preoperative chest/abdomen/pelvis imaging to be recommended.
- Suspected preoperative aerodigestive tract invasion may require tracheoscopy and/or esophagoscopy with/without ultrasonography.
- Locally invasive primary cancers may present with symptoms such as rapid tumor enlargement, vocal cord paralysis, or progressive dysphagia.
- IV contrast should be used in cross-sectional imaging to delineate anatomical relationships between tumor and adjacent structures.
- Consideration of tracheoscopy and/or esophagoscopy with or without ultrasonography for suspected aerodigestive tract invasion prior to resection
- Evaluation of sonographic features of the primary tumor (e.g., posterior capsular penetration, disease reaching the mediastinum) to prompt axial imaging
