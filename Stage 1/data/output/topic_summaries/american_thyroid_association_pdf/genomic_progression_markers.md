# Genomic Progression Markers
*Source: American Thyroid Association.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Genomic progression markers in differentiated thyroid cancer

**Key Clinical Findings:**
- Identify early genomic changes (e.g., BRAFV600E, RAS, fusions like RET/ALK) as baseline markers for papillary carcinoma
- Monitor for progression-associated events (e.g., TERT promoter mutations, TP53, CDKN2A/B loss) in combination with early changes
- Assess genomic markers in follicular carcinoma, IEFVPTC, and oncocytic carcinoma to stratify progression risk
- Evaluate chromosomal alterations (e.g., 2q gain, 22q loss, near-haploid genomes) as potential progression indicators
- Use APOBEC activity and global DNA hypomethylation as biomarkers for aggressive disease behavior
- Identify BRAF non-V600E mutations as early genomic changes in papillary carcinoma
- Evaluate DICER1, EIF1AX, and PTEN mutations as early genomic changes in follicular carcinoma and IEFVPTC

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Tumor tissue must be tested for early genomic changes (mutations/fusions) to establish baseline risk
- Progression-associated genomic events require confirmation alongside clinical or pathological progression
- Testing should prioritize high-risk subtypes (e.g., oncocytic carcinoma) due to higher progression potential
- Combination of early and progression-associated markers must be interpreted in the context of disease stage
- Molecular profiling should align with institutional capabilities and guideline-recommended testing panels
- Testing for DICER1, EIF1AX, and PTEN mutations is required to establish baseline risk in follicular carcinoma and IEFVPTC
- Testing for DAXX, ARHGGAP35, and APC mutations is required to establish baseline risk in oncocytic carcinoma
