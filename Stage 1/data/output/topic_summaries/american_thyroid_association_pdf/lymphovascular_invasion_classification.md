# Lymphovascular Invasion Classification
*Source: American Thyroid Association.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Lymphovascular Invasion Classification

**Key Clinical Findings:**
- Differentiate between angioinvasion and lymphatic invasion for synoptic reporting in tumor staging
- Vascular invasion is associated with increased risk of recurrence and distant metastasis in DTC
- Lymphatic invasion alone may not independently predict recurrence but is linked to adverse prognostic factors
- FTC with extensive vascular invasion (≥4 vessels) is classified as high-risk with 30–55% recurrence risk
- OTC with widely invasive features (≥4 vessels or ≥4 foci of capsular invasion) is associated with higher recurrence and mortality
- Extranodal extension (both micro- and macro-) is associated with increased risk of recurrence and decreased survival, and is an independent predictor of recurrence in multivariable analyses
- Gross extrathyroidal extension (into subcutaneous soft tissues, larynx, trachea, esophagus, or recurrent laryngeal nerve) carries a 23–40% risk of recurrence, higher than minimal extrathyroidal extension (3–9%)

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Synoptic pathology reporting must distinguish between lymphatic and vascular invasion for accurate staging
- Vascular invasion assessment requires quantification of involved vessels (e.g., <4 vs. ≥4 vessels in FTC)
- Lymph node metastasis evaluation must include size (>2 mm) and number of metastatic nodes for risk stratification
- Synoptic pathology reporting must include extranodal extension status (micro- or macro-) for accurate risk stratification
- Lymph node yield (LNY) thresholds for adequacy should consider primary tumor size (e.g., ≥6 nodes in T1b, ≥9 in T2, ≥19 in T3) to predict occult nodal disease
