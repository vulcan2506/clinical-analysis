# Preoperative Lymph Node Evaluation
*Source: American Thyroid Association.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Preoperative Lymph Node Evaluation

**Key Clinical Findings:**
- Preoperative neck ultrasound is recommended for all patients undergoing surgery for malignant cytologic or molecular findings to evaluate cervical lymph nodes and gross extrathyroidal extension.
- Ultrasound-guided FNA of sonographically suspicious lymph nodes >8–10 mm in smallest diameter should be performed to confirm malignancy if it would change management.
- FNA-Tg washout may be performed in select preoperative patients but interpretation may be difficult with an intact thyroid gland.
- Surgical approach may be altered based on preoperative ultrasound findings of suspicious cervical adenopathy in 20% of patients.
- Ultrasound evaluation of extrathyroidal extension is important for surgical planning, often indicating total thyroidectomy and RAI.
- Ultrasound-guided FNA should include cytology and/or FNA-Tg washout for suspicious lymph nodes, with FNA-Tg particularly useful in cystic, discordant, or inadequately sampled lymph nodes.
- CT imaging may be used adjunctively with ultrasound for advanced locoregional disease to improve diagnostic accuracy, despite higher cost and radiation exposure.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Patients must have malignant cytologic or molecular findings to warrant preoperative neck ultrasound.
- Lymph nodes must be sonographically suspicious and >8–10 mm in smallest diameter for ultrasound-guided FNA.
- FNA-Tg washout is conditional and may be performed only in select preoperative patients.
- Suspicious lymph nodes should exhibit sonographic features such as cystic change, microcalcifications, or abnormal vascularity.
- Advanced locoregional disease may require additional CT imaging for surgical planning.
- FNA-Tg washout is valid in the presence of positive serum TgAb, but false positives may occur in the central compartment with an intact thyroid gland.
- FNA-Tg threshold for diagnosing metastatic lymph nodes is not definitively established; thresholds of 1.0 ng/mL (high NPV) or 28.5 ng/mL (high specificity) may be considered based on available evidence.
