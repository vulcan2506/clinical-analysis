# Recurrence Risk Stratification in Thyroid Cancer
*Source: American Thyroid Association.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Recurrence Risk Stratification in Thyroid Cancer

**Key Clinical Findings:**
- Stratify recurrence risk based on tumor size, extrathyroidal extension, lymph node metastases, and distant metastases.
- Incorporate histological subtype (e.g., Hurthle cell, follicular variant) and genetic mutations (e.g., BRAF, TERT promoter) into risk assessment.
- Use lymph node ratio and extranodal extension as prognostic indicators for recurrence.
- Consider patient age (<45 vs ≥45 years) and sex as independent risk modifiers.
- Integrate molecular markers (e.g., NTRK fusions, ALK rearrangements) for risk refinement.
- Stratify recurrence risk based on the presence of minimal extrathyroidal extension (e.g., strap muscle invasion) as an independent prognostic factor.
- Incorporate response-to-therapy variables (e.g., biochemical incomplete response, structural incomplete response) to dynamically refine initial recurrence risk estimates.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Histopathological confirmation of tumor characteristics (e.g., extrathyroidal extension, lymph node status).
- Molecular testing for BRAF, TERT promoter, and other relevant mutations in high-risk cases.
- Baseline thyroglobulin levels and imaging (e.g., ultrasound, PET/CT) for structural disease assessment.
- Documentation of tumor size, multifocality, and presence of distant metastases.
- Comprehensive staging per AJCC/TNM classification.
- Documentation of tumor multifocality and its impact on recurrence risk stratification.
- Molecular testing for DICER1 mutations in follicular-patterned thyroid tumors, particularly in pediatric and young adult patients.
