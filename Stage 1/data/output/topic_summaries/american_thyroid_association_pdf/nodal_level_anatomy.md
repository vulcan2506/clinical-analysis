# Nodal Level Anatomy
*Source: American Thyroid Association.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Nodal Level Anatomy

**Key Clinical Findings:**
- Nodal levels are defined using specific anatomical landmarks for precise surgical and diagnostic reference.
- Anatomical landmarks are standardized to ensure consistent communication among clinicians.
- Nodal levels are used to guide the extent of lymph node dissection in differentiated thyroid cancer.
- Nodal levels are categorized into central (level VI) and lateral (levels II–V) compartments, with level I excluded in thyroid cancer management.
- The sternocleidomastoid muscle, hyoid bone, clavicle, and omohyoid muscle serve as key anatomical landmarks for defining nodal levels.
- Level VII (upper mediastinal nodes) is included in the anatomical classification but may not be routinely dissected unless clinically indicated.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Accurate identification of anatomical landmarks is mandatory for proper nodal level classification.
- Surgical planning must incorporate nodal level anatomy to determine appropriate lymph node dissection.
- Preoperative imaging (e.g., ultrasound or CT) is required to map nodal involvement and guide level-specific dissection.
- Intraoperative identification of the recurrent laryngeal nerve and parathyroid glands is mandatory to avoid injury during level VI dissection.
