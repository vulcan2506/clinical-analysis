# Thyroid Cancer Incidence Trends
*Source: American Thyroid Association.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Thyroid Cancer Incidence Trends + Histomorphology and Molecular Categorization

**Key Clinical Findings:**
- Approximately 90% of thyroid cancer cases are well differentiated and classified based on predominant histomorphology.
- Well-differentiated thyroid carcinomas can now also be categorized based on their molecular profiles.
- Papillary thyroid carcinoma (PTC) is the most common type of differentiated thyroid cancer (DTC).
- PTC is typically indolent and associated with excellent long-term survival.
- The rise in PTC incidence is largely attributed to increased detection from widespread use of sensitive imaging techniques (e.g., neck ultrasounds, CT scans) and fine-needle aspiration biopsies, which identify small, indolent tumors that may not have caused clinical symptoms.
- This phenomenon, termed 'overdiagnosis,' does not reflect a true increase in disease burden but rather a shift in diagnostic thresholds.
- Diagnostic advances like ultrasound and fine-needle aspiration (FNA) biopsy enable the detection of subclinical thyroid cancers, including papillary thyroid microcarcinomas (≤1 cm), which were previously undiagnosed.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Utilization of the 5th edition of the WHO Classification of Thyroid Tumors for descriptions of non-anaplastic follicular cell-derived thyroid carcinomas and NIFTP.
