# Dynamic Risk Stratification Framework
*Source: American Thyroid Association.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Dynamic Risk Stratification Framework + Response to Therapy Categories

**Key Clinical Findings:**
- Recurrence risk is reassessed at every visit and may change over time based on response to therapy.
- Excellent response to therapy (regardless of initial risk) is associated with recurrence rates <15%, supporting de-escalation of monitoring.
- Indeterminate or biochemically incomplete responses warrant continued biochemical and imaging follow-up due to higher recurrence risks (up to 20% and 53%, respectively).
- Structurally incomplete responses require ongoing imaging, with additional cross-sectional or PET/CT if Tg/TgAb levels are rising.
- Reoperation for recurrent disease should consider size, location, growth pace, patient factors, and multidisciplinary input.
- Recurrence risk estimates can be assessed at every clinical visit and may change over time based on therapy response.
- Patients with stable Tg and/or TgAb levels should have ongoing imaging, whereas those with rising titers require additional imaging (cross-sectional or PET/CT).

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Initial pathology, imaging, and clinical evaluations must be completed to estimate risk of recurrence (ROR).
- Response to therapy must be categorized (excellent, indeterminate, biochemically incomplete, structurally incomplete) to guide monitoring.
- For reoperation decisions, prior surgical extent, disease size/location, and patient comorbidities must be assessed.
- Shared decision-making with an experienced multidisciplinary team is required for monitoring vs. intervention decisions.
- Reoperative surgery should be performed in referral centers with expertise in thyroid cancer reoperations.
- Initial tumor risk and response to initial therapy must be estimated to determine individual recurrence risk.
- Decisions regarding monitoring versus intervention must involve shared decision-making with the patient and an experienced multidisciplinary team.
