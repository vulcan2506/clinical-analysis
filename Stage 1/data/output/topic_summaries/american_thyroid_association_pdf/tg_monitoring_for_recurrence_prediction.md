# Tg Monitoring for Recurrence Prediction
*Source: American Thyroid Association.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Tg Monitoring for Recurrence Prediction

**Key Clinical Findings:**
- Serum Tg monitoring is useful for predicting recurrence in patients after total thyroidectomy and RAI ablation, with stimulated Tg ≥30 ng/mL associated with reduced 10-year survival.
- Serum Tg is not predictive of recurrence in patients after thyroid lobectomy.
- A single Tg value ~12 weeks post-lobectomy with normal TSH is recommended to ensure it is not unexpectedly elevated.
- Serum Tg levels up to 1–2.5 ng/mL while on LT4 therapy generally identify low-risk patients after total/near-total thyroidectomy without RAI.
- Stimulated Tg may predict response to RAI ablative therapy, with levels below 4.4 ng/mL indicating successful response.
- TSH-stimulated Tg and its reduction index (pre- and post-RAI ablation) are predictors of disease progression and recurrence.
- Additional RAI for persistently elevated serum Tg is associated with improved long-term outcomes in some patients.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Patients must have undergone total thyroidectomy and RAI ablation for Tg monitoring to predict recurrence.
- TSH-stimulated Tg is required for accurate prediction in high-risk patients.
- Post-lobectomy patients require a single Tg measurement ~12 weeks post-surgery with normal TSH.
- TSH-stimulated serum Tg is required pre-RAI ablation to assess tumor burden and predict response to therapy.
- Post-lobectomy patients not requiring LT4 therapy can be monitored with serum Tg, Tg/TSH ratio, and neck ultrasound for recurrence.
