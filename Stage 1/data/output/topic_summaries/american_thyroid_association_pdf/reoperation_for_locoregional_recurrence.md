# Reoperation for Locoregional Recurrence
*Source: American Thyroid Association.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Reoperation for Locoregional Recurrence + Nonsurgical Local Therapies

**Key Clinical Findings:**
- Reoperation is prioritized for clinically apparent, macroscopic nodal disease identified via imaging rather than serum Tg elevation
- Transdisciplinary coordination and patient preference are central to decision-making
- Compartmental dissection is recommended over 'berry-picking' to reduce recurrence risk
- Percutaneous ethanol ablation and RFA are reserved for poor surgical candidates or as adjuncts
- RAI therapy may be considered post-local therapy or if local therapy is infeasible, but evidence is low quality
- Reoperative central neck dissection should include at least one paratracheal region with prelaryngeal and pretracheal sub-compartments, while bilateral central neck dissection is reserved for bilateral recurrence due to risks of bilateral recurrent nerve injury and permanent hypoparathyroidism
- Revision lateral neck dissection typically involves Levels II, III, IV, and sometimes V, especially if prior compartmental dissection was incomplete

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Cytological confirmation of disease is generally advised unless findings won’t change management
- High-resolution anatomical studies (ultrasound, CT with contrast, 18FDG-PET/CT, or RAI-SPECT/CT) are mandatory for preoperative planning
- Ultrasound-guided FNA with Tg measurement should confirm nodal recurrence when feasible
- Disease target must be clearly defined with a technical goal for reoperation
- Expertise in the technique is required for nonsurgical therapies (ethanol ablation, RFA)
- Bilateral central neck dissection is advised only when required by recurrence on both sides of the neck due to risks of bilateral recurrent nerve injury and permanent hypoparathyroidism
- Percutaneous ethanol ablation and RFA should ideally be performed for disease that is cytologically proven based on FNA when feasible
