# MKI Treatment Adverse Event Prevention
*Source: American Thyroid Association.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** MKI Treatment Adverse Event Prevention

**Key Clinical Findings:**
- Evaluate patients at baseline and monitor no less often than every 2 weeks for the first 2 months of MKI therapy, then generally at 1- or 2-month intervals thereafter
- Implement proactive supportive care, including timely identification and management of adverse events, to minimize dose interruptions, reductions, and optimize treatment adherence
- Educate patients on preventive measures for specific adverse events (e.g., daily home blood pressure monitoring for hypertension, symptom diaries for diarrhea, and lifestyle modifications for HFS)
- Monitor TSH, free T4, serum electrolytes, blood counts, proteinuria, and QTc prolongation periodically during MKI therapy
- Consider baseline cardiac function assessment in high-risk patients (e.g., those with hypertension, coronary artery disease, or diabetes)
- Remove hyperkeratotic areas and calluses on hands and feet before treatment initiation and during therapy
- Apply 10% urea-based cream for HFS prevention and 20–40% urea-based creams for emerging HFS

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Blood pressure must be well controlled prior to initiating MKI therapy
- Baseline echocardiogram or other cardiac function study should be considered in selected high-risk patients
- Patients with certain comorbid illnesses (e.g., uncontrolled hypertension, recent acute cardiac syndrome, or bleeding disorders) may be precluded from safely receiving VEGFR MKIs
- Thyroid hormone replacement therapy must be adjusted to maintain appropriate TSH suppression during MKI therapy
- Prophylactic measures (e.g., urea-based creams, dietary adjustments) should be initiated before or at the start of MKI therapy to prevent HFS and diarrhea
- EKG evaluation is required before initiating treatment to assess for QTc prolongation
- Hyperkeratotic lesions and calluses must be removed before treatment initiation to prevent exacerbation of HFS
