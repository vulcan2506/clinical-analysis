# RAI Refractory DTC Criteria
*Source: American Thyroid Association.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** RAI Refractory DTC Criteria

**Key Clinical Findings:**
- Diagnosis of RAI-refractory DTC requires prior ablative or treatment dose of RAI without evidence of uptake on post-therapy scan despite structurally apparent disease
- Progression of disease within 6 months after therapeutic RAI with documented uptake is a strong indicator of refractoriness
- Absence of 131I uptake on diagnostic or post-therapy whole-body scans in the presence of detectable disease suggests reduced RAI sensitivity
- Patients with heterogeneous RAI uptake across tumor foci may benefit from multimodal therapy rather than RAI alone
- Shared decision-making is essential for determining appropriateness of continued RAI therapy in metastatic DTC
- Response to RAI therapy can be heterogeneous, and there is a spectrum of responsiveness, requiring risk stratification rather than definitive mandates for RAI use
- Patients with uptake in all known lesions who do not achieve a complete response but remain stable (per RECIST) may not necessarily be considered RAI-refractory, and continued RAI may be considered based on high uptake, low 18FDG uptake, and good tolerance

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Prior administration of ablative or treatment dose of RAI to assess refractoriness
- Structural or 18FDG-PET imaging confirming disease presence despite lack of RAI uptake
- Documented progression within 6 months after therapeutic RAI with uptake for definitive refractoriness
- Confirmation of metastatic disease via imaging (CT/MRI/PET) in the absence of RAI uptake
- Patient-specific factors (age, comorbidities, histology) considered for risk stratification
- Patient preparation with low iodine diet and consideration of confounding variables (e.g., recent contrast use) to avoid misinterpreting scans as negative for RAI uptake
