# RAI Therapy De-escalation in Low-Risk DTC
*Source: American Thyroid Association.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** RAI Therapy De-escalation in Low-Risk DTC

**Key Clinical Findings:**
- Total thyroidectomy without RAI is non-inferior to total thyroidectomy with RAI in low-risk DTC patients.
- Excellent biochemical response early on reduces likelihood of recurrence in low-risk DTC.
- Ultrasound of low-risk patients with excellent biochemical responses is not recommended due to high false positive rates.
- Diagnostic radioiodine WBS is not routinely required for low-risk patients with excellent response to therapy.
- SPECT-CT may be used to localize radioiodine uptake and reduce equivocal findings in selected cases.
- Patients with excellent biochemical response (e.g., stimulated Tg <1 mcg/L) do not require routine ultrasound surveillance due to high false positive rates and low clinical utility.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Low-risk DTC patients must achieve excellent biochemical response (e.g., stimulated Tg <1 mcg/L) to avoid routine RAI.
- Post-therapy WBS showing no uptake outside the thyroid bed indicates low need for subsequent diagnostic WBS.
- Patients with rising Tg or TgAb levels may require diagnostic WBS despite negative neck ultrasound.
- Intermediate-high and high-risk patients may undergo diagnostic WBS if clinical suspicion for recurrence exists.
- SPECT-CT is preferred for anatomical localization when planar imaging is equivocal.
- Post-therapy WBS showing no uptake outside the thyroid bed indicates low need for subsequent diagnostic WBS, particularly in low-risk DTC patients with excellent response.
