# Targeted Therapy for BRAF-Mutant DTC
*Source: American Thyroid Association.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Targeted Therapy for BRAF-Mutant DTC

**Key Clinical Findings:**
- Enrollment in a clinical trial or first-line lenvatinib is suggested for progressive RAIR DTC with actionable non-NTRK/RET/ALK/BRAFV600E targets.
- Genomic analysis is recommended to identify potentially actionable oncogenic targets in RAIR DTC.
- RAS mutations (e.g., N/H/K RAS) are attractive targets due to MAPK pathway upregulation in FTCs, FVPTCs, and PDTCs.
- KRAS G12C-specific inhibitors (sotorasib, adagrasib) may be considered for KRAS G12C mutation-positive DTC, despite not being FDA-approved for this indication.
- Tipifarnib, a farnesyl transferase inhibitor, has been studied in HRAS mutation-positive malignancies but showed no response in HRAS mutation-positive thyroid cancer.
- Enrollment in a clinical trial is suggested for progressive RAIR DTC with actionable non-NTRK/RET/ALK/BRAFV600E targets.
- Use of sotorasib or adagrasib may be considered for KRAS G12C mutation-positive DTC despite lack of FDA approval for this indication.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Progressive RAIR DTC with actionable non-NTRK/RET/ALK/BRAFV600E targets.
- Identification of oncogenic targets via genomic analysis.
- Presence of KRAS G12C mutation for consideration of sotorasib or adagrasib.
- HRAS mutation for potential tipifarnib use (though not effective in thyroid cancer).
- Identification of oncogenic targets via genomic analysis in RAIR DTC.
