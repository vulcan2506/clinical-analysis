# TSH Suppression Efficacy in DTC
*Source: American Thyroid Association.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** TSH suppression efficacy in DTC

**Key Clinical Findings:**
- TSH suppression therapy reduces recurrence risk in intermediate- and high-risk DTC patients, particularly when pre-ablation stimulated thyroglobulin is ≥1 ng/mL.
- TSH suppression is less beneficial in low-risk patients, especially those with pre-ablation stimulated thyroglobulin <1 ng/mL.
- Post-lobectomy patients with low- or intermediate-risk DTC show no significant recurrence benefit from TSH suppression.
- TSH suppression increases risks of osteoporosis, fractures, and cardiovascular events, necessitating individualized risk-benefit assessment.
- Active surveillance of low-risk papillary microcarcinoma may avoid unnecessary TSH suppression.
- TSH suppression therapy may increase the risk of atrial fibrillation, particularly in older adults or those with pre-existing cardiac conditions.
- Long-term TSH suppression without monitoring may lead to reduced bone mineral density and increased fracture risk, even in patients without overt osteoporosis.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Pre-ablation stimulated thyroglobulin ≥1 ng/mL for TSH suppression benefit in intermediate/high-risk patients.
- Dynamic risk stratification to guide TSH suppression intensity.
- Bone mineral density monitoring in patients undergoing prolonged TSH suppression.
- Cardiovascular risk assessment in elderly patients or those with pre-existing cardiac conditions.
- Regular thyroglobulin monitoring to assess disease status and adjust TSH suppression.
- Cardiovascular risk assessment, including evaluation for atrial fibrillation, should be performed before initiating TSH suppression in elderly patients or those with cardiac comorbidities.
- Bone mineral density monitoring (e.g., DEXA scans) is required at baseline and periodically during prolonged TSH suppression, especially in postmenopausal women and older men.
