# Management of RAIR Metastatic DTC
*Source: American Thyroid Association.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Management of RAIR Metastatic DTC

**Key Clinical Findings:**
- Monitor asymptomatic or stable RAIR metastatic DTC with TSH-suppressive therapy and serial imaging every 3–12 months
- Apply RECIST 1.1 criteria to identify target lesions (≥1 cm) and define stability as <20% increase in sum of longest diameters without new foci
- Initiate systemic therapy only upon development of worrisome progressive disease, new lesions, or impending symptoms via shared decision-making
- Do not initiate systemic therapy based on increasing Tg levels alone without structural disease progression
- Consider more frequent imaging if rapid Tg rise, initial metastatic identification, or symptom changes occur
- Apply RECIST 1.1 criteria to assess disease progression, defining stability as <20% increase in sum of longest diameters of target lesions (≥1 cm) without new metastatic foci
- Consider more frequent imaging (e.g., <3 months) when metastatic disease is initially identified, Tg levels rise rapidly, or patient symptoms change

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Patient must have asymptomatic, stable, or minimally progressive disease, or have clinically significant comorbidities for observation strategy
- Target lesions must be ≥1 cm on cross-sectional imaging for RECIST 1.1 assessment
- Systemic therapy consideration requires evidence of structural progression or new lesions, not just biochemical changes
- Imaging frequency should be individualized based on disease burden, lesion location, histological subtype, and Tg doubling time
- Bone metastases must be assessed clinically as RECIST 1.1 does not measure them
- Determine the rate of progression of radiographically evident lesions as part of serial assessment
- Assess patient for symptoms, risk of symptomatic progression, and comorbidities before initiating monitoring strategy
