# External Beam Radiotherapy Outcomes
*Source: American Thyroid Association.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** External Beam Radiotherapy (EBRT) Outcomes in Differentiated Thyroid Cancer

**Key Clinical Findings:**
- EBRT is considered for locoregionally advanced or recurrent differentiated thyroid cancer (DTC) with unresectable or gross residual disease.
- Conformal radiotherapy techniques (e.g., intensity-modulated radiation therapy) are recommended to minimize morbidity while optimizing locoregional control.
- Adjuvant EBRT may improve locoregional control in high-risk patients with pT4 tumors or extensive extrathyroidal extension.
- EBRT is typically reserved for patients with high-risk features or those with iodine-refractory disease.
- Combined EBRT and radioactive iodine therapy may be used in select cases for metastatic or recurrent DTC.
- EBRT may be considered in patients with iodine-refractory disease who are not surgical candidates or have unresectable locoregional recurrence.
- Concurrent chemotherapy with EBRT (e.g., doxorubicin) may be used in select cases of advanced or recurrent DTC to enhance local control.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Histologically confirmed differentiated thyroid cancer with locoregionally advanced or recurrent disease.
- Unresectable or gross residual disease after surgery.
- Documented high-risk features (e.g., extensive extrathyroidal extension, lymph node involvement, or distant metastases).
- Prior treatment with radioactive iodine (unless contraindicated or ineffective).
- Performance status and life expectancy sufficient to justify adjuvant therapy.
- Documented iodine-refractory status (e.g., negative iodine scan with elevated thyroglobulin) for EBRT consideration in metastatic disease.
- Assessment of tumor volume and anatomical constraints to guide individualized EBRT planning.
