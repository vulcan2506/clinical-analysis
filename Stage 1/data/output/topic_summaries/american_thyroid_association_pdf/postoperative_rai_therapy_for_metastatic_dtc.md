# Postoperative RAI Therapy for Metastatic DTC
*Source: American Thyroid Association.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Postoperative RAI Therapy for Metastatic DTC

**Key Clinical Findings:**
- RAI therapy is routinely recommended after total thyroidectomy in patients with distant metastases.
- Postoperative RAI can be used for remnant ablation, adjuvant therapy, or treatment of known residual/metastatic disease.
- Decision to administer RAI should consider histopathology, comorbidities, serological biomarkers, metabolic function, logistical feasibility, and patient preferences.
- Favorable response to RAI is observed in tumors with RAS mutations and RET fusions, while resistance is common in BRAFV600E, TERT promoter, and/or TP53 mutations.
- Dosimetry-guided RAI therapy may be preferable in older patients or those with diffuse bilateral pulmonary metastases.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Presence of distant metastases for routine RAI recommendation.
- Total thyroidectomy performed prior to RAI therapy.
- Consideration of patient-specific factors (e.g., histopathology, comorbidities, biomarkers) before RAI administration.
- Multidisciplinary management for determining administered activity level.
- Pre-therapy imaging and metabolic function panel evaluation.
- Absence of suspicious lateral neck lymph nodes on ultrasound prior to randomization in low-risk DTC patients evaluating non-RAI follow-up strategies.
- Use of rhTSH stimulation prior to administration of low-dose RAI (1.1 GBq) in low-risk DTC patients.
