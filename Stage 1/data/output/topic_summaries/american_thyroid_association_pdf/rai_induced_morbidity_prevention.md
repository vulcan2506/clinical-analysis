# RAI-Induced Morbidity Prevention
*Source: American Thyroid Association.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** RAI-Induced Morbidity Prevention

**Key Clinical Findings:**
- Counsel patients about acute/chronic salivary gland morbidity, lacrimal duct stenosis, and secondary malignancy risks before RAI therapy.
- Recommend hydration and dental preventive strategies for patients with xerostomia to reduce dental caries risk.
- Consider surgical correction for nasolacrimal outflow obstruction presenting with epiphora or infection risk.
- Use nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs for salivary gland pain/swelling; reserve short steroids for severe cases.
- Monitor baseline CBC and renal function before RAI therapy; consider dosimetry-guided therapy in renal impairment.
- Recommend local application of ice for patients with painful sialadenitis.
- Counsel patients that thyroid hormone withdrawal is associated with a lower rate of salivary gland swelling compared to rhTSH preparation.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Negative pregnancy test for female patients of reproductive age prior to RAI therapy.
- Avoid RAI in lactating females; defer therapy until 3 months post-lactation or confirm no breast uptake via diagnostic 123I scan.
- Counsel male patients receiving cumulative RAI >14.8 GBq about potential infertility risks.
- Ensure benefits of RAI therapy outweigh risks, particularly with higher administered activities.
- Avoid sour candies/lemon slices within 1 hour of RAI therapy to prevent increased salivary gland radiation exposure.
- Consider dosimetry-guided therapy in patients with significant renal failure.
- Perform a diagnostic 123I scan in recently lactating women to detect breast uptake that may warrant deferral of therapy.
