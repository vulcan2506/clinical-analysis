# Germline Genetic Testing Principles
*Source: American Thyroid Association.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Germline Genetic Testing Principles for DTC

**Key Clinical Findings:**
- Conduct pre-test counseling including three- to four-generation pedigree analysis, patient risk assessment, psychosocial evaluation, education on hereditary cancer syndromes, and informed consent.
- Utilize multigene panel testing due to overlapping phenotypes of hereditary predisposition conditions.
- Perform post-test counseling with verbal and written disclosure of results, cancer risk implications, medical management options, and family implications.
- Communicate test results to treating physicians, especially when counseling is performed by a genetic counselor.
- Offer germline genetic testing via certified genetic counselors or providers with cancer genetics expertise, including telehealth options if in-person access is unavailable.
- Ideally, germline genetic testing should be conducted by a certified genetic counselor or other provider (e.g., endocrinologist, oncologist, geneticist) with expertise in cancer genetics and thyroid cancer.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Clinical suspicion for Cowden/PTEN hamartoma tumor syndrome (e.g., DTC with non-thyroid malignancies or features).
- Diagnosis of cribriform morular thyroid carcinoma (APC gene-associated).
- Pediatric differentiated thyroid cancer with features suggestive of DICER1 tumor predisposition.
- Presence of tumor combinations or family history raising concern for rare hereditary syndromes (e.g., Carney complex, Werner syndrome).
- Evaluation of non-thyroid malignancies in the family for non-syndromic FNMTC to guide genetic testing decisions.
- Evaluation of absolute cancer risks to the patient based on family history during pre-test counseling.
