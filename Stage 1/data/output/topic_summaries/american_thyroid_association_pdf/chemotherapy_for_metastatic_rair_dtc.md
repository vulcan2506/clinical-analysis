# Chemotherapy for Metastatic RAIR DTC
*Source: American Thyroid Association.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Chemotherapy for Metastatic RAIR DTC + Oligometastatic RAIR DTC management

**Key Clinical Findings:**
- Cytotoxic chemotherapy may be considered in patients with RAIR DTC with metastatic, rapidly progressive, symptomatic, and/or imminently threatening disease not amenable to other approaches.
- Use of cytotoxic chemotherapy within the context of a therapeutic clinical trial is preferred.
- Focal ablative treatment may be considered for patients with solitary or oligometastatic RAIR DTC (two to five lesions).
- Local treatment modalities (surgery, thermo-ablation, radiation, or combinations) may be warranted for solitary or oligometastatic RAIR DTC, tailored to patient and disease factors.
- Ablative treatment approaches (e.g., metastectomy, SBRT/SABR, thermal ablation) may be considered for oligometastatic or oligo-progressive disease, especially if amenable.
- Thermal ablation techniques (RFA, cryoablation, microwave ablation) are common treatments for lung and bone metastases in RAIR DTC, with RFA most frequently employed in retrospective reviews.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Metastatic, rapidly progressive, symptomatic, and/or imminently threatening disease not amenable to other approaches.
- Disease must be RAIR (radioactive iodine-refractory) DTC.
- Oligometastatic disease defined as two to five metastatic deposits, limited to one to two organs, or dynamic models including progressive disease.
- Multidisciplinary team evaluation to determine optimal local treatment approach.
- Patient suitability for local ablative therapies (e.g., lesion size, location, performance status).
- Oligometastatic disease may be defined dynamically, incorporating lesions emerging synchronously or metachronously, and including progressive disease.
