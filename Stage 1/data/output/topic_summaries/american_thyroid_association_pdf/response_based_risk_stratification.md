# Response-Based Risk Stratification
*Source: American Thyroid Association.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Response-Based Risk Stratification

**Key Clinical Findings:**
- Stratify patients into Excellent, Indeterminate, Biochemically Incomplete, or Structural Incomplete response categories based on Tg levels, imaging findings, and antibody trends.
- Use nonstimulated or stimulated Tg thresholds to assess disease status post-total thyroidectomy/neck dissection with or without RAI ablation.
- Set TSH goals based on response category: normal range for Excellent/Indeterminate, below normal for Biochemically Incomplete (with consideration of comorbidities).
- Measure serum Tg 6–12 weeks post-lobectomy to ensure it is not unexpectedly elevated (no specific cutoff defined).
- Utilize Tg trends and TSH stimulation (endogenous or rhTSH) to detect residual tumor or recurrence.
- Measurement of serum Tg on one occasion 6–12 weeks after thyroid lobectomy with a normal TSH may be helpful to ensure it is not unexpectedly elevated (Good Practice Statement).
- Both basal Tg and stimulated Tg (endogenous or rhTSH) measurements are recommended postoperatively to identify persistent disease and predict recurrence.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Post-total thyroidectomy/neck dissection with RAI ablation: Nonstimulated Tg <0.2 or stimulated Tg <1 with negative imaging for Excellent response.
- Post-total thyroidectomy/neck dissection without RAI ablation: Nonstimulated Tg <2.5 for Excellent response.
- Post-hemithyroidectomy: Normal/low-risk contralateral nodules or benign biopsy with no abnormal lymph nodes for Excellent response.
- TSH within normal reference range for Excellent/Indeterminate responses.
- TSH below normal reference range for Biochemically Incomplete response (with consideration of comorbidities).
- The optimal background setting to utilize serum Tg as a tumor marker is after total thyroidectomy and ablative RAI treatment (context-dependent utility).
- The median time to reach the nadir serum Tg after thyroidectomy in patients with both benign and malignant disease is 12 weeks.
