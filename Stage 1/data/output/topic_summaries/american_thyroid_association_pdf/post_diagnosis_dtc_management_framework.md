# Post-Diagnosis DTC Management Framework
*Source: American Thyroid Association.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Post-Diagnosis DTC Management Framework + TSH suppression targets

**Key Clinical Findings:**
- Individualize TSH suppression based on patient-specific factors including age, comorbidities, and disease risk.
- Long-term TSH suppression is not suggested for patients with low- or intermediate-risk disease without evidence of recurrence.
- Re-evaluate risks versus benefits of TSH suppression and TSH goals over time.
- TSH suppression targets should balance potential benefits (e.g., reduced recurrence risk in high-risk patients) against risks (e.g., cardiovascular events, osteoporosis).
- Maintain TSH in the normal reference range for low- or intermediate-risk patients after total thyroidectomy or lobectomy.
- Targeting and maintaining specific TSH goals can be difficult in real-world settings, with studies showing only 29% of patients achieving target TSH levels despite good adherence to therapy.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Evidence of biochemical or structural recurrence must be absent for low- or intermediate-risk patients to avoid long-term TSH suppression.
- Patient-specific factors (age, comorbidities, disease risk) must guide TSH suppression decisions.
- TSH goals should be re-evaluated periodically to balance benefits and risks.
- Thyroid hormone replacement therapy should be initiated if TSH is above the normal reference range after thyroidectomy or lobectomy.
- For lobectomy patients, TSH goals should consider the desire to avoid thyroid hormone replacement (e.g., normal TSH range to minimize supplementation).
- Thyroid hormone replacement therapy should be initiated if TSH is above the normal reference range after thyroidectomy or lobectomy, with initiation most frequently occurring in the first 2 years after thyroid lobectomy but potentially delayed in up to a quarter of patients.
