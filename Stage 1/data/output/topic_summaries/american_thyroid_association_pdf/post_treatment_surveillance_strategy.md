# Post-Treatment Surveillance Strategy
*Source: American Thyroid Association.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Post-Treatment Surveillance Strategy for Differentiated Thyroid Cancer

**Key Clinical Findings:**
- Ultrasound is the preferred imaging method for evaluating the thyroid bed and cervical lymph node compartments.
- Cervical ultrasound should be performed 6–12 months after initial therapy, with timing and frequency adjusted based on patient risk and response to therapy.
- Suspicious lymph nodes or lesions <8–10 mm may be followed without FNA unless they grow or threaten vital structures.
- For suspicious lymph nodes ≥8–10 mm, FNA with cytology and Tg measurement in needle washout fluid is recommended if it would influence treatment decisions.
- When Tg or TgAb levels rise with no structural disease on ultrasound, additional cross-sectional imaging (e.g., 18FDG-PET/CT) may be considered.
- Cervical ultrasound should interrogate all lymph node compartments and the thyroid bed using a high-frequency probe (10 MHz) for optimal sensitivity in detecting cervical metastases.
- Masses in the thyroid bed <6 mm in shortest dimension are seldom malignant; punctate echogenicity is worrisome for malignancy.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Serum Tg levels after surgery must be assessed to determine if imaging is needed prior to RAI administration.
- Cervical ultrasound should interrogate all lymph node compartments and the thyroid bed.
- TSH stimulation (via thyroid hormone withdrawal or rhTSH) is required before RAI administration.
- For patients with low-risk DTC and sustained excellent response, routine ultrasound can be discontinued after 5–8 years.
- For patients with low-risk DTC and sustained excellent response for 10–15 years, routine biochemical monitoring can be discontinued.
- Serum Tg levels should be interpreted in the context of TSH stimulation (via thyroid hormone withdrawal or rhTSH) for accurate assessment of disease status.
- TSH stimulation (TSH >30 mIU/L) is required before RAI administration for successful remnant ablation or therapy.
