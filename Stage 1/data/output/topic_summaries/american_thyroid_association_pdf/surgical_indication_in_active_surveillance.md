# Surgical Indication in Active Surveillance
*Source: American Thyroid Association.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Surgical Indication in Active Surveillance

**Key Clinical Findings:**
- Surgical resection is indicated if there is evidence of new biopsy-proven lymph node metastases, growth of the primary tumor by ≥3 mm, distant metastases, or extrathyroidal extension.
- Posterior growth, patient anxiety, inability to follow-up, and/or expressed preference for surgery are indications for surgical intervention.
- Initial or follow-up surgical consultation is indicated when patients develop new biopsy-proven lymph node metastases, ultrasound evidence of extrathyroidal extension, or distant metastases.
- Discussion with the surgeon is appropriate for patients considering active surveillance, particularly those with tumors adjacent to critical structures (e.g., recurrent laryngeal nerve, trachea, or esophagus).
- Invasion into critical structures (e.g., recurrent laryngeal nerve, trachea, or esophagus) should prompt immediate surgical consultation.
- Discussion with the surgeon is appropriate for patients considering active surveillance with tumors adjacent to but not invading critical structures (e.g., recurrent laryngeal nerve, trachea, or esophagus).

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Patients must have biopsy-proven lymph node metastases or ultrasound evidence of extrathyroidal extension to warrant surgical consultation.
- Tumor growth of ≥3 mm in any dimension is a clinical prerequisite for surgical consideration.
- Patients must be evaluated for ability to follow up regularly for monitoring.
- Patient preference for surgery after active surveillance is a valid indication for surgical consultation.
- Exclusion of patients with regional or distant metastases from active surveillance.
- Patients with regional or distant metastases are excluded from active surveillance.
