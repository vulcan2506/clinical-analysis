# Local Treatment for Symptomatic RAIR DTC
*Source: American Thyroid Association.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Local Treatment for Symptomatic RAIR DTC

**Key Clinical Findings:**
- Local treatment is suggested for symptomatic RAIR DTC, including surgery, radiotherapy, and percutaneous thermo-ablative approaches.
- Treatment decision-making should be individualized, considering disease burden, progression pace, symptoms, and anatomical complications.
- Palliative radiotherapy is well tolerated and effective for durable local control, with SBRT preferred for lung lesions.
- Cementoplasty is a palliative approach for osteolytic bone lesions to enhance mechanical stability and reduce pain.
- Percutaneous approaches (RFA, cryotherapy, microwave ablation) are alternatives for symptomatic metastases, particularly after radiotherapy progression.
- RFA should not be used for lesions >3 cm or for lesions near large blood vessels (due to heat loss reducing efficacy) or the pleura (risk of pneumothorax).
- Cryoablation may be preferred for bone metastases due to less post-procedural pain compared with RFA and avoidance of electrical conduction risks.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Patients must have symptomatic unresectable and/or metastatic RAIR DTC.
- Treatment goals are often palliative, focusing on symptom relief or prevention of complications.
- Multidisciplinary review is optimal for patients with symptomatic vertebral metastases.
- Lesion characteristics (e.g., size, location, osteolytic nature) must be suitable for the chosen local treatment.
- RAI-avidity and prior radiotherapy response should be considered in treatment planning.
- Indications for palliative surgery include vertebral metastases associated with spinal cord compression or impending compression and risk of fracture in weight-bearing long-bone metastases.
- Postoperative radiotherapy is typically administered after palliative surgery to reduce the risk of local recurrence.
