# Post-Thyroidectomy Voice Assessment Protocol
*Source: American Thyroid Association.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Post-Thyroidectomy Voice Assessment Protocol

**Key Clinical Findings:**
- Voice assessment should occur after surgery based on patient's subjective report and physician's objective assessment in the office.
- Early detection of vocal fold motion abnormalities after thyroidectomy is important for facilitating prompt intervention.
- Management of recurrent laryngeal nerve (RLN) paralysis may include voice therapy, vocal fold injection techniques, and open vocal fold medialization.
- Rates of vocal fold paralysis after thyroid surgery can only be assessed by laryngeal exam postoperatively.
- Massage and neck range of motion exercises in the immediate post-op period may help minimize neck tightness, globus sensation, or dysphagia.
- Surgeon communication with clinical teams should include a multidisciplinary data set of essential perioperative information requiring documentation and communication, as defined by Carty et al.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Collaboration between pathologist and physician specializing in voice is recommended.
- Laryngeal exam is required to assess vocal fold paralysis postoperatively.
- Documentation of recurrent laryngeal nerve identification and preservation in operative notes.
- Pathology reports must include essential histopathologic features for risk assessment, including status of resection margins, vascular invasion, lymph node status, and extranodal extension.
- Histopathologic subtypes associated with unfavorable or favorable outcomes should be identified and reported.
- Surgeon should relay information regarding completeness of resection and reconcile pathology findings (e.g., involved margins, R0 vs. R1 vs. R2 classification) to inform adjuvant treatments and monitoring.
