# RAI Therapy for Metastatic Thyroid Cancer
*Source: American Thyroid Association.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** RAI Therapy for Metastatic Thyroid Cancer

**Key Clinical Findings:**
- Dosimetry-guided RAI (lesional or maximum tolerated activity) may be considered for locoregional or metastatic disease when administered activities >5.5 GBq (150 mCi) are considered.
- rhTSH-mediated elevation or LT4 withdrawal may be utilized to prepare patients with distant metastatic disease for RAI therapy.
- Use of dosimetry is favored in patients with renal insufficiency, children, older patients (>70 years), and/or extensive pulmonary or bony metastases.
- Shared decision-making is required for dosing, timing, and preparation due to potential risks and patient concerns.
- For pulmonary metastases, RAI dosing strategies include empiric (3.7–7.4 GBq) or dosimetry-guided approaches to limit whole-body retention to 2.96 GBq at 48 hours.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Patients with distant metastatic disease must have elevated TSH (via rhTSH or LT4 withdrawal) prior to RAI therapy.
- Dosimetric approaches require serial blood sampling and/or imaging to determine maximum safely administered activity.
- For bony metastases, RAI-avid disease must be confirmed before treatment.
- Pulmonary function testing and consultation should be obtained if pulmonary fibrosis is suspected.
- Absolute neutrophil count and platelet counts must be monitored for bone marrow suppression.
- For patients with brain or spinal metastases, EBRT prior to RAI is recommended to limit the risk of acute tumor swelling.
