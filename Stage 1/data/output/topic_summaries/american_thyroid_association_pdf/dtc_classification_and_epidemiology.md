# DTC Classification and Epidemiology
*Source: American Thyroid Association.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** DTC Classification and Epidemiology

**Key Clinical Findings:**
- Differentiated thyroid cancer (DTC) includes papillary, follicular, and oncocytic carcinomas, comprising >90% of all thyroid cancers.
- Yearly incidence of thyroid cancer tripled from 4.9 per 100,000 in 1975 to 14.3 per 100,000 in 2015.
- Shift to earlier detection/diagnosis correlates with increased use of neck ultrasonography and ultrasound-guided FNA.
- Incidence of small thyroid cancers (<1 cm) reduced since 2014 due to guideline recommendations discouraging FNA of small nodules without abnormal lymph nodes or local invasion.
- Adoption of the DATA framework (Diagnosis, risk/benefit Assessment, Treatment decisions, response Assessment) for individualized patient-clinician decision-making.
- Approximately 25% of new thyroid cancers diagnosed in 1988–1989 were <1 cm, compared with 39% in 2008–2009, reflecting increased detection of small tumors.
- Major advances in understanding molecular causes of thyroid cancer have created newly approved treatment options for subsets of patients.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Patient age >18 years for guideline applicability.
- Inclusion of noninvasive follicular tumors with papillary-like nuclear features (NIFTP) and follicular tumors with uncertain malignant potential (FUMP) in DTC management.
- Use of systematic reviews and evidence-based methods for guideline development.
- Involvement of multidisciplinary specialists (Endocrinology, Surgery, Nuclear Medicine, Pathology, Medical Oncology, Cancer Genetics, Medical Informatics/Clinical Epidemiology).
- Patient advocate participation in guideline development and survivorship considerations.
- Inclusion of patient-reported outcomes research in survivorship considerations.
- Guidelines apply only to patients with DTC, including NIFTP and FUMP, which are extremely low-risk lesions diagnosed only after surgical excision.
