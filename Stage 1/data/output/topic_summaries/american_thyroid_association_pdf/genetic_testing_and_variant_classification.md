# Genetic Testing and Variant Classification
*Source: American Thyroid Association.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Genetic Testing and Variant Classification

**Key Clinical Findings:**
- When genomic testing is performed on tumor samples, both somatic and germline alterations can be detected.
- If a potentially clinically relevant germline cancer-predisposing variant is detected, evaluate patients and their family histories for clinical correlation.
- Consider referral for genetic counseling for possible germline testing if a germline variant is suspected.
- Tumor sequencing is optimized for somatic variant detection and is not a reliable method to detect germline variants.
- Confirmatory germline testing in the context of genetic counseling should be performed prior to further evaluation of a family.
- Consideration of germline variant potential when consenting patients for tumor sequencing should be explicitly documented in the informed consent process.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Genomic testing must be performed on tumor samples for clinical purposes to detect alterations.
- Evaluation of patients and their family histories is required if a germline variant is detected.
- Referral for genetic counseling is considered if a germline variant is suspected.
- Confirmatory germline testing must be performed prior to further family evaluation if a germline variant is suspected.
- If a likely pathogenic or known pathogenic germline variant is detected by tumor profiling, genetic counseling/testing must be offered based on personal or family history consistent with hereditary syndromes (e.g., PTEN hamartoma tumor syndrome).
