# Adjuvant EBRT Recommendation
*Source: American Thyroid Association.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Adjuvant EBRT Recommendation

**Key Clinical Findings:**
- Adjuvant EBRT may be considered in select high-risk DTC cases with aggressive features (e.g., aggressive histology, gross extrathyroidal extension, positive margins, visceral/soft tissue invasion) if salvage surgery is not feasible.
- EBRT with or without concurrent chemotherapy may be considered for gross residual disease or unresectable locoregional disease to improve locoregional control.
- IMRT is the standard radiation delivery method to reduce toxicity to normal structures.
- Potential benefits of EBRT (improved locoregional relapse-free survival) must be weighed against risks of toxicity and lack of overall survival benefit.
- EBRT is not recommended as a standard of care due to limited high-quality evidence and potential for treatment-related morbidity.
- EBRT with or without concurrent chemotherapy may be considered for unresectable locoregional disease to achieve disease stabilization in select cases, particularly when other systemic therapies (e.g., MKIs) are ineffective or unavailable.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- High-risk features for locoregional progression (e.g., aggressive histology, gross extrathyroidal extension, positive margins, visceral/soft tissue invasion).
- Unresectable disease or inability to achieve locoregional control with surgery.
- Postoperative gross residual disease or locally advanced unresectable disease.
- Patient-specific factors (e.g., age, comorbidities) must be considered due to toxicity risks.
- Dose of 6000-6600 cGy for adjuvant EBRT; 7000 cGy for gross residual disease.
- Consideration of patient prognosis and competing risks (e.g., distant metastases) when evaluating EBRT for locoregional control, as overall survival benefits are unlikely in the presence of high distant metastatic burden.
