# Lymph Node Density and Recurrence Risk
*Source: American Thyroid Association.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Lymph Node Density and Recurrence Risk

**Key Clinical Findings:**
- Lymph node density (number of metastatic lymph nodes divided by total lymph nodes examined) is used to stratify recurrence risk in papillary thyroid cancer.
- Higher lymph node density (>0.2–0.3) is associated with increased recurrence risk and poorer survival outcomes.
- Lymph node density should be integrated with other risk factors (e.g., tumor size, extrathyroidal extension) for comprehensive recurrence risk assessment.
- Patients with high lymph node density may require more aggressive postoperative management, including closer surveillance or adjuvant therapies.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Pathologic examination must include detailed lymph node dissection and accurate counting of metastatic and total lymph nodes.
- Preoperative imaging (e.g., ultrasound, CT) should identify suspicious lymph nodes for targeted dissection.
- Postoperative staging (e.g., AJCC 8th edition) should incorporate lymph node density into risk stratification.
