# Recommendation Grading Methodology
*Source: American Thyroid Association.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Recommendation grading methodology

**Key Clinical Findings:**
- Recommendations developed by subgroup based on systematic reviews, reviewed by full committee, revised, and voted on for final approval
- Approval via group discussion and informal consensus with majority approval from nonconflicted task force members
- Grading system adapted from U.S. Preventive Services Task Force and Cochrane Collaboration criteria
- Recommendations graded as strong or conditional based on benefits/harms balance, certainty, feasibility, acceptability, costs, and equity impacts
- Good Practice Statements (GPS) used when evidence quality is low or insufficient, requiring unanimous consensus
- Task force members used criteria adapted from methods developed by the U.S. Preventive Services Task Force and the Cochrane Collaboration to assess the quality of included papers

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Majority approval from all nonconflicted task force members for final recommendations
- Unanimous consensus required for all Good Practice Statements (GPS)
- Final literature review included manuscripts published through July 1, 2024, with exception for WHO 2025 classification
- Patient representative input requested for all recommendations
- Consensus on acceptability of recommendations and article text reached by all panel members
- Strong recommendations are only indicated when certainty is low or very low in limited circumstances
