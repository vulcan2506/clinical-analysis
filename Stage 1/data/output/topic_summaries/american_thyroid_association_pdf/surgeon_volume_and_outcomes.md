# Surgeon Volume and Outcomes
*Source: American Thyroid Association.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Surgeon Volume and Outcomes in Thyroidectomy

**Key Clinical Findings:**
- Higher surgeon volume is associated with improved clinical outcomes and reduced complications in thyroidectomy.
- Low-volume surgeons have higher rates of incomplete resection and compromised outcomes.
- Surgeon volume significantly influences the risk of permanent hypoparathyroidism, recurrent laryngeal nerve palsy, and hematoma.
- Total thyroidectomy is associated with increased risk of complications for both low- and high-volume surgeons.
- Patients treated at low-volume centers experience higher rates of incomplete resection.
- Surgeon volume-outcome relationships are influenced by both surgeon-specific factors (e.g., technical proficiency, decision-making) and hospital-level factors (e.g., multidisciplinary support, infrastructure).
- Low-volume surgeons are more likely to perform less extensive lymph node dissections in differentiated thyroid cancer, potentially compromising oncologic outcomes.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Surgeons should meet minimum annual thyroidectomy volume thresholds to optimize patient outcomes.
- High-risk thyroid cancer patients should be referred to high-volume surgeons.
- Surgeon training and experience should be considered in the management of differentiated thyroid cancer.
- Surgeons performing thyroidectomy should have access to multidisciplinary teams (e.g., endocrinology, radiology, pathology) to optimize outcomes.
- High-volume surgeons should demonstrate proficiency in managing complex cases, including reoperative thyroid surgery and central neck dissection.
