# candidiasis and pyelonephritis in diabetes
*Source: ICMR_Guidelines_for_Management_of_Type_1_Diabetes.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** management of candidiasis and pyelonephritis in diabetes

**Core Management Principles:**
- Asymptomatic candiduria with indwelling catheters frequently resolves upon catheter removal or change.
- Symptomatic candidiasis treated with oral fluconazole with or without flucytosine for 2-3 weeks.
- Perinephric abscess suspected in patients failing conventional therapy for acute pyelonephritis.
- Emphysematous pyelonephritis (EPN) presents with fever, flank pain, and gas formation within kidney parenchyma.
- EPN management ranges from conservative IV antibiotics and drainage to nephrectomy based on grade and prognostic factors.
- Fungal ball in urinary tract requires removal via cystoscope, ureteroscope, or rarely open surgical intervention
- Emphysematous pyelitis (gas localized to collecting system) is managed with broad-spectrum IV antibiotics and percutaneous drainage

**Clinical Prerequisites:**
- Imaging of kidney and collecting system required if candiduria fails to clear despite catheter removal and antibiotic cessation.
- Blood culture, urine culture, and pus culture collection mandatory before empirical antimicrobial therapy for abscess.
- Antibiotic duration for abscess must be at least 2 to 3 weeks, adjusted based on clinical and laboratory improvement.
- CT abdomen is the imaging modality of choice to identify and stage anatomical extent of gas accumulation in EPN.
- Percutaneous drainage required for abscess and emphysematous infections, with obstruction removal if present.
- Blood culture, urine culture, and pus culture (via USG/CT-guided aspiration) must be collected before empirical antimicrobial therapy for perinephric abscess
- Antibiotic duration for perinephric abscess must be at least 2-3 weeks, adjusted based on clinical/laboratory improvement and abscess resolution
