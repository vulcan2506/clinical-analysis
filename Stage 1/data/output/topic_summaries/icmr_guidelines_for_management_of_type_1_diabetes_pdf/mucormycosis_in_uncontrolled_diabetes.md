# mucormycosis in uncontrolled diabetes
*Source: ICMR_Guidelines_for_Management_of_Type_1_Diabetes.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** mucormycosis management in uncontrolled diabetes

**Core Management Principles:**
- Maintain high index of suspicion for uncontrolled diabetes patients presenting with facial swelling, black eschar, or visual disturbances.
- Initiate prompt aggressive surgical debridement combined with systemic antifungal therapy as the mainstay of treatment.
- Administer liposomal amphotericin B (3-5 mg/kg/day) over conventional amphotericin B (0.5-1.5 mg/kg/day) due to reduced nephrotoxicity.
- Utilize CT and MRI imaging to quantify disease extent at initial presentation and during follow-up.
- Employ oral posaconazole as step-down therapy for patients unable to tolerate amphotericin B.
- Initiate antifungal therapy empirically in high-risk uncontrolled diabetes patients with suggestive clinical features (e.g., facial swelling, visual disturbances) even before definitive fungal culture confirmation, if tissue sampling is delayed or inconclusive.

**Clinical Prerequisites:**
- Demonstration of fungal elements in tissue specimen for definitive diagnosis.
- Monitoring for hypokalemia, hypomagnesemia, and renal dysfunction during amphotericin B therapy.
- Early recognition of poor prognostic factors including cerebral involvement and delayed presentation.
- Availability of surgical facilities for aggressive debridement of infected tissue.
- Assessment of host defense impairment due to hyperglycemia and acidosis affecting neutrophil function.
- Monitor for hypokalemia and hypomagnesemia during amphotericin B therapy, in addition to renal dysfunction.
