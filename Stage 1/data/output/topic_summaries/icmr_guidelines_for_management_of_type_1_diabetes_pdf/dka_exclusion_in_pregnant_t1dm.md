# DKA exclusion in pregnant T1DM
*Source: ICMR_Guidelines_for_Management_of_Type_1_Diabetes.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** DKA exclusion in pregnant T1DM

**Core Management Principles:**
- Exclude DKA in all pregnant women with T1DM who become unwell, even if blood glucose is <200 mg/dl (euglycemic DKA can occur in pregnancy).
- Maintain a high index of suspicion for DKA in diabetic pregnant women with symptoms like nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, fever, hyperventilation, hypotension, or change in mental status.
- Educate all women with pre-existing diabetes (planning or pregnant) about DKA prevention, self-monitoring of blood glucose (SMBG), medical nutrition therapy, and sick day management.
- Teach patients to perform urine ketone measurements during illness or when glucose levels exceed 200 mg/dl and report positive values promptly.
- Educate patients about predisposing factors for DKA (e.g., infection, insulin omission, β-sympathomimetic/glucocorticoid use, protracted vomiting, starvation, dehydration, undiagnosed diabetes, insulin pump failure, or poor glycemic control) to enable proactive prevention.

**Clinical Prerequisites:**
- Multidisciplinary approach for DKA management in pregnancy, including IV fluid therapy, IV insulin therapy, electrolyte correction, and evaluation of precipitating factors.
- Continuous fetal heart rate monitoring and biophysical tests for fetal wellbeing after 24 weeks' gestation during DKA management.
- Prompt correction of DKA to revert fetal distress patterns (usually within 4–8 hours) before considering delivery.
- Delivery is warranted only if fetal status does not improve or maternal condition deteriorates despite aggressive therapy.
- Monitor maternal and fetal responses continuously during DKA management, including evaluation for bicarbonate administration if severe acidosis (pH <7.0) is present.
