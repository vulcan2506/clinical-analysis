# blood glucose monitoring protocols
*Source: ICMR_Guidelines_for_Management_of_Type_1_Diabetes.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Blood glucose monitoring protocols

**Core Management Principles:**
- Self-monitoring of blood glucose (SMBG) is central to diabetes management, with frequency correlating with improved HbA1c and reduced acute complications.
- Structured SMBG programs significantly reduce A1C levels in poorly controlled, noninsulin-treated type 2 diabetes.
- Continuous glucose monitoring (CGM) improves glycemic control and quality of life in type 1 diabetes, especially with impaired hypoglycemia awareness.
- Point-of-care β-hydroxybutyrate testing aids in assessing diabetic ketoacidosis severity prior to treatment.
- Alternative site blood glucose monitoring may introduce errors; fingertip testing remains the gold standard.
- HbA1c measurement should be interpreted with caution in patients with variant hemoglobins (e.g., HbS, HbC, HbD) due to potential interference with assay results, requiring confirmatory testing or alternative glycemic markers (e.g., fructosamine) in affected populations.

**Clinical Prerequisites:**
- Patient education on proper SMBG technique and interpretation is mandatory.
- CGM use requires patient commitment to sensor wear and data review.
- HbA1c measurement must account for variant hemoglobins (e.g., HbF, HbE) that interfere with results.
- Ketone testing is required during hyperglycemia or illness to prevent ketoacidosis.
- Glucose meters must meet performance standards across hypoglycemic, normoglycemic, and hyperglycemic ranges.
- Healthcare providers must ensure patients using CGM systems receive training on sensor insertion, calibration (if required), and data interpretation to optimize glycemic outcomes.
