# diabetic retinopathy management
*Source: ICMR_Guidelines_for_Management_of_Type_1_Diabetes.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** diabetic retinopathy screening and diagnostic work-up in T1DM

**Core Management Principles:**
- Screening for diabetic retinopathy (DR) is essential even in asymptomatic patients due to early microaneurysm presence.
- Fundus photography is recommended for documenting DR status and monitoring progression over time.
- Fundus fluorescein angiography (FFA) is used to identify hyperfluorescent (leaking microaneurysms/neovascularization) and hypofluorescent (ischemic/hemorrhagic) areas.
- Optical Coherence Tomography (OCT) is increasingly used for non-invasive evaluation of diabetic macular edema (DME), its severity, and treatment response.
- Ultra-wide field imaging (fundus photography and FFA) allows visualization of peripheral retinal changes.
- B-Scan Ultrasonography is included as part of the detailed investigative work-up for DR to assess complications like retinal detachment or vitreous hemorrhage.

**Clinical Prerequisites:**
- Screening must be performed even in asymptomatic patients to detect early DR changes.
- FFA requires resuscitation equipment due to risks of dye extravasation and allergic reactions.
- FFA is contraindicated in patients with history of hypersensitivity to fluorescein.
- OCT is required for assessing DME severity and guiding treatment decisions.
- Portable, low-cost fundus photography tools are recommended for accessible screening.
- Resuscitation equipment must be available when performing FFA due to risks of dye extravasation and allergic reactions.
