# air travel diabetes management
*Source: ICMR_Guidelines_for_Management_of_Type_1_Diabetes.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** air travel diabetes management

**Core Management Principles:**
- Carry diabetes supplies (insulin, syringes, glucose tablets) in hand luggage with a doctor's note and prescription
- Monitor blood glucose more frequently due to stress, activity changes, and time zone shifts
- Adjust insulin timing and dosing based on meal schedules and time zone changes
- Stay hydrated and avoid alcohol/caffeine to prevent dehydration and blood sugar fluctuations
- Wear a medical alert bracelet or carry identification indicating diabetes
- Avoid prolonged sitting without movement; perform calf raises, ankle circles, or short walks in the aisle every 1-2 hours to improve circulation and reduce risk of deep vein thrombosis (DVT) in T1DM patients
- Consume a balanced meal with complex carbohydrates before boarding to stabilize blood glucose during the initial phase of the flight

**Clinical Prerequisites:**
- Carry at least double the required insulin and supplies in case of loss or delays
- Have a written prescription and medical certificate for insulin and supplies
- Check airline policies regarding carrying diabetes medications and sharps
- Plan for time zone adjustments by consulting a healthcare provider for insulin schedule modifications
- Ensure travel insurance covers diabetes-related medical emergencies
- Ensure insulin vials/pens are stored in an insulated travel pouch or Frio cooling wallet to maintain temperature between 2°C and 30°C during transit
- Carry a letter from the healthcare provider on official letterhead specifying the medical necessity of carrying diabetes supplies, including sharps, to facilitate security checks
