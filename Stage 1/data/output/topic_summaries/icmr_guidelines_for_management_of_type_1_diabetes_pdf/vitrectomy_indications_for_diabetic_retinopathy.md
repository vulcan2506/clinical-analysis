# vitrectomy indications for diabetic retinopathy
*Source: ICMR_Guidelines_for_Management_of_Type_1_Diabetes.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** vitrectomy indications for diabetic retinopathy

**Core Management Principles:**
- Vitrectomy is indicated for non-resolving vitreous haemorrhage (typically present for more than 3 months)
- Vitrectomy is required for tractional retinal detachment involving or threatening the macula
- Vitrectomy is indicated for tractional rhegmatogenous detachment or proliferative diabetic retinopathy (PDR) unresponsive to aggressive laser PRP
- Surgical outcomes depend on patient-related factors such as macular perfusion and duration of macular detachment
- Blood glucose must be adequately controlled prior to vitrectomy or intravitreal injections to reduce infection risk
- Vitrectomy outcomes are influenced by the duration of macular detachment, with longer durations correlating with poorer visual recovery

**Clinical Prerequisites:**
- Non-resolving vitreous haemorrhage for more than 3 months
- Tractional retinal detachment involving or threatening the macula
- Tractional rhegmatogenous detachment or PDR unresponsive to laser PRP
- Adequate blood glucose control prior to surgical procedures
- Aggressive laser photocoagulation (PRP) must be attempted prior to considering vitrectomy for proliferative diabetic retinopathy (PDR) unresponsive to treatment
