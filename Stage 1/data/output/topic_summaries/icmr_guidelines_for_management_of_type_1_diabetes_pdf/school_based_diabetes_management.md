# school-based diabetes management
*Source: ICMR_Guidelines_for_Management_of_Type_1_Diabetes.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** school-based diabetes management

**Core Management Principles:**
- Diabetes education is the backbone of diabetes care, with systematic self-management education emphasized as essential for effective control.
- Parents and children are taught hands-on skills immediately, including self-testing, insulin injection, and hypoglycemia management, to restore a sense of control.
- School staff (teachers, medical room staff, bus drivers) are educated on diabetes care, emergency responses, and must have access to emergency kits and identification cards.
- Children are encouraged to develop age-appropriate independence in diabetes management, including self-testing, insulin administration, and decision-making.
- Psychosocial care is integrated into diabetes management, addressing family dynamics, mental health, and stigma to prevent deterioration in care.
- Teach safe disposal of sharps from the beginning of diabetes education to prevent injuries and environmental contamination.
- Encourage the entire family to adopt lifestyle modifications (e.g., healthy eating, physical activity) to normalize diabetes care and reduce the child's sense of exclusion.

**Clinical Prerequisites:**
- Diagnosis of type 1 diabetes in childhood or adolescence requiring intensive insulin therapy and frequent glucose monitoring.
- Stabilization of the child's clinical condition post-diagnosis before outpatient education begins.
- Family willingness and ability to participate in hands-on training and ongoing reinforcement of diabetes management skills.
- Access to a multidisciplinary diabetes care team including educators, psychologists, and healthcare providers.
- School and community support systems to ensure safe diabetes management during school hours and extracurricular activities.
- Stabilization of the child's clinical condition (e.g., resolution of DKA) before outpatient education begins, unless the diagnosis is made early without florid symptoms.
- Family willingness to participate in hands-on training and ongoing reinforcement of diabetes management skills, with consideration for socioeconomic barriers (e.g., cost of SMBG supplies).
