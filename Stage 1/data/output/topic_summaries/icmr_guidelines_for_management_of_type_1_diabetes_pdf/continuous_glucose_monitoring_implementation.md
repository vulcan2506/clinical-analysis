# continuous glucose monitoring implementation
*Source: ICMR_Guidelines_for_Management_of_Type_1_Diabetes.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** continuous glucose monitoring (CGM) implementation

**Core Management Principles:**
- CGM provides longitudinal glucose data (every 5-15 minutes) for glycemic trend analysis, unlike SMBG which offers snapshots
- Real-time CGM enables immediate patient/caregiver action via alarms for hypoglycemia/hyperglycemia, while retrospective CGM requires clinic review after data download
- CGM benefits are maximized when used ≥70% of the time and in motivated patients, particularly those with hypoglycemia unawareness or frequent asymptomatic hypoglycemia
- CGM is most effective when paired with insulin pump therapy (sensor-augmented pump) compared to multiple subcutaneous insulin injections
- CGM accuracy is reduced during rapid glucose changes, hypoglycemia, and early sensor use (day 1), necessitating confirmatory finger-prick glucose for critical decisions
- CGMS sensor insertion is typically painless and rapid, with the sensor remaining in subcutaneous tissue (e.g., upper abdomen) for 3-14 days depending on the model
- CGMS data can be analyzed in detail by feeding sensor data to a computer for comprehensive assessment of glucose variability

**Clinical Prerequisites:**
- Patient must be willing to use CGM ≥70% of the time for meaningful glycemic control improvement
- CGM is recommended for patients with hypoglycemia unawareness, severe hypoglycemia (≥1 episode/year), frequent asymptomatic hypoglycemia (>2 episodes/week), extreme fear of hypoglycemia, or persistent hyperglycemia (HbA1c ≥9%)
- CGM requires calibration with capillary blood glucose (2-4x/day) for most devices, except Dexcom G6 which is calibration-free
- CGM is approved for use in children and adults, with sensor insertion typically in subcutaneous tissue (e.g., upper abdomen) for 3-14 days
- Patient selection should prioritize those with motivation to engage with CGM data and insulin therapy adjustments
- Calibration of most CGMS devices requires capillary blood glucose readings 2-4 times daily, except for Dexcom G6 which is calibration-free
- Calibration should be performed during periods of relatively stable blood glucose (e.g., fasting or pre-meal)
