# diabetic autonomic neuropathy
*Source: ICMR_Guidelines_for_Management_of_Type_1_Diabetes.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Diabetic autonomic neuropathy (DAN) screening and management

**Core Management Principles:**
- Screen for autonomic neuropathy in patients with long-standing diabetes or severe hyperglycemia, especially those with other microvascular complications (e.g., retinopathy, nephropathy).
- Assess for autonomic symptoms across organ systems: cardiovascular (e.g., orthostatic hypotension, resting tachycardia), gastrointestinal (e.g., gastroparesis, constipation), and genitourinary (e.g., erectile dysfunction, neurogenic bladder).
- Evaluate risk factors: duration of diabetes, hyperglycemia severity, insulin resistance (e.g., dyslipidemia, hypertension), smoking, alcohol exposure, and genetic predisposition.
- Consider autoimmune contributions (e.g., CIDP, vasculitis) in proximal neuropathies, particularly in T1DM/T2DM patients with atypical presentations.
- Individualize management based on symptom severity and organ system involvement to improve quality of life and reduce complications.
- Assess for heat intolerance and intraoperative cardiovascular instability as part of cardiovascular autonomic neuropathy evaluation
- Evaluate nocturnal diarrhea and fecal incontinence as potential indicators of advanced gastrointestinal autonomic neuropathy

**Clinical Prerequisites:**
- Documented diagnosis of diabetes mellitus (T1DM or T2DM).
- Presence of risk factors: ≥5–10 years diabetes duration, poor glycemic control, or coexisting microvascular complications.
- Exclusion of non-diabetic causes of autonomic neuropathy (e.g., autoimmune disorders, toxic exposures).
- Baseline assessment of autonomic symptoms (e.g., orthostatic vitals, gastrointestinal motility, sexual function).
- Multidisciplinary collaboration (e.g., endocrinology, cardiology, gastroenterology) for complex cases.
- Document height as a potential independent predictor for diabetic autonomic neuropathy due to its association with length-dependent nerve damage
