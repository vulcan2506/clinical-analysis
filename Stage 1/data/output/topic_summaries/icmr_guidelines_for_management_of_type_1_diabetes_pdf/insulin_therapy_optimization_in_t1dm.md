# insulin therapy optimization in T1DM
*Source: ICMR_Guidelines_for_Management_of_Type_1_Diabetes.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** insulin therapy optimization in T1DM

**Core Management Principles:**
- All children and adults with T1DM require insulin as soon as diagnosed and continuously thereafter.
- Optimal insulin use depends on a diabetes team (physician, educator, nutritionist) educating and supporting patients/families.
- Intensive glycemic control using MDII or CSII improves HbA1c and reduces microvascular complications.
- Insulin regimen selection considers motivation, education, and financial resources of the family.
- Patient cooperation and family support are key to achieving good glycemic control.
- Short-acting bovine insulin was historically the first insulin used in T1DM treatment (1922), marking the beginning of insulin therapy evolution.

**Clinical Prerequisites:**
- Physiological insulin regimens (MDII or CSII) should be used as far as feasible in all age groups.
- Diabetes team support (physician, educator, nutritionist) is essential for education and insulin dose adjustment.
- Insulin regimen selection must consider family motivation, education, and financial resources.
- Target HbA1c < 7.5% in children and < 7% in adults should be strived for.
- Insulin analogs should be individualized to settings where distinct advantages exist (e.g., toddlers, school-going children, nocturnal hypoglycemia).
- Recombinant human insulin (available since 1982) is a foundational requirement for modern insulin therapy in T1DM.
