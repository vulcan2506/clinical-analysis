# fungal complications in DKA
*Source: ICMR_Guidelines_for_Management_of_Type_1_Diabetes.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** fungal complications in DKA

**Core Management Principles:**
- Recognize increased incidence of rare fungal infections like rhinocerebral mucormycosis and pulmonary aspergillosis in patients with DKA
- Maintain a high index of suspicion for fungal infections in DKA patients
- Conduct appropriate investigations to identify fungal infections promptly
- Initiate treatment for potentially life-threatening fungal infections early
- Differentiate fungal infections from common bacterial infections in DKA management
- Monitor for early clinical signs of rhinocerebral mucormycosis such as facial pain, periorbital swelling, or black necrotic eschar in nasal mucosa in DKA patients
- Include fungal cultures (e.g., sputum, bronchoalveolar lavage, tissue biopsy) and serum galactomannan testing for suspected pulmonary aspergillosis in DKA patients

**Clinical Prerequisites:**
- High clinical suspicion for fungal infections in DKA patients
- Access to appropriate diagnostic investigations for fungal pathogens
- Awareness of specific fungal species associated with DKA (e.g., Mucorales, Aspergillus)
- Ability to distinguish fungal from bacterial infections clinically
- Timely access to antifungal therapy protocols
- Prompt correction of acidosis and hyperglycemia as part of initial DKA management to reduce fungal proliferation risk
- Availability of contrast-enhanced MRI or CT imaging for early detection of rhinocerebral mucormycosis in DKA patients
