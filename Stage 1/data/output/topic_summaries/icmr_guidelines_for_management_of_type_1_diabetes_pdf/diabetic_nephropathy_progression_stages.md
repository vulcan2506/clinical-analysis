# diabetic nephropathy progression stages
*Source: ICMR_Guidelines_for_Management_of_Type_1_Diabetes.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** diabetic nephropathy progression stages in T1DM

**Core Management Principles:**
- Persistent microalbuminuria (MA) is a marker of progressive diabetic nephropathy, as intermittent MA does not reliably indicate progression.
- Macroalbuminuria stage (severely increased albuminuria) is defined by urinary albumin excretion >300 mg/24 h and is associated with hypertension and declining GFR.
- Renal impairment (Stage 5) involves progressive GFR decline, leading to worsening CKD and ESRD in 25–40% of T1DM patients.
- Non-albuminuric renal dysfunction can occur in T1DM, similar to T2DM, indicating that albuminuria is not always a prerequisite for kidney disease progression.
- Histopathological progression includes GBM thickening, mesangial expansion, nodular sclerosis (Kimmelstiel-Wilson lesion), and advanced glomerulosclerosis.
- Glomerular capillary pressure (GCP) and intraglomerular (IG) dynamics contribute to early hyperfiltration and subsequent albuminuria in T1DM nephropathy progression.

**Clinical Prerequisites:**
- Monitor urinary albumin excretion (UAE) and GFR regularly to assess nephropathy progression.
- Control glycemia, blood pressure, and dyslipidemia to slow diabetic nephropathy progression.
- Screen for familial clustering, ethnicity, smoking, and hypertension as additional risk factors for progression.
- Use histopathological classification (Tervaert et al.) for advanced diagnostic confirmation in unclear cases.
- Assess podocyte integrity and tubular dysfunction (e.g., foot process effacement) in suspected diabetic kidney disease.
- Assess interstitial fibrosis and tubular atrophy (IFTA) scores as part of histopathological evaluation for advanced diagnostic confirmation in unclear cases.
