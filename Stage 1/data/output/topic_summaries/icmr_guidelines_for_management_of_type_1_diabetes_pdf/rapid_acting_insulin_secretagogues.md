# rapid-acting insulin secretagogues
*Source: ICMR_Guidelines_for_Management_of_Type_1_Diabetes.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** rapid-acting insulin secretagogues (aspart, lispro, glulisine) and ultra-rapid analogs (Fiasp, Lyumjev)

**Core Management Principles:**
- Administer 5-10 minutes before meals due to quicker onset than regular insulin
- Useful for reducing immediate post-prandial hyperglycemia due to earlier and higher peak
- Shorter duration decreases risk of delayed hypoglycemia compared to regular insulin
- Ultra-rapid analogs (e.g., Fiasp) can be given immediately before or up to 20 minutes after meals
- Preferred in patients with unpredictable meal timing (e.g., toddlers, school/college students, office workers)
- Ultra-rapid acting insulin lispro (Lyumjev) is approved for use in adults with T1DM and T2DM by the US FDA

**Clinical Prerequisites:**
- Must be used in conjunction with meal timing adjustments for optimal efficacy
- Not suitable for patients unable to monitor or respond to hypoglycemia risks
- Ultra-rapid analogs require careful dose titration to avoid post-meal hyperglycemia
- Ultra-rapid acting insulin analogs (e.g., Fiasp) may be administered immediately before meals in selected cases, not just post-meal
