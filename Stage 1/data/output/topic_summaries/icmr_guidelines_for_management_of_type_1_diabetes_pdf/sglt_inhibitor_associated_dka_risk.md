# SGLT inhibitor-associated DKA risk
*Source: ICMR_Guidelines_for_Management_of_Type_1_Diabetes.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** SGLT inhibitor-associated DKA risk

**Core Management Principles:**
- Euglycemic DKA (blood glucose <250 mg/dl) can occur with SGLT2 inhibitors, particularly in pregnancy, alcohol intake, or reduced carbohydrate intake.
- DKA is a leading cause of morbidity and mortality in children with T1DM, with high case fatality rates in severe cases.
- Cerebral edema is a major contributor to DKA-related mortality, especially in young children with severe presentations.
- Omission of insulin is the most common cause of recurrent DKA, particularly in adolescents, requiring psycho-social assessment.
- DKA at T1DM diagnosis is more common in children <5 years and those with poor socioeconomic background, with a 28.7% prevalence reported in Indian youth (YDR registry).
- Cerebral edema occurs in 0.5-1% of children with DKA and accounts for 60-90% of all DKA-related deaths in children, with a mortality rate of 20-25%.
- Insulin omission is the most common cause of recurrent DKA, particularly in adolescents, and may be influenced by use of non-injectable 'alternate systems of medicine' in some regions.

**Clinical Prerequisites:**
- SGLT inhibitor use should be avoided in patients with conditions predisposing to DKA (e.g., pregnancy, alcohol use, low carbohydrate intake).
- Close monitoring for euglycemic DKA symptoms (e.g., nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain) is required in patients on SGLT inhibitors.
- Insulin omission must be addressed with psycho-social support in recurrent DKA cases.
- Detailed psycho-social assessment is required for patients with recurrent DKA due to insulin omission, particularly in adolescents.
- Risk mitigation strategies must be implemented when considering SGLT inhibitor use in patients with high DKA risk (e.g., pregnancy, alcohol use, low carbohydrate intake).
