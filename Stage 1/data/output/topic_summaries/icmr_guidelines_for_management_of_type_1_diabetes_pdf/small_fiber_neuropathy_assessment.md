# small fiber neuropathy assessment
*Source: ICMR_Guidelines_for_Management_of_Type_1_Diabetes.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** small fiber neuropathy assessment in T1DM

**Core Management Principles:**
- Positive symptoms predominate, including burning or lancinating pain, hyperalgesia, dysesthesia, and allodynia
- Loss of pinprick sensation and abnormal thermal sensation are objective tests for small fiber neuropathy
- Sudomotor dysfunction (e.g., decreased sweating, dry skin) is an early and sensitive marker, detectable via QSART or Sudoscan
- Corneal confocal microscopy (CCM) is an emerging non-invasive diagnostic tool for early detection
- Motor functions and deep tendon reflexes are typically preserved in small fiber neuropathy
- Abnormal thermal sensation is an additional manifestation of small fiber neuropathy
- Sudomotor dysfunction in small fiber DSPN includes impaired blood flow and cold feet

**Clinical Prerequisites:**
- Routine nerve conduction studies (NCV) may not detect small fiber dysfunction due to preserved large fiber conduction
- Skin biopsy for intraepidermal nerve fiber density (IENFD) is not recommended for routine practice
- Sudomotor function tests (QSART or Sudoscan) require specialized equipment and trained personnel
- Corneal confocal microscopy requires access to specialized imaging technology
- Differentiation between small fiber and large fiber neuropathy is essential for appropriate management
- Sudomotor dysfunction assessment requires specialized equipment and trained personnel for QSART and Sudoscan
- Corneal confocal microscopy (CCM) requires access to specialized imaging technology for non-invasive visualization of corneal nerve fibers
