# diabetic nephropathy screening protocol
*Source: ICMR_Guidelines_for_Management_of_Type_1_Diabetes.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** diabetic nephropathy screening protocol in T1DM

**Core Management Principles:**
- Annual albuminuria and renal function assessment in T1DM with disease duration ≥5 years
- Diagnosis of diabetic nephropathy requires persistent albuminuria (>300 mg/24h or ACR >300 mg/g) in 2 out of 3 samples, often with diabetic retinopathy
- Microalbuminuria (30–300 mg/g ACR) is the earliest detectable marker; screening should use early morning spot urine with confirmation via 2 spot samples over 6 months or 24-hour urine collection
- Renal function assessed via serum creatinine-based eGFR (CKD-EPI formula); non-albuminuric phenotype requires separate monitoring
- Screening includes history, small-fiber (temperature/pinprick) and large-fiber (vibration, monofilament, proprioception, ankle reflexes) neuropathy testing

**Clinical Prerequisites:**
- T1DM duration ≥5 years for initial screening
- Confirmation of persistent albuminuria via 2 spot samples over 6 months or 24-hour urine collection
- Use of CKD-EPI formula for eGFR calculation
- Exclusion of non-diabetic causes (e.g., uremia, vitamin B12 deficiency, hypothyroidism) in atypical cases
- Cardiac autonomic neuropathy assessment via heart rate variability and orthostatic hypotension testing
- Exclusion of erectile dysfunction, hypogonadism, and thyroid dysfunction in patients with suspected diabetic nephropathy (via LH, total testosterone, prolactin, total T4, and TSH measurements)
- Biothesiometry threshold >15V as diagnostic criterion for large-fiber neuropathy
