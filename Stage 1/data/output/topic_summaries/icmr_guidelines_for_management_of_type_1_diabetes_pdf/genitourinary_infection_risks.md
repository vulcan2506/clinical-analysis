# genitourinary infection risks
*Source: ICMR_Guidelines_for_Management_of_Type_1_Diabetes.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** genitourinary infection risks and management in T1DM

**Core Management Principles:**
- Genitourinary infections are the commonest infections among patients with diabetes and likely to have a more complicated course.
- All UTIs in T1DM merit treatment and in recurrent cases, imaging should be obtained to exclude obstructive etiology.
- E.coli accounts for the majority of UTIs followed by other Gram negative bacilli such as Proteus, Klebsiella, Acinetobacter and Pseudomonas.
- Enterococci, coagulase negative staphylococcus, beta-hemolytic streptococci and Staphylococcus aureus are other important microorganisms implicated.
- Fungal infections include vulvovaginal candidiasis and invasive candidiasis.
- Emphysematous cystitis and emphysematous pyelonephritis are unique, severe forms of genitourinary infections in T1DM, characterized by gas formation in bladder or renal parenchyma due to gas-producing bacteria

**Clinical Prerequisites:**
- Presence of hyperglycemia
- Metabolic acidosis
- Cystopathy with significant post-void residual urine
- Obstruction due to calculi
- Papillary necrosis or fungal ball
- Presence of papillary necrosis as a risk factor for genitourinary infections in T1DM
