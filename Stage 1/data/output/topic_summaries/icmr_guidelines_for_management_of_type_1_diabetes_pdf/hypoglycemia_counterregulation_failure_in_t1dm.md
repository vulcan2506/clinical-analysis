# hypoglycemia counterregulation failure in T1DM
*Source: ICMR_Guidelines_for_Management_of_Type_1_Diabetes.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** hypoglycemia counterregulation failure in T1DM

**Core Management Principles:**
- Loss of first-line defense: insulin secretion fails to decline as plasma glucose falls below 80-85 mg/dl
- Loss of second-line defense: glucagon secretion fails to increase despite falling glucose (60-65 mg/dl threshold)
- Attenuated epinephrine response (third-line defense) in long-standing T1DM or recurrent hypoglycemia
- Blunted neurogenic symptoms (sympathoadrenal discharge) leading to hypoglycemia unawareness
- Neuroglycopenic symptoms (<50 mg/dl) manifest as cognitive impairment, seizures, or coma requiring external assistance
- Unpredictable activity patterns and fussy eating behavior in children increase hypoglycemia risk
- Permanent neurological damage may occur in prolonged neuroglycopenic hypoglycemia despite neurological recovery post-correction

**Clinical Prerequisites:**
- Monitor plasma glucose at ≤70 mg/dl to trigger hypoglycemia concern
- Avoid aggressive glycemic therapy to prevent recurrent hypoglycemia
- Scrupulously avoid hypoglycemia for 2-3 weeks to reverse hypoglycemia-associated autonomic failure (HAAF)
- Educate patients (especially children) on recognizing non-specific neurogenic symptoms (e.g., hunger, sweating, tremor)
- Adjust insulin dosing to prevent iatrogenic hyperinsulinemia
- Educate young children (especially) on hypoglycemia symptoms due to their inability to communicate symptoms or initiate behavioral eating responses
