# ketone monitoring in T1DM management
*Source: ICMR_Guidelines_for_Management_of_Type_1_Diabetes.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** ketone monitoring in T1DM management

**Core Management Principles:**
- Use urine ketone strips or nitroprusside test for quick bedside monitoring of ketonuria during illness with fever/vomiting or persistent polyuria with elevated blood glucose (>250 mg/dl).
- Measure blood beta-hydroxybutyrate directly using point-of-care meters or laboratory tests when feasible, as the ratio of beta-hydroxybutyrate to acetoacetate increases significantly in DKA.
- Interpret blood ketone levels alongside concurrent blood glucose values for accurate assessment.
- Advise increased fluid and carbohydrate intake if blood glucose is <180 mg/dl and ketone levels are 0.6–1.5 mmol/L; administer additional rapid-acting insulin if blood glucose is >180 mg/dl.
- Emergency admission is warranted for blood ketone levels >3 mmol/L due to high risk of acidosis.
- False positive ketonuria may occur in patients using drugs like penicillamine, captopril, and mesna, requiring clinical correlation for accurate interpretation.
- False negative ketonuria is rarely encountered in patients with DKA and shock due to the liver's conversion of ketones to beta-hydroxybutyrate.

**Clinical Prerequisites:**
- Urine ketone test strips should be available in limited care settings and used during illness with fever/vomiting or persistent polyuria with elevated blood glucose (>250 mg/dl).
- Blood ketone monitoring should be performed using point-of-care meters or laboratory tests when feasible.
- Patients should keep blood or urine ketone strips at home for sick-day use and check expiry dates periodically.
- Urine ketone testing should be performed during illness with fever and/or vomiting, persistent polyuria with elevated blood glucose (>250 mg/dl), especially in the presence of abdominal pain or rapid breathing.
