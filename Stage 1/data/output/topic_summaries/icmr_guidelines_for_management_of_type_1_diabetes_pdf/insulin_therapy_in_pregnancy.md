# insulin therapy in pregnancy
*Source: ICMR_Guidelines_for_Management_of_Type_1_Diabetes.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** insulin therapy in pregnancy

**Core Management Principles:**
- Multiple subcutaneous insulin injections (MSII) are standard of care to achieve glycemic control in pregnancy
- Insulin pump therapy can be offered if glycemic control is difficult with MSII due to significant hypoglycemia
- Rapid-acting insulin analogs (aspart, lispro) and long-acting insulin analogs (detemir) are preferred over conventional insulins
- Insulin requirements vary throughout pregnancy, increasing in the first 9 weeks, decreasing from 9-16 weeks, then increasing until the 37th week
- Insulin requirements may increase by 2-3 times with progression in pregnancy, shifting towards more prandial insulin

**Clinical Prerequisites:**
- Glycemic control is difficult to achieve with multiple subcutaneous insulin injections (MSII)
- Significant hypoglycemia risk is present
- Pregnancy progression (gestational age) must be considered for insulin dosing adjustments
- Glucocorticoid treatment for premature fetal lung maturation may require 40-50% higher insulin doses for up to 1 week
