# T1DM epidemiology and diagnosis
*Source: ICMR_Guidelines_for_Management_of_Type_1_Diabetes.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** T1DM epidemiology and diagnosis

**Core Management Principles:**
- Diagnosis established via FPG, OGTT, HbA1c, or random plasma glucose with characteristic hyperglycemia symptoms
- Peak incidence between 10-14 years of age, though can occur at any age
- Autoimmune disease with insulin deficiency and hyperglycemia in genetically susceptible individuals
- Variable presentations: acute in children, gradual in adults
- Autoantibodies present in 70-90% of individuals with T1DM
- Presence of 'honeymoon period' after diagnosis with partial recovery of β-cell function and minimal/no exogenous insulin requirement, followed by subsequent loss of residual β-cells
- Variable β-cell damage: not complete in certain individuals, with 30-80% of long-term T1DM patients showing insulin micro-secretion detectable via sensitive C-peptide measurements

**Clinical Prerequisites:**
- Presence of two or more islet cell autoantibodies for Stage 1 diagnosis
- Genetic susceptibility (e.g., HLA DR3-DQ2 or DR4-DQ8 haplotypes)
- Characteristic signs and symptoms of hyperglycemia for clinical diagnosis
- Family history of T1DM increases risk (3% for mother, 5% for father, 8% for sibling)
- Differentiation from other diabetes forms, especially in ages 20-40 with heterogeneous presentations
- Sensitive C-peptide measurements required to detect residual β-cell function in long-term T1DM patients for prognostic assessment
- Autoantibody titer, affinity, and type (e.g., IA-2, ZnT8) assessment required for risk stratification and progression prediction in presymptomatic stages
