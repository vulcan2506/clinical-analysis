# diabetic kidney disease epidemiology and risk
*Source: ICMR_Guidelines_for_Management_of_Type_1_Diabetes.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Diabetic Kidney Disease (DKD) epidemiology and risk

**Core Management Principles:**
- DKD is the most common cause of chronic kidney disease (CKD) globally, characterized by albuminuria, reduced GFR, and hypertension with increased cardiovascular risk.
- Non-albuminuric diabetic nephropathy exists, retaining all features except albuminuria.
- Type 2 diabetes is the most common cause of DKD in absolute numbers, but 25-40% of type 1 diabetes mellitus (T1DM) patients develop nephropathy.
- End Stage Renal Disease (ESRD) is a major cause of morbidity and premature mortality in T1DM patients.
- Longer disease duration in T1DM increases nephropathy risk due to improved survival and healthcare access.
- Non-albuminuric diabetic nephropathy phenotype is more prevalent in T2DM than T1DM, with up to 50% of T2DM patients with reduced GFR lacking albuminuria
- Hypertension in DKD often precedes albuminuria and GFR decline, particularly in T1DM, due to early glomerular hyperfiltration and systemic vascular changes

**Clinical Prerequisites:**
- Diagnosis requires assessment of albuminuria, GFR reduction, and hypertension.
- Optimal glycemic control and blood pressure management are critical for prognosis improvement.
- Early diagnosis is essential for effective management of DKD.
- Patient must have diabetes mellitus (type 1 or type 2) to be at risk for DKD.
- Long-term follow-up is required due to progressive nature of DKD.
- Screening for DKD in T1DM should begin 5 years after diagnosis, even in the absence of albuminuria, due to delayed onset of microalbuminuria in some patients
- Use of estimated GFR (eGFR) calculated via CKD-EPI equation is required for accurate risk stratification alongside albuminuria assessment
