# Charcot foot progression and complications
*Source: ICMR_Guidelines_for_Management_of_Type_1_Diabetes.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Charcot foot progression and complications in T1DM

**Core Management Principles:**
- Acute Charcot foot presents with redness, swelling, and elevated local temperature in the foot.
- Differential diagnoses of acute Charcot foot include cellulitis, osteomyelitis, gout, and deep venous thrombosis.
- Untreated Charcot foot leads to recurrent microfractures, bone resorption, and deformity, increasing foot ulceration risk.
- Sensorimotor neuropathy, autonomic neuropathy, and intact peripheral circulation are prerequisites for Charcot neuroarthropathy.
- Osteoporotic changes in foot bones due to long-standing diabetic neuropathy (DN) may precede Charcot foot deformity

**Clinical Prerequisites:**
- Offloading with a total contact cast (TCC) is required in the acute phase of Charcot foot.
- Use of protective braces (e.g., Charcot restraint orthotic walker, patellar tendon-bearing brace) after acute phase.
- Presence of sensorimotor neuropathy, autonomic neuropathy, and intact peripheral circulation for Charcot neuroarthropathy development.
- Intact peripheral circulation is a prerequisite for Charcot neuroarthropathy development
