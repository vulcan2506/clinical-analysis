# diabetic macular edema management strategies
*Source: ICMR_Guidelines_for_Management_of_Type_1_Diabetes.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** diabetic macular edema management strategies

**Core Management Principles:**
- Conservative management is recommended for DME with preserved visual acuity; treatment is considered only if vision loss progresses.
- Treatment for DME should be initiated only after excluding ischemic maculopathy, as inappropriate treatment can worsen vision loss.
- Intravitreal Anti-VEGF agents (Ranibizumab, Aflibercept, Bevacizumab) are the primary treatment for DME, offering better functional and structural outcomes than laser therapy.
- Intraocular corticosteroids (e.g., triamcinolone acetonide, dexamethasone implant, fluocinolone acetonide implant) are used for DME unresponsive to other treatments, with risks of cataract and increased intraocular pressure explained to patients.
- Close monitoring of DR and DME is required during pregnancy, with dilated retinal examinations preconception, each trimester, and postpartum for up to 12 months.
- Posterior sub-tenon injection of triamcinolone acetonide (PST) is a viable corticosteroid option for DME with a duration of action of 4 to 6 weeks.
- Intra-vitreal triamcinolone acetonide (IVTA) is used for DME with a duration of action of 4 to 6 weeks.

**Clinical Prerequisites:**
- Exclusion of ischemic maculopathy before initiating DME treatment.
- Stable and effective control of retinopathy and maculopathy for at least three months prior to cataract surgery.
- Dilated retinal examination preconception for women with T1DM planning pregnancy.
- Collaboration between endocrinologist/diabetologist, ophthalmologist, and obstetrician for optimal outcomes in pregnant women with T1DM.
- Use of non-invasive investigations such as OCT for accurate diagnosis and treatment of DR.
- Stable and effective control of retinopathy and maculopathy for at least three months prior to cataract surgery is required to minimize postoperative worsening of DR.
