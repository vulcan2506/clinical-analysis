# panretinal photocoagulation for proliferative retinopathy
*Source: ICMR_Guidelines_for_Management_of_Type_1_Diabetes.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** panretinal photocoagulation for proliferative retinopathy

**Core Management Principles:**
- PRP involves delivering 1800 laser burns to peripheral retinal tissue to destroy photoreceptors and retinal pigment epithelium.
- PRP converts hypoxic retina (releasing VEGF) into anoxic retina via laser ablation.
- PRP is typically administered in 2 to 3 sessions.
- Side effects include decreased peripheral field of vision and night vision.
- PRP may be performed using either argon green or diode laser, with diode laser preferred in eyes with media opacities like cataract or vitreous hemorrhage

**Clinical Prerequisites:**
- Proliferative diabetic retinopathy (PDR) diagnosis as the primary indication for PRP.
- Assessment of retinal hypoxia and VEGF release prior to PRP.
- PRP should be deferred in patients with active DME until DME is stabilized or treated, as PRP can exacerbate macular edema
- PRP is contraindicated in patients with advanced glaucoma or significant media opacities that preclude adequate laser delivery
