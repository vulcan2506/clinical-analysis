# pregnancy contraindications and risks
*Source: ICMR_Guidelines_for_Management_of_Type_1_Diabetes.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** pregnancy contraindications and risks in T1DM

**Core Management Principles:**
- Active proliferative retinopathy, severe nephropathy (GFR <30 ml/min/1.73 m²), severe autonomic neuropathy, or severe coronary heart disease increase pregnancy risks and require detailed evaluation and stabilization before conception.
- Pregnancy increases short-term risk of proliferative diabetic retinopathy (PDR), especially if baseline non-proliferative retinopathy (NPDR) is moderate to severe; risk persists up to 1 year postpartum.
- Women with chronic kidney disease (CKD) have higher adverse pregnancy outcomes; preconception screening of urine ACR, serum creatinine, and eGFR is mandatory.
- ACE inhibitors/ARBs must be discontinued preconception; intensive glucose and blood pressure control are required, with frequent monitoring and consideration of insulin analogues, CGMS, or pumps.
- Autonomic neuropathy (e.g., CAN, gastroparesis) and hypoglycemia unawareness increase adverse pregnancy outcomes; advanced glucose monitoring technologies are recommended.
- Pregnancy outcome is favorable in women with modest elevations in serum creatinine (below 1.4 mg/dl), proteinuria less than 1 g/24 h, and normal blood pressure; however, serum creatinine above 2 mg/dl, severe hypertension, nephrotic-range proteinuria (≥3 g/24 h), or pre-existing cardiovascular disease are associated with high risk of poor maternal and fetal outcomes

**Clinical Prerequisites:**
- Detailed ocular assessment preconception (if not done in last 6 months) and in first and third trimesters; monthly follow-up for severe retinopathy.
- Preconception renal function assessment (urine ACR, serum creatinine, eGFR) for women with CKD.
- Discontinuation of ACE inhibitors/ARBs preconception; switch to progesterone-only contraceptives in nephropathy.
- Intensive glucose and blood pressure control with frequent monitoring (e.g., serum creatinine and ACR every 4 weeks till 32 weeks, then biweekly).
- Use of advanced technologies (e.g., CGMS, sensor-augmented insulin pumps) for high-risk cases (e.g., hypoglycemia unawareness).
- Involvement of an expert retina specialist for individualized decisions regarding diabetic macular edema (DME) management during pregnancy
