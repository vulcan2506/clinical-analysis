# vascular complications and ulceration risk
*Source: ICMR_Guidelines_for_Management_of_Type_1_Diabetes.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** vascular complications and ulceration risk management

**Core Management Principles:**
- Monitor for micro/macroangiopathy to assess increased risk of skin ulceration
- Screen for secondary infection risks associated with diabetic vascular damage
- Implement preventive strategies to mitigate ulceration in patients with vascular complications
- Perform annual comprehensive foot examination including assessment of peripheral neuropathy, peripheral arterial disease, and foot deformities in T1DM patients with vascular complications
- Educate patients on daily self-foot inspection and proper footwear to prevent ulceration
- Refer patients with high-risk foot (e.g., Charcot foot, previous ulceration) to multidisciplinary foot care team for specialized management

**Clinical Prerequisites:**
- Diagnosis of Type 1 Diabetes Mellitus
- Evidence of micro/macroangiopathy
- Clinical signs of skin ulceration or secondary infection
- Presence of peripheral neuropathy (confirmed by monofilament testing or vibration perception threshold) as a prerequisite for ulceration risk stratification
- Documentation of ankle-brachial index (ABI) or toe-brachial index (TBI) to assess peripheral arterial disease severity in patients with suspected macroangiopathy
