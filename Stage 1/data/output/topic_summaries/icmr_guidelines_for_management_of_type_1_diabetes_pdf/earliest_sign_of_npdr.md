# earliest sign of NPDR
*Source: ICMR_Guidelines_for_Management_of_Type_1_Diabetes.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** earliest sign of NPDR

**Core Management Principles:**
- Presence of microaneurysms as small deep-red dots (25–100µm) within the retina
- Microaneurysms are outpouchings from retinal capillaries due to pericyte loss
- Disease classified as mild non-proliferative diabetic retinopathy (NPDR) at this stage
- Presence of 'featureless retina' as a sign of severe retinal hypoxia in advanced NPDR
- Appearance of venous beading (sausage-like or beaded retinal veins) as a marker of worsening NPDR
- Development of intra-retinal microvascular abnormalities (IRMA) as abnormal fine vascular patterns within the retina

**Clinical Prerequisites:**
- Dilated fundus examination for retinal assessment
- Stereo fundus photography or slit lamp biomicroscopy for detailed evaluation
- Evaluation for diabetic macular edema (DME) requires three-dimensional assessment using dilated slit lamp biomicroscopy and/or stereo fundus photography
- Definition of clinically significant macular edema (CSME) based on ETDRS criteria: retinal thickening within 500 μm of the macula center, hard exudates within 500 μm with adjacent thickening, or retinal thickening within one-disc diameter of the macula center
