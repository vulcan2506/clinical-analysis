# long-acting insulin analogue use in T1DM
*Source: ICMR_Guidelines_for_Management_of_Type_1_Diabetes.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** long-acting insulin analogue use in T1DM

**Core Management Principles:**
- Use relatively peakless long-acting insulin analogues (e.g., glargine, detemir, degludec) instead of intermediate-acting NPH insulin to reduce nocturnal hypoglycemia
- Prefer rapid-acting insulin analogues (e.g., aspart, lispro, glulisine) over regular insulin in patients with delayed pre-meal hypoglycemia
- Consider extra cost of insulin analogues and restrict substitution to cases where hypoglycemia persists despite best measures
- Consider switching to long-acting insulin analogues (glargine, detemir, degludec) if nocturnal hypoglycemia persists despite optimizing NPH dosing and lifestyle adjustments

**Clinical Prerequisites:**
- Hypoglycemia must persist despite best measures (e.g., ensuring mid-meal snack) before switching to insulin analogues
- Ensure mid-meal snacks are provided as a first-line measure before considering insulin analogue substitution
