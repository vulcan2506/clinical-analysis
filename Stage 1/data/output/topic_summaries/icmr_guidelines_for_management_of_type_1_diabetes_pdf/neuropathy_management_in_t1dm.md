# neuropathy management in T1DM
*Source: ICMR_Guidelines_for_Management_of_Type_1_Diabetes.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** neuropathy management in T1DM

**Core Management Principles:**
- Neuropathy is a common microvascular complication in T1DM, contributing significantly to foot ulcers and lower extremity amputations.
- Management focuses on achieving and maintaining good glycemic control (HbA1C <7%) to prevent and potentially reverse early-stage neuropathy.
- Risk factors such as obesity, hypertension, dyslipidemia, smoking, and alcohol use should be addressed as part of management.
- Diagnosis involves clinical tests like ankle jerk, 128 Hz tuning fork vibration test, and 10 g monofilament test for large fiber neuropathy.
- Cardiovascular autonomic function tests (e.g., heart rate variability, 30:15 ratio) are used to assess autonomic neuropathy.
- Neuropathy prevalence in T1DM shows a sharp increase in patients aged >16 years (59.1%) compared to those <16 years (9.5%), and in those with diabetes duration >10 years (35.0%) versus <10 years (9.4%).
- Small fiber neuropathy (81%) is more prevalent than large fiber neuropathy (59%) in long-term T1DM (mean duration 40 years).

**Clinical Prerequisites:**
- HbA1C levels must be maintained below 7% to reduce onset and progression of diabetic neuropathy.
- Diagnostic tests (e.g., monofilament, tuning fork) must be performed to confirm neuropathy and assess severity.
- Cardiovascular autonomic function tests require specific conditions (e.g., resting supine position, respiratory rate of 6/min).
- Pharmacological agents like epalrestat or benfotiamine may be considered, but require monitoring for adverse effects (e.g., hepatic dysfunction).
- Intensive glycemic control is critical, as hyperglycemia is the primary driver of neuropathy progression.
- Cardiovascular autonomic function tests require specific conditions: resting supine position, respiratory rate of 6/min, and ECG monitoring for heart rate variability (HRV) interpretation.
- Diagnostic tests for large fiber neuropathy include ankle jerk, 128 Hz tuning fork vibration test, and 10 g monofilament test (abnormal if loss of protective sensation at one out of four sites).
