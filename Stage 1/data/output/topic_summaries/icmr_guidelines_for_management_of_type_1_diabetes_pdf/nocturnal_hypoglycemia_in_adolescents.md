# nocturnal hypoglycemia in adolescents
*Source: ICMR_Guidelines_for_Management_of_Type_1_Diabetes.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** nocturnal hypoglycemia in adolescents with T1DM

**Core Management Principles:**
- Nocturnal hypoglycemia is a significant concern in adolescents with T1DM due to the longest inter-prandial interval and reduced sympathoadrenal responses during sleep.
- Adolescents are less likely to be awakened by hypoglycemia compared to non-diabetic individuals, increasing the risk of undetected events.
- Fear of undetected nocturnal hypoglycemia leading to severe outcomes (e.g., brain damage or death) is a major psychological burden.
- Bedtime snacks are a traditional approach to prevent nocturnal hypoglycemia.
- The Somogyi phenomenon (midnight hypoglycemia causing high morning fasting blood glucose) should be considered before increasing nighttime insulin doses.

**Clinical Prerequisites:**
- Use of insulin analogs in multiple daily injection (MDI) regimens or continuous subcutaneous insulin infusion (CSII) regimens to reduce nocturnal hypoglycemia risk.
- Consider bedtime administration of slowly digested complex carbohydrates (e.g., uncooked cornstarch) for sustained glucose delivery.
- Evaluate the possibility of the Somogyi phenomenon before adjusting nighttime intermediate or long-acting insulin doses.
