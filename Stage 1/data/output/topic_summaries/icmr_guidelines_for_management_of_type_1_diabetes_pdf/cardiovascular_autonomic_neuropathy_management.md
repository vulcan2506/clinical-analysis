# cardiovascular autonomic neuropathy management
*Source: ICMR_Guidelines_for_Management_of_Type_1_Diabetes.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** cardiovascular autonomic neuropathy (CAN) management in T1DM

**Core Management Principles:**
- CAN increases cardiovascular disease (CVD) risk, with early stages often asymptomatic (reduced heart rate variability during deep breathing may be the only abnormality).
- Advanced CAN may present with resting tachycardia, orthostatic hypotension, exercise intolerance, and loss of nocturnal blood pressure dip.
- CAN is associated with increased mortality, sudden death (malignant arrhythmias), silent myocardial ischemia, and delayed CVD presentation.
- Intensification of blood glucose and blood pressure control in CAN patients may paradoxically increase cardiovascular event risk.
- Smoking cessation is strongly recommended to reduce vascular complications and CVD risk in T1DM.
- CAN may be associated with impaired coronary flow reserve in T1DM patients without CAD, predicting future diastolic dysfunction
- Diabetic dyslipidemia in T1DM is characterized by elevated triglycerides, low HDL-cholesterol, and predominance of small dense LDL particles

**Clinical Prerequisites:**
- Routine screening for CAN in T1DM patients, particularly those with poor glycemic control, hypertension, dyslipidemia, or obesity.
- Assessment of lipid profile (total cholesterol, LDL-C, HDL-C, triglycerides) in T1DM patients with poor glycemic control or obesity.
- Evaluation for silent myocardial ischemia in T1DM patients with CAN due to delayed presentation risk.
- Monitoring for orthostatic hypotension and resting tachycardia in advanced CAN cases.
- Lifestyle modification (diet, physical activity) to address obesity and insulin resistance in T1DM.
- Assessment of lipid profile (total cholesterol, LDL-C, HDL-C, triglycerides) in T1DM patients with poor glycemic control (HbA1c ≥9.5%) or obesity
