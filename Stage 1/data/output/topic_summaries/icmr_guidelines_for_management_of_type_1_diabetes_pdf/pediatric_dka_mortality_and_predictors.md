# pediatric DKA mortality and predictors
*Source: ICMR_Guidelines_for_Management_of_Type_1_Diabetes.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** pediatric DKA mortality and predictors

**Core Management Principles:**
- Cerebral edema is a leading cause of mortality in pediatric DKA, with risk factors including younger age, new-onset diabetes, high blood urea nitrogen, severe acidosis, and bicarbonate therapy.
- Delayed diagnosis or missed presentation of T1DM increases DKA risk at onset, particularly in younger children.
- Hypoglycemia unawareness and counterregulatory hormone defects are associated with increased risk of severe hypoglycemia and DKA.
- Insulin omission or inadequate insulin therapy is a common precipitating factor for DKA in established T1DM.
- Infections and poor adherence to insulin therapy contribute to DKA recurrence and poor outcomes.
- Infections (e.g., mucormycosis, malignant otitis externa, necrotizing fasciitis, and injection-site abscesses) are significant contributors to morbidity and mortality in pediatric T1DM, particularly in poorly controlled diabetes.

**Clinical Prerequisites:**
- Early recognition of DKA symptoms (polyuria, polydipsia, weight loss, vomiting) and immediate fluid resuscitation.
- Monitoring for cerebral edema during DKA management, including neurological assessment and avoidance of rapid fluid shifts.
- Use of standardized DKA protocols with careful insulin and fluid management to prevent complications.
- Education on sick-day management and insulin adjustment to prevent DKA in children with T1DM.
- Regular follow-up to assess glycemic control and address barriers to insulin adherence.
- Use of standardized Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS) for neurological assessment in pediatric DKA to monitor for cerebral edema progression.
