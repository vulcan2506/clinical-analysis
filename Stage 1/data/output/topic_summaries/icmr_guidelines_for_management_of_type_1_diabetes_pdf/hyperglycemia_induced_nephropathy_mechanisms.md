# hyperglycemia-induced nephropathy mechanisms
*Source: ICMR_Guidelines_for_Management_of_Type_1_Diabetes.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** hyperglycemia-induced nephropathy mechanisms in T1DM

**Core Management Principles:**
- Hyperglycemia is the primary initiating factor for nephropathy in T1DM.
- Hyperglycemia alters renal blood flow and glomerular vascular permeability.
- Hyperglycemia stimulates mesangial matrix expansion and mesangial cell apoptosis.
- Hyperglycemia induces mitochondrial superoxide production, leading to oxidative stress and mitochondrial DNA damage.
- Advanced glycation end-products (AGEs) interact with RAGE to promote tubulointerstitial fibrosis.
- Hyperglycemia reduces nitric oxide synthesis in efferent vessels, increasing sensitivity to angiotensin II.
- Hyperglycemia, AGEs, and ROS activate protein kinase C (PKC), promoting glomerular basement membrane thickening.

**Clinical Prerequisites:**
- Monitoring and strict glycemic control to prevent nephropathy progression.
- Assessment of renal function and albuminuria for early detection of nephropathy.
- Assessment of prorenin activity for evaluating fibrogenesis risk in T1DM.
