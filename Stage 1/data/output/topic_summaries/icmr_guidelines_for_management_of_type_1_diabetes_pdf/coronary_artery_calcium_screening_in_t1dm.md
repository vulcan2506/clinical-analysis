# coronary artery calcium screening in T1DM
*Source: ICMR_Guidelines_for_Management_of_Type_1_Diabetes.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** coronary artery calcium (CAC) screening in T1DM

**Core Management Principles:**
- CAC screening by EBCT is suggested as an initial screening tool for CVD risk assessment in T1DM.
- CAC score >400 correlates most strongly with clinical coronary artery disease (CAD) in T1DM.
- CAC prevalence increases with age (11% in <38 years, 88% in 50–55 years) and is higher in males than females.
- CAC testing should be followed by stress testing if CAC score >400.
- CIMT, ABPI, endothelial dysfunction tests, and CCTA/MPI are not routinely recommended for asymptomatic T1DM.
- CAC prevalence in T1DM is higher in males (39%) than females (12%) based on the CACTI study

**Clinical Prerequisites:**
- Suboptimal glycemic control increases CAC progression risk.
- CAC screening should be considered in adults with T1DM, particularly those with poor glycemic control.
- ABPI interpretation requires comparison of ankle and brachial systolic pressures.
- CAC screening should be paired with clinical CAD risk factor assessment.
- ABPI measurement requires comparison of ankle systolic pressure (higher of posterior tibial artery or dorsal pedis artery) and brachial artery pressure (higher of right or left arm)
