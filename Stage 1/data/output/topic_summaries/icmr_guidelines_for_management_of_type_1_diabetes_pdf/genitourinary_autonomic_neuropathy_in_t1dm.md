# genitourinary autonomic neuropathy in T1DM
*Source: ICMR_Guidelines_for_Management_of_Type_1_Diabetes.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** genitourinary autonomic neuropathy in T1DM

**Core Management Principles:**
- Urinary incontinence and diabetic cystopathy (neurogenic bladder) manifesting as nocturia, frequent urination, urgency, and weak urinary stream.
- Genital dysfunction in men includes erectile dysfunction and retrograde ejaculation; in women, decreased sexual desire, pain during intercourse, decreased arousal, and inadequate lubrication.
- Sudomotor dysfunction may present as hypohidrosis/anhidrosis of the lower body (increasing foot ulcer risk) or hyperhidrosis of the upper body, including gustatory sweating.
- Autonomic neuropathy contributes to Charcot neuroarthropathy via increased peripheral blood flow and bone resorption.
- Diabetic ophthalmoplegia (cranial nerve III, VI, VII involvement) presents with ptosis, diplopia, and orbital pain, with pupillary sparing in most cases.
- Carpal tunnel syndrome manifesting as median nerve compression symptoms due to pressure damage under the flexor retinaculum.
- Abdominal pain in dermatomal distribution with asymmetric abdominal bulge due to muscle weakness.

**Clinical Prerequisites:**
- Exclusion of surgical causes (e.g., aneurysm, neoplasms) in cases of diabetic ophthalmoplegia with pupillary involvement.
- Differential diagnosis of genitourinary autonomic symptoms to rule out conditions like herpes zoster or spinal nerve root compression.
- Evaluation of proximal lower limb weakness and pain to distinguish diabetic amyotrophy from CIDP, MGUS, or inflammatory vasculitis.
- Exclusion of surgical causes (aneurysm, neoplasms) specifically for diabetic ophthalmoplegia cases with pupillary involvement.
- Differential diagnosis of abdominal pain to rule out herpes zoster and spinal nerve root compression.
- Differential diagnosis of diabetic amyotrophy to distinguish from CIDP, MGUS, and inflammatory vasculitis.
