# mitochondrial diabetes characteristics
*Source: ICMR_Guidelines_for_Management_of_Type_1_Diabetes.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** mitochondrial diabetes characteristics

**Core Management Principles:**
- Mitochondrial diabetes presents with maternal inheritance and bilateral hearing impairment in most carriers.
- Average age of onset is between 35 and 40 years, later than typical T1DM or other monogenic diabetes.
- May initially resemble T2DM, treated with diet or sulfonylureas, but progresses to insulin dependence due to insulinopenia.
- Metformin is contraindicated due to risk of lactic acidosis.
- Diagnosis requires genetic analysis, often linked to A3243G mutation in mitochondrial DNA.
- Mitochondrial diabetes is also known as maternally inherited diabetes and deafness (MIDD).
- It can initially present with phenotypes resembling T1DM or T2DM.

**Clinical Prerequisites:**
- Genetic testing for confirmation of mitochondrial DNA mutations.
- Family history assessment for maternal transmission pattern.
- Audiological evaluation for bilateral hearing impairment.
- Avoidance of metformin in confirmed or suspected cases.
- Transition to insulin therapy as beta-cell function declines.
- Robust familial clustering assessment for maternal transmission pattern.
- Audiological evaluation for bilateral hearing impairment in suspected cases.
