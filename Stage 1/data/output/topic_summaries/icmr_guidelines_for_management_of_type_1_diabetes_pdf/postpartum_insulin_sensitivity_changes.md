# postpartum insulin sensitivity changes
*Source: ICMR_Guidelines_for_Management_of_Type_1_Diabetes.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Postpartum Insulin Sensitivity & Breastfeeding Management

**Core Management Principles:**
- Insulin requirement declines by 30-40% immediately after delivery due to lack of placental hormonal influence.
- Insulin requirements gradually increase over the next weeks and usually return to pre-pregnancy levels in 2 to 4 months.
- Women should be advised to have a meal or snack before or during breastfeeding to decrease the risk of hypoglycemia.
- Breastfeeding should be initiated within 30 minutes after birth and provided every 2 and 3 hours to avoid neonatal hypoglycemia.
- Medicines for the treatment of diabetes related complications and co-morbidities that were discontinued for safety reasons before or during pregnancy should be avoided during breastfeeding period.

**Clinical Prerequisites:**
- Patient must be in the postpartum period immediately following delivery.
- Patient must be managing Type 1 Diabetes or insulin-dependent diabetes.
- Patient is breastfeeding or planning to breastfeed.
- Patient requires monitoring for diabetes related complications (especially diabetic retinopathy), thyroid status, and mental status (depression).
- Patient requires contraception counseling to avoid unplanned pregnancies.
