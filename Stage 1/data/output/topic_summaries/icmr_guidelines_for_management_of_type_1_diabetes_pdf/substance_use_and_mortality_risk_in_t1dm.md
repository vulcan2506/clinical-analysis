# substance use and mortality risk in T1DM
*Source: ICMR_Guidelines_for_Management_of_Type_1_Diabetes.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** substance use and mortality risk in T1DM

**Core Management Principles:**
- Adolescents with T1DM may engage in experimental high-risk behaviors due to chronic illness stress.
- Smoking worsens glycemic control, increases cardiovascular risk, and accelerates microvascular complications in T1DM.
- Alcohol consumption impairs gluconeogenesis, increases risk of DKA and hypoglycemia, and reduces diabetes self-care behaviors.
- Street drug use is associated with poor glycemic control, acute events, and higher mortality in T1DM.
- Physicians should actively counsel patients against smoking initiation and provide cessation strategies.

**Clinical Prerequisites:**
- Smoking cessation counseling should be a routine component of diabetes care.
- Patients should be educated on 'responsible drinking' including carbohydrate intake and frequent glucose monitoring.
- Peers should be informed about diabetes diagnosis and hypoglycemia risk management.
- Physicians should frequently assess substance use tacitly and refer for psychotherapy as needed.
- ADA recommends against the use of e-cigarettes for smoking cessation.
- Patients should never drink alcohol alone.
- Peers must be informed about the patient's diabetes diagnosis and the specific risks of hypoglycemia and DKA.
