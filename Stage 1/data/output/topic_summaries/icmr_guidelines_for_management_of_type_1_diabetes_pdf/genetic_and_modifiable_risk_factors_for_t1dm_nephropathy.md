# genetic and modifiable risk factors for T1DM nephropathy
*Source: ICMR_Guidelines_for_Management_of_Type_1_Diabetes.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** genetic and modifiable risk factors for T1DM nephropathy

**Core Management Principles:**
- Genetic factors (e.g., FRMD3, CARD, AFF3 genes, chromosome 15q26 intergenic SNP, ERBB4 intronic SNP) contribute to susceptibility or protection from nephropathy in T1DM.
- Hyperglycemia is a critical modifiable risk factor for both initiation and progression of diabetic nephropathy.
- Hypertension, dyslipidemia, and proteinuria are key modifiable risk factors for progression of nephropathy.
- Poor glycemic control early in T1DM has a 'legacy effect' or 'metabolic memory,' increasing long-term risk of complications even after later glycemic improvement.
- Higher BMI, smoking, and oral contraceptive pill use are modifiable risk factors for progression of diabetic nephropathy.

**Clinical Prerequisites:**
- Early and sustained glycemic control is essential to prevent initiation and progression of nephropathy.
- Regular monitoring of blood pressure, lipid levels, and proteinuria is required for risk assessment and management.
- Genetic screening for susceptibility loci may be considered in high-risk families with clustering of T1DM and nephropathy.
