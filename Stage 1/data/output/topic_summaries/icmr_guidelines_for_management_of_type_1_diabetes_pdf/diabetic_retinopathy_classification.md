# diabetic retinopathy classification
*Source: ICMR_Guidelines_for_Management_of_Type_1_Diabetes.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** diabetic retinopathy classification in T1DM

**Core Management Principles:**
- ETDRS classification is complex, requires comparison with standard photographs, and is difficult to apply in clinical settings but best suited for research studies.
- International Classification of Diabetic Retinopathy and Diabetic Macular Edema is recommended for simpler, clinically useful classification.
- DR prevalence in T1DM increases with disease duration (17% at <5 years to 97.5% at ≥15 years).
- Intensive glycemic control reduces DR risk by 76% and slows progression by 54%, with lasting benefits despite HbA1c equalization.
- DR burden is higher in T1DM compared to adult-onset T2DM (77.3% vs. 25.2% for any DR, 32.4% vs. 3.0% for PDR).
- ETDRS classification uses Modified Airlie House classification to label fundus picture areas from 1 to 7 for detailed grading of DR severity.

**Clinical Prerequisites:**
- Fundus photography (e.g., four-field digital retinal color photography) is preferred for accurate DR diagnosis.
- Glycemic control (HbA1c targets) is critical for DR prevention and progression.
- Regular screening for DR is essential, especially in long-duration T1DM.
- Differential diagnosis must exclude retinal vein occlusion, ocular ischemic syndrome, sickle cell retinopathy, and radiation retinopathy.
- Use of standardized classification systems (ETDRS or International Classification) for consistent grading.
- Use of four-field digital retinal color photography is preferred for accurate DR diagnosis in Indian clinical settings, as demonstrated in studies like Rajalakshmi et al.
