# HbA1c monitoring and target guidelines in T1DM
*Source: ICMR_Guidelines_for_Management_of_Type_1_Diabetes.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** HbA1c monitoring and target guidelines in T1DM

**Core Management Principles:**
- HbA1c measurement should be performed at least every 3 months in patients with T1DM using NGSP-certified assays standardized to the DCCT reference assay.
- HbA1c targets should be individualized for each patient, aiming for values as close to normal as possible while avoiding severe hypoglycemia and frequent mild to moderate hypoglycemia.
- For children and adolescents, HbA1c targets vary by guidelines (ISPAD: <7%, NICE: <6.5%, ADA: <7.5%), with special caution in children below six years due to high risk of adverse neurologic outcomes from severe hypoglycemia.
- Adolescents often struggle to achieve HbA1c targets due to hormonal changes, psychological adjustments, and increased independence in diabetes care.
- Hemoglobinopathies (e.g., HbS, HbE, β-thalassemia) may interfere with HbA1c measurement, requiring alternative methods like immunoassays, boronate affinity HPLC, or non-HbA1c biomarkers (fructosamine, glycated albumin, 1-5 anhydroglucitol).
- HbA1c targets in children below six years should prioritize avoidance of severe hypoglycemia due to high risk of adverse neurologic outcomes, even if it means accepting higher HbA1c values than in older children or adults.

**Clinical Prerequisites:**
- HbA1c assays must be NGSP-certified and standardized to the DCCT reference assay for accurate measurement.
- Alternative glycemic biomarkers (e.g., fructosamine) should be used in patients with hemoglobinopathies or accelerated red cell turnover.
- Caregiver involvement is critical for achieving HbA1c targets in children below six years.
- HbA1c targets should be individualized based on age, hypoglycemia risk, and patient-specific factors.
- Healthcare providers should recognize the challenges in adolescents (hormonal, psychological) when setting HbA1c targets.
- For patients with hemoglobinopathies (e.g., HbS, HbE, β-thalassemia), alternative glycemic biomarkers (fructosamine, glycated albumin, 1-5 anhydroglucitol) must be used instead of HbA1c for accurate glycemic monitoring.
