# gastrointestinal neuropathy diagnosis
*Source: ICMR_Guidelines_for_Management_of_Type_1_Diabetes.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Gastrointestinal autonomic neuropathy diagnosis

**Core Management Principles:**
- Assess for esophageal dysmotility, gastroparesis, enteropathy, colonic hypomotility, and fecal incontinence as major manifestations of gastrointestinal autonomic neuropathy.
- Differentiate acute diabetic gastroparesis (reversible with severe hyperglycemia) from chronic gastroparesis (due to autonomic neuropathy).
- Evaluate upper abdominal symptoms like postprandial fullness or glycemic fluctuations in chronic gastroparesis.
- Assess for small intestinal bacterial overgrowth (SIBO) in patients with suspected enteropathy or chronic diarrhea, as it is a common complication of diabetic enteropathy.

**Clinical Prerequisites:**
- Presence of diabetes mellitus as the underlying cause.
- Symptoms consistent with gastrointestinal autonomic dysfunction (e.g., nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, constipation, or fecal incontinence).
- Exclusion of other causes of gastrointestinal symptoms (e.g., structural or medication-related).
- Documentation of persistent symptoms for at least 3 months despite glycemic control optimization before attributing gastrointestinal symptoms to autonomic neuropathy.
