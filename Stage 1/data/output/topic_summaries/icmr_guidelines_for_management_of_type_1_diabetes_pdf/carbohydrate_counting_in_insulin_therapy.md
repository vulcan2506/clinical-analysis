# carbohydrate counting in insulin therapy
*Source: ICMR_Guidelines_for_Management_of_Type_1_Diabetes.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** carbohydrate counting in insulin therapy

**Core Management Principles:**
- Carbohydrate counting is essential for patients using continuous subcutaneous insulin infusion (CSII) and beneficial for those on basal-bolus regimens.
- Three levels of carbohydrate counting are defined: Level 1 (consistent carbohydrate intake), Level 2 (pattern management), and Level 3 (insulin-to-carbohydrate ratios).
- Level 3 (ICRs) is most recommended for patients on multiple daily injections (MDI) or CSII, with initial ICR calculated as 500/TDD (or 450/TDD for regular insulin).
- Divide daily calorie intake into 6-7 meals (3 major meals and 3-4 snacks), with specific caloric distribution (breakfast 20%, lunch/dinner 25-30%, snacks 10%).
- Bedtime snack should include at least 7-8 g of protein to prevent nocturnal hypoglycemia.
- Use of 10-12 g carbohydrate portions or 15 g carbohydrate exchanges as alternative counting methods (none superior to gram counting)
- Adjustment of insulin doses for food and exercise based on pattern management (Level 2) by monitoring blood glucose responses

**Clinical Prerequisites:**
- Patients on MDI or CSII should use insulin-to-carbohydrate ratios (ICRs) for prandial insulin dose adjustment.
- Major meals and snacks containing >10-15 g carbohydrates must be covered with a bolus insulin dose.
- Meal timing should be consistent with no significant deviations to match insulin regimen.
- Nutritional intake must align with the specific insulin regimen (e.g., fixed vs. flexible eating patterns).
- Fine-tuning of ICRs requires checking blood glucose before and 2 hours post-meal to maintain BG increment <60 mg/dl.
- ICR calculation must account for patient-specific factors (age, sex, pubertal status, duration of diagnosis, and activity)
- Meal timing should have no significant and frequent deviations to align with insulin regimen
