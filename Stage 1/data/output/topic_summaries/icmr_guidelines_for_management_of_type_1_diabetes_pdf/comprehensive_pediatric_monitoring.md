# comprehensive pediatric monitoring
*Source: ICMR_Guidelines_for_Management_of_Type_1_Diabetes.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** comprehensive pediatric monitoring in T1DM

**Core Management Principles:**
- Track glycemic control using HbA1c every 3 months or Time in Range (TIR) if using CGMS from the beginning.
- Monitor growth, development, blood pressure, puberty, and retina regularly.
- Perform periodic tests for comorbidities: TSH, tTG-IgA, lipids, hemoglobin, and urine albumin-creatinine ratio.
- Educate families on sick day management: frequent BG checks, ketone monitoring, insulin dose adjustments, and hydration.
- Teach families to recognize emergency signs (e.g., rapid breathing, altered behavior) and know whom to contact.
- Calculate total daily dose (TDD) of insulin by summing all units (long and short-acting) for sick day management adjustments.
- Administer extra rapid/short-acting insulin as a percentage of TDD (e.g., 5-10%) based on blood ketone levels and glucose ranges during sick days.

**Clinical Prerequisites:**
- Regular daily SMBG, multiple dose basal-bolus insulin regimen, and ongoing self-adjustments for glycemic control.
- Blood ketone meters (e.g., Freestyle Optium) should be used for monitoring during sick days.
- Pump users must monitor BG frequently and keep extra rapid/short-acting insulin supplies.
- Sick children should be reviewed by an endocrinologist or pediatrician at illness onset.
- Avoid skipping insulin doses during illness, even if the child is not eating.
- Ensure families have access to blood ketone meters (e.g., Freestyle Optium) and test strips for reliable sick day monitoring.
- Pump users must maintain frequent BG monitoring and keep backup rapid/short-acting insulin supplies to prevent DKA in case of pump malfunction.
