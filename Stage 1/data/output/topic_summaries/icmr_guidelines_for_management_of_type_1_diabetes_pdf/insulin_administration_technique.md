# insulin administration technique
*Source: ICMR_Guidelines_for_Management_of_Type_1_Diabetes.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Insulin administration technique and site rotation

**Core Management Principles:**
- Inject insulin subcutaneously, avoiding intradermal and intramuscular routes to prevent pain and erratic absorption.
- Rotate injection sites daily within a specific quadrant (e.g., moving 0.5-1.0 inch) to prevent lipodystrophy.
- Select abdomen for faster-acting insulin and avoid thigh sites prior to vigorous exercise to prevent hypoglycemia.
- Use smaller, narrow-gauge needles and allow insulin to reach room temperature to reduce injection pain.
- Avoid using alcohol swabs on the injection site to prevent loss of needle silicone coating and subsequent pain.
- Change needles after a single use where possible to reduce pain from blunt tips.
- Take insulin out of the refrigerator approximately 30 minutes before injecting to prevent cold-induced pain.

**Clinical Prerequisites:**
- Patient must be able to identify and rotate specific injection sites within defined quadrants.
- For deltoid and buttock injections, assistance from another person is required.
- In young children, deltoid and buttock sites must be avoided due to small muscle mass and nerve proximity.
- Injection sites must be clean and dry; if visibly dirty, clean with soap and water instead of alcohol.
- Thigh injection sites should be avoided if the patient plans to perform running or similar vigorous exercise.
- Injection on the deltoid region and buttocks requires assistance from another person.
- In young children, the deltoid region and buttocks must be avoided due to small muscle mass and proximity to the sciatic nerve.
