# barriers to T1DM management in poverty
*Source: ICMR_Guidelines_for_Management_of_Type_1_Diabetes.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** cost-driven barriers to T1DM management in poverty

**Core Management Principles:**
- Families may ration insulin or blood glucose (BG) testing due to financial constraints and shame in disclosing this to healthcare teams.
- Parents prioritize cost-effectiveness and affordability in all aspects of diabetes care, including insulin type and delivery methods.
- Reusing lancets, needles, and other supplies with proper care is practiced to reduce costs.
- Families explore bulk purchasing of supplies and income generation to offset expenses.
- Peer support, charitable options, and subsidized programs are leveraged to improve access to care.
- Families may face food insecurity, which directly impacts glycemic control and complicates T1DM management due to inconsistent access to adequate nutrition.

**Clinical Prerequisites:**
- Insulin in vials is preferred over cartridges for cost savings.
- Reusable insulin pens are prioritized over disposable ones to reduce expenses.
- Multiple-dose insulin regimens are used to achieve metabolic control without relying on insulin pumps.
- Proper care and hygiene practices are maintained when reusing supplies like lancets and needles.
- Diabetes care education is emphasized as a mandatory requirement to overcome barriers posed by poverty and illiteracy, enabling families to implement cost-effective strategies.
