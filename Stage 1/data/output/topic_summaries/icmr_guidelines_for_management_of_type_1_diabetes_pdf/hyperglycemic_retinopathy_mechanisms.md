# hyperglycemic retinopathy mechanisms
*Source: ICMR_Guidelines_for_Management_of_Type_1_Diabetes.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** hyperglycemic retinopathy pathophysiology and progression

**Core Management Principles:**
- Chronic hyperglycemia drives retinopathy via polyol pathway accumulation, non-enzymatic protein glycation, PKC activation, and RAAS changes.
- Mechanisms induce oxidative stress through increased reactive oxygen species (ROS) and reduced free oxygen clearance.
- Endothelial damage and pericyte loss lead to blood-retinal barrier breakdown, leakage, edema, and microaneurysm formation.
- Capillary basement membrane thickening and extracellular matrix deposition cause capillary occlusion and retinal ischemia.
- Ischemia stimulates VEGF production, triggering angiogenesis and neovascularization that result in proliferative diabetic retinopathy (PDR).
- Hemodynamic changes (e.g., increased blood flow and shear stress) contribute to endothelial dysfunction and vascular damage in retinal capillaries under hyperglycemia.

**Clinical Prerequisites:**
- Recognition of chronic hyperglycemia as the primary risk factor for retinopathy development.
- Monitoring for clinical signs of endothelial damage, pericyte loss, and capillary occlusion.
- Assessment of VEGF-mediated pathways in cases of proliferative changes or neovascularization.
- Evaluation of oxidative stress markers and inflammatory responses in retinal tissue.
- Differentiation between non-proliferative and proliferative stages based on ischemia and neovascularization.
- Monitoring for subclinical inflammation and leukostasis as early indicators of retinal vascular damage in T1DM patients.
