# 2019 WHO diabetes classification
*Source: ICMR_Guidelines_for_Management_of_Type_1_Diabetes.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** 2019 WHO diabetes classification updates

**Core Management Principles:**
- Removed subtypes for T1DM and T2DM in classification
- Introduced 'hybrid forms of diabetes' (e.g., slowly evolving immune-mediated diabetes of adults, ketosis-prone type 2 diabetes)
- Added 'unclassified diabetes' as a temporary label for unclear diagnoses near onset
- Included 'Diabetes mellitus in Pregnancy' as a distinct entity, with Gestational Diabetes Mellitus under 'Hyperglycemia first detected during pregnancy'
- Renamed 'Latent autoimmune diabetes in adults' (LADA) to 'slowly evolving immune-mediated diabetes of adults'

**Clinical Prerequisites:**
- Diagnosis must consider multiple factors (e.g., age, BMI, autoimmunity, insulin sensitivity) rather than isolated features
- Autoantibody testing required for differentiation in ambiguous cases
- Genetic testing recommended for neonatal diabetes or suspected monogenic diabetes (e.g., MODY)
- C-peptide measurement and insulin dose assessment may aid in revising misdiagnosed T1DM/T2DM cases
- Differentiation from T2DM requires evaluation of insulin resistance features (e.g., acanthosis nigricans, central obesity)
- Consider 'unclassified diabetes' label for provisional diagnoses in the 20-40 age group due to high heterogeneity and misclassification risk
- Genetic testing for neonatal diabetes recommended for all cases diagnosed in infancy (e.g., KCNJ11, ABCC8, INS mutations)
