# laser photocoagulation in diabetic retinopathy
*Source: ICMR_Guidelines_for_Management_of_Type_1_Diabetes.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** laser photocoagulation in diabetic retinopathy

**Core Management Principles:**
- Grid/focal laser photocoagulation is primarily used for diabetic macular edema (DME) but indications are diminishing due to pharmacotherapy availability.
- Panretinal photocoagulation (PRP) is the gold standard for proliferative diabetic retinopathy (PDR) with high-risk characteristics.
- PRP involves administration of 1200-2000 burns in 1-2 visits, with retreatment possible if vessel regression is incomplete.
- Focal/grid laser is a one-off treatment that can be repeated if needed, though its use is diminishing in favor of intravitreal VEGF/steroid injections.
- Intravitreal VEGF injections are increasingly preferred for DME and PDR due to higher efficacy and lower collateral retinal damage.
- PRP may cause transient loss of central vision due to macular edema as a side effect.

**Clinical Prerequisites:**
- PRP is indicated for PDR with high-risk characteristics or severe non-proliferative DR when close follow-up is not feasible.
- Focal/grid laser is indicated for DME, with preference in pregnancy.
- Intravitreal VEGF injections may be used in combination with PRP (one week before or immediately before) to reduce DME.
- Laser treatment is non-invasive for PRP and one-off for focal/grid, with retreatment allowed if necessary.
- PRP is preferred for PDR or severe NPDR when close follow-up is not feasible.
