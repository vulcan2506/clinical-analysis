# insulin dosing for enteral/parenteral nutrition
*Source: ICMR_Guidelines_for_Management_of_Type_1_Diabetes.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** insulin dosing for enteral/parenteral nutrition

**Core Management Principles:**
- Intravenous insulin is the preferred approach for patients with T1DM receiving enteral or parenteral feedings
- Subcutaneous basal-bolus insulin is an alternative for those receiving intermittent feeds similar to major meals and minor snacks
- Initial doses transitioning from intravenous to subcutaneous insulin should use long/intermediate-acting insulin comprising 50% of total daily dose (TDD) with rapid-acting analogs and 25% of TDD with regular insulin
- Correctional doses should be given based on glucose levels and urgency of correction
- Preliminary dose escalation assumes 1 unit of regular human insulin or rapid-acting insulin subcutaneously per 10-15 g carbohydrate before each meal
- For patients on continuous enteral nutrition, intravenous insulin should be titrated to maintain glucose levels between 140-180 mg/dL (7.8-10.0 mmol/L) unless otherwise specified by individual patient goals
- Subcutaneous basal insulin should be initiated at least 2-4 hours before discontinuing intravenous insulin to ensure overlap and prevent rebound hyperglycemia

**Clinical Prerequisites:**
- Patients must be receiving enteral or parenteral nutrition
- For subcutaneous basal-bolus use, feeds must be intermittent and similar to major meals and minor snacks
- Transition from intravenous to subcutaneous insulin requires prior intravenous insulin use
- Dose calculations must account for carbohydrate content of meals
- Correctional dosing requires monitoring of glucose levels and clinical urgency
- Intravenous insulin transition to subcutaneous requires stable enteral/parenteral nutrition with consistent carbohydrate delivery and no planned changes in feeding rate within 24 hours
- Correctional dosing should prioritize rapid-acting insulin analogs over regular insulin for patients with T1DM due to faster onset and shorter duration of action
