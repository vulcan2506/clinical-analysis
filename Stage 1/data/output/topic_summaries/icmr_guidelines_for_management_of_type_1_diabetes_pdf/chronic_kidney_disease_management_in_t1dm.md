# chronic kidney disease management in T1DM
*Source: ICMR_Guidelines_for_Management_of_Type_1_Diabetes.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** chronic kidney disease management in T1DM

**Core Management Principles:**
- Nondiabetic renal disease (NDRD) is rare in T1DM (2–3% incidence), requiring kidney biopsy if hematuria, rapidly deteriorating renal function, sudden gross proteinuria, short diabetes duration, or absence of retinopathy is present.
- Diabetic nephropathy is rare after 30 years of diabetes without prior end-organ involvement.
- Patients with T1DM are at increased risk for renal artery stenosis (presenting as difficult-to-control hypertension, flash pulmonary edema, or progressive renal dysfunction) and papillary necrosis (with hematuria, flank pain, and fever).
- Diabetes-induced hyporeninemic hypoaldosteronism may lead to hyperchloremic metabolic acidosis and hyperkalemia.
- Papillary necrosis in T1DM can occur in the setting of urinary tract infections or intake of nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs).

**Clinical Prerequisites:**
- Kidney biopsy is required to establish the cause of kidney disease in T1DM patients with atypical features (e.g., hematuria, rapid renal decline, or absence of retinopathy).
- Strict glycemic control is emphasized to prevent or delay diabetic nephropathy progression.
- Angiotensin-converting enzyme (ACE) inhibitors or angiotensin II receptor blockers (ARBs) are recommended for patients with microalbuminuria or overt nephropathy, even in normotensive individuals.
- Ambulatory blood pressure monitoring is recommended to assess hypertension in T1DM patients with renal disease, as it provides a more accurate assessment than clinic measurements.
