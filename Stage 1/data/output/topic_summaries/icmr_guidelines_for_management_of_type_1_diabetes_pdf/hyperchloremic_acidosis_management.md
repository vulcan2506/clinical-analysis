# hyperchloremic acidosis management
*Source: ICMR_Guidelines_for_Management_of_Type_1_Diabetes.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** hyperchloremic acidosis management in T1DM

**Core Management Principles:**
- Hyperchloremic acidosis presents as normal anion gap metabolic acidosis, typically 8–12 hours after therapy initiation.
- It occurs due to administration of intravenous fluids with excessive chloride content (e.g., 0.9% normal saline).
- Management involves substituting high-chloride fluids with lower-chloride alternatives (e.g., 0.45% normal saline).
- Hyperchloremic acidosis is more likely in patients receiving large volumes of 0.9% normal saline (>2000 mL/day) or in those with pre-existing renal impairment.

**Clinical Prerequisites:**
- Monitor for normal anion gap metabolic acidosis in patients receiving intravenous fluids.
- Assess timing of acidosis onset (8–12 hours post-therapy) to differentiate from other causes.
- Monitor serum chloride levels in patients receiving prolonged intravenous therapy, especially if 0.9% normal saline is used.
