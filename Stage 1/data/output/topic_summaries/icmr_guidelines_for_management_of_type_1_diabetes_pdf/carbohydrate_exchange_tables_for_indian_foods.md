# carbohydrate exchange tables for Indian foods
*Source: ICMR_Guidelines_for_Management_of_Type_1_Diabetes.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** carbohydrate exchange tables for Indian foods

**Core Management Principles:**
- Use standardized 15 g carbohydrate exchanges for common Indian foods to guide meal planning and insulin dosing.
- Refer to specific portion sizes for breads, cereals, starchy vegetables, snack foods, fruits, biscuits, and sweets.
- Apply portion-based exchanges (e.g., 1 slice white bread = 15 g carbs, 1 small apple = 15 g carbs) for consistent carbohydrate counting.
- Differentiate carbohydrate exchanges based on specific food categories including breads, cereals, starchy vegetables, snack foods, fruits, biscuits, and sweets.
- Specify distinct portion sizes for regional staples like Bajra, Makai, and Jowar rotis compared to wheat bread.
- Account for variations in carbohydrate density by assigning smaller volumes (e.g., ¼ cup) to high-sugar fruits like blueberries versus larger volumes for other fruits.
- Include specific counts for snack items such as Papad (2), Panipuri (6), and Bhelpuri (½ cup) to standardize exchange values.

**Clinical Prerequisites:**
- Portion sizes must be measured accurately to ensure correct carbohydrate estimation.
- Exchange tables should be tailored to regional Indian dietary patterns and food availability.
- Patients must accurately measure specific portion sizes (e.g., 1 slice, ¼ roti, ½ cup) rather than estimating loosely.
- Food preparation methods must be considered, as the tables assume standard preparations (e.g., boiled potatoes) and may require adjustment for fried or mashed variants.
- Selection of foods must align with the specific regional dietary patterns and availability represented in the table categories.
