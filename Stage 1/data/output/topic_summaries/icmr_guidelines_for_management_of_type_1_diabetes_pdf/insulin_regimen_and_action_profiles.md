# insulin regimen and action profiles
*Source: ICMR_Guidelines_for_Management_of_Type_1_Diabetes.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** insulin regimen and action profiles

**Core Management Principles:**
- Short-acting recombinant regular human insulin is the most commonly used bolus (prandial) insulin to prevent post-meal glucose elevation.
- Regular insulin should be administered 20-30 minutes before meals for optimum effect; its effect peaks at 3-4 hours and total duration of action is approximately 6-8 hours.
- Intermediate-acting insulin (NPH) is used as a basal insulin in split-mix, pre-mix, or basal-bolus regimens.
- NPH insulin has a pronounced peak after 6-8 hours and the effect tails off in 12-14 hours, unlike newer basal insulin formulations.
- Regular insulin can be used via the intravenous route in diabetic ketoacidosis, perioperative period, or labor.
- Long-acting insulin analogs (e.g., glargine, detemir) are mentioned as newer basal insulin formulations with flatter, peakless action profiles compared to NPH insulin.

**Clinical Prerequisites:**
- Regular insulin must be administered 20-30 minutes before meals for optimal postprandial glucose control.
- NPH insulin cannot be given by intravenous route or in insulin pumps.
- Basal-bolus regimens require careful titration of intermediate-acting (NPH) or long-acting insulin analogs to avoid nocturnal hypoglycemia due to NPH's pronounced peak.
