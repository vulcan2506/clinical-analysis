# diabetic neuropathy classification and presentation
*Source: ICMR_Guidelines_for_Management_of_Type_1_Diabetes.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** diabetic neuropathy classification and presentation

**Core Management Principles:**
- DN is defined as the presence of signs and/or symptoms of peripheral neuropathy in a patient with diabetes, after excluding other causes.
- Symptoms are often mild and frequently unnoticed in the initial stages of DN.
- DN is mainly classified into diffuse neuropathy (>90%) and focal/multifocal neuropathy (<10%).
- Focal/multifocal neuropathies are characterized by acute/subacute onset, focal deficits, pain, and spontaneous partial/complete recovery.
- DSPN is the classical presentation of neuropathy in diabetes, typically length-dependent, starting in the feet and legs.
- DSPN can present as predominantly small-fiber neuropathy, large-fiber neuropathy, or mixed small and large-fiber neuropathy

**Clinical Prerequisites:**
- Exclusion of other causes of neuropathy before diagnosing DN.
- Recognition that DSPN is a length-dependent polyneuropathy affecting lower limbs first.
- Exclusion of other causes of neuropathy is mandatory before diagnosing DN
