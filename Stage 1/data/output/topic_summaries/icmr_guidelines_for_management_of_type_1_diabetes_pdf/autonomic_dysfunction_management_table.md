# autonomic dysfunction management table
*Source: ICMR_Guidelines_for_Management_of_Type_1_Diabetes.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** autonomic dysfunction management in T1DM

**Core Management Principles:**
- Use postural maneuvers and compression stockings for orthostatic hypotension; avoid hot water baths.
- Administer Fludrocortisone (0.5-2 mg OD) or Midodrine (2.5-10 mg TDS) for orthostatic hypotension.
- Follow a low-fat, small frequent meal diet for gastroparesis; use prokinetics like Metoclopramide or Domperidone.
- Use soluble fiber, antimotility agents (Loperamide), and gut antibiotics (Metronidazole/Rifaximin) for diarrhea.
- Rule out infective, metabolic, or intrinsic GI disorders before treating autonomic diarrhea.
- Check for warmth of water with hands/elbow before rinsing feet to avoid thermal injury in patients with sensory deficits
- Wear clean, cotton socks (preferably white) with seams facing outwards and change daily

**Clinical Prerequisites:**
- Confirm orthostatic hypotension by observing a fall in systolic BP without an increase in heart rate.
- Perform upper GI endoscopy to rule out obstruction in suspected gastroparesis.
- Conduct stool routine microscopy, culture, and HIV serology to exclude infective causes of diarrhea.
- Evaluate for exocrine pancreatic dysfunction, gluten-sensitive enteropathy, and bacterial overgrowth in diarrhea.
- Consider gastroenterology consultation for complex or refractory autonomic dysfunction cases.
- Rule out obstruction via upper GI endoscopy for suspected gastroparesis
- Evaluate for thyrotoxicosis in diarrhea management
