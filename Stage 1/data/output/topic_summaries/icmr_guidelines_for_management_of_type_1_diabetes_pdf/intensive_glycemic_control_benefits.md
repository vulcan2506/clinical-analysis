# intensive glycemic control benefits
*Source: ICMR_Guidelines_for_Management_of_Type_1_Diabetes.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Intensive glycemic control benefits in T1DM

**Core Management Principles:**
- Early intensive glycemic control reduces long-term macrovascular and microvascular complications in T1DM
- Intensive control lowers risk of CVD events by 42% and non-fatal myocardial infarction/stroke/CV death by 57% over 17 years (DCCT/EDIC)
- Intensive control reduces carotid intima-media thickness progression, a marker of CVD risk
- Intensive control lowers incident hypertension risk by 24% (HR 0.76) in long-term follow-up
- Hyperglycemia contributes to endothelial dysfunction, oxidative stress, and atherosclerotic CVD progression
- Acute hyperglycemia is associated with poor post-stroke outcomes (both ischemic and hemorrhagic stroke), increasing cerebral lactate production and reducing salvageable penumbral tissue in ischemic stroke, or expanding hematoma and perihematomal edema in hemorrhagic stroke
- Uncontrolled diabetes contributes to dyslipidemia, as shown in the SEARCH for Diabetes in Youth study, which may exacerbate cardiovascular risk in T1DM

**Clinical Prerequisites:**
- Diagnosis of type 1 diabetes mellitus (T1DM)
- No prior history of coronary artery disease (for macrovascular risk assessment)
- Baseline assessment of glycemic control (HbA1c) and cardiovascular risk factors
- Regular monitoring of blood pressure and kidney function (e.g., albuminuria, GFR)
- Patient eligibility for intensive glycemic control based on age, life expectancy, and hypoglycemia risk
- Assessment of dyslipidemia as part of baseline cardiovascular risk evaluation in T1DM
- Evaluation of stroke risk and acute hyperglycemia management protocols in T1DM patients with cerebrovascular events
