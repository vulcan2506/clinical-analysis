# hypoglycemia prevention strategy
*Source: ICMR_Guidelines_for_Management_of_Type_1_Diabetes.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Hypoglycemia prevention and management strategy

**Core Management Principles:**
- Teach patients and families to avoid hypoglycemia triggers (e.g., delaying/missing meals, exercising without adjusting insulin/calories, insulin overdose)
- Encourage frequent blood glucose (BG) testing when starting new exercise regimens or unexpected activities
- Carry a diabetes I-card with hypoglycemia symptoms and a rapid-acting glucose source (e.g., glucose tablets, sugar cubes, juice)
- Follow rapid-acting glucose with a slowly absorbed snack (e.g., milk, peanuts) to sustain recovery
- Ensure family/staff are trained to recognize hypoglycemia symptoms and administer glucagon in emergencies
- Teach patients to recognize behavioral changes (e.g., irritability, lethargy) as potential hypoglycemia symptoms in toddlers or non-verbal individuals
- Encourage patients to analyze CGM glucose trends proactively to prevent hypoglycemia episodes, especially in high-risk scenarios (e.g., nocturnal periods)

**Clinical Prerequisites:**
- Patient must be educated on hypoglycemia symptoms and triggers at diagnosis, with reinforcement during follow-ups
- Continuous Glucose Monitoring (CGM) should be used to detect asymptomatic or nocturnal hypoglycemia
- Glucagon (injectable or nasal) must be available at home, school, or work for severe hypoglycemia treatment
- BG confirmation is required before treating hypoglycemia, especially with CGM to avoid spurious lows
- Family/staff must be familiar with distinguishing persistent hyperglycemia from rebound hyperglycemia
- BG confirmation is required before treating hypoglycemia, particularly when using CGM to avoid spurious lows
- Glucagon must be available at home, school, or work for severe hypoglycemia treatment, with dosing adjusted for age (<12 years: 0.5 mg; ≥12 years: 1 mg)
