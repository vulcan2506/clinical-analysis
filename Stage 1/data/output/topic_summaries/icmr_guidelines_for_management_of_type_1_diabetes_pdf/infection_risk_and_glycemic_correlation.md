# infection risk and glycemic correlation
*Source: ICMR_Guidelines_for_Management_of_Type_1_Diabetes.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** infection risk and glycemic correlation in T1DM

**Core Management Principles:**
- Patients with diabetes face a 1.5-3.0-fold higher risk for pneumonia, soft tissue infections, and UTIs compared to the non-diabetic population.
- Mortality at 28 days is 4-fold higher in diabetic patients admitted with UTIs compared to non-diabetics.
- Each 1 mmol/L (18 mg/dL) increase in blood glucose increases the risk for pneumonia, soft tissue infection, and UTI by 6-10% after adjustment for confounding factors.
- Diabetic patients are susceptible to specific infections including rhino-orbital-cerebral mucormycosis, malignant otitis externa, necrotizing fasciitis, and diabetic foot infections.
- Infections in diabetic patients are more likely to run a complicated course compared to the general population.
- Diabetic patients are at higher risk for emphysematous cholecystitis, emphysematous pyelonephritis, and Fournier gangrene compared to the general population.

**Clinical Prerequisites:**
- Adjustment for confounding factors is required when assessing the correlation between blood glucose levels and infection risk.
- Monitoring of specific high-risk infections such as mucormycosis and necrotizing fasciitis is essential in diabetic patients.
- Assessment of 28-day mortality outcomes is critical for patients admitted with UTIs to evaluate severity.
- Routine screening for high-risk infections (e.g., mucormycosis, necrotizing fasciitis, Fournier gangrene) is recommended in diabetic patients with poor glycemic control or immunocompromised states.
