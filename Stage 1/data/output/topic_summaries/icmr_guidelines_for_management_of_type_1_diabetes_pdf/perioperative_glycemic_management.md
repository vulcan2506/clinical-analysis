# perioperative glycemic management
*Source: ICMR_Guidelines_for_Management_of_Type_1_Diabetes.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Perioperative glycemic management and insulin transition

**Core Management Principles:**
- Target perioperative blood glucose between 108-180 mg/dl (acceptable 72-216 mg/dl) to reduce morbidity and mortality.
- Prioritize patients with diabetes for the operating list as the first case of the day to avoid prolonged starvation time.
- Switch T1DM patients to basal-bolus regimen or initiate IV insulin infusion at least two hours before surgery.
- Monitor blood glucose every 30-60 minutes during the perioperative period, especially under sedation.
- Transition from IV to subcutaneous insulin only after stable BG levels for 4-6 hours and improvement in oral intake.

**Clinical Prerequisites:**
- HbA1c preferably < 8-8.5% before elective surgery.
- Absence of urine ketones and normal electrolytes and hydration status before surgery.
- Ketone status must be checked whenever blood glucose is > 250 mg/dl.
- Patient must have stable infusion rate and blood glucose in target range for at least 4 to 6 hours before IV-to-subcutaneous transition.
- Patient must be eating scheduled meals or receiving stable enteral/parenteral feeds for transition.
- Comprehensive assessment of associated comorbidities and complications must be performed well in advance of surgery.
- Patients with autonomic neuropathy are at risk for hypoglycemia unawareness, requiring specific vigilance during tight control.
