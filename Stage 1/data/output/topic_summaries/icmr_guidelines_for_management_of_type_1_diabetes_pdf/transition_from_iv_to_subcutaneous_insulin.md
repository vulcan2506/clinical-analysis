# transition from IV to subcutaneous insulin
*Source: ICMR_Guidelines_for_Management_of_Type_1_Diabetes.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** transition from IV to subcutaneous insulin in T1DM

**Core Management Principles:**
- Tapering of IV insulin infusion and initiation of a multiple-dose subcutaneous insulin regimen is recommended when plasma glucose is <200 mg/dl (11.1 mmol/L) and at least two parameters are achieved: AG <12 mmol/L, serum bicarbonate ≥15 mmol/L, venous pH >7.30, beta-hydroxybutyrate <1 mmol/L.
- Insulin infusion should be continued for 1-2 hours after initiation of subcutaneous regular insulin and 30-60 minutes after initiation of subcutaneous rapid-acting insulin analog to prevent recurrence of hyperglycemia or ketoacidosis.
- Sudden discontinuation of IV insulin before the onset of subcutaneous insulin effect is discouraged due to risk of hyperglycemia or ketoacidosis recurrence.
- If the patient is unable to eat, continuation of IV insulin infusion is preferred over transitioning to subcutaneous insulin alone.

**Clinical Prerequisites:**
- Plasma glucose <200 mg/dl (11.1 mmol/L) must be achieved before transitioning.
- At least two of the following must be met: AG <12 mmol/L, serum bicarbonate ≥15 mmol/L, venous pH >7.30, beta-hydroxybutyrate <1 mmol/L.
- Continuation of IV insulin for 1-2 hours (regular insulin) or 30-60 minutes (rapid-acting analog) after subcutaneous insulin initiation.
