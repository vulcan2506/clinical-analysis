# otitis media with effusion complications
*Source: ICMR_Guidelines_for_Management_of_Type_1_Diabetes.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** malignant otitis externa (MOE) management in T1DM

**Core Management Principles:**
- MOE is a necrotizing infection starting in the external auditory canal, rapidly spreading to deep tissues, with Pseudomonas aeruginosa as the primary pathogen.
- Patients present with throbbing otalgia, purulent ear discharge, and potential cranial nerve palsies (facial palsy early, others later).
- Otosopic examination reveals granulation tissue at the bone-cartilage junction with an intact tympanic membrane.
- CT/MRI and nuclear imaging (e.g., Technetium-99m bone scintigraphy, Gallium-67 scan) are used to assess disease extent and progression.
- Achieving glycemic control and regular aural toileting are critical alongside antimicrobial therapy.
- *Staphylococcus aureus*, *Staphylococcus epidermidis*, *Proteus mirabilis*, *Klebsiella oxytoca*, *Aspergillus niger*, *Aspergillus fumigatus*, and *Candida* species are also implicated pathogens in MOE, though *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* remains the most common
- A common inciting event for MOE is trivial trauma following ear irrigation for impacted wax or ear picking

**Clinical Prerequisites:**
- Empirical antimicrobial therapy initiation after pus sampling for cultures.
- Use of imaging (CT/MRI) to define disease extent and guide treatment.
- Surgical intervention for abscess drainage, debridement, or tissue diagnosis in non-responders.
- Total antibiotic duration of 4–6 weeks, adjusted based on culture results.
- Early detection and multidisciplinary management to reduce mortality.
- Initial empirical antimicrobial therapy should include an antipseudomonal penicillin/cephalosporin (e.g., piperacillin-tazobactam, cefoperazone-sulbactam, or ceftazidime) combined with a fluoroquinolone (e.g., ciprofloxacin)
