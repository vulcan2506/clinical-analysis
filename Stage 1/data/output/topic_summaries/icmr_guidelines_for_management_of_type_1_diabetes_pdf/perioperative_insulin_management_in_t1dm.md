# perioperative insulin management in T1DM
*Source: ICMR_Guidelines_for_Management_of_Type_1_Diabetes.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** perioperative insulin management in T1DM

**Core Management Principles:**
- Give basal insulin as usual for minor surgeries (<2 hours) with resumption of regular diet within 2-4 hours.
- Administer subcutaneous rapid-acting insulin analog for blood glucose (BG) ≥ 180 mg/dl or 50% of regular insulin before surgery if meals are allowed.
- Prefer rapid-acting insulin analogs in daycare surgeries due to faster onset (5-15 min) and peak (1-1.5 hours).
- Aim for capillary BG between 108-180 mg/dl (acceptable range 72-216 mg/dl) before surgery.
- Prefer intravenous insulin for surgeries lasting >2 hours, with transition to subcutaneous insulin only after stable BG and oral intake for 4-6 hours.
- Check blood gases if ketone or BG levels are high before surgery.

**Clinical Prerequisites:**
- Check BG, blood beta-hydroxybutyrate (if available), urinary ketones, and serum electrolytes before surgery.
- Delay surgery until correction of diabetic ketoacidosis, circulating volume, and electrolyte deficits.
- Ensure surgery is performed at centers with appropriate expertise and facilities for T1DM management.
- Evaluate patients planned for elective surgery well in advance.
- Monitor BG, ketones, and electrolytes before emergency surgery.
- Ensure oral intake has improved or patients are receiving stable enteral/parenteral feeds before transitioning from intravenous to subcutaneous insulin.
