# standardized insulin preparation labeling
*Source: ICMR_Guidelines_for_Management_of_Type_1_Diabetes.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** standardized insulin preparation labeling and storage

**Core Management Principles:**
- Use universal color coding for insulin preparations, with fixed colors per insulin type regardless of manufacturer.
- Check expiry date and inspect for clumps or discoloration before purchasing insulin.
- Store unopened insulin in the refrigerator shelf until expiry date.
- Store opened insulin vials/cartridges at room temperature (≤25–30°C) for up to 1 month in cooler climates or in the refrigerator in hot climates.
- Avoid freezing insulin and exposure to direct sunlight or heat.
- Inspect electrical supply adequacy and storage conditions at medical shops where insulin is procured.

**Clinical Prerequisites:**
- Maintain extra vials/cartridges of both short/rapid-acting and intermediate/long-acting insulin at home.
- Store insulin in personal luggage during travel; never in check-in luggage.
- Use cooled thermos flasks or earthenware pots with wet sand in hot climates without refrigeration.
- Select needle length (4–6 mm) based on body mass index to avoid intramuscular injections.
- Lift a skin fold and inject at 45–60° angle in lean individuals to minimize intramuscular risk.
- Ensure unopened insulin cartridges/disposable pens are stored in the refrigerator until their expiry date.
- Store insulin pens without needles attached to prevent air entry.
