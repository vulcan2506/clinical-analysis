# FCPD diagnostic criteria
*Source: ICMR_Guidelines_for_Management_of_Type_1_Diabetes.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** FCPD diagnostic criteria and clinical profile

**Core Management Principles:**
- FCPD is characterized by idiopathic non-alcoholic chronic pancreatitis with recurrent abdominal pain, steatorrhea, and pancreatic calcification.
- Diagnosis requires tropical country origin, presence of diabetes, evidence of chronic pancreatitis (pancreatic calculi or ≥3 of: abnormal pancreatic morphology, recurrent abdominal pain since childhood, steatorrhea, abnormal pancreatic function test), and exclusion of other causes of chronic pancreatitis.
- Most patients are lean adolescents or young adults (10–30 years) with asymptomatic or incidental detection despite hyperglycemia.
- Patients require multiple insulin doses for glycemic control but rarely develop ketosis due to endogenous insulin sufficiency, reduced glucagon reserve, or limited non-esterified fatty acids.
- FCPD is predominantly reported from Kerala and Tamil Nadu, with prevalence declining due to improved socioeconomic status and nutrition.

**Clinical Prerequisites:**
- Evidence of chronic pancreatitis (pancreatic calculi on X-ray or ≥3 diagnostic features: abnormal morphology, recurrent abdominal pain since childhood, steatorrhea, abnormal pancreatic function test).
- Exclusion of other causes of chronic pancreatitis.
- Tropical country origin (e.g., India, particularly Kerala/Tamil Nadu).
- Presence of diabetes mellitus.
- Detailed evaluation for pancreatic calcification and morphology (sonography/CT scan).
