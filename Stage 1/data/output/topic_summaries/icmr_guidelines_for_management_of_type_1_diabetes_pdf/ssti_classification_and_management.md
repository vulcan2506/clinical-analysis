# SSTI classification and management
*Source: ICMR_Guidelines_for_Management_of_Type_1_Diabetes.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** SSTI classification and management in T1DM

**Core Management Principles:**
- SSTIs range from mild superficial infections to deep, rapidly spreading, potentially fatal infections.
- Gram-positive cocci (Staphylococcus aureus and Streptococcus pyogenes) are the most common organisms implicated.
- Necrotizing fasciitis often has a polymicrobial etiology (gram-positive cocci, gram-negative bacilli, and anaerobes).
- Management is stratified by IDSA classification (Class 1 to Class 2B) based on infection severity and systemic involvement.
- Necrotizing fasciitis etiology is polymicrobial involving gram-positive cocci, gram-negative bacilli, and anaerobes.

**Clinical Prerequisites:**
- Antibiotic choice should cover common pathogens (e.g., Staphylococcus aureus and Streptococcus pyogenes).
- Surgical drainage is required for purulent SSTIs (e.g., abscesses, furuncles, carbuncles).
- Systemic evaluation for SIRS/sepsis is mandatory in Class 2B infections.
- Management of Class 2A and 2B infections may require parenteral antibiotics.
- Systemically unwell patients (Class 2B) may need short course of admission.
