# CVD risk stratification in T1DM
*Source: ICMR_Guidelines_for_Management_of_Type_1_Diabetes.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** CVD risk stratification in T1DM

**Core Management Principles:**
- CVD prevalence in T1DM increases with age (6% in 15-29 years to 25% in 45-59 years) and diabetes duration (6-9% in 1-7 years to 13-15% in >15 years).
- T1DM attenuates the protective effect of female sex on CVD risk, with higher relative risk in females compared to males.
- Patients with T1DM experience CV events 10-15 years earlier than the general population.
- PAD is underreported in T1DM due to asymptomatic course, neuropathy masking symptoms, and lack of universal screening.
- Presence of PAD in T1DM is a strong predictor of atherosclerotic burden and lower-extremity amputations.
- In the Eurodiab IDDM Complications Study, CVD prevalence in T1DM was slightly higher in females (10%) than males (9%) despite the general population's protective effect of female sex.

**Clinical Prerequisites:**
- Screening for silent myocardial ischemia and inducible ischemia should be actively considered in T1DM patients.
- Regular assessment of CVD risk factors (e.g., age, diabetes duration, sex) is mandatory for risk stratification.
- Monitoring for PAD should include clinical examination and targeted investigations despite asymptomatic presentation.
- Longitudinal follow-up of T1DM patients for CV events and mortality is essential due to elevated risk.
- Diabetic kidney disease progression should be evaluated as a predictor of worse post-stroke outcomes.
- Incorporate standardized mortality ratio (SMR) assessment for total and CV mortality in T1DM risk stratification, with higher priority in females (SMR 13.0 vs. 5.0 for total mortality; 24.7 vs. 8.8 for CV mortality).
