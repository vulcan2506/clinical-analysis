# hyperglycemia-induced renal pathophysiology
*Source: ICMR_Guidelines_for_Management_of_Type_1_Diabetes.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** hyperglycemia-induced renal pathophysiology in T1DM

**Core Management Principles:**
- Hyperglycemia drives renal damage via multiple pathways including AGE accumulation, PKC activation, and TGF-β overexpression.
- Glomerular basement membrane (GBM) thickening and extracellular matrix (ECM) expansion are key structural changes.
- Inflammatory mediators (e.g., MCP, IL6, VEGF) and immune activation (e.g., MBL) contribute to progressive renal insufficiency.
- Hyperglycemia induces renal hypoxia via increased oxygen consumption in the proximal tubule, contributing to tubular injury and fibrosis.
- Activation of the renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system (RAAS) is a key downstream effect of hyperglycemia, exacerbating glomerular hypertension and proteinuria.
- Podocyte injury and loss are critical early events in diabetic kidney disease, leading to glomerular filtration barrier dysfunction.

**Clinical Prerequisites:**
- Early detection of microalbuminuria to initiate renoprotective interventions.
- Strict glycemic control to mitigate downstream hyperglycemia-induced pathways.
- Monitoring of serum creatinine and estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) at least annually in all T1DM patients to assess renal function progression.
- Blood pressure control (target <130/80 mmHg) as a mandatory requirement to reduce renal hyperfiltration and glomerular damage.
