# hypotension and acidosis in adrenal insufficiency
*Source: Sodium and Cloride .pdf*

**Electrolyte Parameter:** hypotension and acidosis in adrenal insufficiency

**Pathophysiology and Clinical Associations:**
- Loss of adrenal cortex function leads to cortisol and mineralocorticoid (aldosterone) deficiency.
- Increased ACTH production in primary Addison's disease exacerbates hypotension and acidosis.
- Hypotension and acidosis are associated with hyponatraemia and hyperkalaemia due to aldosterone deficiency.
- Digoxin toxicity inhibits renal tubular Na/K-ATPase, reducing potassium transport into intracellular space.
- NSAIDs interfere with prostaglandin metabolism, acting as vasodilators and disrupting renin-aldosterone synthesis.
- NSAIDs exacerbate hypotension by causing vasodilation and reducing effective circulating volume, further stimulating renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system (RAAS) in an attempt to compensate

**Clinical Prerequisites:**
- Assess hydration status and renal function to interpret electrolyte imbalances accurately.
- Evaluate concurrent medications (e.g., digoxin, NSAIDs, K-sparing diuretics) for their impact on electrolyte homeostasis.
- Monitor blood pressure and acid-base status to confirm adrenal insufficiency-related hypotension and acidosis.
- Confirm primary Addison's disease via ACTH stimulation testing or baseline ACTH levels.
- Rule out other causes of hyponatraemia and hyperkalaemia (e.g., renal disease, cardiac insufficiency).
- Assess for concurrent gastrointestinal losses (e.g., vomiting, diarrhea) that may worsen hyponatremia and metabolic acidosis in adrenal insufficiency
