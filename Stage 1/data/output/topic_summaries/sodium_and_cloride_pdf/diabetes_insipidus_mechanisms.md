# diabetes insipidus mechanisms
*Source: Sodium and Cloride .pdf*

**Electrolyte Parameter:** diabetes insipidus mechanisms and electrolyte imbalances

**Pathophysiology and Clinical Associations:**
- DI results from decreased central AVP secretion (neurogenic DI) or end-organ resistance (nephrogenic DI)
- Polyuria (PU) with secondary polydipsia (PD) is a hallmark clinical presentation
- Neurogenic DI involves necrosis of AVP-secreting neurons in the hypothalamus
- Nephrogenic DI is often caused by drugs, tubulointerstitial diseases, or obstructive nephropathies
- Urine sodium osmolality is low but higher than serum osmolality due to water loss exceeding sodium loss

**Clinical Prerequisites:**
- Assessment of hydration status to differentiate DI from primary polydipsia
- Evaluation of renal function to rule out concurrent kidney disease
- Measurement of serum and urine osmolality to confirm dilute urine despite hypernatremia
- Consideration of drug history for nephrogenic DI triggers (e.g., lithium, aminoglycosides)
- Exclusion of primary hyperaldosteronism as a confounding cause of sodium retention
