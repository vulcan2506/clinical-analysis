# metabolic alkalosis mechanisms
*Source: Sodium and Cloride .pdf*

**Electrolyte Parameter:** metabolic alkalosis mechanisms and diagnostic interpretation

**Pathophysiology and Clinical Associations:**
- Increase in HCO3 concentration due to conditions preventing renal excretion or loss of gastric juice (H and Cl ions)
- ECFV decreased in gastrointestinal diseases such as vomiting
- ECFV increased in Cushing's disease and hyperaldosteronism
- Diuretics cause reduction in Na reabsorption leading to hypochloraemia
- Hyperaldosteronism and Cushing's disease present with low Cl that cannot be corrected by common salt
- Hypochloremia in metabolic alkalosis may also result from chronic respiratory acidosis compensation (e.g., in chronic obstructive pulmonary disease) due to renal bicarbonate retention

**Clinical Prerequisites:**
- Assessment of gastrointestinal disease status (e.g., vomiting history)
- Evaluation of adrenal function (Cushing's disease, hyperaldosteronism)
- Measurement of ECFV status (decreased vs increased)
- Review of diuretic usage and renal sodium reabsorption
- Correlation of chloride levels with extent of hypokalaemia
- Assessment of respiratory function and acid-base status to rule out compensatory respiratory acidosis contributing to metabolic alkalosis
