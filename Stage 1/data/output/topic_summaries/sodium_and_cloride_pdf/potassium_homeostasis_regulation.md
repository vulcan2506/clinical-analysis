# potassium homeostasis regulation
*Source: Sodium and Cloride .pdf*

**Electrolyte Parameter:** potassium homeostasis regulation

**Pathophysiology and Clinical Associations:**
- Potassium is the most important intracellular cation (>98% intracellular).
- Serum potassium concentration is tightly regulated for membrane potential stability.
- Disorders of potassium balance disrupt skeletal, cardiac, and nerve cell function.
- Potassium homeostasis depends on oral intake, cellular distribution (Na/K-ATPase), and renal elimination.
- 90% of potassium is excreted via kidneys; only a small fraction via intestines.

**Clinical Prerequisites:**
- Assess hydration status and renal function for accurate potassium interpretation.
- Evaluate transmembrane electrochemical gradient via plasma potassium concentration.
- Consider concurrent medications (e.g., diuretics, ACE inhibitors) affecting potassium balance.
- Monitor for clinical signs (e.g., muscle weakness, cardiac arrhythmias) in hypo/hyperkalaemia.
- Check for endocrine disorders (e.g., hyperaldosteronism, ACTH-producing tumors) in persistent hypokalaemia.
