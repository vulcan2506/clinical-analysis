# culturally and linguistically appropriate diabetes education
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Culturally and linguistically appropriate diabetes education

**Core Recommendations:**
- Health systems and professionals must develop or offer educational programs and materials in culturally appropriate languages for patients with limited English proficiency.
- Professional language assistance (e.g., trained interpreters) must be provided to individuals with limited English proficiency or other communication needs at no cost.
- Use of untrained interpreters, including family members, should be avoided to prevent miscommunication or inaccurate information conveyance.
- Written materials should be in the patient's language and at a sixth-grade reading level or lower for accessibility.
- Healthcare professionals should improve cultural competency and address health literacy per National CLAS Standards.
- Health systems should integrate professional language assistance services as a standard of care for all patient interactions involving individuals with limited English proficiency or other communication needs.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Patients with limited English proficiency or other communication needs must be identified.
- Access to professional language assistance must be ensured at no cost to the patient.
- Culturally appropriate educational materials must be available in the patient's preferred language.
- Health literacy levels of patients must be assessed to tailor written materials appropriately.
- Healthcare professionals must adhere to National CLAS Standards for culturally and linguistically appropriate care.
- Healthcare professionals must assess patients' preferred language and communication needs at the point of care before delivering diabetes education.

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Use of untrained interpreters, including family members, for conveying medical information.
- Relying solely on English-language educational materials for non-English-speaking patients.
- Assuming all patients have the same health literacy level without assessment.
- Using complex medical jargon in written materials without simplification.
- Ignoring cultural differences in diabetes education and care delivery.
- Relying on ad-hoc or informal translation methods (e.g., untrained staff, community volunteers) for conveying critical diabetes education materials.

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Integration of professional language assistance services as a standard of care for limited English proficiency patients.
- Development of culturally tailored diabetes education materials in multiple languages.
- Adoption of National CLAS Standards to guide culturally and linguistically appropriate care.
- Use of medical translation tools to address language barriers, with ongoing evaluation of their efficacy.
- Emphasis on health literacy assessment to ensure patient understanding of diabetes education.
- Health systems should evaluate and validate the efficacy of medical translation tools in diabetes care contexts before widespread adoption.
