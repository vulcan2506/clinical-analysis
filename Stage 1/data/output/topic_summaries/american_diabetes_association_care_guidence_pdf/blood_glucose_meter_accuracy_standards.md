# blood glucose meter accuracy standards
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Blood glucose meter accuracy standards and usage

**Core Recommendations:**
- Glucose meters must meet FDA or ISO 15197:2013 accuracy standards, but postmarketing accuracy is monitored by manufacturers, not independent sources.
- People with diabetes should use only unopened and unexpired test strips to ensure accuracy.
- Optimal BGM use requires proper data review and interpretation by both the person with diabetes and healthcare professionals.
- Frequent BGM use (6–10 times daily for intensive insulin therapy) is associated with lower A1C levels and fewer acute complications.
- BGM data should be used to adjust food intake, physical activity, or pharmacologic therapy to achieve treatment goals.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Glucose meters must meet FDA or ISO 15197:2013 accuracy standards for reliable diabetes management.
- BGM is particularly important for people with diabetes treated with insulin therapy to detect and prevent hypoglycemia and hyperglycemia.
- For basal insulin users, assessing fasting glucose with BGM informs dose adjustments to achieve blood glucose goals.
- BGM should be reevaluated at each routine visit to ensure its effective use.
- CGM with BGM backup is preferred for individuals on intensive insulin therapies.
- For individuals using basal insulin, assessing fasting glucose with BGM is required to inform dose adjustments and achieve blood glucose goals.
- BGM monitoring frequency should be reevaluated at each routine clinical visit to ensure continued effective use.

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Assuming glucose meters are accurate solely because they are FDA cleared without verifying postmarketing performance.
- Using preowned or secondhand test strips, which may give incorrect results.
- Relying solely on BGM without integrating data into clinical and self-management treatment plans.

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Meters with integrated speech for visually impaired individuals to read aloud glucose levels.
- Meters with features like reapplying blood to a strip with an insufficient initial sample to reduce user burden.
- Real-time advice from meters when monitoring glucose levels to guide user actions.
- Integration of BGM with health platforms for comprehensive glucose management.
- Evidence supporting CGM use in people with type 2 diabetes for glycemic management and cost-effectiveness.
- Meters with integrated speech features to read aloud glucose levels for visually impaired individuals.
- Meters with the ability to reapply blood to a strip with an insufficient initial sample to reduce user burden.
