# insulin therapy in pregnancy
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** insulin therapy in pregnancy

**Core Recommendations:**
- Insulin is recommended for type 1 diabetes in pregnancy and preferred for type 2 diabetes and GDM management.
- Frequent titration of insulin is required due to changing physiological demands during pregnancy.
- Daily and frequent glucose monitoring is emphasized to adjust insulin doses.
- Insulin requirements typically increase by ~5% per week from 16 weeks to 36 weeks, often doubling prepregnancy doses.
- Bolus insulin requirements increase proportionally more than basal insulin as pregnancy progresses.
- Insulin requirements may decrease in early pregnancy for individuals with type 1 diabetes, increasing hypoglycemia risk.
- Insulin requirement levels off toward the end of the third trimester.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Type 1 diabetes in pregnancy requires insulin therapy.
- Type 2 diabetes or GDM management may require insulin if glycemic goals are not met with lifestyle modifications.
- Interprofessional team management with relevant expertise is recommended for insulin therapy.
- Concentrated insulin formulations may be necessary for achieving glycemic goals in type 2 diabetes.
- Assessment for automated insulin delivery (AID) systems should include glycemic levels, hypoglycemia risk, technology comfort, and psychosocial factors.
- Insulin management should be performed with an interprofessional team with relevant expertise.
- Basal insulin options include insulin glargine, NPH insulin, and insulin degludec; insulin detemir is no longer available.

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Insulin detemir (removed from market by manufacturer).
- Glyburide and metformin as first-line treatments for GDM due to placental crossing and safety concerns.
- Use of insulin preparations without demonstrated safety in RCTs (prefer those with RCT evidence).
- Fixed AID systems without pregnancy-specific glucose targets or algorithms (unless used with assistive techniques and expert guidance).
- Insulin detemir (removed from market by manufacturer and no longer available).

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Insulin glargine (long-acting) and insulin degludec (ultralong-acting) are acceptable basal insulin options in pregnancy.
- Insulin aspart and insulin lispro (rapid-acting) are widely considered safe and effective for use during pregnancy.
- Faster-acting insulin aspart (faster aspart) may improve time-in-range and reduce hypoglycemia in pregnancy.
- Automated insulin delivery (AID) systems with pregnancy-specific glucose targets (e.g., Ai-DAPT trial) show improved TIR and lower A1C.
- AID systems without pregnancy-specific targets may be used in selected individuals with expert guidance and assistive techniques.
- Insulin degludec was shown in an RCT to be noninferior to detemir, though its prolonged duration of action (at least 42 hours) may present challenges in pregnancy due to slower dose titration requirements (3–4 days between changes).
- Faster-acting insulin aspart (faster aspart) may improve time-in-range and reduce hypoglycemia in pregnancy, with secondary analysis showing higher TIR and less severe hypoglycemia compared to insulin aspart in type 1 diabetes.
