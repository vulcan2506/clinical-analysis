# diabetes population health systems
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Systems to Support Diabetes Population Health

**Core Recommendations:**
- Tailoring treatment strategies to address social determinants of health (e.g., socioeconomic status, access to care, food security)
- Implementing structured care coordination across multidisciplinary teams (e.g., endocrinologists, primary care, community health workers)
- Using data-driven approaches to identify high-risk patient cohorts for targeted interventions
- Integrating patient-reported outcomes and social risk screening into routine diabetes care
- Promoting shared decision-making with patients to align treatment plans with their social and cultural contexts
- Engaging family members or caregivers in diabetes care planning to address social and cultural contexts
- Implementing culturally tailored health literacy interventions to improve patient understanding of diabetes management

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Availability of electronic health records (EHRs) with population health management capabilities
- Access to community resources (e.g., nutrition programs, transportation assistance) to address social needs
- Established partnerships with local organizations (e.g., food banks, housing services) for referrals
- Trained staff (e.g., community health workers, social workers) to conduct social risk assessments
- Interoperable data systems to track patient outcomes and care gaps across settings
- Integration of diabetes care plans with local public health initiatives (e.g., diabetes prevention programs, vaccination campaigns)
- Availability of interpreter services or multilingual staff to support diverse patient populations

**Previous Recommendations:**
- One-size-fits-all diabetes management protocols without consideration of social context
- Reliance solely on clinical metrics (e.g., A1C) without addressing social barriers to care
- Fragmented care delivery models that do not coordinate between primary and specialty care
- Screening for social needs without follow-up interventions or resource linkages
- Passive patient education without active engagement in shared decision-making
- Routine hospitalization for glycemic control without addressing outpatient social determinants of health

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Standardized social risk screening tools (e.g., PRAPARE, THRIVE) integrated into EHR workflows
- Community-based diabetes self-management programs tailored to cultural and linguistic preferences
- Automated risk stratification algorithms to prioritize patients for intensive case management
- Use of telehealth and remote monitoring to bridge gaps in access to care for underserved populations
- Policy-level interventions (e.g., housing support, food assistance) as part of diabetes care plans
- Use of artificial intelligence-driven predictive analytics to forecast diabetes complications and resource needs
- Implementation of community health worker-led diabetes education programs in non-clinical settings (e.g., places of worship, community centers)
