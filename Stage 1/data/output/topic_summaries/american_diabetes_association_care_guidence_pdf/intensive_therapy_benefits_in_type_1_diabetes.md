# intensive therapy benefits in type 1 diabetes
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Intensive therapy benefits in type 1 diabetes

**Core Recommendations:**
- Early initiation of intensive insulin therapy (e.g., multiple daily injections or insulin pump) to achieve near-normal glycemia and reduce long-term microvascular and macrovascular complications
- Use of continuous glucose monitoring (CGM) with automated insulin delivery (AID) systems or hybrid closed-loop technology to optimize glycemic control and reduce hypoglycemia
- Implementation of structured diabetes education programs (e.g., DAFNE) to enhance self-management skills, carbohydrate counting, and insulin dose adjustments
- Adoption of rapid-acting insulin analogs (e.g., lispro, aspart) and basal insulin analogs (e.g., glargine, detemir, degludec) to improve postprandial glucose control and reduce hypoglycemia risk
- Regular monitoring of glycemic outcomes (e.g., A1C, time-in-range) and adjustment of therapy based on CGM data and patient-specific factors
- Use of inhaled technosphere insulin (e.g., Afrezza) as an alternative to rapid-acting injectable insulin for postprandial glucose control in type 1 diabetes, particularly in patients with needle phobia or adherence challenges
- Implementation of predictive algorithms for insulin dose adjustments based on CGM trend arrows and patient-specific factors (e.g., carbohydrate counting accuracy, physical activity levels) to optimize glycemic outcomes

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Diagnosis of type 1 diabetes confirmed by clinical criteria (e.g., autoantibodies, C-peptide levels)
- Patient willingness and ability to engage in intensive self-management, including frequent glucose monitoring and insulin dose adjustments
- Access to healthcare resources for ongoing education, technology (e.g., CGM, insulin pumps), and clinical support
- Absence of contraindications to intensive insulin therapy (e.g., severe hypoglycemia unawareness, active malignancy)
- Baseline evaluation of glycemic control (e.g., A1C), hypoglycemia risk, and comorbidities to guide therapy selection
- Patient willingness to use and maintain continuous glucose monitoring (CGM) technology, including sensor wear time and calibration requirements
- Access to healthcare resources for ongoing education, technology training, and clinical support for intensive therapy implementation

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Routine use of neutral protamine Hagedorn (NPH) insulin as basal insulin due to higher hypoglycemia risk and less predictable pharmacokinetics
- Fixed insulin dosing without adjustment for carbohydrate intake or physical activity
- Reliance solely on urine glucose testing or infrequent self-monitoring of blood glucose (SMBG) without CGM integration
- Delayed initiation of intensive therapy in favor of gradual escalation in patients with suboptimal glycemic control
- Use of older, less accurate glucose monitoring methods (e.g., urine strips) without real-time CGM
- Fixed insulin dosing without adjustment for carbohydrate intake, physical activity, or glycemic trends

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Integration of hybrid closed-loop systems (e.g., Control-IQ, MiniMed 780G) for automated insulin delivery to improve time-in-range and reduce hypoglycemia
- Early initiation of CGM within the first year of type 1 diabetes diagnosis to optimize glycemic outcomes and reduce complications
- Use of ultra-rapid insulin analogs (e.g., faster aspart, ultra-rapid lispro) to improve postprandial glucose control and reduce glycemic excursions
- Adoption of predictive low-glucose suspend (PLGS) and automated insulin delivery (AID) systems to mitigate hypoglycemia risk
- Implementation of structured education programs (e.g., DAFNE) to enhance patient empowerment and self-management in intensive therapy
