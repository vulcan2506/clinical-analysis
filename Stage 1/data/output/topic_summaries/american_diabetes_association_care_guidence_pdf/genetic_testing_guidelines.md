# genetic testing guidelines
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Genetic Testing for Monogenic Diabetes

**Core Recommendations:**
- All people diagnosed with diabetes in the first 6 months of life should have genetic testing for neonatal diabetes, regardless of current age.
- Children and young adults without typical type 1 or type 2 diabetes characteristics and with a family history suggestive of autosomal-dominant inheritance should have genetic testing for MODY.
- Consultation with a diabetes genetics specialist is recommended to interpret genetic mutations and guide further evaluation, treatment, and counseling.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Diagnosis of diabetes in the first 6 months of life for neonatal diabetes testing.
- Absence of typical type 1 or type 2 diabetes characteristics and family history of diabetes in successive generations for MODY testing.

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Genetic testing for neonatal diabetes in all infants diagnosed within the first 6 months of life.
- Genetic testing for MODY in children and young adults with atypical diabetes and autosomal-dominant family history.
- Specialized genetic counseling and consultation for interpretation of monogenic diabetes mutations.
- Genetic testing should be considered for monogenic lipodystrophies in patients with insulin resistance syndromes, as these conditions may present with dyslipidemia and cardiovascular risk stratification challenges.
