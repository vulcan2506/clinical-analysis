# high-intensity interval training protocol
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** High-intensity interval training (HIIT) protocol for diabetes care

**Core Recommendations:**
- Perform short bursts of aerobic training at 65-90% VO₂peak or 75-95% heart rate peak for 10s to 4 minutes with 12s to 5 minutes of active or passive recovery
- Monitor blood glucose during and after HIIT due to risk of transient hyperglycemia (type 1) or delayed hypoglycemia (type 2)
- Use bolus correction in type 1 diabetes to mitigate postexercise hyperglycemia
- Incorporate HIIT within a broader activity plan with adequate recovery to prevent injury
- Individualize HIIT participation based on patient willingness and ability to engage in high-intensity activity

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Individuals with type 1 or type 2 diabetes without contraindications to high-intensity exercise
- Baseline assessment of VO₂peak or heart rate peak to determine appropriate intensity zones
- Glucose monitoring capability during and after exercise
- Supervision or structured program for initial HIIT sessions to ensure safety and proper technique
- Gradual progression of HIIT intensity and duration based on patient tolerance and response

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Continuous moderate-intensity exercise as the sole recommended modality for glycemic control in diabetes
- Unsupervised high-intensity exercise without glucose monitoring in type 1 diabetes
- Fixed HIIT protocols without consideration for individual fitness levels or recovery needs

**Updated Recommendations:**
- HIIT as a time-efficient modality for significant physiologic and metabolic adaptations in diabetes
- Superior improvements in glycemia and body composition with HIIT-style walking compared to continuous walking in type 2 diabetes
- Need for careful glucose monitoring and potential bolus correction in type 1 diabetes during HIIT
- Integration of HIIT within broader activity plans with attention to recovery to prevent injury
- Evidence of reduced A1C and insulin requirements with weeks of HIIT in type 1 diabetes
- HIIT may improve β-cell function and cardiac function in addition to glycemia and body composition
- HIIT cycling may not improve cardiovascular response or visceral adiposity compared to continuous cycling despite similar A1C improvements
