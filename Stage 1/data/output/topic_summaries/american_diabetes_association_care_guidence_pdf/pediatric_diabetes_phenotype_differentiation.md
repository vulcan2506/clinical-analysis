# pediatric diabetes phenotype differentiation
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Pediatric diabetes phenotype differentiation and type 2 diabetes management

**Core Recommendations:**
- Distinguish between type 1 and type 2 diabetes in children despite overlapping features such as obesity, autoantibodies, and ketosis.
- Treat both insulin resistance and absolute insulinopenia phenotypes appropriately, acknowledging potential overlap.
- Provide comprehensive lifestyle programs integrated with diabetes management for children and adolescents with overweight/obesity and type 2 diabetes.
- Assess glycemic status at least every 3 months or as clinically indicated in type 2 diabetes.
- Set individualized A1C goals (<6.5% for low hypoglycemia risk; higher for those at risk) and monitor blood glucose or CGM as needed.
- Initiate subcutaneous or intravenous insulin treatment in individuals with ketoacidosis to rapidly correct hyperglycemia and metabolic derangement.
- Consider assessment and treatment for hyperglycemic hyperosmolar state in individuals presenting with severe hyperglycemia (blood glucose ≥600 mg/dL).

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Overweight or obesity with type 2 diabetes diagnosis for lifestyle intervention eligibility.
- A1C <8.5% and asymptomatic status for metformin monotherapy initiation.
- Marked hyperglycemia (A1C ≥8.5% or blood glucose ≥600 mg/dL) requires initial insulin therapy.
- Presence of ketoacidosis necessitates rapid insulin treatment until acidosis resolves.
- Class 2 obesity or higher (BMI 35 to <40 kg/m² or 120% to <140% percentile) with comorbidities for metabolic surgery consideration.
- Resolution of ketosis or ketoacidosis before initiating metformin therapy.
- Random blood glucose concentrations ≥250 mg/dL and/or A1C ≥8.5% as criteria for initial insulin treatment when distinction between types is unclear.

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Fixed numeric A1C targets without individualized risk assessment.
- Reliance solely on metformin for durable glycemic control in all pediatric type 2 diabetes cases.
- Use of linagliptin or saxagliptin as effective A1C-lowering agents in pediatric type 2 diabetes.
- Exclusive use of insulin for long-term management without considering noninsulin therapies.
- Assumption that type 1 and type 2 diabetes phenotypes are mutually exclusive in pediatric cases.
- Reliance solely on metformin for durable glycemic control in all pediatric type 2 diabetes cases (evidenced by TODAY study showing ~50% efficacy).
- Use of linagliptin as an effective A1C-lowering agent in pediatric type 2 diabetes (demonstrated no benefit versus placebo in trials).

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Early consideration of multiple add-on therapies (metformin, GLP-1 RA, SGLT2i) at type 2 diabetes diagnosis.
- Use of GLP-1 receptor agonists (e.g., liraglutide, semaglutide) and SGLT2 inhibitors (empagliflozin, dapagliflozin, canagliflozin) as approved pharmacologic options.
- Recommendation for metabolic surgery in adolescents with class 2 obesity or higher and uncontrolled type 2 diabetes despite lifestyle and pharmacologic therapy.
- Integration of family-centered, culturally appropriate lifestyle interventions for physical activity and nutrition.
- Consideration of CGM in pediatric type 2 diabetes for frequent monitoring needs or insulin use.
