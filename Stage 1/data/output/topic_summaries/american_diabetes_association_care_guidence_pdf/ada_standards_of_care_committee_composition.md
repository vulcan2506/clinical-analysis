# ADA Standards of Care committee composition
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** ADA Standards of Care committee composition and governance

**Core Recommendations:**
- The PPC is an interprofessional expert committee comprising physicians, nurse practitioners, pharmacists, diabetes care and education specialists, registered dietitian nutritionists, behavioral health scientists, and others with diverse expertise.
- Appointment to the PPC is based on excellence in clinical practice and research, with attention to demographic, geographic, work setting, or identity representation.
- A PPC chair or co-chairs oversee the committee, and designated subject matter experts support content development but do not vote on recommendations.
- All members comply with ADA duality of interest policy, disclosing financial, intellectual, or other conflicts annually and before meetings.
- Members with conflicts pertinent to specific recommendations are prohibited from voting on those recommendations.
- The ADA adheres to section 7 of the Council of Medical Specialty Societies 'Code for Interactions with Companies' regarding committee interactions.
- Full disclosure statements from all committee members are solicited and reviewed during the appointment process.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Committee members must have expertise in areas such as endocrinology, epidemiology, public health, cardiovascular risk management, nephrology, ophthalmology, podiatry, and diabetes technology.
- Members must disclose conflicts for a time frame including 1 year prior to appointment until publication of the Standards of Care.
- Potential dualities of interest are evaluated by a panel of experts and the ADA Legal Affairs Division based on financial amount and relevance.
- The majority of the PPC and chairs must remain unconflicted for the subject area.
- Chairs must remain unconflicted for 1 year after publication of the Standards of Care.

**Previous Recommendations:**
- No PPC members or subject matter experts were employees of pharmaceutical or medical device companies during the development of the 2026 Standards of Care.

**Updated Recommendations:**
- New to the 2026 Standards of Care, the National Kidney Foundation (NKF) reviewed and approved section 11, 'Chronic Kidney Disease and Risk Management,' and the International Society for Pediatric and Adolescent Diabetes (ISPAD) reviewed and approved section 14, 'Children and Adolescents.'
- A guideline methodologist with expertise in evidence-based medicine and guideline development methodology oversees methodological aspects and serves as a statistical analyst.
- An information specialist (librarian) is consulted as necessary for systematic literature reviews to identify the latest scientific evidence.
- The American Society for Bone and Mineral Research (ASBMR) reviewed and approved the 'Bone Health' subsection in section 4, 'Comprehensive Medical Evaluation and Assessment of Comorbidities.'
- The Obesity Society (TOS) reviewed and approved section 8, 'Obesity and Weight Management for the Prevention and Treatment of Diabetes.'
- The American College of Cardiology (ACC) reviewed and approved section 10, 'Cardiovascular Disease and Risk Management.'
- The American Geriatrics Society (AGS) reviewed and approved section 13, 'Older Adults.'
