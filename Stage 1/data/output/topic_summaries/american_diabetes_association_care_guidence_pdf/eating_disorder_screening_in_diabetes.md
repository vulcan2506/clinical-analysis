# eating disorder screening in diabetes
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Eating disorder screening in diabetes

**Core Recommendations:**
- Screen for disordered or disrupted eating using validated screening measures (e.g., DEPS-R for type 1 diabetes, mSCOFF, QEWP-5, Binge Eating Scale, Night Eating Questionnaire for type 2 diabetes).
- Reevaluate treatment plans for individuals with symptoms of disordered eating, in consultation with qualified professionals trained in diabetes and eating behaviors.
- Monitor for insulin omission, binge eating, night eating syndrome, and emotional distress in people with diabetes.
- Address eating disorders in the context of diabetes treatment, avoiding stigmatizing language and rigid carbohydrate counting.
- Consider technology use cautiously, with more research needed on risks and benefits.
- Review the medical treatment plan to identify potential treatment-related effects on hunger/caloric intake as part of disordered eating screening.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Individuals with type 1 diabetes (higher risk for eating disorders and insulin restriction).
- Individuals with type 2 diabetes (higher risk for binge eating, night eating syndrome, and intentional insulin omission if treated with insulin).
- Presence of diabetes distress, depression, anxiety, or fear of hypoglycemia.
- Elevated A1C or inconsistent glycemic control.
- Household food insecurity (associated with increased disordered eating behaviors in youth).
- Presence of body image concerns and emotional distress in individuals with type 1 diabetes, which are associated with disordered eating behavior.

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Rigid focus on A1C targets without considering underlying eating behaviors or psychological factors.
- Intensive weight monitoring that may reinforce maladaptive patterns.
- Labeling individuals with diabetes as having a diagnosable eating disorder without comprehensive evaluation.
- Assuming disordered eating is solely psychological without considering physiologic disruptions in hunger/satiety cues.
- Relying solely on non-diabetes-specific screening tools without diabetes context.

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Use of diabetes-specific screening tools (e.g., DEPS-R, mSCOFF) to assess for intentional insulin omission and disordered eating.
- Inclusion of GLP-1 receptor agonists (e.g., incretin therapies) in treatment plans for disrupted eating behaviors, given evidence of reduced binge eating and emotional eating.
- Interprofessional care teams including professionals trained in diabetes and disordered eating behaviors.
- CBT and diabetes education programs for individuals with type 1 diabetes and disordered eating showing improvements in symptoms.
- Pilot self-guided online programs for type 2 diabetes and binge eating disorder showing promise in symptom reduction.
- Consider the use of incretin therapies (GLP-1 RAs and dual GIP/GLP-1 RAs) for treating disrupted or disordered eating behaviors, given evidence of reduced binge eating and emotional eating.
