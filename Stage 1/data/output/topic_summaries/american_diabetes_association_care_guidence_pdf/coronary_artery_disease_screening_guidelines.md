# coronary artery disease screening guidelines
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** CAD Screening & Heart Failure Risk Management

**Core Recommendations:**
- Routine screening for coronary artery disease in asymptomatic individuals is not recommended as it does not improve outcomes.
- Consider investigations for CAD in the presence of signs/symptoms of cardiac disease, carotid bruits, TIA, stroke, claudication, PAD, or ECG abnormalities.
- Screen asymptomatic adults with diabetes for stage B heart failure by measuring BNP or NT-proBNP to facilitate prevention of stage C heart failure.
- In asymptomatic individuals with diabetes and abnormal natriuretic peptide levels, echocardiography is recommended to identify stage B heart failure.
- In asymptomatic individuals with diabetes aged ≥65 years, microvascular disease, foot complications, or end-organ damage, screening for PAD with ankle-brachial index testing is recommended if a PAD diagnosis would change management.
- Perform a careful history prior to exercise engagement to assess cardiovascular risk factors and atypical CAD presentation (e.g., unexplained decrease in exercise tolerance).
- Assess for conditions that may contraindicate certain types of exercise or predispose to injury (e.g., inadequately managed hypertension, untreated proliferative retinopathy, autonomic neuropathy, orthostatic hypotension, peripheral neuropathy, balance impairment, history of foot ulcers, or Charcot foot) before recommending physical activity.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Patient must be asymptomatic for routine CAD screening to be explicitly not recommended.
- Presence of specific signs/symptoms (e.g., claudication, PAD, ECG abnormalities) required to consider CAD investigations.
- Patient must be an adult with diabetes for BNP/NT-proBNP screening consideration.
- Abnormal natriuretic peptide levels (BNP ≥50 pg/mL or NT-proBNP ≥125 pg/mL) required before proceeding to echocardiography.
- PAD diagnosis must be expected to change management before ankle-brachial index testing is recommended.
- Assessment of baseline physical activity and sedentary behavior is required to guide initial increases in physical activity levels.
- Consideration of motivators (e.g., health benefits, enjoyable activities) and barriers (e.g., lack of time, fear of hypoglycemia) to exercise participation when devising physical activity plans.

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Routine screening with adenosine-stress radionuclide myocardial perfusion imaging in asymptomatic people with type 2 diabetes and normal ECGs.
- Routine screening with coronary computed tomography angiography in asymptomatic people with type 1 or type 2 diabetes.
- Routine use of computed tomography calcium scoring for risk stratification in asymptomatic people with diabetes due to radiation exposure and potential for unnecessary invasive testing.
- Routine screening with adenosine-stress radionuclide myocardial perfusion imaging in asymptomatic people with type 2 diabetes and normal ECGs (deprecated due to lack of clinical benefit).
- Routine screening with coronary computed tomography angiography in asymptomatic people with type 1 or type 2 diabetes (deprecated due to lack of reduction in major cardiovascular events).

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Measurement of B-type natriuretic peptide (BNP) or N-terminal pro-BNP (NT-proBNP) to screen for asymptomatic cardiac structural or functional abnormalities (stage B heart failure).
- Recommendation for echocardiography as the next step in individuals with diabetes and abnormal natriuretic peptide levels to identify stage B heart failure.
- Recommendation for ankle-brachial index testing for PAD screening in asymptomatic individuals with diabetes aged ≥65 years or those with microvascular disease/foot complications if diagnosis would change management.
- Consideration of coronary calcium scoring for risk stratification and informed decision-making in asymptomatic individuals, though routine use remains controversial.
- Implementation of an interprofessional approach including a cardiovascular disease specialist for individuals with diabetes and asymptomatic stage B heart failure.
