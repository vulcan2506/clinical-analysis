# standardized CGM data utilization
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** standardized CGM data utilization

**Core Recommendations:**
- Use of standardized CGM tracing is recommended for people with diabetes and clinicians
- Data should be accessible and analyzable by both people with diabetes and healthcare teams
- Data analysis should occur between and at clinic visits
- Data should inform self-management and medication dose titration
- Applicable across settings including connected insulin pens, CGM devices, and AID systems
- Standardized CGM data should be used to identify and address glycemic variability patterns, including time in range (TIR), time below range (TBR), and time above range (TAR)

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- People with diabetes must have access to CGM devices
- Healthcare teams must have tools to access and interpret CGM data
- Data sharing infrastructure must support between-visit and in-clinic analysis
- Patient consent for data sharing between devices and healthcare systems
- Training for both patients and clinicians on standardized CGM data interpretation
- Healthcare systems must ensure interoperability between CGM devices and electronic health records (EHRs) for seamless data integration

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Reliance solely on intermittent fingerstick glucose monitoring for treatment decisions
- Manual transcription of CGM data without standardized reporting formats
- Use of non-standardized or proprietary CGM data formats in clinical workflows

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Integration of CGM data with connected insulin pens for dose optimization
- Standardized CGM reporting formats for consistent data interpretation
- Use of CGM data in automated insulin delivery (AID) systems
- Remote monitoring capabilities for CGM data between clinic visits
- Patient-facing CGM data dashboards for self-management support
- Use of standardized CGM data to guide dietary and exercise interventions based on glucose trend analysis
