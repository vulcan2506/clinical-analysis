# DSMES integration in Standards of Care
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** DSMES Integration in Standards of Care

**Core Recommendations:**
- Advise all people with diabetes to participate in developmentally and culturally appropriate DSMES to facilitate informed decision-making and self-care behaviors.
- Provide DSMES at diagnosis, annually, when not meeting treatment goals, when complicating factors develop, and during transitions in life and care.
- Use behavioral strategies such as motivational interviewing, goal setting, and problem-solving to support DSMES and engagement in health behaviors.
- Communicate DSMES participation with the diabetes care team and ensure culturally and socially appropriate delivery in group or individual settings.
- Assess and weigh social determinants of health (SDOH) to guide the design and delivery of DSMES, leveraging creative delivery options like telehealth.
- Assess and address barriers to DSMES participation at multiple levels (health system, payor, clinic, healthcare professional, and individual) to improve uptake and engagement.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Initial comprehensive health evaluation and assessment of comorbidities.
- Person-centered collaborative care guided by shared decision-making in treatment plan selection.
- Evaluation of medical and behavioral health outcomes, particularly during periods of changes in health and well-being.
- Consideration of the person's burden of treatment, level of self-efficacy, and degree of social and family support.
- Availability of qualified providers such as Diabetes Care and Education Specialists (DCES) or other team members with specialized clinical knowledge.
- Institutional leadership support is required for successful DSMES implementation, including advocacy and resource allocation.

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Incorporation of digital health technologies (e.g., mobile apps, websites, digital coaching, SMS) as effective modes of DSMES intervention delivery.
- Explicit inclusion of community health workers, peer educators, peer support, and lay leaders in providing ongoing diabetes self-management support.
- Integration of SDOH screening and referrals to social needs support as part of a multilevel diabetes care intervention.
- Emphasis on ongoing, continuous DSMES rather than one-time occurrence, with a recommended duration of >10 hours over 6–12 months.
- Use of positive, strengths-based language to avoid judgmental words that may increase feelings of shame and guilt.
- Documented evidence that DSMES interventions with >10 hours over 6–12 months yield better outcomes, including A1C reduction and improved quality of life.
