# sexual dysfunction screening guideline
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Sexual dysfunction screening in diabetes

**Core Recommendations:**
- Screen men with diabetes/prediabetes for erectile dysfunction (ED), prioritizing those with high cardiovascular risk, retinopathy, CVD, CKD, neuropathy, long diabetes duration, depression, hypogonadism, or suboptimal glycemic control.
- Inquire about sexual health (e.g., low libido, ED) in men with diabetes/prediabetes; screen for hypogonadism with morning serum total testosterone if symptoms/signs are present.
- In women with diabetes/prediabetes, screen for desire, arousal, and orgasm difficulties, particularly in those with depression/anxiety or recurrent UTIs.
- Evaluate cardiovascular and endocrine risk factors in men presenting with ED, including glycemic assessment, lipid profile, and morning testosterone levels.
- Encourage lifestyle interventions (e.g., Mediterranean diet, exercise, smoking cessation) and glycemic optimization to improve sexual function in women with diabetes.
- In men with diabetes/prediabetes, screen for ED in those with hypertension, dyslipidemia, metabolic syndrome, obesity, or use of medications such as antidepressants and opioids.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Men with diabetes/prediabetes and high cardiovascular risk, retinopathy, CVD, CKD, neuropathy, long diabetes duration, depression, hypogonadism, or suboptimal glycemic control must be screened for ED.
- Symptoms/signs of hypogonadism in men with diabetes/prediabetes require morning serum total testosterone screening.
- Women with diabetes/prediabetes and depression/anxiety or recurrent UTIs should be screened for sexual dysfunction.
- Men newly presenting with ED require glycemic assessment, lipid profile, and morning total/free testosterone if indicated.
- Women with diabetes/prediabetes should have psychosocial factors (e.g., depression, anxiety) addressed as part of sexual health screening.
- Men newly presenting with ED require evaluation of physical activity levels as part of cardiovascular and endocrine risk assessment.

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Routine use of high-dose PDE5Is as first-line therapy without optimizing comorbidities or considering daily therapy in non-responders.
- Assuming obesity is the sole cause of low testosterone in men with diabetes without evaluating hypogonadism.
- Ignoring sexual health discussions in women with diabetes due to perceived lack of guidelines or patient reluctance.
- Relying solely on numeric A1C targets to assess sexual dysfunction risk without considering broader cardiovascular and endocrine factors.
- Overlooking the bidirectional relationship between ED and depression in men with diabetes.
- Assuming vascular disease is the sole primary risk factor for ED in men with diabetes without considering the multifactorial interplay of obesity, poor nutrition, and lack of exercise.

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Morning serum total testosterone screening for men with diabetes/prediabetes and hypogonadism symptoms/signs.
- Glycemic assessment, lipid profile, and testosterone evaluation in men newly presenting with ED.
- Screening women with diabetes/prediabetes for desire, arousal, and orgasm difficulties, especially with depression/anxiety or recurrent UTIs.
- Lifestyle interventions (e.g., Mediterranean diet, exercise) and glycemic optimization to improve sexual function in women with diabetes.
- Consideration of FDA-approved treatments (e.g., flibanserin, bremelanotide) for hypoactive sexual desire disorder in women with diabetes.
- Consider testosterone replacement therapy in men with diabetes and symptomatic hypogonadism, with caution due to potential increased coronary artery plaque volume in older men.
