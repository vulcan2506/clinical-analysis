# diabetes complications research
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Diabetes complications research: retinopathy, neuropathy, and foot care

**Core Recommendations:**
- Implement individualized A1C targets based on patient-specific factors such as life expectancy, cognitive function, and hypoglycemia risk.
- Use teleophthalmology and AI-based screening for diabetic retinopathy to improve early detection and reduce ungradable image rates.
- Adopt intravitreal anti-VEGF therapies (e.g., aflibercept, ranibizumab) as first-line treatment for diabetic macular edema and proliferative diabetic retinopathy.
- Screen for diabetic peripheral neuropathy using validated tools like the Ipswich Touch Test and prioritize early intervention with intensive glycemic control.
- Apply multidisciplinary foot care teams and offloading strategies to prevent and manage diabetic foot ulcers, including Charcot neuro-osteoarthropathy.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Documented diagnosis of type 1 or type 2 diabetes with confirmed microvascular or macrovascular complications.
- Baseline retinal imaging and neuropathy assessment prior to initiating intensified therapy.
- Patient consent and adherence to follow-up protocols for screening and treatment.
- Access to specialized care including ophthalmology, endocrinology, podiatry, and vascular surgery.
- Integration of patient-reported outcomes and symptom tracking in routine diabetes care.

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Routine use of fixed A1C numeric targets without individualized adjustment.
- Laser photocoagulation as the sole first-line therapy for proliferative diabetic retinopathy without consideration of anti-VEGF options.
- Reliance on oral hypoglycemic agents alone for neuropathy management without addressing glycemic control and lifestyle factors.
- Use of outdated neuropathy screening tools lacking sensitivity or specificity.
- Empiric opioid therapy for painful diabetic neuropathy without prior evaluation of non-opioid alternatives.

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Adoption of 3-year screening intervals for sight-threatening retinal lesions in type 2 diabetes patients without retinopathy.
- Integration of GLP-1 receptor agonists and SGLT2 inhibitors in patients with diabetes and cardiovascular or renal risk to reduce complications.
- Implementation of automated teleretinal screening programs to expand access to diabetic retinopathy detection.
- Use of faricimab and other extended-duration intravitreal therapies for diabetic macular edema with dosing up to every 16 weeks.
- Application of digital health technologies, including smart mats and remote monitoring, for early detection of foot ulcers and neuropathy.
