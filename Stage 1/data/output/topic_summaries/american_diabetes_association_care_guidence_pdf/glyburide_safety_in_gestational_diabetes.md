# glyburide safety in gestational diabetes
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Glyburide safety and efficacy in gestational diabetes

**Core Recommendations:**
- Associated with higher rates of neonatal hypoglycemia and macrosomia compared to insulin or metformin
- Demonstrated failure to meet noninferiority criteria against insulin for preventing large-for-gestational-age infants
- Linked to increased risk of maternal hypoglycemia when used in oral glucose-lowering strategies
- Exhibits significant placental transfer with umbilical cord plasma concentrations reaching 50–70% of maternal levels
- Lacks available long-term safety data for offspring exposed in utero
- Increased neonatal abdominal circumference observed with glyburide compared to insulin or metformin

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Patient must have gestational diabetes mellitus (GDM)
- Clinical decision must weigh risks of neonatal hypoglycemia and macrosomia against oral medication convenience
- Assessment of maternal hypoglycemia risk prior to initiation
- Consideration of alternative agents (insulin or metformin) due to safety profile concerns
- Absence of long-term follow-up capability for exposed offspring limits recommendation strength
- Prior assessment of maternal hypoglycemia risk before initiating any oral glucose-lowering strategy in GDM

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Use of glyburide as a first-line oral agent in GDM without insulin comparison
- Reliance on glyburide for GDM management based on noninferiority to insulin
- Assumption of safety equivalence between glyburide and insulin for neonatal outcomes
- Use of glyburide in strategies aiming to prevent large-for-gestational-age infants
- Application of glyburide without acknowledging the 50–70% placental transfer rate
- Use of glyburide as part of an oral glucose-lowering medication strategy without insulin substitution for inadequate glycemic control

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Preference for insulin or metformin over glyburide in GDM management
- Recognition of glyburide's failure in noninferiority trials against insulin
- Incorporation of maternal hypoglycemia risk as a primary concern for oral agents in GDM
- Adoption of composite outcome measures including neonatal hypoglycemia, macrosomia, and hyperbilirubinemia
- Implementation of cautious prescribing due to lack of long-term offspring safety data
- Adoption of composite outcome measures including neonatal hyperbilirubinemia when evaluating glyburide's efficacy against insulin
