# GLP-1 RA treatment in older adults
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** GLP-1 RA treatment in older adults

**Core Recommendations:**
- GLP-1 RAs and tirzepatide are highly effective glucose-lowering medications with low hypoglycemia risk
- GLP-1 RAs can be used in older adults with reduced eGFR, including during dialysis
- GLP-1 RAs demonstrate cardiovascular benefits in older adults with ASCVD, CKD, or heart failure with preserved ejection fraction
- Weight loss from GLP-1 RAs benefits conditions like obstructive sleep apnea, knee osteoarthritis, and MASH
- Injectable administration (except oral semaglutide) requires visual, motor, and cognitive skills; weekly dosing preferred
- GLP-1 RAs may be associated with nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, and/or constipation, requiring slow dose titration to minimize gastrointestinal side effects

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Established atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease (ASCVD) or high ASCVD risk
- Chronic kidney disease or symptomatic heart failure with preserved ejection fraction
- Adequate protein intake and resistance/strength training to mitigate sarcopenia risk
- Regular monitoring for dehydration and excessive weight loss
- Slow dose titration to minimize gastrointestinal side effects
- Monitoring for dehydration and excessive weight loss to prevent sarcopenia and bone density loss

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Fixed A1C targets without individualized consideration of life expectancy, cognitive function, or hypoglycemia risk
- Use of GLP-1 RAs in older adults with unexplained weight loss or undernutrition
- Use of GLP-1 RAs in older adults with problematic constipation, significant gastroparesis, recurrent ileus, or bowel obstruction

**Updated Recommendations:**
- GLP-1 RAs as preferred agents for older adults with ASCVD or high cardiovascular risk
- Consideration of tirzepatide (GIP/GLP-1 RA) for enhanced glucose-lowering and weight loss
- Shared decision-making for stepwise treatment plan adjustments in older adults
- Emphasis on deintensifying or simplifying regimens based on safety and burden
- Monitoring for sarcopenia and bone density loss with protein intake and strength training
- Stepwise approach for assessing difficulties in the diabetes treatment plan, reevaluating glycemic goals through shared decision-making, deintensifying, simplifying, or modifying the treatment plan, and reassessing the safety and burdens of any interventions
