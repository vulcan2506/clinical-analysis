# cardiovascular and kidney risk reduction
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Cardiovascular and kidney risk reduction in type 2 diabetes

**Core Recommendations:**
- Prioritize agents that reduce cardiovascular and kidney disease risk in treatment plans
- Engage in shared decision-making considering glucose-lowering efficacy, side effects, accessibility, and affordability
- Use higher-efficacy approaches (e.g., combination therapy) to achieve individualized glycemic goals
- Monitor treatment plans continuously for efficacy, side effects, hypoglycemia, and treatment burden
- Initiate insulin for severe hyperglycemia (e.g., blood glucose ≥300 mg/dL, A1C >10%, or catabolic features)
- Consider weight management and MASLD/MASH risk mitigation as distinct treatment goals alongside glycemic control when selecting therapy in patients without ASCVD, HF, or CKD
- Engage in shared decision-making that prioritizes agents with proven cardiorenal benefits (e.g., GLP-1 RAs, SGLT2 inhibitors) even if glycemic targets are achievable with other agents

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Patient must have type 2 diabetes requiring glucose-lowering therapy intensification
- eGFR ≥30 mL/min/1.73m² for metformin use (avoid if eGFR <30)
- Consider patient-specific factors (e.g., ASCVD, HF, CKD, weight management needs, MASLD/MASH risk)
- Baseline A1C ≥1.5% above individualized goal may require dual-combination therapy or potent agents
- Severe hyperglycemia (e.g., A1C >10%, symptoms, or catabolic features) warrants insulin initiation
- eGFR 30–45 mL/min/1.73m² requires caution with metformin due to increased risk of periodic eGFR drops to ≤30 mL/min/1.73m²
- Periodic vitamin B12 testing for patients on long-term metformin therapy due to risk of deficiency and neuropathy worsening

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Sulfonylureas as first-line therapy due to higher hypoglycemia risk and less favorable cardiovascular outcomes
- DPP-4 inhibitors as preferred second-line agents due to lower A1C reduction efficacy
- Fixed numeric A1C targets without individualized consideration of patient factors
- Metformin use in patients with eGFR <30 mL/min/1.73m² due to lactic acidosis risk
- Routine use of sulfonylureas for severe hyperglycemia when GLP-1 RAs or tirzepatide are available

**Updated Recommendations:**
- GLP-1 RAs (e.g., semaglutide) and tirzepatide as preferred agents for glycemic control and cardiovascular/kidney risk reduction
- SGLT2 inhibitors for patients with type 2 diabetes and HF or CKD regardless of glycemic control
- Extended-release metformin formulations to mitigate gastrointestinal side effects
- Periodic vitamin B12 testing for patients on long-term metformin therapy
- Dual GIP/GLP-1 receptor agonists (e.g., tirzepatide) for superior weight, cardiovascular, and liver outcomes
- Metformin extended-release formulations to mitigate gastrointestinal side effects
