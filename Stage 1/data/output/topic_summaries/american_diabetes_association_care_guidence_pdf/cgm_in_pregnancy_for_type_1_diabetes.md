# CGM in pregnancy for type 1 diabetes
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** CGM in pregnancy for type 1 diabetes

**Core Recommendations:**
- Use real-time CGM in addition to standard care for pregnant individuals with type 1 diabetes to improve A1C, maternal glucose time in range (TIR), and time above range (TAR) without increasing hypoglycemia
- Monitor CGM-reported mean glucose, lower mean glucose, lower SD, and higher TIR to reduce risks of large-for-gestational-age births and other adverse neonatal outcomes
- Use CGM time in range (TIR) for glycemic outcome assessment in type 1 diabetes pregnancy, though it does not provide actionable data for fasting/postprandial hypoglycemia or hyperglycemia
- Follow pregnancy-specific CGM glucose goals: TIR >70% (63–140 mg/dL), level 1 TBR <4% (<63 mg/dL), level 2 TBR <1% (<54 mg/dL), and TAR <25% (>140 mg/dL)
- Select CGM device based on individual circumstances, preferences, and needs, considering nonadjunctive use and accuracy validated in pregnancy
- Use CGM-reported mean glucose as the primary metric for glycemic assessment in pregnancy, given its superiority over estimated A1C or glucose management indicator (GMI) due to pregnancy-induced changes in red blood cell turnover and plasma volume
- Follow finger stick glucose measurements in specific circumstances: post-hypoglycemia correction (due to plasma-interstitial glucose time lag), symptom mismatch with sensor readings, and first day of sensor use when accuracy may be lower

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Pregnancy with type 1 diabetes diagnosis
- Optimization of pre- and postprandial glucose goals as part of standard care
- Individualized device selection considering accuracy, alarms, and patient preferences
- Use of CGM systems validated for pregnancy (e.g., demonstrated %20/20 agreement rates ≥78.6%)
- Finger stick glucose measurements in specific circumstances (e.g., post-hypoglycemia correction, symptom mismatch, first day of use)
- Pregnancy-specific CGM accuracy validation (e.g., demonstrated %20/20 agreement rates ≥78.6% on day 1, ≥96.3% on days 4–7, and ≥97.3% on day 10) for device selection in type 1 diabetes pregnancy
- Nonadjunctive CGM use in pregnancy requires device accuracy validated for pregnancy (e.g., %20/20 agreement rates ≥96.3% by day 4–7)

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Reliance solely on estimated A1C or glucose management indicator without CGM data in pregnancy
- Use of capillary blood glucose monitoring as the primary method for glycemic control in type 1 diabetes pregnancy
- Fixed numeric A1C targets without consideration of CGM-derived TIR and pregnancy-specific goals

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Adoption of CGM in type 1 diabetes pregnancy as standard care based on RCT evidence (CONCEPTT trial)
- Pregnancy-specific CGM glucose targets (TIR >70%, TBR <4%/<1%, TAR <25%) endorsed by international consensus
- Use of CGM-reported mean glucose as superior to estimated A1C for glycemic assessment in pregnancy
- Nonadjunctive CGM use in pregnancy with validated accuracy (e.g., %20/20 agreement rates ≥96.3% by day 4–7)
- Cost-effectiveness of CGM in pregnancy offset by improved maternal and neonatal outcomes
- International consensus endorses pregnancy-specific CGM glucose goal ranges (TIR >70%, TBR <4%/<1%, TAR <25%) for type 1 diabetes but does not specify device type, accuracy thresholds, or alarm requirements
- CGM systems for pregnancy do not require calibrations and are approved for nonadjunctive use, though finger stick measurements are advised in specific circumstances
