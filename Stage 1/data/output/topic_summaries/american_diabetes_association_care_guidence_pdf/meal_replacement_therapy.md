# meal replacement therapy
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** meal replacement therapy for diabetes care

**Core Recommendations:**
- Use of partial or total meal replacements as an additional strategy for energy restriction
- Meal replacements are prepackaged foods (bars, shakes, and soups) with fixed macronutrient and micronutrient content
- Improves nutrient quality, glycemic management, and reduces portion size and energy intake
- Short-term strategy (<6 months) for weight loss in landmark clinical trials (Look AHEAD, DiRECT, PREVIEW)
- Long-term follow-up and support from the diabetes care team are required to optimize self-efficacy and maintain behavioral changes
- Meal replacements may be used as part of a structured very-low-calorie diet (VLCD) or low-calorie diet (LCD) protocol for diabetes management

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Patient eligibility for energy restriction strategies
- Supervision or guidance from a diabetes care team member
- Consideration of patient-specific factors (e.g., comorbidities, lifestyle) for suitability
- Patient must have access to or be prescribed medically appropriate meal replacements by a healthcare provider
- Meal replacements should be integrated into a comprehensive diabetes care plan with regular monitoring of glycemic outcomes and nutritional status

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Inclusion of meal replacements as a recommended strategy in diabetes care guidelines
- Evidence-based support from meta-analyses and landmark trials for weight loss and glycemic improvements
- Inclusion of meal replacements in structured weight management programs for patients with type 2 diabetes, particularly those with obesity
