# metformin dosing and safety
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Metformin dosing, safety, and monitoring in older adults

**Core Recommendations:**
- Use metformin safely in individuals with eGFR ≥ 30 mL/min/1.73 m², utilizing lower doses for eGFR 30–45 mL/min/1.73 m²
- Temporarily discontinue metformin before procedures involving iodinated contrast, during hospitalizations, or during acute illness compromising kidney or liver function
- Minimize gastrointestinal side effects by implementing slow uptitration at initiation
- Consider extended-release formulation as an alternative for patients with medication adherence difficulties or gastrointestinal effects
- Monitor vitamin B12 levels annually for patients taking metformin long term (>4 years)
- Consider vitamin B12 supplementation if deficiency is detected in patients on long-term metformin (>4 years)
- Assess for signs of lactic acidosis (e.g., unexplained malaise, myalgias, respiratory distress, somnolence) in patients with risk factors (e.g., advanced age, renal impairment, hepatic dysfunction)

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Patient must have an estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) ≥ 30 mL/min/1.73 m²
- eGFR must be monitored every 3–6 months in patients at risk for decline in kidney function
- Patient must not have advanced kidney disease
- Patient must not have hypoperfusion, hypoxemia, or impaired hepatic function
- Patient must be taking metformin for a duration greater than 4 years to trigger annual B12 monitoring
- Ensure baseline eGFR is measured before initiating metformin
- Reassess metformin therapy if eGFR falls below 30 mL/min/1.73 m² and discontinue if eGFR < 30 mL/min/1.73 m²

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Avoid metformin in patients with heart failure requiring pharmacologic therapy due to increased risk of lactic acidosis (historical concern, though newer evidence suggests lower risk in compensated HF)

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Consider metformin in patients with mild cognitive impairment or dementia, balancing benefits of glycemic control against potential risks of hypoglycemia and gastrointestinal effects
