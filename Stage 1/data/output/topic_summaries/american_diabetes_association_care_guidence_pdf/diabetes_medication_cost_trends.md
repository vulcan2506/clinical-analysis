# diabetes medication cost trends
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Diabetes medication cost trends and financial screening

**Core Recommendations:**
- Costs for noninsulin and insulin diabetes medications have increased dramatically over the past two decades, with a growing proportion of costs passed to patients and families.
- Medication costs can cause significant stress and negatively impact medication-taking behavior.
- Cost-reducing strategies may improve medication adherence in some cases.
- Screen all people with diabetes for financial concerns and cost-related barriers to care.
- Engage healthcare team members (e.g., pharmacists, social workers) to identify cost-saving opportunities.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Patients with high-deductible health plans or no insurance may incur very high out-of-pocket expenses.
- Financial screening should include assessment of out-of-pocket costs for medications, medical equipment, and glucagon.
- Cost-saving strategies must be tailored to individual patient circumstances and insurance coverage.
- No out-of-pocket cost caps currently exist for diabetes medical equipment (e.g., glucose monitoring devices, insulin administration supplies) or for noninsulin medications.

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Reliance on average wholesale prices (AWP) or National Average Drug Acquisition Costs (NADAC) as actual patient costs, as these do not account for discounts, rebates, or price adjustments.

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Implementation of insulin cost caps ($35 per prescription/month) for Medicare and state-regulated commercial insurance plans in 26 states and D.C.
- Introduction of insulin manufacturer cost reductions and copayment assistance programs, though with variable eligibility and coverage.
- Emphasis on screening for financial barriers and engaging multidisciplinary teams to address cost-related challenges.
- Cost caps for insulin include a tiered structure for state-regulated commercial insurance plans, offering either $35 per prescription per month or $100 per total monthly insulin payment.
