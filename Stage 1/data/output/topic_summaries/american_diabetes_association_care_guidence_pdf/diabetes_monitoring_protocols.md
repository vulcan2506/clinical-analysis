# diabetes monitoring protocols
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Hospital diabetes monitoring protocols + CGM and insulin pump use

**Core Recommendations:**
- Perform point-of-care (POC) blood glucose monitoring before meals in hospitalized individuals with diabetes who are eating
- Monitor glucose every 4–6 hours in hospitalized individuals with diabetes who are not eating
- Use more frequent POC monitoring (every 30 min to every 2 h) for safe intravenous insulin therapy
- Confirm POC glucose results with laboratory testing if results do not correlate with clinical status, especially for asymptomatic hypoglycemia
- Continue personal CGM use during hospitalization if clinically appropriate, with confirmatory POC for insulin dosing and hypoglycemia assessment
- Perform repeat POC glucose testing if initial result does not correlate with clinical status before confirming with laboratory testing
- Review and document CGM and AID data daily (e.g., before meals and at bedtime) with pump setting adjustments as needed

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Use FDA-approved POC hospital-calibrated glucose monitoring systems
- Ensure institutional protocols for CGM validation and accuracy (e.g., 20/20 criterion: ±20% or ±20 mg/dL of POC measurement)
- Provide staff training and ongoing competency assessments for diabetes technology use
- Implement institutional policies for safe use of insulin pumps and AID systems
- Cross-check CGM readings with POC or laboratory blood glucose at least once daily or when clinically appropriate
- Ensure institutional policies for safe use of hospital-owned CGM systems with EHR integration and remote monitoring capabilities
- Confirm CGM accuracy by cross-checking CGM readings with POC or laboratory blood glucose at least once daily or when clinically appropriate

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Reliance solely on capillary (finger-stick) POC glucose monitoring without confirmatory testing when results are inconsistent with clinical status
- Use of non-FDA-approved POC glucose monitoring systems in hospital settings
- Unvalidated CGM use for insulin dosing without confirmatory POC measurements
- Discontinuation of insulin pump or AID systems during hospitalization without clinical necessity

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Adoption of hybrid protocols combining CGM with periodic POC monitoring for intravenous insulin titration to reduce hourly testing burden
- Validation criteria for CGM accuracy in hospital settings (20/20 criterion) to ensure safe insulin dosing
- Encouragement of hospital-wide CGM policies with EHR integration and remote monitoring capabilities
- Support for continuation of insulin pumps and AID systems during hospitalization when clinically appropriate and with proper training
- Daily review and documentation of CGM and AID data (e.g., before meals and at bedtime) with pump setting adjustments as needed
- Implementation of remote monitoring (e.g., glucose telemetry systems) for CGM data in hospital settings
- Development of institutional policies for safe use of hospital-owned CGM systems with EHR integration
