# medication-induced weight gain management
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Medication-induced weight gain management in diabetes care

**Core Recommendations:**
- Review individual’s concomitant medications to identify those that promote weight gain
- Engage other care team members to minimize or provide alternatives for weight-promoting medications when clinically appropriate
- Avoid or substitute medications with high weight gain potential (e.g., antipsychotics, tricyclic antidepressants, glucocorticoids, β-blockers) when possible
- Monitor weight changes closely in patients on obesogenic medications
- Consider dose-dependent impact of multiple obesogenic medications on weight loss outcomes
- Assess the patient’s baseline weight and metabolic status prior to initiating obesogenic medications to establish a reference for future monitoring

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Patient must be on medications with known weight-promoting effects
- Clinical stability and safety must allow for medication review or substitution
- Multidisciplinary care team involvement is feasible
- Patient must be monitored for weight changes and metabolic outcomes
- Prioritize alternatives that do not compromise primary treatment goals
- Documentation of the rationale for selecting or continuing weight-promoting medications when alternatives are not feasible, including patient preferences and clinical necessity

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Routine use of weight-promoting medications without consideration of alternatives
- Assuming lifestyle interventions alone can overcome medication-induced weight gain
- Ignoring the cumulative effect of multiple obesogenic medications on weight loss
- Assuming that switching to a single obesogenic medication will have negligible impact on weight management without considering cumulative effects

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Systematic review of medication lists for weight-promoting effects as part of diabetes care
- Integration of pharmacologic alternatives with lower weight gain liability
- Dose optimization or deprescribing of obesogenic medications when clinically feasible
- Patient education on medication-related weight gain risks and management strategies
- Use of weight loss benchmarks (e.g., 5% or 10%) as outcome measures in patients on obesogenic medications
- Incorporate shared decision-making tools to discuss medication-related weight gain risks and alternatives with patients before prescribing obesogenic drugs
