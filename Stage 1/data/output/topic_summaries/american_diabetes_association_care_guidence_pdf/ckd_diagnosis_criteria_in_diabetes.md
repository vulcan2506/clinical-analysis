# CKD diagnosis criteria in diabetes
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** CKD diagnosis and staging in diabetes

**Core Recommendations:**
- CKD is diagnosed based on albuminuria and/or reduced eGFR in the absence of other primary causes of kidney damage
- Both eGFR and albuminuria must be quantified to guide treatment decisions and risk stratification
- A 30% increase in serum creatinine with RAS blockers or SGLT2 inhibitors is not AKI and medications should not be discontinued
- Referral to a nephrologist is recommended for atypical features such as active urinary sediment or rapidly declining eGFR
- Without a kidney biopsy, the condition should be stated as CKD in a person with diabetes or presumed diabetic nephropathy
- Elevations in serum creatinine (up to 30% from baseline) with RAS blockers (ACE inhibitors/ARBs) must not be confused with AKI and should not prompt medication discontinuation in the absence of volume depletion
- Similar rises in creatinine at initiation of SGLT2 inhibitors or GLP-1 RAs due to hemodynamic changes in tubuloglomerular feedback should not lead to discontinuation of these medications

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Presence of albuminuria and/or reduced eGFR
- Absence of signs or symptoms of other primary causes of kidney damage
- Exclusion of alternative causes such as active urinary sediment, nephrotic syndrome, or rapidly decreasing eGFR
- Quantification of eGFR levels and urine albumin levels
- Assessment of diabetes duration and presence of retinopathy to rule out non-diabetic causes
- Quantification of both eGFR and albuminuria to guide treatment decisions, including medication dosing and selection of antihypertensive or glucose-lowering therapies
- Assessment of observed history of eGFR loss to predict CKD progression and adverse health outcomes

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Discontinuing ACE inhibitors or ARBs due to a <30% increase in serum creatinine
- Discontinuing SGLT2 inhibitors or GLP-1 RAs due to hemodynamic changes causing creatinine rise
- Relying solely on retinopathy presence to confirm diabetic nephropathy in type 2 diabetes
- Using fixed numeric A1C targets without individualization for CKD patients
- Ignoring the risk of AKI associated with NSAIDs and iodinated radiocontrast agents in diabetic patients

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Use of urine biomarkers (e.g., β2-microglobulin, KIM-1) to assess tubule function and injury
- Subclassification of CKD by degree of albuminuria at any eGFR level to assess CVD and mortality risk
- Recognition that SGLT2 inhibitors do not promote AKI in randomized controlled trials of advanced kidney disease
- Consideration of kidney biopsy for definitive diagnosis when multiple causes of CKD are suspected
- Monitoring of observed history of eGFR loss to predict CKD progression and adverse health outcomes
- Use of urine biomarkers (e.g., β2-microglobulin, α1-microglobulin, uromodulin, interleukin-18, KIM-1, MCP-1, and TFF3) to assess tubule function, injury, inflammation, and repair in CKD diagnosis and monitoring
- Subclassification of CKD by degree of albuminuria at any eGFR level to assess cardiovascular disease risk, CKD progression, and mortality
