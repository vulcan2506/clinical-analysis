# pregnancy glycemic management strategy
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** pregnancy glycemic management strategy

**Core Recommendations:**
- glycemic goals in pregnant individuals are stricter than in nonpregnant individuals
- pregnant people with diabetes should consume consistent amounts of carbohydrates to match prandial insulin dosages or use insulin-to-carbohydrate ratios
- medical nutrition therapy (MNT) is essential to establish food plans and weight gain goals
- lifestyle modification (nutrition, exercise, weight management) is the first-line treatment after GDM diagnosis
- RDN referral is important for individualized nutrition plans and glycemic management
- avoidance of trans fats and excess saturated fats in the diet
- promotion of n-3 fatty acids from nuts, seeds, and fish to support weight management

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- preexisting diabetes or GDM diagnosis
- RDN involvement for nutrition plan development
- consistent carbohydrate intake or insulin-to-carbohydrate ratio use
- evaluation of carbohydrate quality and macronutrient balance
- adherence to National Academy of Medicine dietary reference intake guidelines
- nutrition plan must provide adequate calorie intake to promote fetal, neonatal, and maternal health
- nutrition plan must achieve appropriate weight gain according to 2009 National Academy of Medicine recommendations

**Previous Recommendations:**
- ketogenic eating patterns due to lack of carbohydrates
- paleo eating patterns due to dairy restriction
- excessive restriction of any macronutrient class
- severe carbohydrate restriction without ketone monitoring
- fixed calorie intake without individualized assessment

**Updated Recommendations:**
- minimum 175g carbohydrate intake (~35% of 2,000-calorie diet) for all pregnant people
- emphasis on whole foods (fruits, vegetables, legumes, whole grains, lean protein, healthy fats)
- limitation of processed foods, fatty red meat, and sweetened foods/beverages
- use of fasting ketone testing to identify severe carbohydrate restriction
- promotion of higher-quality, nutrient-dense carbohydrates to improve glycemic control and vascular benefits
- minimum dietary reference intake of 71 g of protein for all pregnant people
- minimum dietary reference intake of 28 g of fiber for all pregnant people
