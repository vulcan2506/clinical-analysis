# ADA Standards of Care framework
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** ADA Standards of Care framework

**Core Recommendations:**
- Diabetes care requires continuous, comprehensive risk-reduction strategies beyond glycemic management.
- Ongoing diabetes self-management education and support are critical for empowering patients and preventing complications.
- Recommendations are based on rigorous evidence evaluation and updated annually with emerging clinical considerations.
- Guidelines are applied with clinical judgment, adjusted for individual preferences, comorbidities, and person-specific factors.
- Shared decision-making between healthcare professionals and individuals with diabetes is emphasized.
- The Standards of Care includes discussion of evidence and clinical practice recommendations intended to optimize care by assisting healthcare professionals and individuals in making shared decisions about diabetes care.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Annual updates by the ADA Professional Practice Committee for Diabetes (PPC).
- Evidence must be systematically reviewed, graded (A, B, C, or E), and approved by 80% consensus of the PPC.
- Recommendations must align with patient-specific factors such as age, comorbidities, and values.
- External peer review, ADA leadership review, and Board of Directors approval are required.
- Living standards are updated immediately if new evidence or regulatory changes (e.g., medication approvals) warrant it.
- The Standards of Care subcommittee for each section creates an initial list of relevant clinical questions reviewed and discussed by the PPC, with systematic literature searches conducted in consultation with a systematic review expert and librarian.

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Previously published ADA scientific documents on clinical topics within the Standards of Care purview are superseded.
- Fixed numeric A1C targets for older adults are no longer recommended.
- Guidelines that preclude clinical judgment or ignore individual circumstances are outdated.
- Recommendations without evidence grading (e.g., ungraded expert opinions) are now explicitly categorized (E-level).
- Comprehensive treatment plans for specific complications (e.g., diabetic retinopathy) are no longer provided; only screening and referral guidance remain.
- The Standards of Care no longer provides comprehensive treatment plans for diabetes-associated complications (e.g., diabetic retinopathy or diabetic foot ulcers), offering only screening, referral guidance, and management in primary care settings.

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Living standards allow immediate updates based on new evidence or regulatory changes (e.g., medication or technology approvals).
- Explicit grading system (A, B, C, E) for all recommendations to clarify evidence strength.
- Consumer-facing plain language summaries for people with diabetes to improve accessibility.
- Inclusion of psychosocial and behavioral health screening guidance in primary care settings.
- Expanded audience coverage to include payors, policymakers, and advocacy groups for policy alignment.
- The Standards of Care includes a consumer-facing plain language summary for people with diabetes to improve accessibility and understanding of guidelines.
