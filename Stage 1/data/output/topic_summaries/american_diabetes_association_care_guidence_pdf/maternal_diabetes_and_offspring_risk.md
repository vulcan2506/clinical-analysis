# maternal diabetes and offspring risk
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Maternal diabetes and offspring risk management

**Core Recommendations:**
- Optimize preconception glycemic control (A1C <6.5% or <7% if at risk of hypoglycemia) to reduce congenital anomalies and adverse pregnancy outcomes.
- Implement continuous glucose monitoring (CGM) in pregnancy for type 1 and type 2 diabetes to improve time-in-range and neonatal outcomes.
- Use automated insulin delivery (AID) systems in pregnancy complicated by type 1 diabetes to achieve tighter glycemic targets.
- Screen for gestational diabetes mellitus (GDM) at 24–28 weeks (or earlier in high-risk women) using IADPSG criteria.
- Administer low-dose aspirin (81 mg/day) from 12–36 weeks in women with pregestational diabetes to reduce preeclampsia risk.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Preconception care must include glycemic optimization, folic acid supplementation (400–800 mcg/day), and evaluation of microvascular complications.
- Women with pregestational diabetes require prepregnancy counseling on contraception, glycemic targets, and fetal risk reduction.
- GDM diagnosis requires fasting plasma glucose ≥92 mg/dL or 1-hour postprandial ≥180 mg/dL or 2-hour ≥153 mg/dL after 75g OGTT.
- CGM use in pregnancy requires healthcare provider training and patient education on data interpretation and insulin adjustments.
- Aspirin prophylaxis for preeclampsia requires initiation between 12–16 weeks in women with diabetes and chronic hypertension.

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Routine use of glyburide for GDM due to higher neonatal hypoglycemia and macrosomia risk compared to insulin.
- Postprandial glucose targets based solely on fasting glucose without CGM integration.
- Universal first-trimester screening for GDM using HbA1c alone without confirmatory OGTT.
- Routine use of metformin in early pregnancy for GDM prevention without individualized risk assessment.
- Targeting A1C <6.0% in pregnancy without consideration of hypoglycemia risk in type 1 diabetes.

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Early GDM screening (before 24 weeks) in high-risk women using HbA1c ≥5.9% (41 mmol/mol) as a threshold.
- Integration of CGM-derived time-in-range metrics (e.g., >70% in target range) as primary glycemic targets in pregnancy.
- Use of AID systems (e.g., hybrid closed-loop insulin delivery) in type 1 diabetes pregnancy to reduce hyperglycemia and hypoglycemia.
- Postpartum screening for type 2 diabetes at 4–12 weeks using OGTT or HbA1c in women with GDM.
- Lifestyle interventions (diet, exercise) and metformin for diabetes prevention in women with prior GDM.
