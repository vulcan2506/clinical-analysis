# ICI-induced autoimmune diabetes
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** ICI-induced autoimmune diabetes

**Core Recommendations:**
- Fulminant onset of autoimmune diabetes with DKA and low/undetectable C-peptide levels
- Fewer than half of cases have islet autoantibodies
- Most cases do not experience a phase of partial remission
- Majority occurs in individuals with high-risk HLA susceptibility haplotypes for type 1 diabetes
- Risk cannot be predicted by family history or autoantibodies
- ICI-induced diabetes typically presents with severe hyperglycemia and rapid progression to DKA, often within weeks of ICI initiation

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Healthcare professionals administering ICIs or caring for exposed individuals must be mindful of this adverse effect
- All exposed individuals should be educated and monitored appropriately
- Prompt initiation of insulin therapy is required upon diagnosis, as β-cell destruction is irreversible and endogenous insulin production is permanently lost

**Updated Recommendations:**
- ICI-induced diabetes occurs in 0.6–1.4% of treated individuals
- Higher frequency with PD-1/PD-L1 pathway blockade, alone or combined with CTLA-4 inhibitors
- Consider baseline HbA1c and fasting glucose monitoring prior to ICI initiation in high-risk patients (e.g., those with pre-existing type 2 diabetes or high-risk HLA haplotypes)
