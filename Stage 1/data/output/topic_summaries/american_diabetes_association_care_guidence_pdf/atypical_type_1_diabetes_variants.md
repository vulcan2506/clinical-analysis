# atypical type 1 diabetes variants
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Atypical type 1 diabetes variants (autoantibody-negative insulinopenic diabetes)

**Core Recommendations:**
- Individuals have permanent insulinopenia and are prone to DKA but lack evidence of β-cell autoimmunity.
- Only a minority of people with type 1 diabetes fall into this autoantibody-negative category.
- DKA can occur spontaneously in these variants despite absence of autoimmune markers.
- DKA can occur in atypical type 1 diabetes variants despite the absence of precipitating factors (e.g., illness, medications) that typically trigger DKA in type 2 diabetes

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Documentation of permanent insulinopenia (absolute insulin deficiency).
- Exclusion of β-cell autoimmunity via islet autoantibody testing.
- Absence of type 2 diabetes features (e.g., insulin resistance, obesity).
- Documentation of absolute insulinopenia via fasting C-peptide levels or insulin levels in atypical type 1 diabetes variants to confirm insulin deficiency

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Reliance solely on clinical phenotype (e.g., age, BMI) to classify diabetes without autoantibody testing.
- Assuming all insulinopenic diabetes cases are autoimmune type 1 diabetes.
- Assuming DKA in adults is exclusively associated with type 2 diabetes without further evaluation for atypical type 1 diabetes variants

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Consider islet autoantibody testing in adults without traditional type 2 diabetes risk factors or of younger age to exclude type 1 diabetes.
- Recognition of atypical type 1 diabetes variants as a distinct subgroup within type 1 diabetes.
- Consider islet autoantibody testing in adults without traditional type 2 diabetes risk factors (e.g., obesity, older age) or of younger age to exclude type 1 diabetes, including atypical variants
