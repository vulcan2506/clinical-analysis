# cognitive impairment screening
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Cognitive impairment screening in older adults with diabetes

**Core Recommendations:**
- Screen annually for cognitive impairment or dementia in adults ≥65 years at initial visit and as clinically indicated.
- Use validated tools (e.g., Mini-Cog, Mini-Mental State Examination, Montreal Cognitive Assessment) for screening.
- Screen when significant decline in clinical status (e.g., self-care errors, insulin dosing mistakes, hypoglycemia) is observed.
- Refer individuals screening positive for formal cognitive/neuropsychological evaluation if feasible.
- Simplify diabetes care plans and engage support structures when cognitive dysfunction is identified.
- Screen for delirium using the 4AT tool when acute changes in mental status are suspected.
- Discuss findings of cognitive screening with the individual and their care partners.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Adults ≥65 years with diabetes.
- Presence of geriatric syndromes (e.g., cognitive impairment, depression) or functional decline.
- Significant decline in self-care activities (e.g., insulin dosing errors, skipped meals).
- Prior assessment of hypoglycemia risk, polypharmacy, and frailty.
- Availability of caregiver support for individuals with cognitive impairment.
- Presence of significant decline in clinical status due to increased problems with self-care activities and medication management (e.g., errors in calculating insulin dose, difficulty counting carbohydrates, skipped meals, skipped insulin doses, difficulty recognizing/preventing/treating hypoglycemia).
- Availability of care partners or support systems to assist with care when cognitive dysfunction is identified.

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Routine use of non-validated cognitive screening tools.
- Rigid A1C targets without consideration of cognitive function.
- Complex insulin regimens without caregiver support in cognitively impaired individuals.
- Ignoring hypoglycemia risk in older adults with cognitive decline.
- Intensive treatment protocols aimed to achieve an A1C <6.0% with complex drug plans in older adults with cognitive impairment or complex health needs.
- Rigid A1C targets without consideration of cognitive function and functional limitations.

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Annual cognitive screening for early detection of mild cognitive impairment or dementia.
- Integration of Mini-Cog and other validated tools into routine diabetes care.
- Use of GLP-1 RAs and SGLT2 inhibitors for potential cognitive benefits.
- Simplification of care plans and caregiver engagement for cognitively impaired individuals.
- Consideration of diabetes-related dementia as a distinct clinical entity.
- Recognition of 'diabetes-related dementia' as a distinct clinical entity characterized by slower progression, absence of typical neuroimaging findings, advanced age, elevated A1C, long duration of diabetes, high frequency of insulin use, frailty, sarcopenia, and dynapenia.
- Use of validated hypoglycemia risk calculators (e.g., Kaiser Hypoglycemia Model) to stratify future risk for hypoglycemia.
