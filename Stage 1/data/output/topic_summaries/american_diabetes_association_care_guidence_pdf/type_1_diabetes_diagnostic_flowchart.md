# type 1 diabetes diagnostic flowchart
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Type 1 Diabetes Diagnostic Flowchart & Staging

**Core Recommendations:**
- GAD65 is the primary antibody to measure, followed by IA-2 and/or ZnT8 if negative.
- A negative antibody result does not exclude type 1 diabetes in individuals <35 years old.
- C-peptide testing is indicated only in insulin-treated individuals and should be measured prior to insulin discontinuation.
- Monogenic diabetes is suggested by A1C <58 mmol/mol, family history, specific features, or prediction model probability >5%.
- Type 1 diabetes staging (Stage 1-3) is based on autoimmunity presence and glycemic status (normoglycemia to overt hyperglycemia).

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Patient must be newly diagnosed or suspected of having type 1 diabetes.
- C-peptide testing requires the patient to be receiving insulin treatment.
- C-peptide testing should not be performed within 2 weeks of a hyperglycemic emergency.
- Classification of monogenic diabetes requires genetic testing confirmation or specific clinical features.
- Autoantibody screening is recommended for those with family history or elevated genetic risk.
- C-peptide testing should be performed within 5 hours of eating if using a random sample to replace formal stimulation tests.
- C-peptide testing is indicated for individuals not yet treated with insulin only if they require careful monitoring for rapid insulin initiation.

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Relying on nonvalidated autoantibody tests for diagnosing latent autoimmune diabetes in adults (LADA).
- Using a single clinical feature to confirm type 1 diabetes in isolation.
- Testing C-peptide within 2 weeks of a hyperglycemic emergency.
- Using the term LADA as a distinct disease entity separate from type 1 diabetes (now included under type 1).
- Repeating C-peptide tests for very low levels (<80 pmol/L) without clinical indication.

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Use of diabetes prediction models (e.g., diabetesgenes.org/exeter-diabetes-app) to suggest monogenic diabetes.
- Incorporation of teplizumab as an approved treatment for high-risk individuals with multiple autoantibodies.
- Recognition that a diagnosis of type 1 diabetes does not preclude having features of type 2 diabetes (e.g., obesity, insulin resistance).
- Standardized islet autoantibody tests recommended for adults with overlapping phenotypic risk factors.
- Use of random C-peptide samples (with concurrent glucose) to replace formal stimulation tests in classification.
- Use of teplizumab as an approved treatment for high-risk individuals with multiple autoantibodies.
