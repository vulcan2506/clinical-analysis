# statin-associated diabetes risk
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** statin-associated diabetes risk

**Core Recommendations:**
- Statin use is associated with a modestly increased risk of incident type 2 diabetes, particularly in individuals with diabetes risk factors or impaired glucose tolerance.
- The cardiovascular benefits of statins (e.g., reduction in vascular events) generally outweigh the small absolute risk of new-onset diabetes.
- The absolute risk increase of diabetes with statins is small (e.g., 1.5% vs. 1.2% over 5 years with rosuvastatin vs. placebo).
- Statin-associated diabetes risk may be dose-dependent, with higher statin doses associated with a greater increase in diabetes risk compared to lower doses.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Patients with diabetes risk factors or impaired glucose tolerance should be monitored for new-onset diabetes when initiating statin therapy.
- Statin therapy should not be withheld solely due to diabetes risk if cardiovascular benefits are deemed significant.
- Patients initiating statin therapy should have baseline fasting plasma glucose or HbA1c measured to assess diabetes risk prior to treatment.

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Routine screening for diabetes is recommended in high-risk patients prior to and during statin therapy to detect incident diabetes early.
- Consideration of non-statin lipid-lowering therapies may be explored in patients at very high diabetes risk if statin therapy is contraindicated or not tolerated, balancing cardiovascular benefits against diabetes risk.
