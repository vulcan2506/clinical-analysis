# hyperglycemia management in older adults
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Hyperglycemia management in older adults in PALTC and end-of-life settings

**Core Recommendations:**
- Prioritize avoidance of hypoglycemia and severe hyperglycemia in PALTC residents with type 2 diabetes, using oral agents or basal insulin as alternatives to prandial insulin.
- Implement standardized alert systems for blood glucose excursions (e.g., <70 mg/dL, >250 mg/dL, or symptomatic hyperglycemia) with timely notifications to healthcare professionals.
- Conduct assessments at least every 30 days for the first 90 days post-admission and every 60 days thereafter in PALTC, with treatment adjustments via telephone, fax, or in-person visits.
- In palliative care, deintensify or simplify diabetes medications to prioritize comfort, symptom management, and quality of life, avoiding strict glycemic control.
- Use CGM to monitor for hypoglycemia and hyperglycemia in PALTC residents and palliative care patients when frequent blood glucose monitoring is burdensome.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Residents must have documented hypoglycemia risk factors (e.g., impaired cognitive/renal function, polypharmacy, variable nutritional intake) before initiating intensive glycemic management.
- PALTC facilities must have standardized alert systems in place to notify healthcare professionals of blood glucose excursions.
- For palliative care, goals of care discussions must include the individual, care partners, and healthcare team to align treatment intensity with patient preferences.
- CGM use requires training for staff and integration into care plans for residents with insulin-treated diabetes in PALTC.
- Assessments in PALTC must account for life expectancy, functional status, and comorbid conditions to individualize glycemic targets.

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Fixed numeric A1C targets for older adults in PALTC or end-of-life settings.
- Routine use of sulfonylureas or insulin regimens with high hypoglycemia risk in frail older adults.
- Daily healthcare professional evaluations in PALTC beyond federal minimum assessment intervals.
- Rigid adherence to strict glycemic control in hospice or palliative care patients.
- A1C monitoring in end-of-life care due to limited utility in guiding treatment.

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Oral agents (e.g., DPP-4 inhibitors, SGLT2 inhibitors) as first-line alternatives to basal insulin in PALTC residents with type 2 diabetes.
- CGM-guided insulin administration in long-term care facilities to reduce hypoglycemia and hyperglycemia.
- Deintensification protocols for diabetes medications in older adults with limited life expectancy or high hypoglycemia risk.
- Telehealth programs using CGM and connected insulin devices to improve glycemic management in nursing homes.
- Use of supportive and palliative care tools (e.g., SPICT, Gold Standards Framework) to guide end-of-life diabetes management.
