# frailty phenotype in diabetes
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** frailty phenotype in type 2 diabetes

**Core Recommendations:**
- Frailty phenotype in type 2 diabetes often includes obesity alongside physical frailty
- Frailty may present at an earlier age in people with type 2 diabetes compared to the general population
- Functional exercise capacity in middle-aged adults with type 2 diabetes resembles that of non-diabetic individuals over a decade older
- Frailty phenotype in type 2 diabetes may include cognitive impairment alongside physical frailty
- Frailty in type 2 diabetes is associated with higher risk of hypoglycemia due to impaired counterregulatory responses

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Screening for frailty phenotype in type 2 diabetes should incorporate both physical performance measures (e.g., gait speed, grip strength) and metabolic markers (e.g., HbA1c, insulin resistance)
- Frailty assessment in diabetes requires evaluation of sarcopenic obesity using dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry (DXA) or bioelectrical impedance analysis

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Routine use of intensive glycemic control targets (e.g., HbA1c <6.5%) in frail older adults with type 2 diabetes is no longer recommended due to increased hypoglycemia risk

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Implementation of structured resistance training programs as first-line therapy for frailty phenotype in type 2 diabetes, with progressive overload principles
- Use of GLP-1 receptor agonists (e.g., semaglutide) as preferred agents in frail patients with type 2 diabetes due to weight loss benefits and lower hypoglycemia risk
