# non-nutritive sweetener use in diabetes
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** non-nutritive sweetener use in diabetes

**Core Recommendations:**
- NNS may be an acceptable substitute for nutritive sweeteners in people with diabetes accustomed to sugar-sweetened foods or beverages, when consumed in moderation (no more than acceptable daily intake).
- NNS do not significantly affect glycemic management.
- NNS can reduce overall calorie and carbohydrate intake if individuals do not compensate with additional calories from other food sources.
- Healthcare professionals should promote water as the healthiest beverage option but may recommend no-calorie or low-calorie sweetened products for people with overweight or obesity and diabetes to avoid deprivation.
- Healthcare professionals should regularly assess individual NNS use based on acceptable daily intake and recommend moderation.
- NNS have undergone extensive safety evaluation by regulatory agencies and are continually monitored for harm.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- People with diabetes accustomed to regularly consuming sugar-sweetened foods or beverages may consider NNS as substitutes.
- NNS use should not exceed the acceptable daily intake levels established by regulatory agencies.
- Individuals should not compensate for NNS use with additional calories from other food sources to achieve calorie reduction.
- Healthcare professionals should encourage reductions in foods and beverages with added sugars and promote reducing overall sugar intake and calories with or without the use of NNS.

**Updated Recommendations:**
- NNS are recommended as potential substitutes for nutritive sweeteners in moderation for people with diabetes.
- Healthcare professionals are encouraged to regularly assess NNS use and promote moderation based on acceptable daily intake.
- No-calorie or low-calorie sweetened products may be used by people with overweight or obesity and diabetes to avoid deprivation, with direction of benefit similar to water.
- A meta-analysis and systematic review of RCTs found no evidence that NNS raise liver enzymes.
