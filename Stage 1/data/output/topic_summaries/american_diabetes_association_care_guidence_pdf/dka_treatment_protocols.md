# DKA treatment protocols
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** DKA treatment protocols

**Core Recommendations:**
- Bicarbonate administration is restricted to cases where pH <7.0
- Phosphate supplementation is reserved for severe cases with muscle weakness, respiratory compromise, and phosphate <1.0 mmol/L or <0.32 mmol/L
- Continuous intravenous insulin infusion is required for severe DKA and HHS, with nurse-driven protocols allowing variable rates but no less than 1 unit/h
- Transition from intravenous to subcutaneous insulin requires basal insulin administration 2–4 hours before stopping IV insulin to prevent rebound hyperglycemia
- Mild to moderate DKA may be managed with subcutaneous rapid-acting insulin every 1–2 hours, provided aggressive fluid replacement and frequent glucose monitoring are ensured
- Use of fixed-rate intravenous insulin infusion for severe DKA and HHS management alongside nurse-driven variable-rate protocols

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Diagnosis of DKA requires glucose ≥200 mg/dL (11.1 mmol/L) or prior diabetes history, ketosis (β-Hydroxybutyrate ≥3.0 mmol/L or urine ketone strip ≥2+), and metabolic acidosis (pH <7.3 and/or bicarbonate <18 mmol/L)
- Treatment must address underlying causes such as sepsis, myocardial infarction, or stroke
- For severe cases, continuous intravenous insulin infusion is mandatory
- Subcutaneous insulin use in mild/moderate DKA requires adequate fluid replacement and frequent point-of-care glucose monitoring
- Individualized treatment is necessary due to variability in DKA presentation (e.g., euglycemic DKA, mild to moderate hyperglycemia, or severe dehydration/coma)
- Administration of basal insulin 2–4 hours before stopping intravenous insulin to prevent recurrence of ketoacidosis and rebound hyperglycemia
- Aggressive fluid replacement and frequent point-of-care blood glucose monitoring when using subcutaneous rapid-acting insulin for mild to moderate DKA

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Routine bicarbonate administration for DKA (no benefit in acidosis resolution or discharge time)
- Fixed high-dose insulin protocols without consideration of patient-specific factors
- Exclusive reliance on intravenous insulin for mild to moderate DKA without evaluating subcutaneous options
- Delayed transition from intravenous to subcutaneous insulin without basal insulin overlap
- Use of phosphate supplementation without meeting severe criteria (muscle weakness, respiratory compromise, and low phosphate levels)
- Routine use of bicarbonate in DKA regardless of pH level (no benefit in acidosis resolution or discharge time)

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Early low-dose basal insulin analog administration alongside intravenous insulin to prevent rebound hyperglycemia without increased hypoglycemia risk
- Nurse-driven insulin infusion protocols with variable rates (≥1 unit/h) for severe DKA/HHS management
- Subcutaneous rapid-acting insulin for mild to moderate DKA in emergency or step-down units, if fluid replacement and monitoring are adequate
- Emphasis on individualized treatment pathways due to clinical variability (e.g., euglycemic DKA, mixed DKA-HHS presentations)
- Inclusion of β-Hydroxybutyrate (BOHB) concentration as a key diagnostic criterion for ketosis
- Administration of low-dose basal insulin analog early in treatment alongside intravenous insulin to prevent rebound hyperglycemia without increased hypoglycemia risk
