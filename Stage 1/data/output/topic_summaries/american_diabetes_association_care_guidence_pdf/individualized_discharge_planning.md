# individualized discharge planning
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** individualized discharge planning for diabetes

**Core Recommendations:**
- Tailor discharge plans to individual needs, capabilities, and preferences
- Begin discharge planning at admission and update as needs change
- Include diabetes self-management education prior to discharge
- Conduct diabetes medication reconciliation with attention to access, affordability, and follow-up scheduling
- Schedule outpatient follow-up within 1 month (earlier if glycemic management is suboptimal)
- Provide clear communication to outpatient healthcare professionals via hospital discharge summaries or direct contact to ensure safe transitions of care

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Patient-specific factors such as diabetes type, severity, illness effects on blood glucose, and individual circumstances
- Consideration of discharge setting capabilities (home, assisted living, rehabilitation, skilled nursing)
- Glycemic management medications changed or suboptimal glucose control at discharge
- Hyperglycemia and/or hypoglycemia during hospitalization
- Facility capabilities for diabetes management if not discharged to home

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Discharge algorithm for glycemic medication adjustment based on admission A1C, pre-admission medications, and insulin use
- Structured discharge communication including root cause of hyperglycemia, complications, and recommended treatments
- Frequent post-discharge contact to adjust therapy and avoid hyperglycemia/hypoglycemia
- Minimum discharge plan components: medication reconciliation, structured communication, and patient education
- Shared decision-making in creating a safe and effective discharge plan
- Discharge algorithm for glycemic medication adjustment based on admission A1C, pre-admission medications, and insulin usage during hospitalization to guide treatment decisions
