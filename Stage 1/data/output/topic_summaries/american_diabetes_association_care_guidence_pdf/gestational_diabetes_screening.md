# gestational diabetes screening
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Gestational diabetes screening and early pregnancy hyperglycemia management

**Core Recommendations:**
- Screen individuals planning pregnancy with risk factors for undiagnosed prediabetes or diabetes.
- Test all individuals of childbearing potential for undiagnosed prediabetes or diabetes before 15 weeks of gestation if not screened preconception.
- Screen for abnormal glucose metabolism (A1C 5.9–6.4% or FPG 110–125 mg/dL) before 15 weeks of gestation to identify high-risk individuals.
- Screen for GDM at 24–28 weeks of gestation in pregnant individuals not previously diagnosed with diabetes or high-risk abnormal glucose metabolism.
- Screen individuals with GDM for prediabetes or diabetes at 4–12 weeks postpartum using 75-g OGTT.
- Monitor fasting and postprandial glucose levels 3–4 times per week in early pregnancy if treatment is initiated for abnormal glucose metabolism (fasting glucose ≥110 mg/dL before 15 weeks).
- Use individualized glycemic goals for pregnant individuals with type 2 diabetes treated with nutrition alone (less stringent goals may apply if hypoglycemia risk is significant).

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Risk factors for screening include obesity, dyslipidemia, hypertension, or family history of diabetes.
- Standard diagnostic criteria must be used for testing before 15 weeks of gestation.
- GDM screening at 24–28 weeks applies to pregnant individuals without prior diabetes diagnosis.
- Postpartum screening requires 75-g OGTT and non-pregnancy diagnostic criteria.
- Shared decision-making is recommended for treatment of early abnormal glucose metabolism (fasting glucose ≥110 mg/dL before 15 weeks).
- Exclude individuals with fasting glucose ≥110 mg/dL (≥6.1 mmol/L) from early treatment RCTs due to safety concerns, limiting evidence for this subgroup.
- Consider regression of GDM when retested at 24–28 weeks' gestation; outcomes among those with regression are similar to individuals without GDM, reducing potential benefit of early treatment.

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Universal treatment of early pregnancy hyperglycemia less than overt diabetes without risk stratification.
- Routine use of less stringent glucose goals without consideration of hypoglycemia risk.
- Assumption that all early abnormal glucose metabolism will persist or require treatment.

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Consider testing all individuals of childbearing potential for undiagnosed prediabetes or diabetes.
- Screen for abnormal glucose metabolism (A1C 5.9–6.4% or FPG 110–125 mg/dL) before 15 weeks of gestation to assess risk.
- Use shared decision-making for treatment of early abnormal glucose metabolism (fasting glucose ≥110 mg/dL before 15 weeks).
- Monitor fasting and postprandial glucose levels in early pregnancy if treatment is initiated.
- Postpartum screening for prediabetes or diabetes at 4–12 weeks using 75-g OGTT.
- Aim for less stringent glycemic goals in pregnancy if standard goals cannot be achieved without significant hypoglycemia, based on clinical experience and individualization of care.
- Postprandial glucose monitoring is associated with better glycemic outcomes and lower risk of preeclampsia in pregnant individuals with diabetes.
