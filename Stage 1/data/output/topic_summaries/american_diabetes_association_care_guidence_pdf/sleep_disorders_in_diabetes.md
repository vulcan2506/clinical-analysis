# sleep disorders in diabetes
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Sleep disorders in diabetes management

**Core Recommendations:**
- Sleep duration, quality, and timing (chronotype) are critical factors linked to diabetes risk and management.
- Obstructive sleep apnea (OSA) is highly prevalent in diabetes (up to 58% in type 2 diabetes, >50% in type 1 diabetes) and should be evaluated in symptomatic individuals.
- Sleep disturbances reduce diabetes self-management engagement and increase hypoglycemia risk in type 1 diabetes.
- Shift work and circadian rhythm disorders are associated with elevated A1C, neuropathy, and psychological distress in diabetes.
- Sleep disorders (OSA, insomnia, restless leg syndrome) are common in diabetes and linked to microvascular/macrovascular complications.
- Sleep disturbances in parents of children/adolescents with diabetes are common, particularly after diagnosis, and may impact family sleep patterns and diabetes management.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Symptoms suggestive of OSA (e.g., excessive daytime sleepiness, snoring, witnessed apnea) warrant evaluation.
- Comprehensive lifestyle assessment including sleep duration, shift work, and work schedules is recommended.
- Diabetes technology (e.g., AID systems) may be beneficial but requires individualized assessment for sleep impact.
- Referral to sleep specialists is advised for significant sleep difficulties, in collaboration with the diabetes care team.
- Patient-specific factors (e.g., obesity, central obesity) increase OSA risk and require targeted screening.
- Screening for sleep-disordered breathing in individuals with obesity and type 2 diabetes should be prioritized, as prevalence may exceed 80% in this subgroup.

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Fixed A1C targets without consideration of sleep disorders or circadian misalignment.
- Routine use of sedative-hypnotics for sleep without addressing underlying sleep disorders in diabetes.
- Ignoring sleep hygiene education as a primary intervention for sleep disturbances in diabetes.
- Assuming sleep duration within normal ranges (7 hours) is universally optimal without individualization.
- Excluding sleep assessment in standard diabetes care protocols.

**Updated Recommendations:**
- CBT for insomnia (including sleep extension) improves sleep outcomes, A1C, fasting glucose, and depressive symptoms in diabetes.
- GLP-1 RAs and dual GIP/GLP-1 RAs (e.g., tirzepatide) show promise in reducing OSA severity and body weight in obesity-related OSA.
- Sleep education (sleep hygiene) improves sleep quality, reduces A1C, and decreases insulin resistance in type 2 diabetes.
- Pharmacologic options (e.g., suvorexant, ramelteon, lemborexant) may be considered for insomnia in diabetes with careful monitoring.
- Standardized screening for sleep disorders (OSA, insomnia, restless leg syndrome) is now part of diabetes care.
- Sleep extension interventions are now recognized as evidence-based strategies to improve sleep outcomes and potentially reduce insulin resistance in diabetes.
