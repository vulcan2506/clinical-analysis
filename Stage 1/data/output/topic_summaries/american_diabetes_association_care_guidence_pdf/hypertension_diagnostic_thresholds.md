# hypertension diagnostic thresholds
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Hypertension diagnostic thresholds and management in diabetes

**Core Recommendations:**
- Elevated blood pressure is defined as systolic 120–129 mmHg with diastolic <80 mmHg.
- Hypertension is defined as systolic ≥130 mmHg or diastolic ≥80 mmHg.
- Blood pressure should be measured at every routine clinical visit or at least every 6 months.
- Individuals with elevated blood pressure should have confirmation using multiple readings on separate days to diagnose hypertension.
- A systolic blood pressure goal of <120 mmHg should be encouraged in individuals with high cardiovascular or kidney risk.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Blood pressure must be measured in a seated position with feet on the floor and arm supported at heart level after 5 minutes of rest.
- Cuff size must be appropriate for the upper-arm circumference.
- Diagnosis of hypertension requires an average of two or more measurements obtained on two or more occasions.
- Individuals with CVD and blood pressure ≥180/110 mmHg may be diagnosed with hypertension at a single visit.
- Postural changes in blood pressure and pulse may require checking lying, sitting, and standing measurements to assess autonomic neuropathy.
- Blood pressure measurements should be taken by a trained individual following general population guidelines for accurate readings.

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Fixed numeric A1C targets for older adults (now individualized).
- Diastolic blood pressure goals of <90 mmHg or <85 mmHg (HOT trial showed no significant difference compared to <80 mmHg in diabetes).
- Standard systolic blood pressure goal of <140 mmHg for all individuals with diabetes (now discouraged in favor of <130/80 mmHg or <120 mmHg for high risk).
- Exclusion of people with diabetes from intensive blood pressure lowering trials (SPRINT excluded diabetics, but evidence supports intensive goals).
- Lack of high-quality data guiding blood pressure goals in people with type 1 diabetes (now recommends similar goal of <130/80 mmHg).
- Diastolic blood pressure goals of <90 mmHg or <85 mmHg without consideration of achieved systolic blood pressure or cardiovascular risk stratification.

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Recommendation to support a blood pressure goal of <130/80 mmHg in people with diabetes.
- Encouragement of a systolic blood pressure goal of <120 mmHg in individuals with high cardiovascular or kidney risk based on BPROAD and ESPRIT trials.
- Use of home blood pressure self-monitoring and 24-h ambulatory blood pressure monitoring to detect white coat or masked hypertension.
- Shared decision-making process for individualizing blood pressure goals addressing cardiovascular risk, adverse effects, and patient preferences.
- Recommendation of ACE inhibitors or ARBs for treatment of hypertension in people with coronary artery disease or elevated urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio.
- Recommendation to use ACE inhibitors or ARBs for hypertension treatment in people with coronary artery disease or elevated urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio (≥30 mg/g).
- Use of home blood pressure self-monitoring and 24-h ambulatory blood pressure monitoring to confirm hypertension diagnosis and monitor treatment efficacy.
