# food insecurity impact on diabetes
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Food insecurity impact on diabetes management

**Core Recommendations:**
- Food insecurity is associated with increased risk of type 2 diabetes and higher-than-recommended glycemia.
- Individuals facing food insecurity have lower engagement in self-care behaviors and medication use.
- Food insecurity is linked to higher rates of depression, diabetes distress, and worse glycemic management.
- Older adults with food insecurity are more likely to have emergency department visits and hospitalizations.
- Hyperglycemia risk in food-insecure individuals may stem from consumption of inexpensive carbohydrate-rich processed foods, binge eating, financial constraints, anxiety, depression, and poor sleep.
- Food insecurity disproportionately affects historically marginalized groups, low-income households, and households headed by single mothers.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Screening for food insecurity using a validated two-item tool (e.g., statements about food running out or not lasting).
- Assessment of patient-specific factors such as socioeconomic status, household composition, and access to community resources.
- Consideration of local food prescription programs or community resources to address food insecurity.
- Evaluation of medication adherence and barriers to filling prescriptions due to financial constraints.
- Monitoring for severe hyperglycemia or hypoglycemia in patients with diabetes and food insecurity.
- Use of a validated two-item screening tool with specific statements about food running out or not lasting within the past 12 months.

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Assuming fixed A1C targets without considering food insecurity-related barriers to self-care.
- Relying solely on medication adjustments without addressing underlying food insecurity.
- Ignoring the impact of socioeconomic factors on diabetes outcomes.

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Integration of food prescription programs into primary care settings to address food insecurity.
- Use of validated screening tools to identify food insecurity in clinical practice.
- Prioritizing mitigation of severe hyperglycemia and hypoglycemia in patients with diabetes and food insecurity.
- Considering local resources and community partnerships to support food access for patients with diabetes.
- Integration of food prescription programs into primary care settings to address food deserts in underserved communities.
