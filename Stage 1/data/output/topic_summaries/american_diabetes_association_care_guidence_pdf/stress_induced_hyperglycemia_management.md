# stress-induced hyperglycemia management
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Stress-induced hyperglycemia management in diabetes

**Core Recommendations:**
- Assess need for more frequent glucose monitoring during stressful events (illness, trauma, surgery)
- Monitor urine or blood ketones in ketosis-prone individuals during stress
- Reevaluate and adjust diabetes treatment during stressful events
- Hold metformin and SGLT2 inhibitors if oral intake is compromised or acute kidney injury is suspected
- Consider holding GLP-1 receptor agonists during illnesses with significant gastrointestinal symptoms
- Assess for acute kidney injury (AKI) in patients on metformin or SGLT2 inhibitors during stress-induced hyperglycemia
- Evaluate for gastrointestinal symptom severity (e.g., nausea, vomiting, diarrhea) before holding GLP-1 receptor agonists during illness

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Underlying clinical complexity or polypharmacy increases risk for hyperglycemia/hypoglycemia
- Severe stress may precipitate life-threatening hyperglycemic crises requiring immediate care
- Ketosis-prone individuals require ketone monitoring during stress
- Oral intake status must be assessed before holding metformin/SGLT2 inhibitors
- Gastrointestinal symptom severity must be evaluated before holding GLP-1 receptor agonists
- Baseline renal function (e.g., serum creatinine, eGFR) must be assessed prior to holding metformin or SGLT2 inhibitors during stress
- Prior to holding GLP-1 receptor agonists, assess hydration status and risk of dehydration in patients with significant gastrointestinal symptoms

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Urine or blood ketone monitoring for ketosis-prone individuals during stress
- Holding metformin/SGLT2 inhibitors during compromised oral intake or AKI concern
- Holding GLP-1 receptor agonists during significant gastrointestinal illness
- Consider holding DPP-4 inhibitors in patients with severe gastrointestinal illness or acute kidney injury during stress
