# perioperative glycemic management
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Perioperative glycemic management

**Core Recommendations:**
- Maintain preoperative A1C <8% (<64 mmol/mol) within 3 months of elective surgery or use 14-day glucose management indicator <8% or time in range >50%
- Monitor and maintain blood glucose between 100–180 mg/dL (5.6–10.0 mmol/L) before, during, and after surgery
- Use basal-bolus insulin (basal insulin plus premeal short/rapid-acting insulin) for noncardiac general surgery to improve glycemic outcomes
- Hold metformin and other oral glucose-lowering agents on the day of surgery
- Reduce basal insulin dose by 25% the evening before surgery (or 50% for NPH, 75–80% for long-acting analogs) to lower hypoglycemia risk
- Monitor blood glucose at least every 2–4 hours while the individual takes nothing by mouth (NPO) and administer insulin as needed
- Use insulin as the only recommended glucose-lowering medication in the perioperative period

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Elective surgery patients with diabetes or prediabetes
- Assessment of hypoglycemia risk and surgical type to individualize glycemic goals
- Institutional support for insulin pump use during surgery if applicable
- Interprofessional approach for insulin pump management during surgery
- Availability of continuous glucose monitoring (CGM) data for preoperative evaluation
- Consider facility capabilities for diabetes management when discharging individuals not to their home
- Assess surgical stress and counterregulatory hormone release risk for hyperglycemia in major general surgery patients

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Postponing surgery based solely on A1C or glucose management indicator without clinical justification
- Using oral glucose-lowering agents (e.g., metformin) on the day of surgery
- Relying solely on CGM for intraoperative glucose monitoring
- Routine use of correction-only insulin without basal insulin in noncardiac general surgery
- Strict perioperative glycemic goals without individualized risk-to-benefit assessment

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Use of 14-day CGM glucose management indicator <8% or time in range >50% as an alternative to A1C for preoperative glycemic assessment
- Recommendation to maintain blood glucose 100–180 mg/dL (5.6–10.0 mmol/L) throughout the perioperative period
- Inclusion of SGLT2 inhibitor and GLP-1 RA considerations in perioperative glycemic management (with specific holding protocols)
- Development of preoperative optimization programs to lower A1C before elective surgery
- Reduction of basal insulin doses (25% for evening before surgery) to minimize hypoglycemia risk
- Optimal preoperative A1C goal between 7% and 8% associated with better perioperative glycemic management and decreased length of hospital stay
