# T1DM epidemiology and diagnosis
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** T1DM epidemiology, diagnosis, and risk stratification

**Core Recommendations:**
- Increasing incidence and prevalence of type 1 diabetes observed globally.
- Acute symptom presentation with markedly elevated blood glucose levels.
- 25–50% of T1DM cases present with life-threatening diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA).
- Family history increases risk but ~90% of cases occur without known relatives with T1DM.
- Islet autoantibody testing identifies individuals at high risk for progression to T1DM.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Confirmation of first autoantibody-positive test with a second test within 3 months in a laboratory meeting Islet Autoantibody Standardization Program (IASP) standards.
- Screening programs should include counseling on diabetes symptoms, DKA prevention, and referral pathways for autoantibody-positive individuals.
- Teplizumab-mzwv infusion for stage 2 T1DM requires discussion with patients ≥8 years in a setting with trained personnel.
- Genetic risk scores may identify high-risk subsets for targeted autoantibody testing, particularly in populations of European ancestry.
- Screening programs should ensure high-quality assays and resources for follow-up before implementation.
- Confirmation of ≥2 islet autoantibodies (e.g., GAD65, IA-2, insulin, ZnT8) is required to define stage 2 T1DM for teplizumab-mzwv eligibility in individuals ≥8 years.

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Routine reliance on single autoantibody testing without confirmation.
- Fixed A1C targets for T1DM diagnosis without consideration of acute symptom presentation.
- Generalized screening without validated assays or follow-up infrastructure.
- Routine single autoantibody testing without confirmatory retesting within 3 months.

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Nationwide screening programs for T1DM in general population (e.g., Italy’s program for ages 1–17 years).
- Teplizumab-mzwv approved to delay stage 3 T1DM in stage 2 patients ≥8 years, with HLA-DR4 and absence of HLA-DR3/antizinc transporter 8 predicting response.
- Use of genetic risk scores in newborn screening to identify high-risk subsets for autoantibody testing.
- Evidence linking higher glycemic index diets and sugar intake to increased progression risk in autoantibody-positive children.
- Moderate to vigorous physical activity associated with reduced progression risk in children with multiple autoantibodies.
- Presence of HLA-DR4 and absence of HLA-DR3/antizinc transporter 8 (ZnT8) antibodies predict better response to teplizumab-mzwv in stage 2 T1DM.
- Genetic risk scores in newborn screening can identify >77% of future T1DM cases within 10% of the general population, prioritizing autoantibody testing in high-risk subsets.
