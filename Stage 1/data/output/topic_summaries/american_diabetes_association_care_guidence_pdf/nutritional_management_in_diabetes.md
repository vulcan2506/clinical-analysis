# nutritional management in diabetes
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Nutritional management in diabetes

**Core Recommendations:**
- Shift focus from macronutrients/micronutrients to overall eating patterns and nutrient-dense foods (e.g., vegetables, fruits, legumes)
- Encourage individualized meal plans based on cultural preferences, health literacy, and food access
- Promote collaborative development of eating plans with RDNs, emphasizing shared decision-making
- Limit processed foods, added sugars, salt, and saturated fats; prioritize whole foods and plant-based proteins
- Use practical tools like the diabetes plate method or simplified carbohydrate counting for meal planning

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Referral to a registered dietitian nutritionist (RDN) experienced in diabetes care at diagnosis and as needed
- Assessment of patient-specific factors (e.g., comorbidities like CKD, heart failure, or pregnancy-related nutrition concerns)
- Evaluation of food literacy, numeracy, and willingness to make behavioral changes
- Monitoring of medication adjustments in relation to nutrition care plans (e.g., insulin dose modifications)
- Consideration of cultural appropriateness and socioeconomic barriers to healthy eating

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Emphasizing fixed macronutrient ratios (e.g., low-carbohydrate diets as universally optimal)
- Routine use of dietary supplements (e.g., vitamins, minerals, herbal supplements) without deficiency
- One-size-fits-all eating patterns or rigid meal plans
- Low-protein diets for diabetes-related CKD (albuminuria/eGFR reduction)
- Reliance on glycemic index (GI) alone for glycemic management without considering overall eating patterns

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Integration of adaptive closed-loop AID systems that reduce reliance on precise carbohydrate counting
- DASH4D eating pattern (moderate carbs, rich in fruits/vegetables/whole grains, low in saturated fat/sodium) for T2D with hypertension
- Avoidance of very-low-carbohydrate diets in patients on SGLT2 inhibitors due to ketoacidosis risk
- Recommendation to limit sodium to <2,300 mg/day, with stronger emphasis on whole foods to reduce processed food intake
- Focus on plant-based proteins and replacement of animal proteins to improve cardiometabolic outcomes
