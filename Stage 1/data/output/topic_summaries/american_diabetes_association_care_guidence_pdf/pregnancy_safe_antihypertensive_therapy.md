# pregnancy-safe antihypertensive therapy
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** pregnancy-safe antihypertensive therapy

**Core Recommendations:**
- Avoid ACE inhibitors, ARBs, direct renin inhibitors, MRAs, SGLT2 inhibitors, and neprilysin inhibitors in pregnancy due to fetal harm risk
- Switch to pregnancy-safe antihypertensives (e.g., methyldopa, labetalol, long-acting nifedipine) prior to conception if using contraindicated medications
- Use hydralazine only for acute management of hypertension in pregnancy or severe preeclampsia
- Avoid diuretics for routine blood pressure management in pregnancy, except for volume management in late-stage pregnancy
- Monitor blood pressure for 72 hours postpartum and at 7–10 days postpartum in individuals with gestational hypertension, preeclampsia, or superimposed preeclampsia

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Sexually active individuals of childbearing potential not using reliable contraception must avoid kidney-protective medications harmful in pregnancy
- Individuals intending to become pregnant should switch from contraindicated antihypertensives to pregnancy-safe alternatives prior to conception
- Pregnancy-safe antihypertensives (methyldopa, labetalol, long-acting nifedipine) are preferred for chronic management
- Hydralazine is reserved for acute hypertensive crises or severe preeclampsia
- Long-term cardiovascular risk follow-up is recommended for individuals with gestational hypertension, preeclampsia, or superimposed preeclampsia
- Special consideration should be taken for individuals of child-bearing potential

**Previous Recommendations:**
- ACE inhibitors
- ARBs
- Direct renin inhibitors
- MRAs
- Neprilysin inhibitors

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Methyldopa as a first-line pregnancy-safe antihypertensive
- Labetalol as a preferred pregnancy-safe antihypertensive
- Long-acting nifedipine for chronic hypertension management in pregnancy
- Postpartum blood pressure monitoring for 72 hours in hospital and 7–10 days postpartum
- Long-term cardiovascular risk follow-up for individuals with hypertensive disorders of pregnancy
