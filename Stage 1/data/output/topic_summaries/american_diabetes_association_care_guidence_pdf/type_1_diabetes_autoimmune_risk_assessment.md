# type 1 diabetes autoimmune risk assessment
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Type 1 diabetes autoimmune risk assessment and screening

**Core Recommendations:**
- Screen for islet autoantibodies (GAD65, IA-2, IA-2b, ZnT8, insulin autoantibodies) in high-risk individuals (e.g., first-degree relatives of type 1 diabetes patients, those with other autoimmune diseases, or genetic risk factors).
- Monitor metabolic status (random glucose, A1C) at intervals of 6 months to 3 years based on age and risk profile to detect progression to stage 2 or 3 type 1 diabetes.
- Perform HLA typing (DQB1, DRB1 haplotypes) to assess genetic risk, particularly for DR3-DQ2 and DR4-DQ8 alleles (predisposing) or DRB1*1501 and DQA1*0102-DQB1*0602 (protective).
- Screen for autoimmune comorbidities (thyroid dysfunction, celiac disease, pernicious anemia) at diagnosis and periodically, especially in symptomatic individuals or high-risk groups.
- Avoid delays in diagnostic testing for stage 3 type 1 diabetes to prevent DKA, particularly in children and adolescents.
- Screen for celiac disease using serum IgA and tissue transglutaminase (tTG) IgA antibodies (or tTG IgG/IgG deamidated gliadin peptide if IgA deficient) at diagnosis, 2 years later, and 5 years later in type 1 diabetes, or sooner if symptomatic (e.g., poor growth, increased hypoglycemia).
- Repeat thyroid function tests after achieving metabolic stability and glycemic goals if initially performed during acute hyperglycemia, ketosis, or ketoacidosis to avoid misleading results.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Presence of one or more islet autoantibodies for metabolic monitoring and progression risk assessment.
- Family history of type 1 diabetes or other autoimmune diseases for targeted screening.
- Metabolic stability (e.g., post-hyperglycemia resolution) before thyroid function testing to avoid misleading results.
- Symptoms suggestive of autoimmune comorbidities (e.g., gastrointestinal symptoms for celiac disease, fatigue for thyroid dysfunction) for diagnostic workup.
- High-risk genetic profile (e.g., HLA-DR3-DQ2 or DR4-DQ8) for prioritized autoantibody screening.
- Metabolic stability and achievement of glycemic goals before repeating thyroid function tests if initially performed during acute hyperglycemia, ketosis, or ketoacidosis.
- Consider bone mineral density monitoring via dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry in older adults (≥65 years) or younger individuals with multiple risk factors every 2–3 years.

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Routine fixed-interval screening without consideration of age, antibody specificity, or genetic risk.
- Assuming obesity precludes type 1 diabetes diagnosis.
- Relying solely on single autoantibody results without metabolic evaluation.
- Performing thyroid function tests during acute metabolic instability (e.g., DKA) without repeat testing.
- Discontinuing celiac disease screening after 5 years in asymptomatic individuals without risk factors.

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Stage-based classification (Stage 1: multiple autoantibodies + normoglycemia; Stage 2: autoantibodies + dysglycemia; Stage 3: overt hyperglycemia) for risk stratification.
- Semiannual OGTT in high-risk family members to reduce DKA risk at diagnosis.
- Genetic screening (HLA-DQB1/DRB1) to identify high-risk populations for early intervention trials.
- Increased vigilance for SARS-CoV-2 as a potential trigger for type 1 diabetes in susceptible individuals, with ongoing research via registries like CoviDIAB.
- Consideration of vitamin B12 deficiency screening in individuals with peripheral neuropathy or unexplained anemia.
- Genetic testing for HLA-DQ2/DQ8 may not be necessary for celiac disease screening in type 1 diabetes due to near-universal presence in affected individuals.
- Consider osteoporosis drug therapy in older adults with diabetes at increased fracture risk (e.g., T-score ≤−2.5, history of fragility fracture, or elevated FRAX score).
