# A1C test standardization guidelines
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** A1C test standardization and diagnostic use

**Core Recommendations:**
- A1C testing must use NGSP-certified methods traceable to the DCCT reference assay for diagnosis and monitoring.
- Point-of-care A1C testing for diagnosis is restricted to FDA-approved devices in CLIA-certified laboratories with trained personnel.
- Confirmatory testing is required for diagnosis unless classic hyperglycemic symptoms or hyperglycemic crisis are present.
- Discordant glucose and A1C results require evaluation for analytical problems, interferences, or alternative biomarkers (e.g., fructosamine, glycated albumin).
- A1C results near diagnostic thresholds (e.g., 6.5–7.0%) should prompt repeat testing in 3–6 months and patient education.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- NGSP certification or IFCC traceability for A1C assays.
- CLIA certification and annual competency assessments for point-of-care testing personnel.
- Three times per year proficiency testing participation for CLIA-certified sites.
- Confirmation of diagnosis via two abnormal tests (same or different) unless symptomatic hyperglycemia or crisis is present.
- Use of plasma glucose criteria in conditions altering A1C-hemoglobin relationship (e.g., hemoglobin variants, pregnancy, G6PD deficiency).
- Use of plasma glucose criteria for diagnosis in conditions altering the A1C-hemoglobin relationship (e.g., anemia, erythropoietin treatment, hemodialysis, HIV treatment, thalassemia, folate deficiency).
- Requirement for two abnormal test results (same or different tests) for diagnosis unless classic hyperglycemic symptoms or hyperglycemic crisis are present.

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Non-NGSP-certified or non-traceable A1C assays for diagnosis.
- Point-of-care A1C devices not FDA-approved for diagnostic use.
- Reliance on single A1C tests for diagnosis without confirmatory testing.
- Use of race/ancestry as proxies for genetic differences in A1C interpretation.
- A1C assays known to be affected by hemoglobin variants without interference checks.

**Updated Recommendations:**
- IFCC-certified assays as acceptable alternatives to NGSP-certified methods outside the U.S.
- Updated list of A1C assays with hemoglobin variant interferences (available at ngsp.org/interf.asp).
- Recommendation to use alternative biomarkers (fructosamine, glycated albumin) for monitoring in cases of A1C discordance.
- Emphasis on individualized diagnostic confirmation based on test concordance and clinical context.
- AABBCC approach proposed as a tool to aid diabetes type classification.
- Use of alternative validated biomarkers (fructosamine, glycated albumin) for monitoring in cases of discordant glucose and A1C results.
