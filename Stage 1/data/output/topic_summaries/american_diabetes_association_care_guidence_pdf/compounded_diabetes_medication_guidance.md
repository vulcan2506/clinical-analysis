# compounded diabetes medication guidance
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** compounded diabetes medication guidance

**Core Recommendations:**
- Use of compounded products not FDA-approved is not recommended due to safety, quality, and effectiveness concerns.
- Switch to an FDA-approved medication with similar efficacy if a glucose-lowering medication is unavailable (e.g., in shortage).
- Reassess the appropriateness of resuming the original FDA-approved medication upon resolution of unavailability.
- Counsel individuals of childbearing potential on contraception options and the impact of glucose-lowering medications on contraception efficacy.
- Use a person-centered shared decision-making approach for preconception planning addressing glycemic goals and medication adjustments.
- Educate patients on the risks and signs of diabetic ketoacidosis when treated with SGLT inhibitors, including methods of risk mitigation and discouragement of ketogenic eating patterns.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Patient must have a confirmed need for glucose-lowering medication.
- Unavailability of the prescribed medication must be documented (e.g., shortage).
- Patient must be assessed for hyperglycemia risk during immunotherapy or other high-risk treatments.
- Individuals of childbearing potential must be counseled on contraception and glycemic management before pregnancy.
- Post-transplantation patients must have individualized glycemic goals based on organ type and treatment plan.
- Assess and adjust glycemic management based on specific glucocorticoid treatment plans with frequent reassessment of glucose levels.

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Use of compounded diabetes medications not FDA-approved.
- Routine use of non-FDA-approved medications for glucose management.
- Fixed A1C targets without consideration of patient-specific factors in older adults.
- Routine use of noninsulin therapies in postoperative hyperglycemia without preference for insulin.
- Ignoring the impact of glucose-lowering medications on contraception efficacy.

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Switching to FDA-approved alternatives during medication shortages.
- Shared decision-making for preconception planning in individuals of childbearing potential.
- Immediate insulin initiation for immunotherapy-associated hyperglycemia or diabetic ketoacidosis risk.
- Metformin as first-line treatment for hyperglycemia due to mTOR or PI3K inhibitors.
- Use of GLP-1 receptor agonists for long-term glycemic management in post-transplantation diabetes mellitus.
- Consider DPP-4 inhibitors for mild hyperglycemia in postoperative management of adults with posttransplantation diabetes mellitus (PTDM) or preexisting type 2 diabetes.
