# continuous glucose monitoring implementation
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Continuous Glucose Monitoring (CGM) implementation

**Core Recommendations:**
- CGM is recommended at diabetes onset and anytime thereafter for individuals on insulin therapy, noninsulin therapies that can cause hypoglycemia, or any treatment where CGM aids management.
- In people on insulin therapy, CGM devices should be used as close to daily as possible for maximal benefit with uninterrupted access to supplies.
- During pregnancy for individuals with type 1 diabetes, CGM can help achieve glycemic goals and A1C targets.
- People who wear CGM devices should be educated on potential interfering substances and factors affecting accuracy.
- Skin reactions from CGM devices should be assessed and addressed to aid successful use.
- Healthcare professionals should monitor for signs of overbasalization during insulin therapy, such as significant bedtime-to-morning or postprandial-to-preprandial glucose differential, occurrences of hypoglycemia (aware or unaware), and high glycemic variability.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Individuals must be on insulin therapy, noninsulin therapies that can cause hypoglycemia, or any diabetes treatment where CGM aids management.
- For pregnancy with type 1 diabetes, CGM is recommended to achieve glycemic goals.
- Healthcare professionals must be aware of factors impairing CGM accuracy, such as oxygen tension, temperature, and interfering substances.
- Individuals should have uninterrupted access to CGM supplies for consistent use.
- Education on CGM use and potential interfering substances is required for successful implementation.
- Glucagon must be prescribed for all individuals taking insulin or at high risk for hypoglycemia, with preferred non-reconstitution preparations.

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Historically, CGM devices were categorized as real-time CGM (rtCGM) or intermittently scanned CGM (isCGM), but modern devices often combine features.
- Unmodified glucose dehydrogenase pyrroloquinoline quinone (GDH/PQQ) enzyme methods for glucose monitoring are no longer recommended due to interference risks.

**Updated Recommendations:**
- CGM is now recommended for broader indications, including noninsulin therapies that can cause hypoglycemia and any treatment where CGM aids management.
- Modern CGM devices typically require no swiping and have similar characteristics across brands.
- Glucagon should be prescribed for all individuals taking insulin or at high risk for hypoglycemia, with preferred non-reconstitution preparations.
- Routine assessment for financial obstacles to diabetes management is emphasized to improve access to CGM and other evidence-based care.
- Lower-cost medications (e.g., metformin, sulfonylureas, thiazolidinediones, human insulin) should be considered for adults with cost-related barriers.
- CGM measures interstitial glucose, which correlates well with plasma glucose but may lag during rapid glucose changes (e.g., rising or falling levels).
- Healthy behaviors, diabetes self-management education and support, avoidance of therapeutic inertia, and consideration of social determinants of health are essential components of glucose-lowering management in diabetes.
