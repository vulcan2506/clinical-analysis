# diabetes-periodontal disease coordination
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Diabetes-periodontal disease coordination + Disability assessment in diabetes

**Core Recommendations:**
- Coordinate medical and dental teams to adjust glucose-lowering medications before and after dental procedures, especially for patients on insulin, sulfonylureas, or meglitinides.
- Include dental professionals in the diabetes care team using a collaborative interprofessional approach.
- Assess patients with diabetes for disability at initial visit and monitor for functional decline at subsequent visits.
- Conduct psychosocial assessments to screen for depression, anxiety, and social support issues in patients with diabetes-related disabilities.
- Perform functional capacity evaluations and use standardized disability questionnaires (e.g., WHODAS 2.0, Diabetes Distress Scale) to measure disability and emotional burden.
- Conduct a dental history and oral examination as part of the clinical assessment for people with diabetes.
- Ensure dental professionals are informed about hepatic, cardiovascular, kidney, and pulmonary conditions to assist in appropriate dosing of antibiotics.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Patient must have diabetes with confirmed periodontal disease or risk factors for periodontal disease.
- Dental professionals must be informed about the patient’s glycemic goals, medications, and comorbid conditions.
- Blood glucose monitoring equipment, carbohydrates, and glucagon must be available during dental procedures for patients at risk of hypoglycemia.
- Patient must consent to interprofessional coordination between medical and dental teams.
- Disability assessment requires evaluation of activities of daily living (ADLs) and instrumental activities of daily living (IADLs).
- Dental professionals must be aware of high A1C levels in patients, as this population may have lower oral healing capacity.
- Dental professionals must know about comorbidities including hepatic, cardiovascular, kidney, and pulmonary conditions.

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Routine use of fixed A1C targets without considering periodontal disease severity or dental procedure risks.
- Excluding dental professionals from the diabetes care team.
- Assuming all patients with diabetes have equal access to dental care without addressing barriers.
- Ignoring the bidirectional relationship between periodontal disease and diabetes in treatment planning.
- Not adjusting glucose-lowering medications prior to invasive dental procedures.

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Mandatory coordination between medical and dental teams to adjust diabetes medications before and after dental procedures.
- Inclusion of dental professionals in the diabetes care team for collaborative interprofessional management.
- Standardized disability assessments at initial and follow-up visits for patients with diabetes.
- Use of functional capacity evaluations and standardized disability questionnaires (e.g., WHODAS 2.0) to guide rehabilitation referrals.
- Emphasis on addressing social determinants of health in patients with diabetes-related disabilities.
- Inclusion of dental health professionals in the diabetes care team using a collaborative interprofessional care approach.
- Emphasis on early detection of oral health problems by clinicians to promote prompt referral and mitigate extensive procedures.
