# lifestyle intervention for diabetes remission
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Lifestyle intervention for diabetes remission

**Core Recommendations:**
- Create a 500–750 kcal/day energy deficit via reduced energy intake and increased physical activity
- Achieve and maintain ≥5% weight loss to improve glycemia, lipids, and blood pressure
- Engage in ≥16 intensive behavioral sessions over 6 months focusing on durable nutritional changes and physical activity
- Monitor and adjust nutrition intake to prevent micronutrient deficiencies, especially with very-low-calorie diets
- Encourage 150–300 minutes/week of physical activity for overall health and glycemic management
- Replace ultraprocessed foods with less processed alternatives to improve weight loss outcomes (e.g., trunk fat reduction)

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Patients with type 2 diabetes and overweight/obesity eligible for intensive lifestyle intervention
- Trained professionals to deliver interventions (face-to-face or remote, individual or group)
- Medical supervision for very-low-calorie interventions (800–1,000 kcal/day)
- Assessment of motivation, life circumstances, cultural considerations, and socioeconomic factors
- Long-term (≥1 year) weight maintenance programs with monthly contact and self-monitoring
- Assess systemic, structural, and socioeconomic factors impacting food access, physical activity opportunities, and diabetes risk before initiating lifestyle interventions

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Fixed numeric A1C targets without individualized consideration
- Reliance on nutrition supplements (herbs, vitamins, minerals, etc.) for weight loss
- Low-carbohydrate diets (>50g–150g/day) as superior to balanced carbohydrate diets for glycemic control
- Unsupervised very-low-calorie interventions without medical monitoring
- Commercial weight loss programs lacking evidence-based practices
- Reliance on nutrition supplements (e.g., herbs, vitamins, minerals, amino acids) for obesity management or weight loss due to lack of clear evidence and high risk of bias in trials

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Culturally tailored behavioral interventions to improve intervention impact
- Very-low-calorie structured eating patterns (800–1,000 kcal/day) for rapid initial weight loss under medical supervision
- Long-term comprehensive weight maintenance programs (≥1 year) with interprofessional teams
- Micronutrient screening and supplementation for individuals consuming <1,200 kcal/day or with rapid weight loss
- Emphasis on protein optimization and resistance training to preserve lean mass during weight loss
- Screen for disordered eating behaviors during diabetes self-management education, medical nutrition therapy, and routine medical visits
- Prophylactic or corrective micronutrient supplementation for individuals undergoing metabolic surgery with documented deficiencies
