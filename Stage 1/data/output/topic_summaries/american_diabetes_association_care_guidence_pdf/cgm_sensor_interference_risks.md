# CGM sensor interference risks
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** CGM sensor interference risks from medications/substances

**Core Recommendations:**
- Routinely review medications and supplements to identify potential interfering substances with CGM sensors.
- Advise patients on the need to use additional blood glucose monitoring (BGM) if sensor values are unreliable due to identified interfering substances.
- Recognize that CGM sensor interference can cause temporary measurement errors, including falsely elevated glucose readings.
- Document and report suspected CGM sensor interference events to the device manufacturer and relevant regulatory bodies to contribute to post-market surveillance data.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Patient must be using a CGM device for glucose monitoring.
- Healthcare provider must assess patient's medication and supplement list for known interfering substances.
- Healthcare providers must verify the specific CGM device model in use, as interference risks may vary by manufacturer and sensor generation.

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Hydroxyurea identified as a newly recognized interfering substance causing temporary increases in sensor glucose values.
- Mannitol and sorbitol (when administered intravenously or in peritoneal dialysis) identified as substances causing falsely elevated CGM readings.
- Acetaminophen (paracetamol) in high doses (>1000 mg) identified as a potential interfering substance in some CGM systems, causing falsely elevated glucose readings in certain models.
