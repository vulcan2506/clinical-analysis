# insulin therapy in older adults
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Insulin therapy in older adults

**Core Recommendations:**
- Insulin therapy requires good visual, motor, cognitive abilities for dosing, administration, and hypoglycemia management
- Insulin doses and plans should be individualized to glycemic goals and hypoglycemia avoidance
- Age alone does not limit technology use; CGM or automated insulin delivery systems should be encouraged if safe and effective
- Once-daily basal insulin is preferred over multiple daily injections in many older adults
- Insulin pens should be preferred over syringes for older adults with functional impairment if affordable
- Simplification of insulin regimens should follow an algorithmic approach to match self-management abilities and support needs
- Shared decision-making should include care partners and social support networks in diabetes management discussions

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Good visual, motor, and cognitive abilities for insulin management or caregiver support
- Individualized glycemic goals based on patient-specific factors
- Assessment of social support and living situation for tailored care planning
- Evaluation of functional status and coexisting chronic illnesses
- Consideration of affordability for insulin pens
- Evaluation of living situation (e.g., assisted living, nursing home, home) to determine medication administration support needs
- Assessment of access to long-term services and support to ensure continuity of care

**Previous Recommendations:**
- NPH insulin for older adults due to higher risk of hypoglycemia-related emergency visits
- Multiple daily insulin injections for older adults with advanced complications or limited support
- Rigid, complex insulin regimens without simplification
- Ignoring social support and living situation in care planning
- Strict metabolic management in palliative care without symptom prioritization

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Encouragement of CGM or automated insulin delivery systems in capable older adults
- Preference for once-daily basal insulin over multiple daily injections
- Simplification of insulin plans to match self-management abilities and support
- Inclusion of care partners and social support networks in diabetes management
- Tailored care plans considering transitions to PALTC and palliative care goals
- Use of algorithmic approaches (e.g., Figure 13.3) to guide insulin regimen simplification in older adults
- Inclusion of care partners in shared decision-making for insulin therapy and glycemic goals
