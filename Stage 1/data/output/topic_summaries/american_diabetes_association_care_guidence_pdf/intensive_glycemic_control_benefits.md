# intensive glycemic control benefits
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Intensive glycemic control benefits and individualized A1C targets

**Core Recommendations:**
- Intensive glycemic control (mean A1C ~7%) reduces microvascular complications (retinopathy, neuropathy, chronic kidney disease) by 50–76% in type 1 diabetes (DCCT/EDIC).
- Early glycemic lowering in type 2 diabetes reduces microvascular complications, with long-term benefits persisting despite diminished glycemic separation (UKPDS, Kumamoto).
- A curvilinear relationship exists between A1C and microvascular complications; greatest benefits occur when lowering from very high to moderate A1C levels.
- Further A1C reduction from 7% to 6% yields smaller absolute risk reductions but increases hypoglycemia risk with hypoglycemia-prone medications (e.g., insulin, sulfonylureas).
- CGM and automated insulin delivery systems improve glycemic control without increasing hypoglycemia, enabling safer achievement of lower A1C goals.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Patient must have type 1 or type 2 diabetes with sufficient life expectancy to derive long-term benefits from glycemic lowering.
- Low hypoglycemia risk or use of non-hypoglycemic medications (e.g., metformin, GLP-1 RAs, SGLT2 inhibitors).
- Individualized A1C goals based on duration of diabetes, age, life expectancy, comorbid conditions, and hypoglycemia risk (Fig. 6.1).
- Shared decision-making incorporating patient preferences, resources, and support systems.
- Postprandial glucose monitoring if A1C goals are unmet despite preprandial glucose targets.
- Individuals must have sufficient life expectancy to derive long-term benefit from glucose lowering.
- Goals must be individualized based on known cardiovascular disease or advanced microvascular complications.

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Near-normalization of A1C (e.g., <6%) in long-standing type 2 diabetes using high-risk hypoglycemic medications (e.g., insulin, sulfonylureas) without cardio-kidney-metabolic benefits.
- Fixed numeric A1C targets without consideration of patient-specific factors (e.g., life expectancy, hypoglycemia risk).
- Intensive glycemic control in older adults with complex/intermediate or very complex/poor health without individualized goal adjustment.
- Reliance on pre-DCCT/UKPDS glycemic management strategies (e.g., insulin monotherapy without CGM or automated systems).
- Postprandial glucose monitoring timing not aligned with meal-induced peak levels (1–2 hours post-meal).
- Treating diabetes to near-normal A1C goals in people with long-standing type 2 diabetes using medications with a high risk for hypoglycemia and with other adverse effects and no additional cardio-kidney-metabolic benefits.

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Use of CGM and automated insulin delivery systems to achieve glycemic goals with reduced hypoglycemia risk.
- Prioritization of non-hypoglycemic medication classes (e.g., GLP-1 RAs, SGLT2 inhibitors) for intensive glycemic control.
- Individualized A1C targets favoring more stringent goals in patients with short diabetes duration, low hypoglycemia risk, and long life expectancy.
- Emphasis on early glycemic lowering in type 2 diabetes to maximize long-term microvascular benefits.
- Shared decision-making frameworks for glycemic goal setting, incorporating patient preferences and support systems.
- Use of dual GIP and GLP-1 RA as a glucose-lowering medication class unlikely to cause hypoglycemia.
