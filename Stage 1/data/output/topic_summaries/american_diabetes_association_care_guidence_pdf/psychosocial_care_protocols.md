# psychosocial care protocols
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Psychosocial care protocols in diabetes

**Core Recommendations:**
- Provide routine psychosocial care using a collaborative, person-centered, culturally informed approach by trained healthcare professionals.
- Implement annual or change-triggered screening for psychosocial concerns using age-appropriate, standardized, and validated tools.
- Integrate brief, person-centered psychosocial interventions into routine care (e.g., motivational interviewing, CBT techniques, problem-solving, emotion regulation strategies).
- Refer to behavioral health professionals with diabetes expertise for further assessment and treatment when psychosocial concerns are identified.
- Monitor and address SDOH, diabetes distress, suicidality, and family/caregiver psychosocial concerns as part of routine care.
- Health care professionals can integrate brief, person-centered psychosocial interventions by creating a supportive environment for emotional disclosure, validating experiences, and employing empathetic listening.
- Practical examples of interventions include using 'importance' and 'confidence' rulers, conducting week-long behavioral experiments, demonstrating breathing exercises for mindfulness, setting SMART goals, and teaching grounding techniques.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Screening should occur at diagnosis, routine visits, hospitalizations, transitions in care, or when glycemic goals or self-management are not met.
- Screening tools must be age-appropriate, standardized, and validated (e.g., diabetes distress, fear of hypoglycemia, depression, anxiety).
- Referrals require access to behavioral health professionals with specialized diabetes training or embedded within diabetes care teams.
- Psychosocial interventions should be tailored to patient-specific factors (e.g., life expectancy, cognitive function, cultural background).
- SDOH screening should be incorporated to identify barriers to self-management (e.g., financial, social, emotional resources).
- Screening for suicidality may be appropriate given elevated rates of suicidality among people with diabetes.
- When caregivers or family members play a significant role in diabetes care, their psychosocial concerns should be assessed and addressed by appropriate professionals.

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Routine reliance on informal verbal inquiries without standardized screening tools.
- Waiting for overt psychological deterioration before addressing psychosocial concerns.
- Excluding family/caregiver psychosocial assessments in diabetes care plans.
- Using non-validated or non-age-appropriate psychosocial screening methods.
- Limiting psychosocial care to crisis intervention rather than proactive, routine monitoring.

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Mandatory annual psychosocial screening with validated tools (e.g., diabetes distress, suicidality) for all people with diabetes.
- Integration of brief CBT and mindfulness-based interventions into routine diabetes care.
- Embedding behavioral health professionals within diabetes care teams for collaborative care.
- Digital health platforms for delivering psychosocial interventions (e.g., telehealth, group-based appointments).
- Preventive psychosocial interventions (e.g., resilience training, behavioral family interventions) even in absence of positive screeners.
- Preventive psychosocial interventions (e.g., resilience-promoting interventions, behavioral family interventions) are indicated even in the absence of positive psychosocial screeners.
- Psychosocial interventions can be delivered via digital health platforms or integrated into group-based or shared diabetes appointments.
