# gestational diabetes mellitus definition
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** GDM definition and diagnostic strategy

**Core Recommendations:**
- GDM is defined as any degree of glucose intolerance first recognized during pregnancy, though this includes preexisting undiagnosed diabetes.
- Diagnosis can be accomplished using either the one-step 75-g OGTT (IADPSG) or the two-step approach (50-g screen followed by 100-g OGTT).
- The one-step strategy identifies more cases of GDM and is based on pregnancy outcomes, while the two-step approach is supported by ACOG and the NIH consensus panel.
- Universal early screening at <15 weeks of gestation for undiagnosed diabetes is considered over selective screening in high-risk populations.
- Individuals diagnosed with GDM should receive lifelong screening for prediabetes and type 2 diabetes after delivery.
- Early abnormal glucose metabolism in pregnancy is defined as fasting glucose ≥110 mg/dL (6.1 mmol/L) or A1C ≥5.9% (41 mmol/mol), which identifies individuals at higher risk of adverse pregnancy outcomes and later GDM diagnosis.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Individuals must not have been previously diagnosed with diabetes prior to the screening period.
- For the one-step strategy, the 75-g OGTT must be performed at 24–28 weeks of gestation after an overnight fast of at least 8 hours.
- For the two-step strategy, the initial 50-g GLT is nonfasting and performed at 24–28 weeks of gestation.
- The 100-g OGTT in the two-step approach must be performed when the individual is fasting.
- Diagnostic criteria for undiagnosed diabetes in early pregnancy are the same as those used in nonpregnant individuals.
- The 75-g OGTT for the one-step strategy must be performed in the morning after an overnight fast of at least 8 hours.
- The 100-g OGTT for the two-step strategy must be performed when the individual is fasting.

**Previous Recommendations:**
- The definition of GDM as any degree of glucose intolerance regardless of hyperglycemia severity without distinguishing preexisting diabetes.
- Selective screening approaches in favor of universal early screening for undiagnosed diabetes in high-risk populations.
- Use of A1C at 24–28 weeks of gestation as a primary screening test for GDM due to inferior performance compared to GLT.
- The older O'Sullivan and Mahan criteria without conversion to Carpenter-Coustan or National Diabetes Data Group thresholds.
- Relying solely on two-step diagnostic criteria without considering the potential benefits of the one-step IADPSG criteria for identifying long-term risks.
- Use of A1C thresholds of 5.7% (39 mmol/mol) for early pregnancy risk stratification due to inconsistent association with adverse perinatal outcomes.

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Adoption of the IADPSG one-step 75-g OGTT criteria internationally as the preferred approach for GDM diagnosis.
- Recommendation for universal early screening at <15 weeks of gestation to identify undiagnosed diabetes in high-risk populations.
- Use of A1C thresholds of 5.9% (41 mmol/mol) or fasting glucose of 110 mg/dL (6.1 mmol/L) in early pregnancy to identify individuals at higher risk of adverse outcomes.
- Implementation of lifelong screening for prediabetes and type 2 diabetes for individuals diagnosed with GDM postpartum.
- Recognition that the one-step strategy identifies long-term risks of maternal prediabetes, diabetes, and offspring glucose intolerance and adiposity.
- Adoption of the IADPSG one-step 75-g OGTT criteria as the preferred international approach for GDM diagnosis based on pregnancy outcome data from the HAPO study.
- Recommendation for universal early screening at <15 weeks of gestation to identify undiagnosed diabetes in high-risk populations, addressing racial and ethnic disparities in undiagnosed diabetes prevalence.
