# postpartum diabetes screening protocol
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Postpartum diabetes screening protocol for individuals with GDM

**Core Recommendations:**
- Test for persistent diabetes or prediabetes at 4–12 weeks postpartum using a fasting 75-g OGTT with nonpregnancy criteria.
- Use OGTT over A1C at 4–12 weeks postpartum due to potential interference from pregnancy-related factors.
- Consider A1C at 6–12 months postpartum only if OGTT is declined or intolerable.
- Conduct ongoing screening for prediabetes or type 2 diabetes every 1–3 years after initial postpartum testing.
- Monitor individuals with a history of GDM for lifetime increased risk of diabetes (50–60%) and higher type 2 diabetes risk.
- Consider A1C at 6–12 months postpartum only if OGTT is declined or intolerable, with sensitivity of 80% and specificity of 80.8% for detecting 2-h plasma glucose ≥200 mg/dL (≥11.1 mmol/L)

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- History of gestational diabetes mellitus (GDM).
- Nonpregnancy criteria for OGTT interpretation.
- Patient tolerance and willingness to undergo OGTT.
- Consideration of A1C only if OGTT is declined or intolerable.
- Baseline assessment of BMI and lifestyle factors for risk stratification.
- Baseline assessment of prepregnancy BMI and inter-pregnancy weight changes for risk stratification
- Evaluation of adherence to healthy eating patterns as a modifier of diabetes risk

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Routine use of A1C at 4–12 weeks postpartum due to reduced sensitivity from pregnancy-related factors.
- OGTT testing immediately postpartum (during hospitalization) due to variably reduced sensitivity.
- Fixed numeric A1C thresholds without consideration of pregnancy-related confounders.

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Emphasis on OGTT at 4–12 weeks postpartum as the preferred screening method.
- Recommendation for ongoing screening every 1–3 years post-delivery.
- Integration of lifestyle interventions and metformin for diabetes prevention in high-risk individuals.
- Recognition of inter-pregnancy weight management as a critical risk modifier.
- Lifetime risk assessment for diabetes in individuals with a history of GDM.
- Recognition that inter-pregnancy weight gain increases risk of adverse pregnancy outcomes and subsequent type 2 diabetes
- Emphasis on postdelivery lifestyle interventions and metformin for diabetes prevention in individuals with prediabetes and a history of GDM
