# diabetes-specific bone health screening
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Diabetes-specific bone health screening and fracture risk management

**Core Recommendations:**
- Perform BMD testing in individuals aged ≥65 years
- Perform BMD testing in postmenopausal women and men aged ≥50 years with diabetes-specific risk factors
- Consider individual fracture risk and medication effects on bone metabolism in type 2 diabetes care plans
- Avoid aggressive therapeutic approaches in frail older adults to prevent hypoglycemic events and falls
- Monitor and manage diabetes complications (e.g., neuropathy, retinopathy, nephropathy) that increase fracture risk
- Consider hip fracture risk assessment in individuals with diabetes, given their higher mortality and delayed recovery post-fracture compared to non-diabetic peers

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Age ≥65 years
- Postmenopausal women or men aged ≥50 years with history of adult-age fracture
- Diabetes duration >10 years
- Presence of diabetes-specific risk factors (e.g., frequent hypoglycemia, neuropathy, retinopathy, nephropathy, frequent falls, glucocorticoid use)
- Type 2 diabetes diagnosis for medication-specific risk assessment
- Glucocorticoid use: prednisone at doses >2.5 mg per day for ≥3 months

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Routine use of thiazolidinediones (TZDs) in patients at high fracture risk due to doubled fracture incidence
- Long-term sulfonylurea use in patients with high hypoglycemia risk
- Unmonitored insulin therapy in older adults or those with frequent hypoglycemia
- Ignoring fracture risk when selecting glucose-lowering medications
- Assuming all glucose-lowering medications have neutral effects on bone health
- Routine use of sulfonylureas in patients with high hypoglycemia risk due to increased fracture risk (RR 1.30 [95% CI 1.18–1.43])

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Prefer glucose-lowering medications with a good bone safety profile (e.g., metformin, DPP-4 inhibitors, GLP-1 RAs at low doses)
- Discontinue thiazolidinediones in patients at high fracture risk due to reversible fracture risk reduction after discontinuation
- Monitor fracture risk in patients on SGLT2 inhibitors, though overall evidence suggests neutral effects
- Incorporate fall prevention strategies in patients with frequent falls or neuropathy
- Individualize care plans for older adults or frail patients to minimize hypoglycemia and fall risks
- Prefer GLP-1 RAs at low doses over higher doses due to potential counteractive skeletal risks from rapid weight loss
- Monitor fracture risk in patients on insulin therapy, as it is associated with a doubling of hip fracture risk
