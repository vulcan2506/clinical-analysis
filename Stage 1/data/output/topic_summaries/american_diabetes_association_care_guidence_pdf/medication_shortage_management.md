# medication shortage management
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** medication shortage management in diabetes care

**Core Recommendations:**
- Use FDA Drug Shortages Database to identify medications in shortage
- Avoid use of non-FDA-approved compounded products due to safety and efficacy concerns
- Switch to a different FDA-approved medication when available during shortages
- Reassess patients to determine appropriateness of resuming original medication once supply is restored
- Report adverse events or safety concerns related to compounded or alternative medications to FDA MedWatch or other pharmacovigilance systems

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Medication must be listed on the FDA Drug Shortages Database to qualify for compounding or alternative strategies
- Patient-specific clinical appropriateness must guide switching decisions
- Pharmacy compounding must comply with regulatory standards for safety and quality
- Compounding pharmacies must be registered with the FDA and comply with current good manufacturing practices (cGMP) for outsourcing facilities during shortages

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Use of non-FDA-approved compounded incretin products (e.g., GLP-1 RAs, dual GIP/GLP-1 RAs)
- Incorporation of untested additional ingredients (e.g., vitamin B12, vitamin B6) into compounded products
- Use of salt forms of active ingredients not proven safe or effective for human use in compounded products
- Administration of compounded products in nonstandard concentrations, multidose vials, or prefilled syringes without proper education or labeling

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Switching to alternative FDA-approved medications when shortages occur
- Reassessment of patients after shortage resolution to determine resumption of original therapy
- Use of FDA-approved compounding facilities for copies of marketed products during shortages
- Adherence to FDA guidance for compounding during drug shortages to ensure safety and efficacy
- FDA determination of 'critical supply gap' as a prerequisite for allowing compounding of marketed product copies during shortages
