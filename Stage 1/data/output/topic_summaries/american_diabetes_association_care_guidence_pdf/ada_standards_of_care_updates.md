# ADA Standards of Care updates
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** ADA Standards of Care 2026: Person-centered, evidence-based diabetes care

**Core Recommendations:**
- Annual updates to Standards of Care incorporate person-first and inclusive language, emphasizing shared decision-making based on individual values, preferences, prognoses, comorbidities, and informed financial considerations.
- Health systems adopt continuous quality improvement with benchmarking programs and interprofessional team engagement to improve care quality and outcomes.
- Digital self-management tools, community health workers, and telehealth are integrated to support diabetes management, particularly in underserved communities.
- Comprehensive medical evaluations include assessment of social determinants of health, structural barriers, and individualized care plans with ongoing reassessment.
- Obesity management is prioritized as a primary treatment goal alongside glycemic control, with weight loss targets of 5–7% for intermediate benefits and >10% for sustained remission and cardiovascular benefits.
- Minor updates to figures and tables to align with current accessibility standards.
- Health systems should facilitate in-person and virtual team-based care, include those knowledgeable and experienced in diabetes management as part of the team, and utilize patient registries, decision support tools, proactive care planning, and community involvement to meet needs of individuals with diabetes.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Diagnosis confirmation requires two abnormal results from different tests (e.g., A1C and FPG) or the same test at two different time points in the absence of unequivocal hyperglycemia.
- Shared decision-making must incorporate patient-specific factors including life expectancy, cognitive function, hypoglycemia risk, and social determinants of health.
- Comprehensive medical evaluation includes confirmation of diabetes diagnosis, assessment of glycemic status, evaluation for complications, and identification of care partners and resources.
- Weight management interventions require individualized approaches based on medical history, life circumstances, and preferences, with accommodations for privacy and nonjudgmental communication.
- Hospitalized individuals with diabetes require A1C testing at admission if no result is available from the prior 3 months and structured order sets for glycemic management.
- In people with prediabetes, monitor for the development of diabetes at least annually; modify frequency of testing based on individual risk assessment.
- In people with presymptomatic type 1 diabetes, monitor for disease progression using A1C approximately every 6 months and 75-g oral glucose tolerance test (i.e., fasting and 2-h plasma glucose) annually; modify frequency of monitoring and consider augmenting with other glycemic assessment tools such as continuous glucose monitoring metrics based on individual risk assessment incorporating age, number and type of autoantibodies, and glycemic metrics.

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Fixed numeric A1C targets for older adults are no longer recommended; individualized targets based on patient-specific factors are emphasized.
- Non-judgmental language replaces terms like 'noncompliance' or 'nonadherence' to avoid blaming patients for suboptimal self-management outcomes.
- Population-based screening programs for type 1 diabetes have not been established as cost-effective in the U.S., though they are implemented outside the country.
- Continuous glucose monitoring (CGM) is not currently recommended for screening or diagnosis of prediabetes or diabetes due to insufficient evidence.
- Structured, very-low-calorie meal interventions (800–1,000 kcal/day) should only be prescribed by trained practitioners in medical settings with close monitoring.

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Recommendations emphasize the role of community health workers in supporting management of kidney disease risk factors, diabetes, and cardiovascular disease risk in underserved communities.
- New guidance includes monitoring postprandial or random plasma glucose in individuals on long-term glucocorticoid therapy and counseling on hyperglycemia risk with immune checkpoint inhibitors and PI3Kα inhibitors.
- Annual oral glucose tolerance test (OGTT) starting at age 10 is the preferred screening test for cystic fibrosis-related diabetes when feasible, with A1C as an alternative two-step strategy.
- SGLT inhibitors and GLP-1 receptor agonists are now considered fundamental elements of cardiovascular and kidney risk reduction in type 2 diabetes due to demonstrated benefits.
- Recommendation 2.8 was divided into two components to emphasize the importance of prompt evaluation for stage 3, overt type 1 diabetes in people with one or more islet autoantibodies.
- Recommendation 2.9 was added to highlight that people with a confirmed single IA-2 autoantibody should be monitored similarly to people with stage 2 type 1 diabetes but negative for IA-2 autoantibodies, as they have a comparable risk of progression to stage 3.
- Recommendation 2.18 was added to reinforce monitoring of postprandial or random plasma glucose in the setting of recurrent or long-term treatment with glucocorticoids.
