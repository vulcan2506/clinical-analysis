# tobacco cessation counseling
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Tobacco and Cannabis Cessation Counseling

**Core Recommendations:**
- Ask people with diabetes routinely about the use of tobacco or vape products at every visit
- Advise complete avoidance of tobacco, vaping, and recreational cannabis in any form
- Provide or refer for combination treatment consisting of cessation counseling and pharmacologic therapy for users
- Consider hyperglycemic ketosis cannabis hyperemesis syndrome in type 1 diabetes patients with specific lab criteria
- Utilize diabetes education programs to systematically deliver cessation interventions
- Advise against using smokeless tobacco products (e.g., dip, chew) due to increased cardiovascular disease and oral cancer risks

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Routine assessment of all types of tobacco use at every visit with every person with diabetes
- Presence of elevated A1C, macrovascular complications, or microvascular complications in smokers
- Diagnosis of type 1 diabetes for specific cannabis-related hyperglycemic ketosis risk assessment
- Patient presentation of symptoms such as severe nausea, abdominal pain, and vomiting in cannabis users
- Laboratory confirmation of blood glucose ≥250 mg/dL, anion gap >10, and serum β-hydroxybutyrate >0.6 mmol/L
- Consider cannabis hyperemesis syndrome in type 1 diabetes patients with pH ≥7.4 and bicarbonate >15 mmol/L in the presence of ketosis

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Perception that e-cigarettes are less harmful than combustible cigarettes
- Use of e-cigarettes as a primary cessation strategy without concurrent avoidance of combustible cigarettes
- Fixed numeric A1C targets for older adults without individualization based on life expectancy and hypoglycemia risk
- Reliance on unregulated CBD products for diabetes management without monitoring for psychoactive contaminants
- Failure to provide evidence-based pharmacologic therapy to more than two-thirds of patients attempting to quit
- Perception that smokeless tobacco products are safer than combustible cigarettes

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Recommendation of varenicline as first-line pharmacotherapy for tobacco dependence in adults with diabetes
- Emerging research suggesting GLP-1 receptor agonists may improve smoking cessation and reduce postcessation weight gain
- Specific diagnostic criteria for hyperglycemic ketosis cannabis hyperemesis syndrome including pH ≥7.4 and bicarbonate ≥15 mmol/L
- Integration of cessation interventions into diabetes education clinics to increase quit rates and long-term abstinence
- Advisory against using both combustible and electronic cigarettes simultaneously or using e-cigarettes as a recreational drug
- Recommendation to avoid delta-8 THC and other psychoactive cannabinoids due to unregulated formulations and adverse effects
- Consideration of fluoxetine as a potential intervention to prevent postcessation weight gain
