# preconception diabetes care framework
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Preconception diabetes care framework

**Core Recommendations:**
- Incorporate preconception counseling into routine diabetes care starting at puberty for individuals with diabetes and childbearing potential.
- Discuss family planning and prescribe effective contraception (including long-acting reversible contraception) until glycemic goals (A1C <6.5%) and treatment plans are optimized for pregnancy.
- Achieve preconception glycemic goals (A1C <6.5%, fasting glucose 80–110 mg/dL, 2-hour postprandial <155 mg/dL) without excessive hypoglycemia, using therapies approved for pregnancy.
- Discontinue GLP-1 receptor agonists and dual GIP/GLP-1 receptor agonists prior to pregnancy and achieve glycemic goals with insulin therapy before attempting conception.
- Provide interprofessional preconception care including endocrinology, maternal-fetal medicine, dietitians, and diabetes educators, with comprehensive screening for comorbidities and complications.
- Incorporate weight management counseling and lifestyle interventions (including referral to a registered dietitian nutritionist) into preconception care, with specific guidance on achieving ≥5% weight loss prior to pregnancy for individuals with overweight/obesity and a history of GDM.
- Include comprehensive nutrition assessment and recommendations (e.g., meal planning, correction of nutritional deficiencies, caffeine intake, safe food preparation) as part of preconception education.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Individuals must have diabetes (type 1, type 2, or history of GDM) and childbearing potential.
- Glycemic goals (A1C <6.5%) must be achieved prior to conception using pregnancy-approved therapies.
- Contraception must be used until glycemic goals are met and pregnancy is planned.
- Medication review and adjustments are required to avoid teratogenic drugs (e.g., ACE inhibitors, statins) before pregnancy.
- Comprehensive preconception evaluation including eye exams, renal function, lipid panel, and infectious disease screening must be completed.
- Individuals with prediabetes or a history of GDM must undergo preconception screening for diabetes or prediabetes prior to conception; if not screened preconception, testing should occur before 15 weeks of gestation.
- If type 2 diabetes is diagnosed preconception, initiate treatment with a goal to achieve and maintain an A1C of <6.5% (<48 mmol/mol) prior to conception using therapies approved for use in pregnancy.

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Use of GLP-1 receptor agonists or dual GIP/GLP-1 receptor agonists during pregnancy.
- Reliance on estimated A1C or glucose management indicator calculations during pregnancy.
- Fixed numeric A1C targets without consideration of hypoglycemia risk in older adults or pregnancy.
- Postpartum OGTT replacement with A1C alone in all cases without clinical justification.
- Unplanned pregnancy management without prior preconception optimization.
- Use of estimated A1C or glucose management indicator calculations during pregnancy (explicitly stated as not to be used in pregnancy).

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Updated preconception glucose goals (fasting 80–110 mg/dL, 2-hour postprandial <155 mg/dL) to guide therapeutic adjustments.
- Recommendation to discontinue GLP-1 RA and dual GIP/GLP-1 RA therapy prior to pregnancy with specific timing guidance (e.g., semaglutide 2 months prior).
- Consolidation of CGM and AID use guidance in pregnancy into a single section for clarity.
- Blood pressure threshold of 140/90 mmHg for initiation or titration of antihypertensive therapy in pregnancy.
- Consideration of A1C at 6–12 months postpartum as supplementary diagnostic information only when OGTT is declined or intolerable.
- Recommendation to achieve ≥5% weight loss prior to pregnancy for individuals with overweight/obesity and a history of GDM, as it is associated with lower risk of GDM recurrence (OR 0.18, 95% CI 0.04–0.88).
