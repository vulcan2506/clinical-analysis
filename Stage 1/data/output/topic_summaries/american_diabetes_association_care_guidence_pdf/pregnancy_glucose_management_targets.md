# pregnancy glucose management targets
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Pregnancy glucose management targets

**Core Recommendations:**
- A1C targets in pregnancy should be individualized (<6% to <7% [<42–53 mmol/mol]) to balance fetal outcomes and maternal hypoglycemia risk.
- A1C is a secondary measure of glycemia in pregnancy due to physiological changes and limited capture of postprandial hyperglycemia.
- A1C levels should be monitored more frequently (e.g., monthly) due to altered red blood cell turnover in pregnancy.
- A1C <6% (<42 mmol/mol) in the second and third trimesters is associated with the lowest risk of large-for-gestational-age infants, preterm delivery, and preeclampsia.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Patient must be pregnant with or without preexisting diabetes.
- Glycemic goals should account for maternal hypoglycemia risk.
- A1C should be interpreted alongside glucose monitoring data.
- Evidence from the HAPO study links increasing A1C levels within the normal range to adverse outcomes in individuals without preexisting diabetes.

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Reliance solely on A1C as the primary measure of glycemia in pregnancy.
- Fixed numeric A1C targets without consideration of individual patient factors.

**Updated Recommendations:**
- A1C <6% (<42 mmol/mol) as an optimal target in pregnancy if achievable without significant hypoglycemia.
- Monthly A1C monitoring during pregnancy due to physiological changes.
- Emphasis on postprandial glucose monitoring to address macrosomia risk.
- Observational studies indicate the lowest rates of adverse fetal outcomes are associated with A1C <6–6.5% (<42–48 mmol/mol) early in gestation.
