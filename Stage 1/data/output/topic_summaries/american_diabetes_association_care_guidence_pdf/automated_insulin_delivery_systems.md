# automated insulin delivery systems
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Automated Insulin Delivery (AID) Systems

**Core Recommendations:**
- AID systems integrate insulin pump, CGM, and algorithm to adjust insulin delivery in real time based on glucose levels
- Modulates insulin by increasing, decreasing, or pausing delivery every 5 minutes using predicted glucose trends (e.g., 30 min or 1 h ahead)
- Provides automated correction doses and allows manual mode (though not recommended due to loss of CGM modulation benefits)
- Adjusts glucose targets dynamically (87–180 mg/dL) with 24-hour or segmented settings (up to 8 segments/day)
- Requires manual carbohydrate entry for meal announcements or qualitative meal estimation for prandial dosing
- Algorithm hosting location varies by system: pump body, insulin pod, or phone app
- AID systems may provide automated correction doses via microdose adjustments every 5 minutes or additional correction boluses with variable frequency and threshold glucose based on algorithm type

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- FDA-approved or -cleared AID systems must be used in individuals with type 1 diabetes capable of safe device use (independently or with caregiver support)
- Prior use of CGM and insulin pump therapy is recommended for optimal AID system integration
- Individual preferences, cost, insulin type, dosing plan, and self-management capabilities should guide AID system selection
- Training, access, and support for AID system use must be available for effective implementation
- Pivotal trials and real-world studies support AID use in individuals ≥2 years old, including older adults
- Individuals must be capable of safe device use independently or with caregiver support (explicitly stated as a requirement in the full text)

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Sensor-augmented pumps (SAPs) with predictive low-glucose suspend are no longer commonly used due to superior AID systems
- Manual insulin dosing without CGM modulation is discouraged as it may reduce glycemic benefits
- Routine use of fixed glucose targets without dynamic adjustment is outdated
- Non-automated insulin delivery methods (e.g., multiple daily injections without CGM) are less effective for glycemic control
- Historical reliance on SAPs for hypoglycemia prevention is superseded by AID systems

**Updated Recommendations:**
- AID systems are now the standard of care for type 1 diabetes, replacing SAPs and manual dosing methods
- Real-world studies confirm AID systems' superiority in improving TIR, reducing hypoglycemia, and lowering A1C
- Dynamic glucose targets (87–180 mg/dL) and segmented settings (up to 8/day) are now standard features
- AID systems provide psychosocial benefits and are cost-effective for glycemic management
- AID systems are recommended for Medicare/Medicaid beneficiaries with type 1 or type 2 diabetes based on individual needs
- AID systems have largely replaced other methods of continuous subcutaneous insulin delivery due to their advantages in insulin modulation and minimizing hypoglycemia and hyperglycemia
- Real-world studies have retrospectively analyzed longer duration of AID system use (average 9 months) compared to pivotal trials, confirming sustained glycemic benefits
