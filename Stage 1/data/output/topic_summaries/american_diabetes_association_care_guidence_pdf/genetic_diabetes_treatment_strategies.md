# genetic diabetes treatment strategies
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Genetic diabetes treatment strategies for MODY and neonatal diabetes

**Core Recommendations:**
- Individuals with HNF1A or HNF4A mutations (MODY) are treated with low-dose sulfonylureas but may require insulin therapy.
- For HNF1A mutations, adding a DPP-4 inhibitor to sulfonylurea may improve glycemic variability and goal attainment.
- Neonatal diabetes due to KCNJ11 or ABCC8 mutations is treated with high-dose sulfonylureas.
- Neonatal diabetes due to INS, GATA6, EIF2AK3, or FOXP3 mutations requires insulin therapy.
- Individuals with HNF1B mutations (MODY) may respond to sulfonylureas but often require insulin therapy over time due to progressive beta-cell dysfunction.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Confirmation of specific genetic mutations (e.g., HNF1A, HNF4A, KCNJ11, ABCC8, INS, GATA6, EIF2AK3, FOXP3) via genetic testing.
- Diagnosis of maturity-onset diabetes of the young (MODY) or neonatal diabetes based on clinical and genetic criteria.
- Monitoring for sulfonylurea response and glycemic variability in HNF1A mutation carriers.
- Genetic testing for HNF1B mutations in suspected MODY cases with renal cysts or genital tract abnormalities.

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Empirical insulin therapy as first-line treatment for HNF1A or HNF4A MODY without trial of sulfonylureas.
- Routine use of metformin as primary therapy in KCNJ11 or ABCC8 neonatal diabetes without sulfonylurea trial.

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Genetic testing to guide targeted therapy selection in suspected MODY or neonatal diabetes.
- Use of DPP-4 inhibitors as adjunctive therapy in HNF1A MODY with sulfonylurea.
- High-dose sulfonylureas as first-line therapy for KCNJ11 or ABCC8 neonatal diabetes.
- Consider genetic testing for HNF1B mutations in patients with MODY phenotype and extra-pancreatic features (e.g., renal cysts, genital tract abnormalities) to guide treatment selection.
