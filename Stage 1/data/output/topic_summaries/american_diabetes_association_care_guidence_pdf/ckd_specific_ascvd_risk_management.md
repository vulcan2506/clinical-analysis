# CKD-Specific ASCVD Risk Management
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** PCSK9 Inhibitor Use in CKD-Specific ASCVD Risk Management

**Core Recommendations:**
- PCSK9 monoclonal antibodies (evolocumab, alirocumab) reduce LDL-C by 52–65% when added to maximally tolerated statin therapy.
- PCSK9 inhibitors reduce major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE) by 15–20% in high-risk ASCVD patients (FOURIER and ODYSSEY OUTCOMES trials).
- Similar relative risk reduction in MACE across diabetes status (type 1, type 2, no diabetes), with greater absolute risk reduction in type 1 diabetes.
- PCSK9 siRNA (inclisiran) reduces LDL-C by 49–52% but lacks cardiovascular outcome trial data; monoclonal antibodies are preferred.
- PCSK9 inhibitors are supported for aggressive LDL-C lowering in individuals with type 1 diabetes and established ASCVD.
- No cardiovascular outcome trials have been performed to assess whether PCSK9 inhibitor therapy reduces ASCVD event rates in individuals at low or moderate risk for ASCVD (primary prevention).
- Participants with type 1 diabetes randomly assigned to placebo had a higher rate of major cardiovascular events (Kaplan-Meier event rate 20.4%) than those with type 2 diabetes (15.2%) and those without diabetes (11.0%).

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Participants must be at high risk for ASCVD or have established ASCVD to benefit from PCSK9 inhibitor therapy.
- Therapy should be added to maximally tolerated statin therapy.

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Use of PCSK9 siRNA (inclisiran) as a first-line PCSK9-targeting therapy without outcome data.

**Updated Recommendations:**
- PCSK9 monoclonal antibodies (evolocumab, alirocumab) are preferred over inclisiran for established ASCVD or high-risk patients due to proven cardiovascular outcome benefits.
- Aggressive LDL-C lowering with PCSK9 inhibitors is supported in type 1 diabetes with established ASCVD.
- Therapy with PCSK9 monoclonal antibodies (alirocumab and evolocumab) is preferred over PCSK9 siRNA (inclisiran) due to the lack of completed cardiovascular outcome trials for inclisiran.
