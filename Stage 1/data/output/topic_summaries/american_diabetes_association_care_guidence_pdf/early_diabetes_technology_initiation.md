# early diabetes technology initiation
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Early diabetes technology initiation

**Core Recommendations:**
- Diabetes technology should be initiated soon after diagnosis and anytime thereafter based on patient needs and wishes
- CGM and BGM devices should be started from the outset of diabetes diagnosis, especially in insulin-requiring individuals
- Early CGM initiation in children/adolescents with type 1 diabetes reduces A1C and improves parental satisfaction
- AID systems are preferred for type 1 diabetes and insulin-deficient type 2 diabetes on MDI or basal insulin not meeting targets
- Early AID therapy initiation (within 21 days of diagnosis) improves time in range and lowers A1C in youth with type 1 diabetes
- Diabetes technology initiation should be tailored to patient preferences and lifestyle, with shared decision-making between healthcare providers and patients/families

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Insulin requirement for CGM/BGM initiation
- Patient willingness and need for technology use
- Training on CGM alarm/alert settings to prevent alarm fatigue
- Consistent access to CGM devices to avoid treatment interruption
- No requirement for C-peptide, islet autoantibodies, or diabetes duration to determine suitability for AID therapy
- Shared decision-making process between healthcare providers and patients/families to determine technology suitability and goals of care

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Delayed initiation of diabetes technology after diagnosis
- Reliance on fixed A1C targets without individualized assessment
- Exclusion of type 2 diabetes patients from CGM/AID therapy based on insulin production status

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Early CGM use in type 2 diabetes not using insulin with observed glycemic and cost-effectiveness benefits
- AID system initiation within 21 days of type 1 diabetes diagnosis for improved outcomes
- CGM use to reduce burden of frequent BGM in insulin-requiring individuals
- AID therapy consideration for type 2 diabetes on basal insulin not meeting targets
- No data supporting exclusion of patients from technology based on C-peptide or autoantibody status
- Consideration of AID therapy for insulin-deficient type 2 diabetes on basal insulin not meeting targets, regardless of insulin production status
