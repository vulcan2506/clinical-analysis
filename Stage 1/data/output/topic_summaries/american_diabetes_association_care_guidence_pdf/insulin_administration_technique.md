# insulin administration technique
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Insulin administration technique

**Core Recommendations:**
- Administer insulin into subcutaneous tissue (abdomen, thigh, buttock, upper arm) to avoid unpredictable absorption and hypoglycemia
- Use 4-mm pen needles to reduce risk of intramuscular (IM) insulin delivery across all ages and body types
- Rotate injection/infusion sites to prevent lipohypertrophy and ensure consistent insulin absorption
- Educate patients/caregivers on proper injection technique, device use, and site rotation
- Administer rapid-acting insulin immediately post-meal (not too early) to match carbohydrate intake and minimize hypoglycemia risk
- Perform routine examination of injection/infusion sites for lipohypertrophy as part of comprehensive diabetes evaluations
- Provide education on proper care of injection or infusion sites to avoid infection or other complications

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Patient or caregiver must demonstrate understanding of correct insulin administration technique
- Selection of administration method (pen, pump, patch, inhaled) must align with patient-specific factors, cost, and preferences
- Regular reassessment of administration technique during routine follow-up visits
- Preprandial glucose monitoring should coincide with insulin administration and meal timing
- For older adults in PALTC facilities, meals should be tailored to culture/preferences to ensure adequate nutrition
- Selection of administration method (vial/syringe, pen, patch, inhaled, connected devices, pump) must align with individual-specific factors, cost, coverage, and preferences
- Assessment of administration device use and injection technique as key components of a comprehensive diabetes evaluation and treatment plan

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Use of longer needles (e.g., >4 mm) without consideration of IM risk, particularly in lean individuals
- Fixed rotation patterns (e.g., always moving clockwise) without assessing for lipohypertrophy
- Routine use of short-acting insulin for postprandial dosing instead of rapid-acting insulin
- Administering insulin too early before meals without balancing hypoglycemia risk
- Ignoring site examination for lipohypertrophy during diabetes evaluations
- Routine use of longer needles (e.g., >4 mm) without consideration of intramuscular (IM) risk, particularly in lean individuals or injections into limbs rather than truncal sites

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Emphasis on 4-mm pen needles as the standard to minimize IM risk across all body types
- Postprandial insulin administration (with rapid-acting insulin) to match carbohydrate intake in older adults
- Integration of FITTER Forward expert recommendations for advanced injection technique education
- Routine site examination for lipohypertrophy as part of comprehensive diabetes evaluations
- Consideration of inhaled insulin as an alternative administration method for eligible patients
- Integration of FITTER Forward expert recommendations for advanced insulin injection technique education
