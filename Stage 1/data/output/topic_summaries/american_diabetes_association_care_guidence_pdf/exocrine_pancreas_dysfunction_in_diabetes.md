# exocrine pancreas dysfunction in diabetes
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Exocrine pancreas dysfunction and bidirectional diabetes relationship

**Core Recommendations:**
- Diabetes is linked to exocrine pancreas diseases like pancreatitis, disrupting global architecture and physiology.
- Up to half of individuals with diabetes may have impaired exocrine pancreas function.
- People with diabetes are at an approximately twofold higher risk of developing acute pancreatitis.
- Prediabetes and/or diabetes develops in approximately one-third of individuals after an episode of acute pancreatitis.
- Postpancreatitis diabetes may include either new-onset disease or previously unrecognized diabetes.
- Incretin-based therapies for diabetes may be associated with a higher frequency of pancreatitis, though causality remains unestablished and results are mixed

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Individuals requiring total pancreatectomy for medically refractory chronic pancreatitis.
- Presence of postsurgical diabetes risk following total pancreatectomy.
- Careful consideration of personal factors for the individual with diabetes.
- Careful consideration of disease factors when deciding indications and timing of surgery.
- Surgery must be performed in skilled facilities with demonstrated expertise in islet autotransplantation.
- Consideration of incretin-based therapy risks in patients with a history of pancreatitis or exocrine pancreas dysfunction

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Islet autotransplantation should be considered for individuals requiring total pancreatectomy for medically refractory chronic pancreatitis to prevent postsurgical diabetes.
- Observational studies report islet graft function persisting up to a decade post-total pancreatectomy with islet autotransplantation in some individuals
