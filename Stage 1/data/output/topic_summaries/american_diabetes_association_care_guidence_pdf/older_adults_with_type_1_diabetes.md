# older adults with type 1 diabetes
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Older adults with type 1 diabetes

**Core Recommendations:**
- Insulin is essential for survival; basal insulin must be maintained to prevent DKA even with reduced oral intake.
- Insulin delivery options include injections, AID systems, or insulin pumps based on patient preference, capability, and circumstances.
- CGM should be used with any insulin delivery system to improve A1C and reduce hypoglycemia.
- Treatment goals and strategies are personalized using comprehensive geriatric assessment.
- Special considerations include neurocognitive function, hypoglycemia risk, and end-of-life care.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Patient must have type 1 diabetes diagnosis.
- Age over 65 years or advanced age-related considerations.
- Assessment of life expectancy, cognitive function, and hypoglycemia risk.
- Access to insulin therapy (basal insulin required to prevent DKA).
- Availability of CGM or insulin delivery systems (pump, AID, injections).

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Fixed numeric A1C targets without individualized consideration.
- Discontinuation of basal insulin in older adults with reduced oral intake.
- Routine use of sulfonylureas or other non-insulin therapies in type 1 diabetes.
- Exclusion of CGM due to age or setting limitations.
- One-size-fits-all insulin regimens without personalization.

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Integration of CGM with any insulin delivery method for improved outcomes.
- Personalized treatment plans based on geriatric assessment.
- Emphasis on hypoglycemia prevention and neurocognitive function monitoring.
- Consideration of end-of-life care preferences in treatment planning.
- Use of AID systems or insulin pumps tailored to individual capability and circumstances.
