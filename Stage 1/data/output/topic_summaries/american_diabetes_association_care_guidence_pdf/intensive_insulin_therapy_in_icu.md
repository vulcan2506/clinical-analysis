# intensive insulin therapy in ICU
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Intensive insulin therapy in ICU

**Core Recommendations:**
- Intensive insulin therapy targeting 80–110 mg/dL (4.4–6.1 mmol/L) is no longer recommended due to increased hypoglycemia and mortality risks.
- Moderate glycemic goals of 140–180 mg/dL (7.8–10.0 mmol/L) are recommended for most critically ill individuals.
- More stringent goals (110–140 mg/dL) may be appropriate for selected patients (e.g., cardiac surgery) if hypoglycemia can be avoided.
- Persistent hyperglycemia ≥180 mg/dL (≥10.0 mmol/L) warrants insulin initiation.
- Clinical judgment must incorporate illness severity, nutritional status, and medication effects (e.g., glucocorticoids).
- Fasting glucose levels <100 mg/dL (<5.6 mmol/L) are predictors of hypoglycemia within the next 24 hours and necessitate basal insulin dose adjustments.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Persistent hyperglycemia ≥180 mg/dL (≥10.0 mmol/L) must be present before initiating insulin therapy.
- Selected patients (e.g., cardiac surgery) may require more stringent glycemic goals if hypoglycemia risk is low.
- Ongoing assessment of glucose measures, illness severity, and nutritional status is required.
- Concomitant medications affecting glucose levels (e.g., glucocorticoids) must be considered.
- Terminally ill patients or those with advanced kidney failure may tolerate higher glycemic levels (up to 250 mg/dL).
- Insulin and/or other therapies should be initiated for the treatment of persistent hyperglycemia ≥180 mg/dL (≥10.0 mmol/L).
- Glycemic goals of 110–140 mg/dL (6.1–7.8 mmol/L) may be appropriate for selected individuals (e.g., critically ill individuals undergoing cardiac surgery) only if they can be achieved without significant hypoglycemia.

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Intensive insulin therapy targeting 80–110 mg/dL (4.4–6.1 mmol/L) in all ICU patients.
- Fixed numeric glycemic targets without patient-specific adjustments.
- Routine use of tight glycemic control in medical and surgical ICUs without risk stratification.
- Routine use of tight glycemic control (80–110 mg/dL) in medical and surgical ICUs without risk stratification.

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Moderate glycemic goals of 140–180 mg/dL (7.8–10.0 mmol/L) for most critically ill individuals.
- More stringent goals (110–140 mg/dL) for selected patients if hypoglycemia can be avoided.
- Initiation of insulin therapy only for persistent hyperglycemia ≥180 mg/dL (≥10.0 mmol/L).
- Higher glycemic targets (up to 250 mg/dL) for terminally ill or high-risk patients.
- Emphasis on avoiding hypoglycemia, with fasting glucose <100 mg/dL (<5.6 mmol/L) requiring basal insulin adjustments.
- Higher glycemic levels (up to 250 mg/dL [13.9 mmol/L]) may be acceptable in selected populations (terminally ill individuals with short life expectancy, advanced kidney failure [and/or on dialysis], high risk for hypoglycemia, and/or labile glycemic excursions).
- For hospitalized individuals without critical illness, a glycemic goal of 100–180 mg/dL (5.6–10.0 mmol/L) is recommended, whether due to newly diagnosed diabetes, stress hyperglycemia, or pre-existing diabetes.
