# CGM implementation in elderly
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** CGM implementation in elderly

**Core Recommendations:**
- CGM devices improve glycemic management and are acceptable for older adults with type 1 or insulin-requiring type 2 diabetes
- CGM reduces hypoglycemia and improves glycemic outcomes in older adults not treated with insulin
- Older adults may require more time to learn and adapt to CGM technology
- Caregiver engagement is beneficial for successful CGM implementation in older adults
- Cognitive and functional capacity for technology use must be assessed before CGM initiation
- CGM use in older adults not treated with insulin reduces hypoglycemia and improves other glycemic outcomes, including mean glucose levels

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Assessment of cognitive and functional capacity for technology use
- Availability of caregiver support or care partner engagement, if appropriate
- Patient resides in a setting where glucose monitoring by a surrogate is required (e.g., group homes, assisted living facilities)
- Physical or cognitive limitations necessitating surrogate glucose monitoring
- Baseline glycemic management (e.g., well-managed blood glucose with mean A1C ~7.4%)
- Baseline well-managed blood glucose (mean A1C ~7.4%) is a prerequisite for advanced insulin delivery device trials in older adults

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Hybrid closed-loop advanced insulin delivery systems improve time in range (TIR) in older adults
- Automated insulin delivery (AID) strategies reduce hypoglycemia in older adults with type 1 diabetes
- AID use in older adults with type 2 diabetes on multiple daily injections increases TIR by 27% over 12 weeks
- Real-world evidence shows hybrid closed-loop insulin delivery improves mean glucose and increases TIR by 10% in Medicare-eligible older adults
- High acceptance rate (91%) of AID systems in older adults aged 65+ years
- AIDE T1D study demonstrated 91% acceptance rate of AID systems in older adults aged 65+ years after crossover trial participation
