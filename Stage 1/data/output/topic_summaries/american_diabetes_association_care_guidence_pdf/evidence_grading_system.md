# evidence grading system
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** ADA evidence-grading system and Standards of Care development process

**Core Recommendations:**
- Public comment is actively solicited during a year-long period to identify gaps in care and improve transparency.
- Feedback is reviewed by the Professional Practice Committee (PPC) and considered for urgent updates published as living standards.
- External peer reviewers, endorsing societies (e.g., ACC, AGS, NKF), and ADA leadership review the Standards of Care for methodological rigor, accuracy, and implementation.
- The ADA adheres to the CMSS Principles for Clinical Guideline Development, ensuring multi-stakeholder review and approval.
- Final approval requires consensus from healthcare professionals, scientists, and other stakeholders before publication.
- The ADA Medical Affairs scientific team reviews the Standards of Care for methodological rigor, accuracy, clarity, and implementation before external peer review.
- The Standards of Care undergoes a sequential review process involving external peer review, ADA leadership review, and ADA Board of Directors review and approval.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Evidence must be derived from well-conducted randomized controlled trials, meta-analyses, cohort studies, or case-control studies with minimal methodological flaws.
- Public feedback must be systematically compiled and addressed by the PPC during the annual update cycle.
- External peer review and endorsing society input are mandatory before final approval.
- Living standards updates may be published between annual revisions based on urgent evidence or stakeholder feedback.
- Methodological rigor, clarity, and implementation feasibility are prerequisites for guideline approval.
- Evidence for Level A must come from well-conducted, generalizable randomized controlled trials that are adequately powered.
- Level A evidence specifically includes evidence from a well-conducted multicenter trial or a meta-analysis that incorporated quality ratings in the analysis.

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Evidence from poorly controlled or uncontrolled studies (e.g., case series with historical controls) is no longer considered high-quality support.
- Recommendations based on conflicting evidence with weak supporting weight are deprioritized.
- Major methodological flaws in randomized trials (e.g., inadequate power, ungeneralizable populations) invalidate high-level evidence claims.
- Evidence from randomized clinical trials with one or more major methodological flaws that could invalidate the results is categorized as Level C.
- Evidence from observational studies with high potential for bias, such as case series with comparison with historical controls, is categorized as Level C.
- Conflicting evidence where the weight of evidence supports the recommendation is categorized as Level C.

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Introduction of living standards updates to incorporate urgent evidence or feedback between annual revisions.
- Expanded public comment period to include people with diabetes, caregivers, and broader clinical stakeholders.
- Mandatory adherence to CMSS Principles for guideline development, enhancing methodological transparency.
- Structured multi-tiered review process involving external peer reviewers, endorsing societies, and ADA leadership.
- Year-long public comment period to systematically address gaps in care and improve guideline relevance.
- The list of endorsing societies providing feedback has been expanded to include ASBMR, ISPAD, and TOS in addition to ACC, AGS, and NKF.
