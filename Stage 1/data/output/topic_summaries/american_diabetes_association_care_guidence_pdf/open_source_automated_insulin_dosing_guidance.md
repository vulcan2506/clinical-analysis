# open-source automated insulin dosing guidance
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Open-source automated insulin dosing (OS-AID) guidance

**Core Recommendations:**
- OS-AID algorithms provide transparent, community-driven code for automated insulin dosing, enabling healthcare professionals and users to understand risks and benefits.
- OS-AID systems (e.g., OpenAPS, Loop) are implemented on various platforms and supported by real-world studies and RCTs.
- Healthcare professionals should support tuning of algorithm settings (e.g., basal rates, insulin-to-carbohydrate ratios) for safe and effective use.
- Direct management of algorithm aggressiveness is possible through conventional pump settings.
- Backup insulin treatment plans are advisable for all AID systems.
- OS-AID algorithms (e.g., OpenAPS, Loop) are implemented on multiple platforms (e.g., Android smartphones as AndroidAPS, iPhone as iAPS/Trio) and supported by both real-world studies and randomized controlled trials (RCTs).
- Loop algorithm supports direct management of algorithm aggressiveness through conventional pump settings, enabling healthcare professionals to adjust basal rates, insulin-to-carbohydrate ratios, or insulin sensitivity factors.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Use of cleared CGM systems and insulin pump components with OS-AID algorithms.
- Healthcare professional understanding of OS-AID system tuning and settings.
- Patient willingness to engage with community-driven systems and online resources.
- Institutional protocols for technology use in inpatient settings if hospitalized.
- Compliance with data privacy and security standards for digital health solutions.
- Healthcare professionals must be knowledgeable about OS-AID system tuning and settings to provide safe and effective support for patients using these systems.
- Institutional protocols must be established for the use of OS-AID systems in inpatient settings, including training, competency assessments, and resource availability.

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Use of the term 'DIY' to describe open-source systems, as it inaccurately implies individual coding.
- Reliance on non-transparent or proprietary automated glycemic controllers without source code availability.
- Assumption that all users of OS-AID systems have built the software themselves.

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Recognition of OS-AID as the preferred term for open-source automated insulin dosing systems.
- FDA clearance of Tidepool Loop, a variant of the Loop algorithm, supporting commercial implementation.
- Integration of online or virtual licensed coaching with CGM, insulin pumps, and diabetes apps to improve glycemic outcomes.
- Use of FDA-cleared digital therapeutics ('digiceuticals') for diabetes management.
- Emphasis on data privacy and security compliance (e.g., HIPAA) for cloud-based data aggregators like Tidepool and Glooko.
- Recognition of digital therapeutics ('digiceuticals') as FDA-cleared digital health technologies for diabetes management, with emphasis on their clinical validation and regulatory approval.
- Recommendation to use cloud-based data aggregators (e.g., Tidepool, Glooko) that comply with HIPAA and other data privacy standards for monitoring and sharing diabetes data.
