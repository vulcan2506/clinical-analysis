# individualized care in older adults
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Individualized care in older adults with diabetes

**Core Recommendations:**
- Screening for diabetes complications prioritized based on potential to impair functional status or quality of life
- Blood pressure goals individualized (<130/80 mmHg if safe; <140/90 mmHg for those with poor health, limited life expectancy, or high adverse effect risk)
- Cardiovascular risk factor treatment individualized considering time frame of benefit and life expectancy
- Diabetes self-management education and support reassessed after clinical changes, hospitalizations, or functional decline
- Glycemic goals individualized based on life expectancy, cognitive/physical function, hypoglycemia risk, and patient preferences
- Avoidance of hypoglycemia and symptomatic hyperglycemia (>180 mg/dL) in older adults with complex health status to prevent dehydration, weakness, infection, and poor wound healing
- Deintensification of diabetes medications and removal of most agents for type 2 diabetes as organ failure develops in palliative/end-of-life care

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Life expectancy assessment using validated tools (e.g., LEAD) for older adults with diabetes
- Cognitive and physical functional assessment using age-normalized tools when self-care abilities decline
- Evaluation of health literacy, numeracy, and support systems at treatment onset and throughout care
- Consideration of competing mortality and time-to-benefit for intensive management decisions
- Shared decision-making with older adults to align treatment intensity with goals and preferences
- Assessment of oral health, vision, hearing loss, foot care needs, fall risk, and depression status to improve quality of life
- Evaluation of financial cost and difficulties of administration when balancing risks and benefits of diabetes medications

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Fixed numeric A1C targets for all older adults without consideration of individual factors
- Routine use of intensive glycemic management in frail older adults with limited life expectancy
- Reliance solely on A1C for glycemic monitoring in conditions affecting red blood cell turnover (e.g., kidney failure, recent blood loss)
- Aggressive blood pressure targets (<130/80 mmHg) for all older adults regardless of health status
- Routine use of antiplatelet therapy in older adults with diabetes without life expectancy ≥ trial time frames

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Use of LEAD tool to estimate life expectancy in older adults with diabetes for goal-setting
- CGM or blood glucose monitoring for glycemic management in conditions affecting A1C accuracy
- Deintensification of diabetes medications in palliative/end-of-life care to avoid hypoglycemia
- Prioritization of functional status and quality-of-life outcomes over strict glycemic targets
- Classification of older adults into health status categories (healthy, complex/intermediate, very complex) for tailored care
- Use of Time in Range (TIR) and Time Below Range (TBR) metrics alongside A1C for glycemic goal setting in older adults
- Classification of older adults into specific health status categories (Healthy, Complex/intermediate, Very complex) based on comorbidities, ADL impairments, and cognitive status
