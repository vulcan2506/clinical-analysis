# hospital glycemic management protocol
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Hospital glycemic management protocol

**Core Recommendations:**
- Individualized glycemic management considering prior insulin/noninsulin therapy, insulin resistance, A1C, current glucose, nutrition, diabetes duration, and treatment goals
- Use validated written/computerized protocols for continuous intravenous insulin infusion in critical care to achieve glycemic goals and avoid hypoglycemia
- Transition from IV insulin to subcutaneous basal insulin 2 hours before discontinuation to prevent rebound hyperglycemia
- Administer low-dose basal insulin analog (0.15–0.3 units/kg) alongside IV insulin in hyperglycemia/DKA to reduce infusion duration and hospital stay
- Use basal-bolus insulin (basal + prandial + correction) as preferred treatment for noncritically ill hospitalized individuals with adequate nutrition
- Provide prandial coverage with rapid-acting subcutaneous insulin for individuals eating but requiring continuous intravenous insulin infusion to prevent large fluctuations in IV insulin requirements and reduce hypoglycemia risk
- Use scheduled subcutaneous insulin orders for hyperglycemia management in hospitalized individuals with diabetes

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Prior home insulin/noninsulin therapy and doses must be assessed for individualized dosing
- Continuous IV insulin infusion requires predefined adjustments based on glycemic fluctuations and prior insulin rates
- Subcutaneous basal insulin must be given 2 hours before IV insulin discontinuation to prevent rebound hyperglycemia
- Basal-bolus insulin is preferred for noncritically ill patients with adequate nutritional intake
- Correctional insulin dosing scales should be proportional to total daily insulin dose (low <40 units/day, medium 40–80 units/day, high >80 units/day)
- Total daily insulin dose calculation should account for 60% of the insulin infusion rate to adjust for glucotoxicity when estimating from IV insulin
- Initial total daily insulin dose may be estimated from prior hospitalization insulin dose or 80% of home insulin dose if applicable

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Prolonged use of correction-only insulin (sliding scale) without basal insulin in noncritical care
- Use of insulin mixtures (e.g., 70/30, 75/25) due to increased hypoglycemia risk
- Routine use of concentrated insulins (e.g., U-500) without diabetes team consultation due to dosing errors and hypoglycemia risk
- Fixed A1C targets without individualized consideration of life expectancy, cognitive function, or hypoglycemia risk
- Unvalidated insulin infusion protocols without predefined rate adjustments
- Use of intermediate-acting insulin mixtures (e.g., 70/30 NPH/regular) due to increased hypoglycemia risk compared to basal-bolus therapy
- Prolonged use of correction-only insulin without basal insulin in noncritical care for type 2 diabetes with blood glucose ≥180 mg/dL

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Low-dose basal insulin analog (0.15–0.3 units/kg) added to IV insulin in hyperglycemia/DKA to reduce infusion duration and hospital stay
- Use of insulin pens with safety protocols (nursing education, bar code scanning) to improve safety and nurse satisfaction
- Weight-based total daily insulin dose (0.3–0.6 units/kg/day) as starting dose for hospitalized individuals
- Basal-bolus insulin plan improving glycemic outcomes and reducing complications compared to correction-only insulin in type 2 diabetes
- Transition protocols for IV insulin discontinuation with subcutaneous basal insulin to prevent rebound hyperglycemia
- Use of DPP-4i for mild hyperglycemia in appropriate hospitalized patients as an alternative to insulin
- Consideration of diabetes care team consultation when using concentrated insulins (e.g., U-300, U-200, U-500) to reduce dosing errors and hypoglycemia risk
