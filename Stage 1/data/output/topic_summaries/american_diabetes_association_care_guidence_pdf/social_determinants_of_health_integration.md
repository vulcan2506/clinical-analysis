# social determinants of health integration
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Social determinants of health integration in diabetes care

**Core Recommendations:**
- Integrate social support assessment (community and personal network instrumental support) into diabetes care plans
- Address SDOH such as racism and discrimination as potential drivers of adverse health outcomes
- Leverage community resources and health care community linkages for nutrition and physical activity recommendations
- Utilize CHWs, community paramedics, peer supporters, and lay leaders to deliver DSMES services, particularly in underserved communities
- Delegate limited clinical tasks to CHWs under direct supervision of a health professional and in compliance with state laws
- Identify and document specific SDOH (e.g., racism, discrimination, food insecurity) during patient encounters to inform tailored diabetes care plans

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Patient engagement in self-care behaviors and medication use may be influenced by SDOH, necessitating tailored interventions
- Community-based care delivery requires collaboration with organizations like the American Medical Association and AHRQ
- CHWs must be part of an evidence-based strategy to improve diabetes and cardiovascular risk management in underserved communities
- Community paramedics require training in chronic disease monitoring, medication management, and SDOH
- Delegated clinical tasks to CHWs must follow state health care laws and be supervised by a licensed health professional
- Community paramedics must operate under the direction of a medical director with documented training in chronic disease monitoring, medication management, and SDOH

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Routine clinical practice that does not address SDOH such as racism and discrimination
- Exclusive reliance on traditional clinical interventions without community resource linkages
- Unsupervised delegation of clinical tasks to non-clinical personnel

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Integration of CHWs as trusted frontline public health workers to provide social support and basic health education
- Use of community paramedics for diabetes education, medication management, and care coordination
- Adoption of health care community linkages to translate clinical recommendations into practice
- Implementation of peer supporters and lay leaders to enhance DSMES services in underserved populations
- Delegation of limited clinical tasks to CHWs under direct supervision and state-compliant protocols
- Use of community paramedics for wound care in diabetes management as part of expanded scope of practice
