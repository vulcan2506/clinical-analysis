# intensive glycemic control cardiovascular outcomes
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Intensive glycemic control and cardiovascular outcomes

**Core Recommendations:**
- Intensive glycemic management does not consistently reduce composite cardiovascular disease (CVD) events during intervention periods in type 1 or type 2 diabetes.
- Long-term follow-up of intensive glycemic control shows reduced myocardial infarctions and major CVD events, particularly in type 1 diabetes (e.g., EDIC cohort).
- Severe hypoglycemia is strongly associated with increased cardiovascular events and mortality; aggressive near-normal A1C targets should be avoided if unsafe.
- SGLT2 inhibitors and GLP-1 receptor agonists (including dual GIP/GLP-1 RAs) are recommended for individuals with type 2 diabetes and established CVD, chronic kidney disease, or heart failure, regardless of A1C or metformin use.
- Cardiovascular benefits of SGLT2 inhibitors and GLP-1 RAs are mediated by mechanisms beyond glycemic control (e.g., weight loss, hemodynamic effects, anti-inflammatory changes).
- Blood glucose is a weaker independent cardiovascular disease (CVD) risk factor compared to hypertension or hypercholesterolemia, explaining the mixed results of intensive glycemic control trials.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Established cardiovascular disease, chronic kidney disease, or heart failure in individuals with type 2 diabetes for SGLT2 inhibitor or GLP-1 RA initiation.
- Prior treatment with dual or multiple glucose-lowering therapies before considering switching to SGLT2 inhibitors or GLP-1 RAs.
- A1C goal achievement or individualized A1C target in individuals with CVD for cardiovascular benefit from SGLT2 inhibitors or GLP-1 RAs.
- Vigilance in hypoglycemia prevention when setting glycemic targets.
- Post-trial observational follow-up data supporting long-term CVD benefits of intensive glycemic control.
- Post-trial observational follow-up data (e.g., EDIC, UKPDS, VADT, ADVANCE-ON, ACCORDION) should be considered when evaluating long-term cardiovascular benefits of intensive glycemic control.

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Rapid escalation of therapies and early use of large insulin doses in intensive glycemic control (as seen in ACCORD).
- Fixed intensive A1C targets without consideration of patient-specific factors (e.g., hypoglycemia risk, life expectancy).
- Reliance solely on A1C reduction as a surrogate for cardiovascular risk reduction.
- Use of intensive glycemic control strategies in older adults with long-standing diabetes and high CVD risk without individualized assessment.
- Reliance on composite CVD event reduction as the sole metric for evaluating intensive glycemic control effectiveness, given heterogeneity in trial outcomes (e.g., no reduction in ADVANCE-ON vs. significant reductions in EDIC and VADT follow-ups).

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Initiation of SGLT2 inhibitors or GLP-1 RAs (including dual GIP/GLP-1 RAs) in type 2 diabetes with CVD, kidney disease, or heart failure independent of A1C or metformin use.
- Long-term follow-up data demonstrating reduced myocardial infarctions and major CVD events with prior intensive glycemic control.
- Emphasis on hypoglycemia prevention as a critical component of glycemic management to reduce CVD risk.
- Recommendation to switch to SGLT2 inhibitors or GLP-1 RAs in individuals already on dual or multiple glucose-lowering therapies without these agents.
- Post-trial observational data showing heterogeneity in CVD outcomes (e.g., ADVANCE-ON vs. EDIC, VADT, UKPDS follow-ups).
- Initiation of SGLT2 inhibitors or GLP-1 RAs (including dual GIP/GLP-1 RAs) in individuals with type 2 diabetes and obesity, independent of A1C, metformin use, or glycemic targets, for cardiovascular and kidney benefits.
