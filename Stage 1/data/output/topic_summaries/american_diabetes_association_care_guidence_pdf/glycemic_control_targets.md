# glycemic control targets
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Glycemic control targets + Time in Range + Hypoglycemia risk management

**Core Recommendations:**
- Glycemic targets are individualized based on patient-specific factors including life expectancy, cognitive function, hypoglycemia risk, and comorbidities.
- Time in Range (TIR) is emphasized as a complementary metric to HbA1c for assessing glycemic control, with targets of ≥70% in 70–180 mg/dL range for most adults.
- Severe hypoglycemia prevention is prioritized, particularly in older adults, those with impaired awareness, and patients with CKD or cardiovascular disease.
- Continuous Glucose Monitoring (CGM) is recommended for real-time glycemic monitoring, hypoglycemia detection, and treatment adjustments.
- Structured education (e.g., DAFNE, BGAT) and hypoglycemia awareness training are integrated into care plans for high-risk patients.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Patient-specific factors (e.g., age, comorbidities, hypoglycemia risk) must be assessed before setting glycemic targets.
- HbA1c should be measured using NGSP-certified assays, with consideration of hemoglobinopathies or conditions affecting HbA1c accuracy.
- CGM or frequent self-monitoring of blood glucose (SMBG) is required for patients on insulin or with hypoglycemia unawareness.
- Multidisciplinary care (endocrinology, nephrology, cardiology) is recommended for patients with CKD, cardiovascular disease, or complex diabetes.
- Sick-day management protocols must be in place for patients at risk of hyperglycemic crises (e.g., DKA, HHS).

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Fixed numeric HbA1c targets (e.g., <7% for all patients) without individualized adjustments.
- Reliance solely on HbA1c for glycemic assessment without considering TIR or glycemic variability.
- Use of sulfonylureas in older adults or patients with CKD due to high hypoglycemia risk.
- Discontinuation of insulin therapy in type 1 diabetes based on HbA1c alone.
- Routine use of urine ketone testing without blood ketone monitoring in sick-day management.

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Adoption of TIR (70–180 mg/dL) as a primary glycemic metric alongside HbA1c, with targets of ≥70% for most adults.
- Integration of CGM data into clinical decision-making for insulin dosing and hypoglycemia prevention.
- Risk stratification tools (e.g., hypoglycemia prediction models) for identifying high-risk patients.
- Nasal glucagon and ready-to-use liquid glucagon formulations for emergency hypoglycemia treatment.
- SGLT2 inhibitor protocols with ketone monitoring to mitigate DKA risk in type 1 diabetes.
