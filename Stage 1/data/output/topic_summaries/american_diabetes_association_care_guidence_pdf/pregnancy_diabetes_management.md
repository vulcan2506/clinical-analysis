# pregnancy diabetes management
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Pregnancy diabetes management

**Core Recommendations:**
- Implement preconception counseling and glycemic optimization before pregnancy
- Maintain strict glycemic control throughout pregnancy to reduce maternal and fetal complications
- Screen for and manage preeclampsia with low-dose aspirin prophylaxis in high-risk patients
- Monitor and adjust non-glucose-lowering medications for safety during pregnancy
- Provide structured postpartum care including glucose tolerance testing and contraception counseling
- Initiate preconception folic acid supplementation (400–800 mcg/day) at least 1–3 months before conception in patients with pregestational diabetes to reduce neural tube defect risk
- Provide patient education on hypoglycemia recognition and management during pregnancy, including glucagon administration training for caregivers

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Preconception glycemic control (A1C <7% ideally, <6.5% if safely achievable)
- Diagnosis of pregestational or gestational diabetes based on standard criteria
- Baseline assessment including retinal and renal evaluations
- Multidisciplinary care team including obstetrics, endocrinology, and nutrition
- Regular fetal monitoring with growth ultrasounds and non-stress tests
- Preconception thyroid function testing (TSH) in patients with type 1 diabetes due to increased risk of thyroid dysfunction
- Baseline electrocardiogram (ECG) in patients with pregestational diabetes and cardiovascular risk factors before considering metformin use

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Use of ACE inhibitors or ARBs for hypertension in pregnancy
- Routine use of oral hypoglycemic agents (e.g., sulfonylureas) in first trimester
- Postpartum glucose screening without prior risk stratification
- Universal bed rest for gestational diabetes without medical indication
- Routine use of high-dose vitamin supplementation beyond standard prenatal recommendations
- Routine use of thiazolidinediones (e.g., pioglitazone) for glycemic control in pregnancy due to limited safety data and potential adverse fetal effects
- Use of statins for primary prevention of cardiovascular disease in pregnant patients with diabetes

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Early initiation of low-dose aspirin (81 mg/day) from 12 to 36 weeks for preeclampsia prevention in high-risk patients
- Use of metformin as an adjunct to insulin in gestational diabetes if glycemic targets are not met with lifestyle alone
- Continuous glucose monitoring (CGM) for real-time glycemic trend monitoring in pregnancy
- Postpartum 75g OGTT at 4-12 weeks for all gestational diabetes patients
- Immediate postpartum contraception counseling and initiation for patients with diabetes
- Consider SGLT2 inhibitors (e.g., empagliflozin) in select patients with type 2 diabetes preconception or early pregnancy if benefits outweigh risks, under specialist supervision
- Use of fetal echocardiography at 18–22 weeks in patients with pregestational diabetes and poor glycemic control (A1C ≥8%) to assess for congenital heart defects
