# diabetes-related fracture risk assessment
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Diabetes-related fracture risk assessment

**Core Recommendations:**
- Fracture risk is significantly increased in people with type 1 or type 2 diabetes in both sexes, with a 34% overall increase compared to those without diabetes.
- Type 1 diabetes increases fracture risk by 4.35x for hip fractures, 1.83x for upper-limb fractures, and 1.97x for ankle fractures, occurring 10–15 years earlier than in non-diabetic individuals.
- Type 2 diabetes increases hip fracture risk by 1.79x and vertebral fracture risk by 35%, despite normal or higher BMD, with greater bone loss velocity with age.
- Fracture risk escalates with poor glycemic control (A1C >9% increases risk by 29%), longer diabetes duration (>10 years), hypoglycemia (RR 1.52), and presence of microvascular complications.
- BMD underestimates fracture risk in type 1 diabetes, while type 2 diabetes shows higher BMD but lower bone strength and impaired microarchitecture.
- Fracture risk in type 1 diabetes is independently associated with microvascular complications or neuropathy (RR not specified).
- Fracture risk in type 1 diabetes is increased with average A1C >7.9% (RR 3.57), diabetes duration >26 years (RR 7.6), and family history of fractures (RR 2.64).

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Assess fracture history and risk factors in all people with diabetes.
- Measure BMD via DXA in older adults with type 2 diabetes (≥65 years) or younger individuals (≥50 years) with diabetes-related risk factors (e.g., insulin use, diabetes duration >10 years).
- Consider BMD assessment in type 1 diabetes after age 50 or earlier with low-trauma fractures or additional risk factors.
- Monitor BMD every 2–3 years in type 2 diabetes, or every 2 years in individuals undergoing metabolic surgery.
- Use FRAX with adjustments (e.g., T-score reduction by 0.5 SD, age increase by 10 years) or trabecular bone score to improve fracture risk prediction in type 2 diabetes.
- Assess fracture history and risk factors in all people with diabetes before recommending BMD measurement.
- Consider DXA-assisted vertebral fracture assessment as a low-cost alternative to traditional lateral spine X-rays in type 2 diabetes.

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Reliance on FRAX without adjustments for type 2 diabetes, as it systematically underestimates fracture risk in this population.
- Use of standard BMD thresholds without considering diabetes-specific adjustments (e.g., T-score interpretation without -0.5 SD correction).
- Assuming normal BMD in type 2 diabetes indicates low fracture risk, given the disconnect between BMD and actual fracture risk.
- Routine bone densitometry in children/adolescents with type 1 diabetes without additional risk factors (e.g., celiac disease).

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Adjust FRAX inputs for type 2 diabetes (e.g., T-score reduction by 0.5 SD, age increase by 10 years, or trabecular bone score incorporation).
- Recommend DXA-assisted vertebral fracture assessment as a low-cost alternative to traditional lateral spine X-rays.
- Incorporate trabecular bone score (TBS) to enhance fracture risk prediction in type 2 diabetes.
- Monitor BMD more frequently (every 2 years) in individuals undergoing metabolic surgery.
- Consider BMD assessment in type 1 diabetes after age 50 or earlier with low-trauma fractures or additional risk factors.
- Incorporate diabetes-specific adjustments (e.g., T-score reduction by 0.5 SD) into FRAX inputs for type 2 diabetes to improve fracture risk prediction.
- Use rheumatoid arthritis input as a surrogate for type 2 diabetes in FRAX when no other adjustments are available.
