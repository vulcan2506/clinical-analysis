# diabetes screening protocols
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Diabetes screening protocols and settings

**Core Recommendations:**
- Screening intervals are typically 3 years to balance false positives and timely detection of complications
- Shorter screening intervals may be used for high-risk individuals (e.g., near-diagnostic cut points or on high-risk medications)
- Screening should ideally occur within healthcare settings to ensure follow-up and treatment access
- Community screening is generally discouraged unless a robust referral system is established
- Dental settings may be explored for screening due to the bidirectional relationship between periodontal disease and diabetes

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Screening should be conducted in settings with established follow-up and treatment pathways
- High-risk individuals may require shorter screening intervals based on prior test results or medication use
- Referral systems must be in place for positive tests in community screening scenarios
- Screening in dental settings requires further research to confirm feasibility and cost-effectiveness
- Screening should target populations at higher risk of undiagnosed diabetes or prediabetes
- Screening in community settings requires an adequate referral system established beforehand for positive tests
- Community screening must target high-risk groups and avoid testing low-risk individuals or those already diagnosed

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Community screening without a referral system for positive tests
- Screening in settings lacking follow-up care pathways
- Routine screening in low-risk populations or those already diagnosed

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Exploration of dental settings for diabetes screening due to periodontal disease-diabetes link
- Consideration of shorter screening intervals for high-risk individuals
- Emphasis on healthcare setting-based screening for follow-up and treatment access
