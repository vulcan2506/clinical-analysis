# hypoglycemia prevention strategy
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Hypoglycemia prevention strategy

**Core Recommendations:**
- Use CGM for older adults with type 1 or type 2 diabetes on insulin to improve glycemic outcomes, reduce hypoglycemia, and reduce treatment burden
- Provide structured education on hypoglycemia prevention and treatment through diabetes self-management education programs or trained specialists
- Educate individuals at risk for hypoglycemia on definitions, precipitating situations (e.g., fasting, delayed meals, physical activity), blood glucose self-monitoring, avoidance of driving with hypoglycemia, and glucagon use
- Reevaluate diabetes treatment plans for individuals with Level 2 or 3 hypoglycemic events, considering deintensification of therapy
- Offer training to reestablish hypoglycemia awareness for individuals with impaired hypoglycemia awareness
- Provide ketone monitoring tools (urine or blood) to individuals at risk for hyperglycemic crises, particularly those on SGLT2 inhibitors or with type 1 diabetes, and educate on their use during illness or hyperglycemia
- Offer sick day advice and insulin dose adjustment guidance to prevent DKA in individuals on intensive insulin therapy, emphasizing the importance of not stopping basal insulin even when not eating

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Individuals must have a documented history of hypoglycemia or risk factors for hypoglycemia
- Structured education should be provided by a diabetes care and education specialist or equivalent trained professional
- CGM is recommended for insulin-treated individuals, especially those using multiple daily insulin injections or continuous subcutaneous insulin infusion
- Training for impaired hypoglycemia awareness should be offered to individuals with type 1 diabetes or those with recurrent hypoglycemia
- Medication adjustments (e.g., sulfonylureas) should be considered when interacting with antimicrobials that increase hypoglycemia risk
- Individuals on SGLT2 inhibitors, particularly those with type 1 diabetes, should be monitored for ketone levels (fasting β-hydroxybutyrate ≥0.8 mmol/L) as a risk factor for DKA
- Pregnant individuals with pregestational diabetes should be counseled on DKA risk and provided with ketone monitoring tools due to elevated risk during pregnancy

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Routine use of fixed numeric A1C targets without individualized consideration of hypoglycemia risk
- Non-structured or non-evidence-based hypoglycemia education approaches
- Lack of consideration for medication interactions (e.g., sulfonylureas with fluoroquinolones) in hypoglycemia prevention
- Routine discontinuation of basal insulin during illness or fasting without providing detailed sick day management instructions
- Reliance solely on urine ketone monitoring without consideration of blood ketone monitoring for more accurate DKA risk assessment

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Introduction of CGM for hypoglycemia detection and prevention in insulin-treated individuals
- Standardized terminology for care in post-acute and long-term care (PALTC) settings
- Specific guidance on protein intake (at least 0.8 g/kg body weight/day) in lifestyle management
- Stepwise approach for assessing difficulties in diabetes treatment plans and shared decision-making for glycemic goals
- Emphasis on ketone monitoring tools and
- for hyperglycemic crisis prevention
