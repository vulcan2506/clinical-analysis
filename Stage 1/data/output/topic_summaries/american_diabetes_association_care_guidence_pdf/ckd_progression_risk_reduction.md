# CKD progression risk reduction
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** CKD progression risk reduction

**Core Recommendations:**
- Optimize glucose management to reduce CKD risk, acknowledging a lag time of 2-10 years for intensive control benefits.
- Maintain on-treatment blood pressure goals of <130/80 mmHg, with consideration for <120 mmHg systolic in selected patients.
- Use ACE inhibitors or ARBs as first-line therapy for hypertension and albuminuria, uptitrated to maximum tolerated doses.
- Initiate SGLT2 inhibitors for type 2 diabetes and CKD (eGFR ≥20 mL/min/1.73 m²) to reduce progression and cardiovascular events.
- Prescribe nonsteroidal mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists (e.g., finerenone) for patients with albuminuria and eGFR ≥25 mL/min/1.73 m².
- Monitor for blood pressure variability in CKD patients, as higher variability is associated with increased risk of kidney failure (HR 1.47 per 10-mmHg increase).
- Uptitrate ACE inhibitors or ARBs to maximum tolerated doses even in advanced CKD (eGFR <30 mL/min/1.73 m²), as studies demonstrate outcome benefits on mortality and slowed CKD progression.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Patient must have type 1 or type 2 diabetes with established CKD (eGFR <60 mL/min/1.73 m²) and/or albuminuria.
- Blood pressure must be elevated or patient must have moderately to severely increased albuminuria (UACR ≥30 mg/g) for RAS blockade.
- Serum potassium must be ≤4.8 mmol/L prior to initiating nonsteroidal MRAs.
- eGFR must be ≥20 mL/min/1.73 m² for SGLT2 inhibitor initiation.
- eGFR must be ≥25 mL/min/1.73 m² for nonsteroidal MRA initiation.
- Serum potassium must be monitored 1 month after initiating nonsteroidal MRAs (e.g., finerenone).
- For GLP-1 receptor agonists, glucose-lowering efficacy is preserved at low eGFR, though kidney-protective effects are independent of glycemic control.

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Combined use of ACE inhibitors and ARBs due to increased adverse event rates without added benefit.
- Routine use of ACE inhibitors or ARBs for primary CKD prevention in normotensive patients with normal UACR and eGFR.
- Steroidal mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists due to higher hyperkalemia risk compared to nonsteroidal agents.
- Fixed numeric A1C targets without individualization based on comorbidity and hypoglycemia risk in prevalent CKD.
- Routine monitoring of glycated albumin and fructosamine only in early CKD stages rather than advanced stages where A1C is unreliable.
- Routine use of ACE inhibitors or ARBs for primary CKD prevention in normotensive patients with normal UACR (<30 mg/g creatinine) and normal eGFR, as outcome trials show no benefit in this setting.

**Updated Recommendations:**
- SGLT2 inhibitors recommended for type 2 diabetes and CKD regardless of baseline A1C or glycemic control status.
- GLP-1 receptor agonists recommended to reduce kidney disease progression and cardiovascular risk in type 2 diabetes and CKD.
- Finerenone (nonsteroidal MRA) recommended for reducing CKD progression and cardiovascular events in patients with albuminuria.
- Continuation of RAS blockade despite mild-to-moderate creatinine rise (≤30%) without signs of volume depletion or hyperkalemia.
- Individualized A1C goals for patients with prevalent CKD and substantial comorbidity to minimize hypoglycemia risk.
- SGLT2 inhibitors reduce oxidative stress in the kidney by >50% and blunt increases in angiotensinogen while reducing NLRP3 inflammasome activity, providing kidney protection beyond glycemic control.
- GLP-1 receptor agonists exert kidney protection through anti-inflammatory, antioxidative, and immunomodulatory actions, independent of their glucose-lowering effects.
