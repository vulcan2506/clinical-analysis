# diabetic retinopathy progression timing
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Diabetic retinopathy progression timing and screening optimization

**Core Recommendations:**
- Retinopathy most commonly occurs after puberty and 5–10 years of diabetes duration
- Low risk of vision-threatening retinal lesions prior to 12 years of age
- Lower frequency of eye examinations recommended in adolescents with A1C near goal range
- Autonomous AI screening increases access to routine diabetic retinopathy screening
- Referrals to eye care professionals with diabetic retinopathy expertise for pediatric patients
- Retinopathy risk increases with longer diabetes duration and post-puberty status due to cumulative hyperglycemic damage to retinal microvasculature

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Diabetes duration of 5–10 years for retinopathy risk
- Post-puberty status for retinopathy development
- Age under 12 years for low risk of vision-threatening lesions
- A1C near goal range for reduced eye examination frequency
- Access to autonomous AI screening or specialized eye care professionals
- Expertise in pediatric diabetic retinopathy counseling for referred eye care professionals

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Previously recommended annual eye examinations for all adolescents regardless of A1C control
- Routine screening protocols not accounting for age or puberty status
- Exclusive reliance on traditional ophthalmologic screening without AI augmentation

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Reduced eye examination frequency in adolescents with A1C near goal range
- Autonomous AI screening for diabetic retinopathy to improve access
- Referral to eye care professionals with pediatric diabetic retinopathy expertise
- Risk stratification based on age, puberty status, and diabetes duration
- Emphasis on early detection and intervention counseling for pediatric patients
- Use of DCCT adolescent cohort data to justify reduced eye examination frequency in well-controlled adolescents
