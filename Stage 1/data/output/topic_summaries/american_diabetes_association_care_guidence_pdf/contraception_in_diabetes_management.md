# contraception in diabetes management
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Contraception in diabetes management for individuals of childbearing potential

**Core Recommendations:**
- Individuals of childbearing potential with diabetes must use one or two forms of contraception when taking glucose-lowering medications with unknown risks, limited safety evidence, or known risks during pregnancy, regardless of pregnancy intention.
- Contraception options and their interactions with glucose-lowering medications should be discussed with all individuals of childbearing potential with diabetes.
- Medications affecting gastrointestinal emptying (e.g., GLP-1 RAs, dual GIP/GLP-1 RAs like tirzepatide) may reduce the effectiveness of oral contraception, particularly at initiation or dose increases.
- Individuals starting or increasing tirzepatide doses should use a second form of contraception until the maintenance dose is achieved and used for at least 4 weeks.
- Preconception counseling should be part of routine care for individuals with diabetes of childbearing potential, including medication risks, benefits, and adjustments prior to pregnancy.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Individuals must be of childbearing potential (e.g., not postmenopausal, surgically sterilized, or otherwise unable to conceive).
- Glucose-lowering medications must have unknown risks, limited safety evidence, or known risks during pregnancy.
- Contraception counseling must include interactions between glucose-lowering medications and contraceptive methods.
- For tirzepatide users, a second contraceptive method is required until maintenance dose is achieved and sustained for 4 weeks.
- Preconception counseling must address medication adjustments and glycemic goals prior to planned pregnancy.

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Assuming contraception is unnecessary for individuals with diabetes who do not intend to become pregnant.
- Relying solely on oral contraception without considering interactions with glucose-lowering medications like GLP-1 RAs or tirzepatide.
- Not counseling individuals on the impact of gastric-emptying-altering medications on contraceptive efficacy.

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Mandatory use of one or two contraceptive methods for individuals of childbearing potential on high-risk glucose-lowering medications.
- Inclusion of contraceptive efficacy discussions in routine diabetes care for childbearing individuals.
- Requirement for dual contraception during tirzepatide initiation or dose escalation until maintenance dose is achieved.
- Integration of preconception counseling into standard diabetes care for individuals of childbearing potential.
- Emphasis on the transient but significant impact of GLP-1 RAs and tirzepatide on oral contraceptive absorption.
