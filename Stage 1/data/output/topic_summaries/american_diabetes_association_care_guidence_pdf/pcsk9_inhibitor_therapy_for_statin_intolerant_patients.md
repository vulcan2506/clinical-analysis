# PCSK9 inhibitor therapy for statin-intolerant patients
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** PCSK9 inhibitor therapy for statin-intolerant patients

**Core Recommendations:**
- PCSK9 monoclonal antibodies (alirocumab, evolocumab) reduce LDL cholesterol by ~55% in statin-intolerant patients
- Fewer skeletal muscle-related adverse effects compared to statins or ezetimibe in statin-intolerant populations
- Inclisiran (PCSK9 siRNA) reduces LDL cholesterol by ~45% in statin-intolerant patients, with sustained effects over 4 years
- Inclisiran reduces LDL cholesterol by ~45% in statin-intolerant patients regardless of background statin therapy status (including those not on statins)
- Low-dose statins in combination with PCSK9 inhibitors increase the incidence of musculoskeletal adverse effects compared to PCSK9 inhibitors alone

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Documented statin intolerance (e.g., muscle-related adverse effects preventing statin use)
- Moderate to very high cardiovascular risk
- Primary hypercholesterolemia or high LDL cholesterol despite other lipid-lowering therapies
- Documented statin intolerance must include muscle-related adverse effects (e.g., myalgia, myopathy) that prevent statin use

**Previous Recommendations:**
- High-dose statin rechallenge in statin-intolerant patients due to increased musculoskeletal adverse effects
- Routine use of ezetimibe alone as primary therapy for statin-intolerant patients with high cardiovascular risk

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Alirocumab and evolocumab as preferred PCSK9 inhibitors for statin-intolerant patients with high LDL cholesterol
- Inclisiran as an alternative PCSK9 inhibitor option for statin-intolerant patients, including those not on statin therapy
- Use of low-dose statins in combination with PCSK9 inhibitors in select statin-intolerant patients to balance efficacy and tolerability
- Inclisiran as an option for statin-intolerant patients regardless of statin therapy status (including those not on statins)
- Use of low-dose statins in combination with PCSK9 inhibitors is associated with increased musculoskeletal adverse effects and should be approached with caution
