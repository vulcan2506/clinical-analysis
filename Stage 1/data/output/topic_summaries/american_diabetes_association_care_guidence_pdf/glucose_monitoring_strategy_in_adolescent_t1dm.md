# glucose monitoring strategy in adolescent T1DM
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Early and sustained CGM use in adolescent T1DM

**Core Recommendations:**
- Initiate continuous glucose monitoring (CGM) within the first year of T1DM diagnosis to improve glycemic outcomes
- Maintain uninterrupted CGM access to sustain HbA1c reductions in youth with public insurance
- Use structured individualized education to enhance CGM adoption and adherence in poorly controlled T1DM
- Leverage team-based care models (e.g., Teamwork, Targets, Technology, and Tight Control) for early CGM initiation
- Prioritize real-time CGM over intermittently scanned CGM in adolescents for tighter glycemic control
- Integrate peer mentoring programs to support CGM adoption and reduce diabetes distress in underserved adolescent T1DM populations
- Utilize professional CGM (pCGM) for data-driven clinical decision-making in adolescents with poorly controlled T1DM

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Newly diagnosed or existing adolescent T1DM patients
- Access to diabetes education and technology training
- Healthcare team with CGM implementation expertise
- Insurance coverage or financial support for CGM devices
- Patient/family willingness to engage in frequent glucose monitoring
- Healthcare team proficiency in interpreting CGM data and providing actionable feedback
- Access to dermatology or allergy specialists for managing skin-related adverse reactions to CGM devices

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Routine reliance on self-monitoring of blood glucose (SMBG) as the sole monitoring strategy in adolescents
- Delayed CGM initiation beyond the first year post-diagnosis
- Intermittently scanned CGM (isCGM) as the primary monitoring method in high-risk adolescents
- Non-structured or ad-hoc diabetes education for CGM users
- Discontinuation of CGM due to transient technical or skin-related issues
- Discontinuation of CGM due to minor technical issues without attempting troubleshooting or device replacement
- Reliance on self-monitoring of blood glucose (SMBG) as the sole monitoring strategy in adolescents with suboptimal glycemic control

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Early CGM initiation (<1 year post-diagnosis) linked to improved long-term glycemic outcomes
- Uninterrupted CGM access associated with sustained HbA1c reductions in underserved youth
- Structured education programs to optimize CGM use in adolescents with T1DM
- Team-based care models integrating CGM for tight glycemic control in newly diagnosed T1DM
- Real-time CGM prioritization over isCGM for adolescents with suboptimal glycemic control
- Peer mentoring programs improve CGM use and reduce diabetes distress in underserved adolescent T1DM populations
- Professional CGM (pCGM) enhances clinical decision-making and glycemic outcomes in adolescents with poorly controlled T1DM
