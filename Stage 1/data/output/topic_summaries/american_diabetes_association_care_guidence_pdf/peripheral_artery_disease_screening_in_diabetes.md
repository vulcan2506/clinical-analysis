# peripheral artery disease screening in diabetes
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Peripheral artery disease (PAD) screening in diabetes

**Core Recommendations:**
- Screen all people with diabetes aged ≥50 years using ankle-brachial index (ABI) and repeat every 5 years if normal
- Perform initial PAD screening with history of leg fatigue, claudication, rest pain, and physical examination including lower-extremity pulses, capillary refill, rubor on dependency, pallor on elevation, and venous filling time
- Use toe systolic blood pressure (<30 mmHg) for more accurate PAD diagnosis in people with diabetes due to noncompressible vessels affecting ABI accuracy
- Refer individuals with abnormal pulse volume recordings and toe pressures <30 mmHg with foot ulcers for formal vascular evaluation and angiography
- Intensify pharmacologic therapies (e.g., LDL-lowering, low-dose rivaroxaban, GLP-1 RA) in people with diabetes and PAD to improve cardiovascular and limb outcomes
- Assess individuals' understanding of foot care education and their physical ability to conduct proper foot surveillance and care, with assistance from family members or caregivers if needed
- Use advanced wound therapy (e.g., topical oxygen therapy) for chronic DFUs failing standard care after 4 weeks of appropriate wound management (50% reduction in wound size not achieved)

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Diabetes diagnosis with age ≥50 years
- Presence of microvascular disease, clinical foot complications, or end-organ damage from diabetes
- Duration of diabetes ≥10 years
- History of smoking, hypertension, dyslipidemia, or prior cardiovascular disease
- Symptoms of leg fatigue, claudication, or rest pain, or abnormal physical examination findings
- Presence of microvascular disease (e.g., neuropathy, retinopathy) as a risk factor for adverse outcomes in PAD
- History of prior cardiovascular disease (CVD) as a risk factor for PAD in diabetes

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Reliance solely on classical claudication symptoms for PAD diagnosis due to high prevalence of asymptomatic PAD in diabetes
- Use of ABI alone without corroborating clinical findings or toe pressures in people with diabetes
- Routine screening for PAD without subsequent intensification of pharmacologic therapies
- Self-care education alone without reinforcement or assessment of patient understanding and physical ability
- Use of canagliflozin in individuals with significant lower-limb complications due to increased risk of amputation
- Routine use of hyperbaric oxygen therapy for nonhealing DFUs without ischemia and/or infection due to lack of robust supporting evidence

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Incorporation of toe systolic blood pressure (<30 mmHg) as a more accurate diagnostic tool for PAD in diabetes
- Recommendation for screening all people with diabetes aged ≥50 years using non-invasive arterial studies, with repeat screening every 5 years if normal
- Use of GLP-1 RA agents (e.g., semaglutide) for PAD management in diabetes, showing benefits in reducing major adverse limb events and improving walking capacity
- Adoption of the Wound Ischemia foot Infection (WIFI) staging system for diabetic foot lesions to stage PAD severity and predict DFU healing
- Implementation of advanced wound therapies (e.g., topical oxygen therapy) for chronic DFUs failing standard care, with preference for grade A evidence-based products
- Adoption of smart mats, smart insoles, or socks for self-monitoring of foot temperatures as part of an interactive prevention protocol for early identification of impending ulceration
- Use of FDA-approved living bioengineered skin substitutes (e.g., Apligraf, Dermagraft) and growth factor products (e.g., becaplermin) for advanced wound care in chronic DFUs
