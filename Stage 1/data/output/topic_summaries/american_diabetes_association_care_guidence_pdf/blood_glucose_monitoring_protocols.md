# blood glucose monitoring protocols
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Blood glucose monitoring protocols

**Core Recommendations:**
- People with diabetes should be provided with BGM devices as indicated by circumstances, preferences, and treatment.
- People using CGM must also have access to BGM at all times.
- Insulin users should check blood glucose levels when appropriate, including fasting, pre/post meals, bedtime, during hypoglycemia, after treatment, and before critical tasks like driving.
- Healthcare professionals must use FDA-approved meters with proven accuracy and properly stored, unexpired test strips.
- Glucose monitoring results should guide therapy adjustments, medical nutrition therapy, physical activity, and medication changes.
- Healthcare professionals should be aware of potential interference of medications and substances on glucose levels measured by blood glucose meters when interpreting BGM results.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- BGM devices are indicated based on patient circumstances, preferences, and treatment type.
- Insulin users must check blood glucose levels at specific times relevant to their therapy.
- Only FDA-approved meters with proven accuracy and properly stored test strips should be used.
- CGM users must have access to BGM for calibration, suspected inaccuracies, or rapid glucose changes.
- Glucose monitoring should be integrated into multifactorial interventions for insulin-treated individuals.
- BGM may be helpful in noninsulin therapies when modifying meal plans, physical activity plans, and/or medications (particularly those that can cause hypoglycemia) as part of a treatment adjustment program.

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Routine fixed-frequency BGM schedules without patient-specific tailoring.
- Use of non-FDA-approved or unproven blood glucose meters.
- Reliance solely on CGM without access to BGM for critical situations.

**Updated Recommendations:**
- CGM as a preferred method for glucose monitoring in many settings due to improved outcomes.
- Patient-specific BGM and CGM frequency and timing based on individual needs and goals.
- Awareness of medication and substance interference with glucose meter accuracy.
- BGM use in noninsulin therapies when modifying meal plans, activity, or medications.
- Integration of glucose testing results into therapy adjustments for medical nutrition and physical activity.
- Glucose monitoring (BGM or CGM) results should be integrated into therapy adjustments to guide medical nutrition therapy, physical activity, and medication changes, particularly prandial insulin or correction bolus doses.
