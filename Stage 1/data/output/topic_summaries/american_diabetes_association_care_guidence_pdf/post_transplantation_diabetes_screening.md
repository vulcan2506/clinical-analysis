# post-transplantation diabetes screening
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Post-transplantation diabetes screening and management

**Core Recommendations:**
- Screen for hyperglycemia after organ transplantation.
- Use OGTT as the preferred diagnostic test for PTDM.
- Diagnose PTDM once the patient is stable on maintenance immunosuppression (typically 3 months) and free of acute infection.
- Treat hyperglycemia appropriately regardless of immunosuppression type, prioritizing insulin in the hospital setting.
- Use noninsulin glucose-lowering therapies for long-term management based on side effects, drug interactions, and cardiovascular/kidney benefits.
- Exclude pretransplant undiagnosed diabetes from PTDM classification when using the term 'new-onset diabetes after transplantation (NODAT)'.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Patient must be stable on an immunosuppressive plan before formal PTDM diagnosis.
- Absence of acute infection at the time of screening.
- Screening should occur as early as 3 months post-transplant.
- Use of immunosuppressive plans optimized for graft survival and patient outcomes.
- Consider FPG and/or A1C for initial high-risk identification before OGTT.
- Screening for high-risk individuals using FPG and/or A1C before OGTT to reduce unnecessary testing burden.

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Routine diagnosis of PTDM during early post-transplant period (e.g., first few weeks) when hyperglycemia is often stress- or glucocorticoid-induced and transient.
- Use of fixed numeric A1C targets for PTDM diagnosis without considering timing or clinical stability.
- Exclusive reliance on FPG or A1C for definitive PTDM diagnosis without confirmatory OGTT.

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Formal definition of PTDM as diabetes in the post-transplant setting irrespective of onset timing.
- OGTT as the preferred diagnostic test for PTDM.
- Emphasis on individualized treatment of hyperglycemia based on immunosuppression interactions and patient-specific risks.
- Recognition of PTDM as a significant risk factor for cardiovascular disease and chronic kidney disease in transplant recipients.
- Use of insulin as the agent of choice for hyperglycemia management in the hospital setting post-transplant.
- Recognition of transplant-specific risk factors for PTDM beyond general diabetes risks (e.g., immunosuppressant use).
- Acknowledgment that early posttransplant hyperglycemia (~90% prevalence) is often transient and resolves by discharge, necessitating delayed formal PTDM diagnosis.
