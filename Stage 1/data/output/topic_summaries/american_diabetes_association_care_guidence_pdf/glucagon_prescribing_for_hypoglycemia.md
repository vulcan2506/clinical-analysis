# glucagon prescribing for hypoglycemia
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Glucagon prescribing and administration for hypoglycemia

**Core Recommendations:**
- Prescribe glucagon to all individuals treated with insulin or at high risk of hypoglycemia
- Routinely review access to glucagon due to low current prescribing rates
- Instruct close contacts (family, staff, etc.) on glucagon use and explicitly educate them never to administer insulin during hypoglycemia
- Prefer intranasal or ready-to-inject glucagon over powder formulations for ease and speed of administration
- Verify insurance coverage and prescribe formulary products whenever possible
- Store glucagon products according to specific product instructions to maintain stability and efficacy
- Replace glucagon products when they reach their expiration date to ensure safe and effective use

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Patient must be treated with insulin or be at high risk of hypoglycemia
- Patient must be unable or unwilling to consume carbohydrates by mouth
- Close contacts must be explicitly educated on administration and contraindications (insulin administration)
- Individuals unable or unwilling to consume carbohydrates by mouth must be prescribed glucagon

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Routine dispensing of glucagon powder requiring reconstitution prior to injection

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Use of intranasal and ready-to-inject glucagon preparations as preferred options
- Mandatory education for non-healthcare professionals (e.g., family, coaches) on glucagon administration
- Healthcare providers should check insurance coverage and prescribe formulary products to optimize accessibility and affordability
