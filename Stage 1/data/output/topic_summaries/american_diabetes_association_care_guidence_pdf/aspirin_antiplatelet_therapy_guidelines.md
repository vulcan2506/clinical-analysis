# aspirin antiplatelet therapy guidelines
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Aspirin antiplatelet therapy in diabetes

**Core Recommendations:**
- Use aspirin (75–162 mg/day) for secondary prevention in diabetes with a history of ASCVD.
- Use clopidogrel (75 mg/day) for ASCVD patients with documented aspirin allergy.
- Determine dual antiplatelet therapy duration post-acute coronary syndrome, stroke, or TIA via interprofessional team including cardiovascular or neurological specialists.
- Consider low-dose aspirin (81 mg) plus rivaroxaban (2.5 mg twice daily) for stable coronary/PAD with low bleeding risk.
- Individualize primary prevention aspirin use in diabetes with increased cardiovascular risk after shared decision-making on benefits vs. bleeding risk.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Documented ASCVD for secondary prevention aspirin use.
- Confirmed aspirin allergy for clopidogrel substitution.
- Low bleeding risk for dual therapy with aspirin and rivaroxaban.
- Increased cardiovascular risk (e.g., ≥50 years with additional risk factors) for primary prevention consideration.
- Shared decision-making including bleeding risk assessment for primary prevention.
- Absence of increased bleeding risk (e.g., older age, anemia, kidney disease) for primary prevention aspirin consideration.
- Presence of at least one additional major ASCVD risk factor (hypertension, dyslipidemia, smoking, obesity, or CKD) for primary prevention in adults aged ≥50 years.

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Fixed-dose aspirin recommendations without consideration of bleeding risk.
- Primary prevention aspirin use in older adults (>70 years) due to unfavorable risk-benefit ratio.
- Aspirin use in low ASCVD risk individuals (<50 years with no additional risk factors).
- High-dose aspirin (325 mg/day) as superior to low-dose (81 mg) in established CVD.
- Routine aspirin use in primary prevention without individualized risk assessment.

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Dual therapy with low-dose aspirin and rivaroxaban (2.5 mg twice daily) for stable coronary/PAD with low bleeding risk.
- Interprofessional team approach to determine dual antiplatelet therapy duration post-acute events.
- Shared decision-making framework for primary prevention aspirin in diabetes with high cardiovascular risk.
- Coronary calcium scoring to tailor primary prevention aspirin therapy in low-risk individuals.
- Emphasis on lowest effective aspirin dose (75–162 mg/day) to minimize side effects.
- Use of coronary calcium scoring to tailor aspirin therapy in low-risk individuals.
- Recommendation against aspirin use in adults >70 years for primary prevention due to unfavorable risk-benefit ratio.
