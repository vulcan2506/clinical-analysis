# telehealth integration in diabetes care
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Telehealth integration in diabetes care

**Core Recommendations:**
- Telehealth should complement, not replace, in-person visits for optimal glycemic management.
- Telehealth uses digital tools (video conferencing, mobile apps, remote monitoring) to deliver clinical care, education, and administrative support.
- Interactive strategies (web portals, text messaging, medication adjustment) improve glycemic outcomes.
- Telehealth reduces geographic and transportation barriers for underresourced populations and individuals with disabilities.
- DSMES delivered via mHealth shows modest A1C benefits compared to usual care.
- Telemedicine (subset of telehealth) focuses specifically on remote clinical care, supporting diagnosis, treatment, and consultations through real-time communication.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Infrastructure investments (laptops, mobile access, internet) and targeted support (digital literacy education, community health workers) are necessary for success.
- Telehealth design must consider accessibility, affordability, connectivity, trust, and contextual tailoring.
- Curricula should be tailored to demographic characteristics, diabetes knowledge, emotional support, and cultural beliefs.
- DSMES must integrate clinical content, behavioral strategies (goal setting, problem-solving), and engagement with psychosocial concerns.
- Patient populations with limited physical access to healthcare (e.g., rural, disabled) benefit most from telehealth.
- High-quality DSMES must integrate clinical content, behavioral strategies (goal setting, problem-solving), and engagement with psychosocial concerns per national standards.

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Reliance solely on in-person visits without telehealth integration for glycemic management.
- One-size-fits-all DSMES without addressing digital divide barriers.
- Telehealth interventions that do not incorporate medication adjustment or interactive communication.
- DSMES programs that fail to tailor content to cultural beliefs or emotional support needs.
- Telehealth strategies that ignore infrastructure and digital literacy requirements.

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Telehealth as a complementary tool to in-person care for glycemic management.
- mHealth-based DSMES with modest A1C benefits (DSME: –0.3%, DSMS: –0.4%, DSMES: –0.2%).
- Targeted telehealth support for marginalized populations (e.g., Black and Hispanic adults with type 2 diabetes: A1C reduction of –0.47%).
- Telehealth for pediatric populations (type 1 diabetes) and young adults addressing SDOH.
- Systematic integration of digital literacy education and community health workers in telehealth programs.
- Telehealth interventions for type 1 diabetes in children and adolescents show effectiveness in glycemic management.
