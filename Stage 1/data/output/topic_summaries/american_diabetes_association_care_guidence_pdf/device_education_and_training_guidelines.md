# device education and training guidelines
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Device education and training guidelines

**Core Recommendations:**
- Education and training recommendations are now device-specific (e.g., CGM, AID systems, CSII).
- Healthcare professionals must provide tailored support for device initiation and use.
- Schools and workplaces must accommodate device management (e.g., time for glucose monitoring, device adjustments).
- CGM use is recommended at diabetes onset and anytime thereafter for insulin users and those on hypoglycemia-inducing therapies.
- AID systems are the preferred insulin delivery method for type 1 diabetes and certain type 2 diabetes cases.
- Healthcare professionals should receive device-specific training to support patients effectively, including understanding operational complexities and failure modes of CGM, CSII, and AID systems.
- Education and training should address the unique risks associated with each device type, such as hypoglycemia management with AID systems and device integration into daily life.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Device initiation should not require C-peptide levels, islet autoantibodies, or duration of insulin treatment for CSII or AID.
- Individuals using CGM must also have access to blood glucose monitoring.
- Children and adolescents in school settings require support for diabetes technology use.
- Adults (≥18 years) in workplaces need reasonable accommodations for device management.
- AID systems may be considered for type 2 diabetes on basal insulin not meeting glycemic goals.
- Individuals using CGM must have access to blood glucose monitoring for calibration or confirmation of readings, even if CGM is the primary monitoring method.
- Children and adolescents in school settings require structured support for diabetes technology use, including training for school staff on device management and emergency protocols.

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Intermittently scanned CGM (isCGM) is no longer discussed as a primary device type.
- Fixed numeric A1C targets are no longer emphasized for device initiation decisions.
- Intermittently scanned CGM (isCGM) is no longer endorsed as a primary device type, though it may still be referenced as an older option.

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Real-time CGM (rtCGM), over-the-counter CGM (OTC-CGM), and professional CGM are now the defined CGM device types.
- Recommendation 7.3a: Guidance for prescribing, initiating, and following CGM devices.
- Recommendation 7.3b: Guidance for prescribing, initiating, and following AID systems.
- Recommendation 7.8a: No prerequisites for CSII or AID initiation based on C-peptide, autoantibodies, or insulin duration.
- Recommendation 7.25a: AID systems are preferred for type 1 diabetes and select type 2 diabetes cases.
- Recommendation 7.15: CGM use is recommended at diabetes onset and anytime thereafter for insulin users, those on hypoglycemia-inducing therapies, and any diabetes treatment where CGM aids management, regardless of age, sex, education, income, or baseline diabetes characteristics.
- Recommendation 7.25a: AID systems are the preferred insulin delivery method for type 1 diabetes and for adults and children with type 2 diabetes on multiple daily injections, CSII, or sensor-augmented pump therapy, as well as other forms of insulin-deficient diabetes.
