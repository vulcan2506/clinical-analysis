# diabetic foot examination protocol
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Diabetic foot examination protocol

**Core Recommendations:**
- Perform a comprehensive foot evaluation at least annually to identify risk factors for ulcers and amputations.
- Include inspection of skin, assessment of foot deformities, neurological assessment (10-g monofilament or Ipswich touch test with at least one additional assessment: pinprick, temperature, or vibration), and vascular assessment (pulses in legs and feet).
- Inspect feet at every visit for individuals with sensory loss, prior ulceration, or amputation.
- Obtain history of ulceration, amputation, Charcot foot, vascular interventions, smoking, retinopathy, and renal disease, and assess current symptoms of neuropathy and vascular disease.
- Provide general preventive foot self-care education to all people with diabetes, including daily foot inspection techniques.
- Assess capillary refill time, rubor on dependency, pallor on elevation, and venous filling time as part of initial PAD screening.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Diabetes diagnosis
- Evidence of sensory loss, prior ulceration, or amputation (for more frequent examinations)
- History of foot complications or high-risk conditions (e.g., PAD, Charcot foot, dialysis)
- Patient willingness to participate in self-care education
- Access to appropriate diagnostic tools (e.g., 10-g monofilament, vascular assessment tools)
- Referral for ankle-brachial index with toe pressures for individuals with history of leg fatigue, claudication, rest pain relieved with dependency, or decreased/absent pedal pulses.

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Reliance on 10-g monofilament testing alone for diagnosing early diabetic peripheral neuropathy (DPN).
- Use of nerve conduction studies, electromyography, nerve biopsy, or intraepidermal nerve fiber density biopsies for routine DPN diagnosis.
- Fixed-frequency foot examinations without risk stratification.
- Exclusion of smoking cessation counseling in high-risk patients.

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Incorporation of Ipswich touch test as an alternative to 10-g monofilament for LOPS screening.
- Referral to foot care specialists for individuals with high-risk feet (e.g., dialysis, Charcot foot, prior ulcers/amputations, PAD).
- Use of specialized therapeutic footwear for high-risk individuals (e.g., LOPS, deformities, ulcers, poor circulation).
- Consideration of adjunctive treatments (e.g., negative-pressure wound therapy, skin substitutes, topical oxygen therapy) for chronic non-healing foot ulcers.
- Interprofessional approach facilitated by a podiatrist for high-risk foot care.
- Refer individuals who smoke and have high-risk features (prior lower-extremity complications, LOPS, structural abnormalities, or PAD) to smoking cessation counseling.
