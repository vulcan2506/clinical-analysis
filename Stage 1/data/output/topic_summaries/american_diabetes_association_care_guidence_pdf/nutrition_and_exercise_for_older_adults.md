# nutrition and exercise for older adults
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Nutrition and exercise for older adults with diabetes

**Core Recommendations:**
- Recommend healthful eating with adequate protein intake (at least 0.8 g/kg body weight/day) to maintain lean body mass and function
- Recommend regular physical activity including aerobic, weight-bearing, and resistance training as tolerated
- Tailor lifestyle management to frailty status, addressing sarcopenia, dynapenia, and osteopenia risks
- Consider intensive lifestyle interventions (dietary changes, physical activity, 5–7% weight loss) for older adults with type 2 diabetes and overweight/obesity who can exercise safely
- Focus on functional status and quality of life rather than weight loss in frail older adults
- Consider malnutrition screening (e.g., MNA-SF) and interventions for older adults with diabetes, given its association with sarcopenia, frailty, and functional decline
- Match complexity of treatment plans to self-management ability and available social/medical support (e.g., independent living, caregiver availability, facility-based care)

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Older adults with diabetes and the capacity to exercise safely
- Assessment of frailty status, malnutrition risk, and functional limitations
- Individualized protein intake goals (0.8–1.5 g/kg body weight/day or 15–20% of calories)
- Structured physical activity programs (e.g., LIFE study) for frail older adults
- Shared decision-making to align goals with patient-specific factors (life expectancy, cognitive function, hypoglycemia risk)
- Assessment of hypoglycemia risk factors (e.g., cognitive impairment, renal impairment, polypharmacy) before selecting or deintensifying medications
- Evaluation of self-management capacity (e.g., ability to perform glucose monitoring, medication administration) and social support systems (e.g., caregiver availability, living arrangement)

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Fixed numeric A1C targets for older adults
- High-protein diets beyond general recommendations (0.8–1.5 g/kg/day) for glycemic or cardiovascular outcomes
- Intensive weight-loss interventions in frail older adults without functional capacity
- Complex treatment plans with high hypoglycemia risk (e.g., insulin, sulfonylureas) without deintensification
- One-size-fits-all dietary or exercise recommendations without tailoring to frailty or sarcopenia
- Fixed A1C targets without individualized consideration of hypoglycemia risk, life expectancy, or functional status
- Intensive glycemic management with high hypoglycemia risk (e.g., insulin, sulfonylureas) in older adults with complex health status or limited life expectancy

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Moderately higher protein intake (20–30%) for satiety and diabetes management in select older adults
- Combined protein supplementation and resistance exercise for sarcopenia management
- Deintensification of hypoglycemia-causing medications (e.g., insulin, sulfonylureas) in high-risk older adults
- Cost-of-care considerations to reduce barriers to medication adherence and self-management
- Use of the 4S pathway for assessing treatment difficulty and deintensifying therapy
- Use the 4S pathway (Fig. 13.2) for assessing treatment difficulty, reevaluating glycemic goals via shared decision-making, and deintensifying/simplifying treatments in older adults
- Consider cost-of-care and insurance coverage when selecting glucose-lowering agents to reduce barriers to medication adherence and self-management
