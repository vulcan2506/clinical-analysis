# insulin therapy indications
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** insulin therapy indications and management in type 2 diabetes

**Core Recommendations:**
- Insulin therapy is indicated for progressive type 2 diabetes when oral agents are insufficient to achieve glycemic goals.
- Basal insulin alone is the preferred initial insulin treatment, with doses estimated at 0.1–0.2 units/kg/day and individualized titration.
- Prandial insulin may be added if basal insulin is titrated to goal fasting glucose but A1C remains above target or postprandial hyperglycemia persists.
- GLP-1 receptor agonists or dual GIP/GLP-1 receptor agonists should be considered before prandial insulin to reduce hypoglycemia and weight gain risks.
- Insulin therapy should be tailored to patient literacy, numeracy, and self-management capabilities with comprehensive education.
- Insulin therapy may be used temporarily for acute situations such as hospitalization, acute illness, or high-dose glucocorticoid therapy, even if oral agents are still effective.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Type 2 diabetes with inadequate glycemic control despite noninsulin therapies.
- Documented progressive hyperglycemia or failure to achieve individualized A1C targets.
- Patient education and involvement in insulin management, including self-titration and hypoglycemia prevention.
- Assessment of hypoglycemia risk, health literacy, and numeracy to ensure safe insulin use.
- Regular glucose monitoring (self-monitored blood glucose or CGM) to guide titration and adjustments.
- Conversion between different basal insulin formulations may require an initial dose reduction of 10–20% when switching from human NPH insulin or U-300 glargine to another insulin, particularly in individuals in very tight management or at high risk for hypoglycemia.

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Use of sulfonylureas, meglitinides, and DPP-4 inhibitors as adjuncts to insulin due to lack of additional benefits and increased hypoglycemia/weight gain risks.
- Routine use of NPH insulin over long-acting basal analogs for reduced nocturnal hypoglycemia risk.
- Fixed numeric A1C targets without consideration of patient-specific factors such as life expectancy or hypoglycemia risk.
- Reliance on premixed human insulins (e.g., 70/30 NPH/regular) when insulin analogs offer better flexibility and reduced hypoglycemia.
- Use of inhaled insulin in patients with chronic lung disease or those who smoke or recently quit.
- Routine use of sulfonylureas, meglitinides, and DPP-4 inhibitors as adjuncts to insulin due to lack of additional benefits on cardiovascular, kidney, weight, or liver outcomes, and increased risks of hypoglycemia and weight gain.

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Emphasis on individualized insulin therapy with avoidance of insulin as a threat or sign of personal failure.
- Use of long-acting basal analogs (e.g., U-300 glargine, degludec) to reduce nocturnal hypoglycemia risk.
- Integration of GLP-1 receptor agonists or dual GIP/GLP-1 receptor agonists with basal insulin before prandial insulin intensification.
- Consideration of concentrated insulin preparations (e.g., U-500, U-300, U-200) for patients requiring high doses to improve convenience and adherence.
- Use of inhaled insulin with mandatory spirometry screening and contraindication in chronic lung disease or recent smoking.
- Concentrated insulin preparations (e.g., U-500 regular insulin, U-300 glargine, U-200 degludec) may be used for individuals requiring high doses to improve convenience, comfort, and treatment engagement, with specific dosing considerations for U-500 regular insulin (e.g., use of U-500 syringes to minimize dosing errors).
- Inhaled insulin requires mandatory spirometry (FEV₁) testing before and after initiation to screen for chronic lung disease and is contraindicated in individuals with asthma, COPD, or those who smoke or recently quit smoking.
