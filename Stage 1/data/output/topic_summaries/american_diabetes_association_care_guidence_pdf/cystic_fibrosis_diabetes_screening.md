# cystic fibrosis diabetes screening
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Cystic fibrosis diabetes screening

**Core Recommendations:**
- Annual screening for cystic fibrosis–related diabetes (CFRD) should begin by age 10 years in all people with cystic fibrosis, preferably using OGTT.
- If OGTT is not feasible, A1C can be used as an alternative first-line screening tool with A1C 5.5–6.4% prompting OGTT within 3 months.
- A1C ≥6.5% (≥48 mmol/mol) is consistent with a diagnosis of CFRD and requires confirmatory testing.
- Weight loss or failure of expected weight gain in cystic fibrosis patients should prompt screening for CFRD regardless of age.
- Annual monitoring for diabetes complications is recommended beginning 5 years after CFRD diagnosis.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- People with cystic fibrosis starting at age 10 years.
- OGTT preferred if feasible; A1C used as alternative when OGTT is not feasible.
- A1C 5.5–6.4% requires follow-up OGTT within 3 months.
- Weight loss or failure to gain weight in cystic fibrosis patients.
- Confirmation of A1C ≥6.5% with repeat A1C or OGTT.
- A1C of 5.5% provides 98% negative predictive value for CFRD detection, supporting its use to identify patients who do not require further testing.
- A1C of 5.8% provides 95% sensitivity to detect CFRD by OGTT.

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Screening for CFRD before age 10 years without established benefit for weight, height, BMI, or lung function.
- Use of FPG alone for diagnosis due to lower sensitivity in people with cystic fibrosis.
- Routine use of CGM for CFRD screening outside research settings due to lack of validated thresholds.

**Updated Recommendations:**
- A1C as a two-step screening strategy alternative to OGTT for CFRD.
- A1C 5.5% threshold with 91% sensitivity and 98% negative predictive value for CFRD detection.
- Confirmation of A1C ≥6.5% with repeat A1C or OGTT for CFRD diagnosis.
- Annual monitoring for diabetes complications starting 5 years after CFRD diagnosis.
- Insulin therapy for people with CFRD to attain individualized glycemic goals.
- Use of A1C as a first-line screening tool to identify patients who should not undergo further testing with OGTT (negative predictive value approach).
- Use of A1C as a first-line screening tool to identify patients who should proceed to OGTT (sensitivity approach).
