# housing insecurity impact on diabetes self-management
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Housing insecurity impact on diabetes self-management

**Core Recommendations:**
- Directly associated with reduced ability to maintain appropriate diabetes self-management
- Coexists with food insecurity, lack of health insurance, cognitive impairment, behavioral health concerns, and low literacy/numeracy skills
- Requires secure storage for diabetes medications and supplies, including refrigerator access for insulin
- Assessed using validated brief risk assessment tools (e.g., for veterans)
- Healthcare professionals should connect patients to housing resources or social workers to improve diabetes care
- Prevalence of diabetes among people experiencing housing insecurity is estimated to be around 8% in the U.S.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Patient must have diabetes and experience housing insecurity
- Assessment of coexisting barriers (e.g., food insecurity, insurance status)
- Access to secure medication storage and refrigeration for insulin
- Use of validated housing insecurity risk assessment tools
- Availability of social work or housing support resources
- Healthcare professionals must be familiar with local resources to support housing-insecure individuals or have access to social workers

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Integration of housing insecurity risk assessment into diabetes care workflows
- Routine screening for housing insecurity in diabetes management plans
- Collaboration with social workers to address housing as part of diabetes care
- Provision of secure medication storage solutions for housing-insecure patients
- Education on insulin storage requirements for patients with unstable housing
