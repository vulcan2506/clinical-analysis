# depressive symptom screening
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Depressive symptom screening in diabetes

**Core Recommendations:**
- Screen for depressive symptoms in all people with diabetes at least annually and more frequently among those with a history of depression
- Refer to qualified behavioral health professionals or other healthcare professionals with experience using evidence-based treatment approaches for depression in collaboration with the diabetes care team
- Conduct annual screening for depression, anxiety, and diabetes distress to distinguish overlapping symptoms
- Perform a comprehensive clinical assessment following positive screening findings to ensure accurate diagnosis
- Incorporate person-centered integrated care approaches to improve both depression and glycemic outcomes
- Query origins and exacerbating factors for depressive symptoms to distinguish between diabetes distress and depression
- Monitor and potentially adjust the medical treatment plan in response to reduction in depressive symptoms

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- History of depression
- Current depression or antidepressant medication use
- Diabetes-related stigma (particularly in type 2 diabetes)
- Elevated depressive symptoms or depressive disorders
- Patient consent for referral to behavioral health professionals
- Use of age-appropriate validated measures for routine monitoring
- Implementation of multisite screening protocols in diabetes clinics

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Screening for depressive symptoms only in symptomatic patients
- Relying solely on non-validated measures for depression screening
- Isolated treatment of diabetes without addressing comorbid depressive symptoms
- Assuming depressive symptoms are solely due to diabetes distress without further assessment

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Use of age-appropriate validated measures for routine monitoring of depressive symptoms
- Implementation of collaborative care models integrating behavioral health professionals into the diabetes care team
- Consideration of GLP-1 receptor agonists for potential improvement in depressive symptoms in type 2 diabetes
- Integration of psychological interventions (e.g., CBT via internet, phone, or self-guided) for depressive symptoms
- Lifestyle interventions targeting nutrition and/or physical activity to improve depressive symptoms and A1C
- Use of GLP-1 receptor agonists with potential for improvement in depressive symptoms (noted as mixed evidence in meta-analyses)
- Psychological interventions delivered via internet, phone, or self-guided formats (e.g., CBT)
