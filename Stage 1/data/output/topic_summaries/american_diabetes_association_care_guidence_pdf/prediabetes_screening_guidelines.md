# prediabetes screening guidelines
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Prediabetes screening guidelines

**Core Recommendations:**
- Screening for prediabetes and type 2 diabetes risk should be done in asymptomatic adults using risk factor assessment or validated risk calculators.
- Testing should be considered in adults of any age with overweight/obesity and one or more risk factors (e.g., first-degree relative with diabetes, high-risk race/ethnicity, hypertension, dyslipidemia, polycystic ovary syndrome).
- Screening should begin at age 35 for all adults without risk factors.
- Repeat screening is recommended at minimum 3-year intervals, sooner with symptoms or risk changes (e.g., weight gain).
- Screening should be considered in children/adolescents with overweight/obesity and additional risk factors (e.g., maternal history of diabetes/GDM, family history of type 2 diabetes).
- Screening for prediabetes/diabetes should be considered in adults of any age with overweight/obesity and one or more risk factors (e.g., first-degree relative with diabetes, high-risk race/ethnicity, hypertension, dyslipidemia, polycystic ovary syndrome).
- Screening should begin at no later than age 35 years for all adults without risk factors.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Asymptomatic adults require risk factor assessment or validated risk calculator for screening.
- Adults with overweight/obesity (BMI ≥25 kg/m² or ≥23 kg/m² in Asian ancestry) must have one or more risk factors for testing.
- Children/adolescents must have overweight (BMI ≥85th percentile) or obesity (BMI ≥95th percentile) plus one or more risk factors.
- People with prediabetes (A1C 5.7–6.4%, IGT, or IFG) should be tested annually.
- People with a history of gestational diabetes mellitus (GDM) should be tested every 1–3 years.
- For OGTT screening, adequate carbohydrate intake (at least 150 g/day) should be assured for 3 days prior to testing.
- Screening for prediabetes/diabetes in people with HIV should use plasma glucose criteria (FPG preferred) due to A1C underestimation in this population.

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Fasting plasma glucose (FPG) as the sole screening method for glucocorticoid-induced hyperglycemia (postprandial or random glucose preferred).
- Reliance on A1C alone for screening in people with HIV (plasma glucose criteria preferred due to underestimation).
- Screening intervals based solely on fixed numeric targets without consideration of risk changes.

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Screening for prediabetes/diabetes in people on high-risk medications (e.g., statins, thiazide diuretics, some HIV medications, second-generation antipsychotics).
- For second-generation antipsychotics, screen at baseline, 12–16 weeks post-initiation, and annually thereafter.
- For people with HIV, screen with FPG before/during antiretroviral therapy (ART) and 3–6 months after ART initiation/switch; annual FPG if normal.
- Monitor postprandial or random glucose in people on long-term glucocorticoids (not fasting glucose).
- Use ADA risk test or similar tools to guide screening decisions.
- Screening for prediabetes/diabetes should be performed in people treated with glucocorticoids, statins, thiazide diuretics, some HIV medications, and second-generation antipsychotic medications due to increased risk of glucose abnormalities.
- For people on glucocorticoids, check postprandial or random glucose levels (not fasting glucose) due to glucocorticoid-induced hyperglycemia typically resulting from insulin resistance.
