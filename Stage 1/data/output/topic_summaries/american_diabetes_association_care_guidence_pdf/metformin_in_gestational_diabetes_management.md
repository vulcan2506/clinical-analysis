# metformin in gestational diabetes management
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Metformin use in gestational diabetes management

**Core Recommendations:**
- Metformin is associated with lower risk of neonatal hypoglycemia and less maternal weight gain compared to insulin in systematic reviews and RCTs.
- Metformin treatment monotherapy failure occurs in 14–46% of individuals.
- Metformin does not reduce the risk of GDM in high-risk individuals with obesity, polycystic ovary syndrome, or preexisting insulin resistance.
- Metformin exposure results in smaller neonates with an acceleration of postnatal growth, leading to higher BMI in childhood.
- Metformin should not be used in pregnant people with hypertension, preeclampsia, or those at risk for intrauterine growth restriction.
- Metformin treatment in pregnancy does not reduce the risk of GDM in high-risk individuals with obesity, polycystic ovary syndrome, or preexisting insulin resistance, as demonstrated in a meta-analysis of 11 RCTs (143).
- RCTs comparing metformin with other therapies for ovulation induction in individuals with polycystic ovary syndrome have not demonstrated benefit in preventing spontaneous abortion or GDM (146).

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Patient must be unable to use insulin safely or effectively due to cost, comprehension, or cultural influences.
- Known risks and the need for more long-term safety data in offspring must be discussed with the patient.
- Continuation of metformin in early pregnancy is considered safe as first-trimester use has not been associated with increased risk of major congenital malformations.
- If used, metformin can be discontinued at the end of the first trimester to avoid sudden deterioration of glycemia and associated increased risk of malformations.
- Patient must not have hypertension, preeclampsia, or be at risk for intrauterine growth restriction.
- Oral agents, including metformin, may be considered as an alternative for individuals with GDM requiring medical therapy who cannot use insulin safely or effectively due to cost, comprehension, or cultural influences, after discussing known risks and the need for long-term safety data in offspring.

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Continuation of metformin in individuals with polycystic ovary syndrome for preventing spontaneous abortion or GDM.
- Use of metformin in pregnant people with hypertension or preeclampsia.
- Use of metformin in individuals at risk for intrauterine growth restriction.
- Metformin as a standard alternative for GDM without discussing known risks and need for long-term safety data.
- Metformin for ovulation induction in individuals with polycystic ovary syndrome to prevent GDM.
- Continuation of metformin in individuals with polycystic ovary syndrome for preventing spontaneous abortion or GDM (146, 147–149).

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Metformin as an alternative oral agent for GDM when insulin is not feasible due to cost, comprehension, or cultural influences.
- Recommendation to discontinue metformin at the end of the first trimester to avoid sudden deterioration of glycemia and associated increased risk of malformations.
- Recognition that metformin readily crosses the placenta, resulting in umbilical cord blood levels as high or higher than simultaneous maternal levels.
- Evidence that metformin exposure results in smaller neonates with an acceleration of postnatal growth, resulting in higher BMI in childhood.
- Evidence that metformin treatment in pregnancy does not reduce the risk of GDM in high-risk individuals with obesity, polycystic ovary syndrome, or preexisting insulin resistance.
- Metformin can be discontinued at the end of the first trimester (after organogenesis) to avoid sudden deterioration of glycemia and an associated increased risk of malformations due to hyperglycemia (158).
- Continuation of metformin in early pregnancy is considered safe, as first-trimester metformin use has not been associated with increased risk of major congenital malformations (159,160).
