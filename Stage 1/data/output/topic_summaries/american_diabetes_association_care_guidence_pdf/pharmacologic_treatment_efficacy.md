# pharmacologic treatment efficacy
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Pharmacologic treatment efficacy in diabetes care

**Core Recommendations:**
- Medications are evaluated for glucose-lowering efficacy, hypoglycemia risk, weight effects, cardiovascular (CV) effects, kidney effects, and MASH (metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease) effects.
- Dosing adjustments are required based on kidney function for certain medications (e.g., DPP-4 inhibitors like sitagliptin, saxagliptin, alogliptin).
- Insulin therapy requires lower doses with decreased eGFR and careful titration to avoid hypoglycemia, especially in patients with impaired kidney function.
- Postmarketing concerns about pancreatitis with DPP-4 inhibitors, leading to recommendations for discontinuation if suspected

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Patient-specific factors such as kidney function must be assessed before selecting pharmacologic treatments.
- Hypoglycemia risk must be considered, particularly with insulin or human insulin formulations (NPH or premixed).
- Cardiovascular and kidney effects of medications must be evaluated to guide treatment choices.
- Assessment of heart failure (HF) risk is required when considering DPP-4 inhibitors, particularly saxagliptin, due to potential increased risk of HF hospitalization

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Fixed A1C targets without consideration of patient-specific factors (e.g., life expectancy, hypoglycemia risk).
- Use of DPP-4 inhibitors with unadjusted dosing in patients with significant kidney impairment (e.g., sitagliptin, saxagliptin, alogliptin).
- Use of human insulin (NPH or premixed formulations) in patients with impaired kidney function without careful titration and hypoglycemia risk assessment

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Linagliptin (DPP-4 inhibitor) does not require dose adjustment based on kidney function.
- Postmarketing concerns about joint pain and bullous pemphigoid with DPP-4 inhibitors, leading to recommendations for discontinuation if suspected.
- Inhaled regular insulin formulation available for patients requiring non-injectable insulin therapy.
- Inhaled regular insulin formulation available as an alternative for patients requiring non-injectable insulin therapy, with specific device compatibility considerations
