# SGLT2 inhibitor cardiovascular outcomes
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** SGLT2 inhibitor cardiovascular outcomes and hospital management

**Core Recommendations:**
- SGLT2 inhibitors reduce rates of MI, stroke, or cardiovascular death with empagliflozin and canagliflozin.
- Dapagliflozin reduces hospitalization for heart failure or cardiovascular death despite no reduction in MI/stroke.
- Ertugliflozin and bexagliflozin have not demonstrated evidence of cardiovascular risk reduction.
- SGLT2 inhibitors should be initiated or continued during hospitalization for heart failure if indicated and no contraindications exist.
- SGLT2 inhibitors should be stopped 3 days before scheduled surgeries (4 days for ertugliflozin).

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Patient must have type 2 diabetes and heart failure for hospital initiation/continuation.
- No contraindications such as severe illness, ketonemia, ketonuria, or prolonged fasting.
- Acute illness must have recovered before initiating SGLT2 inhibitors in the hospital setting.
- Proactive adjustment of insulin and diuretic dosing is required during hospitalization.
- Collaboration with a cardiology/heart failure consultation team is recommended.
- Admission glucose must be <180–200 mg/dL for the use of DPP-4 inhibitors with or without basal insulin in hospitalized patients with mild to moderate hyperglycemia.

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Use of SGLT2 inhibitors for glycemic management in the hospital setting.
- Use of SGLT2 inhibitors during prolonged fasting and surgical procedures.
- Use of SGLT2 inhibitors in cases of severe illness.
- Use of SGLT2 inhibitors in patients with ketonemia or ketonuria.
- Continuation of saxagliptin and alogliptin in patients who develop heart failure.

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Initiation or continuation of SGLT2 inhibitors during hospitalization for heart failure.
- Specific preoperative discontinuation timing: 3 days for most SGLT2 inhibitors, 4 days for ertugliflozin.
- Proactive adjustment of insulin and diuretic dosing during hospitalization and discharge.
- Individualized A1C targets based on patient-specific factors including life expectancy and hypoglycemia risk.
- Use of DPP-4 inhibitors with or without basal insulin for mild to moderate hyperglycemia on admission.
