# pediatric psychosocial screening
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Pediatric psychosocial screening in diabetes care

**Core Recommendations:**
- Screen children and adolescents with type 1, type 2, and other forms of diabetes for psychosocial concerns (e.g., diabetes distress, depressive symptoms, anxiety, disordered eating behaviors) using age-appropriate standardized and validated tools.
- Assess family factors, social determinants of health (e.g., food insecurity, housing stability, health literacy, financial barriers), and social adjustment (peer relationships, school performance).
- Encourage developmentally appropriate family involvement in diabetes management to prevent diabetes distress and deterioration in glycemia.
- Offer adolescents private time with healthcare professionals during visits.
- Incorporate reproductive health and preconception counseling into routine visits for individuals of childbearing potential.
- Screen for diabetes distress using validated tools as early as 7 or 8 years of age in children with diabetes.
- Inquire about diabetes-specific family relationships, including family teamwork and conflict, during visits.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Screening should occur at diagnosis and during routine follow-up care.
- Screening for depression should begin at age 12 years and continue thereafter.
- Screening for anxiety should begin at age 8 years and above.
- Screening for fear of hypoglycemia should be considered in children experiencing severe or frequent hypoglycemia or substantial hyperglycemia.
- Screening for disordered eating behaviors should be conducted when signs or symptoms are present.
- Referral to a behavioral health professional with experience in pediatric diabetes when psychosocial concerns are identified.
- Provide education about contraception to prevent unplanned pregnancy in individuals of childbearing potential at the onset of puberty or at diagnosis of diabetes.

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Routine use of non-validated or non-standardized psychosocial screening tools.
- Premature or unsupportive transfer of diabetes care responsibility to children without considering developmental readiness.
- Exclusion of family involvement in diabetes management for children and adolescents.
- Lack of consideration for social determinants of health in treatment decisions.
- Absence of private adolescent healthcare visits.

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Integration of behavioral health professionals as integral members of the pediatric diabetes interprofessional team.
- Use of decision aids and self-care contracts to engage children and adolescents in shared decision-making.
- Developmentally tailored education and gradual transfer of self-care responsibilities with parental guidance.
- Incorporation of reproductive health and preconception counseling for individuals of childbearing potential.
- Routine screening for food insecurity, housing stability, and other social determinants of health.
- Use of decision aids to engage children and adolescents in shared decision-making about treatment options.
- Creation of self-care contracts to facilitate shared decision-making and enhance care.
