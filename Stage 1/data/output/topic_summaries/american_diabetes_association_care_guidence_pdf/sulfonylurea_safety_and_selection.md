# sulfonylurea safety and selection
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Sulfonylurea safety and selection

**Core Recommendations:**
- Sulfonylureas are associated with hypoglycemia, bone loss, and fracture risk and should be used with caution.
- Shorter-acting sulfonylureas (e.g., glipizide) may be preferred, though glipizide has a higher risk of major adverse cardiovascular events.
- Glyburide, a longer-acting sulfonylurea with multiple active metabolites, should be avoided in older adults due to hypoglycemia risk.
- Antimicrobials (e.g., fluoroquinolones, sulfamethoxazole-trimethoprim) interact with sulfonylureas to increase hypoglycemia risk; sulfonylureas should be reduced or temporarily discontinued in these cases.
- Insulin secretagogues may need dose reduction or discontinuation during illness, fasting, or impaired eating patterns.
- Sulfonylureas may contribute to bone loss and fracture risk, independent of hypoglycemia, warranting caution in patients with osteoporosis or high fracture risk.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Consistent and adequate eating patterns are essential to reduce hypoglycemia risk.
- Older adults treated with insulin secretagogues should be routinely screened for hypoglycemia.
- Hypoglycemia awareness should be assessed in older adults on sulfonylureas.
- Prevention and treatment strategies for hypoglycemia should be reinforced in older adults.
- Renal function should be assessed before initiating sulfonylureas, as reduced clearance in renal impairment increases hypoglycemia risk.

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Glyburide should be avoided in older adults due to its hypoglycemia risk.
- Longer-acting sulfonylureas with multiple active metabolites are discouraged in older adults.

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Shorter-acting sulfonylureas (e.g., glipizide) may be preferred despite cardiovascular risk concerns.
- Routine screening for hypoglycemia in older adults on insulin secretagogues is recommended.
- Assessment of hypoglycemia awareness in older adults is emphasized.
- Consider alternative agents (e.g., DPP-4 inhibitors, SGLT2 inhibitors) in patients with a history of hypoglycemia unawareness or recurrent severe hypoglycemia.
