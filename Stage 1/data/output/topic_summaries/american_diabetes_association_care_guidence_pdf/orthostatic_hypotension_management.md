# orthostatic hypotension management
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** orthostatic hypotension management in diabetes

**Core Recommendations:**
- Therapeutic goal is to minimize postural symptoms rather than restore normotension
- Encourage physical activity and exercise to avoid deconditioning
- Use nonpharmacologic measures such as adequate salt intake, avoiding aggravating medications, and compressive garments
- Volume repletion with fluids and salt is critical
- Treat supine hypertension at bedtime with shorter-acting drugs affecting baroreceptor activity
- Encourage small-particle eating pattern to improve symptoms in diabetic gastroparesis associated with orthostatic hypotension

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Patient must have symptomatic orthostatic hypotension
- Prior evaluation to exclude reversible causes (e.g., medications, volume depletion)
- Assessment of supine hypertension before bedtime treatment
- Consideration of patient tolerance and comorbidities before initiating pharmacologic therapy
- Monitoring for adverse effects of pharmacologic agents
- Assess risk-benefit balance before withdrawing GLP-1 receptor agonists due to their potential benefits despite adverse gastrointestinal motility effects

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Use of metoclopramide beyond 12 weeks due to risk of extrapyramidal effects
- Long-term use of erythromycin due to tachyphylaxis
- Routine use of gastric electrical stimulation for diabetic gastroparesis
- Fixed numeric blood pressure targets in orthostatic hypotension management
- Uncritical continuation of GLP-1 receptor agonists without assessing gastrointestinal motility impact
- Use of gastric electrical stimulation for diabetic gastroparesis due to limited data supporting efficacy in diabetic populations

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Use of midodrine and droxidopa (FDA-approved for orthostatic hypotension)
- Bedtime treatment of supine hypertension with shorter-acting agents (e.g., guanfacine, clonidine, isradipine, atenolol, metoprolol tartrate)
- Small-particle eating pattern for symptom relief in diabetic gastroparesis
- Withdrawal of drugs with adverse gastrointestinal motility effects (e.g., opioids, anticholinergics, TCAs, GLP-1 RAs, pramlintide)
- Limited use of metoclopramide reserved for severe gastroparesis unresponsive to other therapies
- Use of enalapril as an alternative shorter-acting agent for bedtime treatment of supine hypertension when preferred agents are not tolerated
