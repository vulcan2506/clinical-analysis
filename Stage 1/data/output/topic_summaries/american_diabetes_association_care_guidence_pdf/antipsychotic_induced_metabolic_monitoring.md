# antipsychotic-induced metabolic monitoring
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** antipsychotic-induced metabolic monitoring in serious mental illness

**Core Recommendations:**
- Monitor body weight, glycemia, and lipids every 12–16 weeks in patients prescribed second-generation antipsychotics
- Adjust treatment plans based on metabolic changes
- Include nonmedical caretakers in treatment decision-making for patients with impaired judgment
- Provide enhanced care and self-management support tailored to individual capacity and needs
- Coordinate diabetes care with mental health professionals for holistic management
- Monitor glycemia, body weight, and lipids every 12–16 weeks in adolescents prescribed second-generation antipsychotics
- Provide diabetes training for community support personnel and healthcare aides involved in the care of patients with serious mental illness

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Prescription of second-generation antipsychotic medications
- Diagnosis of serious mental illness (e.g., schizophrenia, thought disorders)
- Established diabetes or prediabetes status
- Assessment of patient's ability to engage in self-management behaviors
- Inclusion of a nonmedical caretaker for patients with impaired judgment
- Established diagnosis of type 2 diabetes or prediabetes in adolescents prescribed second-generation antipsychotics
- Assessment of psychological distress levels in patients with serious mental illness to tailor enhanced care and self-management support

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Routine monitoring of glycemia, body weight, and lipids every 12–16 weeks for antipsychotic-treated patients
- Incorporation of nonmedical caretakers in treatment planning for patients with serious mental illness
- Enhanced care models including remote monitoring and diabetes training for caregivers
- Collaborative care between diabetes and mental health teams
- Use of group support and educational interventions for behavioral change
- Systematic review evidence showing nonpharmacologic interventions reduce psychiatric symptoms, total cholesterol, and LDL cholesterol in patients with type 2 diabetes and serious mental illness
