# pediatric diabetes immunization guidelines
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Pediatric diabetes immunization guidelines

**Core Recommendations:**
- Follow the American Academy of Pediatrics (AAP) Recommended Child and Adolescent Immunization Schedule for Ages 18 Years or Younger.
- Ensure routine vaccinations are up to date, including influenza, pneumococcal, and hepatitis B for children with diabetes.
- Prioritize annual influenza vaccination for all children with diabetes aged 6 months and older.
- Administer pneumococcal vaccination based on age and risk factors, including children with diabetes at higher risk for infections.
- Screen for and administer hepatitis B vaccination in unvaccinated children with diabetes, particularly those at risk for exposure.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Children with diabetes should receive all routine childhood vaccinations as per AAP guidelines.
- Annual influenza vaccination is recommended for all children with diabetes aged 6 months and older.
- Pneumococcal vaccination is recommended for children with diabetes who are at increased risk for pneumococcal infections.
- Hepatitis B vaccination is recommended for unvaccinated children with diabetes, particularly those at risk for exposure.
- Vaccination status should be reviewed at each clinical visit to ensure compliance with immunization schedules.

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Routine use of live attenuated vaccines in severely immunocompromised children with diabetes is no longer recommended without specific risk assessment.
- Delaying vaccinations due to mild illness is no longer considered necessary; vaccinations should proceed unless the child is severely ill.
- Routine use of live attenuated vaccines in children with diabetes who are not severely immunocompromised is no longer contraindicated; individualized risk assessment is recommended.

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Increased emphasis on annual influenza vaccination for all children with diabetes, including those with type 1 and type 2 diabetes.
- Expanded recommendations for pneumococcal vaccination in children with diabetes, particularly those with additional risk factors.
- Integration of vaccination status checks into routine diabetes care visits to improve compliance.
- Consideration of hepatitis B vaccination for all unvaccinated children with diabetes, regardless of risk factors.
- Updated guidance on the use of live attenuated vaccines in children with diabetes, with individualized risk assessment.
- Consideration of meningococcal vaccination (including serogroup B) for children with diabetes, particularly those with additional risk factors such as asplenia or complement deficiencies.
- Integration of vaccination status checks into routine diabetes care visits to include documentation of vaccine receipt in electronic health records.
