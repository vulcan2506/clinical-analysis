# individualized glycemic management
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Individualized glycemic management with shared decision-making

**Core Recommendations:**
- Glycemic goals and management should be individualized using shared decision-making to integrate patient needs, goals, and preferences.
- Therapeutic inertia should be avoided to reduce risks of acute/chronic complications and treatment burden.
- Glycemic goals should be reevaluated over time to balance individualized risks and benefits as clinical complexity increases.
- Severe or frequent hypoglycemia requires modification of treatment plans, including setting higher glycemic goals.
- Deintensification of high-hypoglycemia-risk medications (e.g., insulin, sulfonylureas) should be considered where hypoglycemia risk is high.
- Blood glucose goals should be modified in children with frequent hypoglycemia or hypoglycemia unawareness.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Patient-specific factors must be considered (e.g., life expectancy, cognitive function, hypoglycemia risk, comorbidities).
- Shared decision-making must integrate individual needs, goals, and preferences.
- Metabolic memory/legacy effect may justify finite periods of intensive glucose lowering even if later deintensified.
- Newly diagnosed individuals or those without limiting comorbidities may benefit from more intensive goals.
- Hypoglycemia risk assessment must guide medication adjustments.
- Consideration of medication burdens beyond hypoglycemia, including tolerability, administration difficulties, impact on education or employment, and financial cost, when balancing benefits of glycemic lowering and disease-specific benefits.

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Fixed numeric A1C targets without consideration of patient-specific factors.
- Rigid application of glycemic frameworks without clinical judgment.
- Failure to deintensify high-hypoglycemia-risk medications despite evidence of risk.
- Therapeutic inertia leading to unnecessary treatment burden or complications.
- Ignoring patient preferences or goals in glycemic management decisions.

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Use of shared decision-making to personalize glycemic goals and treatment plans.
- Consideration of metabolic memory/legacy effect for early intensive glucose lowering.
- Deintensification of diabetes treatment when risks outweigh benefits, including hypoglycemia risk.
- Switching to lower-hypoglycemia-risk therapies (e.g., SGLT2 inhibitors, GLP-1 RAs) for individualized goals or disease-specific benefits.
- Partnering with patients during deintensification to align goals, monitoring, and care plans.
- Use of SGLT2 inhibitors in the setting of heart failure or chronic kidney disease and GLP-1 RAs in the setting of cardiovascular disease or obesity as lower-hypoglycemia-risk therapies for individualized goals or disease-specific benefits.
