# gastrointestinal neuropathy diagnosis
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Gastrointestinal neuropathy diagnosis and management

**Core Recommendations:**
- Gastrointestinal neuropathies are diagnosed after excluding reversible/iatrogenic causes (e.g., medications like metformin, GLP-1-based therapies, opioids) and organic causes (e.g., gastric outlet obstruction, peptic ulcer disease).
- Gastroparesis should be suspected in individuals with erratic glycemic control or unexplained upper gastrointestinal symptoms.
- Diagnostic gold standard for gastroparesis is scintigraphy of digestible solids at 15-min intervals for 4 hours post-meal intake.
- ¹³C octanoic acid breath test is an approved alternative to scintigraphy for gastric emptying measurement.
- Evaluation of bladder function is recommended for individuals with diabetes and recurrent urinary tract infections, pyelonephritis, incontinence, or palpable bladder.
- Genitourinary disturbances (e.g., erectile dysfunction, retrograde ejaculation in men; decreased sexual desire, pain during intercourse, decreased arousal, and inadequate lubrication in women) are recognized as potential manifestations of diabetic autonomic neuropathy.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Exclusion of reversible causes (e.g., medication-induced symptoms, organic obstruction) prior to diagnosis or specialized testing.
- Endoscopy and/or imaging to rule out organic causes of gastrointestinal symptoms.
- Scintigraphy or ¹³C octanoic acid breath test for confirmed gastroparesis diagnosis.
- Assessment of glycemic control and symptom patterns to identify potential gastroparesis.
- Evaluation of bladder function in patients with recurrent urinary tract infections or incontinence.
- Evaluation of bladder function is specifically recommended for individuals with diabetes who have recurrent urinary tract infections, pyelonephritis, incontinence, or a palpable bladder.

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Routine use of fixed A1C targets for diagnosing or managing gastrointestinal neuropathies.
- Empiric treatment without prior exclusion of reversible/iatrogenic causes.
- Reliance solely on symptom reporting without objective testing (e.g., scintigraphy) for gastroparesis diagnosis.
- Assumption that glycemic control alone reverses established gastrointestinal neuropathy.

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Use of ¹³C octanoic acid breath test as an approved alternative to scintigraphy for gastric emptying assessment.
- Focus on individualized treatment strategies for symptom relief in autonomic neuropathy (e.g., painful DPN, bladder dysfunction).
- Emphasis on modifiable risk factor management (e.g., obesity, lipids, blood pressure) to slow progression of diabetic neuropathies.
- Incorporation of bladder function evaluation in patients with recurrent urinary tract infections or incontinence.
- Recognition of genitourinary disturbances (e.g., erectile dysfunction, sexual dysfunction) as potential manifestations of diabetic autonomic neuropathy.
- Recognition of biliary dysfunction as a potential manifestation of gastrointestinal autonomic neuropathy.
