# diabetes psychosocial care guidelines
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Diabetes psychosocial care guidelines

**Core Recommendations:**
- Screen all patients with diabetes for psychosocial distress, depression, anxiety, and disordered eating at diagnosis and annually, or when clinically indicated.
- Integrate diabetes distress assessment into routine care using validated tools (e.g., PAID, T1-DDAS, T2-DDAS) to guide treatment planning.
- Provide patient-centered communication that avoids stigmatizing language and fosters shared decision-making.
- Implement collaborative care models linking mental health professionals with diabetes care teams to address comorbid conditions.
- Offer evidence-based psychological interventions (e.g., CBT, mindfulness, motivational interviewing) tailored to patient needs and cultural context.
- Screen for disordered eating behaviors and eating disorders (e.g., binge eating, night eating syndrome) in patients with diabetes, particularly those with type 1 diabetes, using validated tools (e.g., mSCOFF, Binge Eating Scale, Night Eating Questionnaire) at diagnosis and annually.
- Assess fear of hypoglycemia using validated tools (e.g., Hypoglycemia Fear Survey-II) in patients with diabetes, especially those on insulin therapy, to guide targeted interventions.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Diagnosis of type 1 or type 2 diabetes mellitus.
- Patient consent for psychosocial screening and intervention.
- Availability of trained mental health professionals or referral pathways.
- Access to validated psychosocial screening tools (e.g., PHQ-9, GAD-7, PAID).
- Integration of psychosocial care into multidisciplinary diabetes care plans.
- Training for healthcare providers in culturally tailored communication and interventions to address disparities in mental health outcomes among diverse patient populations.
- Access to digital mental health tools (e.g., telehealth platforms, mobile apps) for patients with limited access to in-person mental health services.

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Routine use of unvalidated psychosocial screening tools without follow-up intervention.
- Dismissing diabetes distress as a normal part of living with diabetes without addressing it.
- Using punitive approaches to address non-adherence without exploring underlying psychosocial factors.
- Assuming all patients with diabetes require the same psychosocial interventions without individualized assessment.
- Delaying mental health referrals until severe symptoms or crises occur.
- Relying solely on unstructured or non-validated psychosocial screening tools without follow-up interventions or referrals.
- Assuming that all patients with diabetes will benefit equally from the same psychosocial interventions without individualized assessment of their specific needs and cultural context.

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Adoption of structured diabetes distress assessment tools (e.g., T1-DDAS, T2-DDAS) for routine screening.
- Integration of digital mental health interventions (e.g., telehealth, mobile apps) for accessible psychosocial support.
- Emphasis on culturally tailored psychosocial interventions to address disparities in mental health outcomes.
- Inclusion of family-focused interventions to support adolescents and young adults with diabetes.
- Use of automated insulin delivery systems to reduce diabetes distress related to glycemic variability.
