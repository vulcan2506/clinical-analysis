# statin-fibrate combination therapy risks
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** statin-fibrate combination therapy risks

**Core Recommendations:**
- Combination therapy (statin and fibrate) is associated with increased risk for abnormal transaminase levels, myositis, and rhabdomyolysis.
- Risk of rhabdomyolysis is higher with higher statin doses, renal insufficiency, and when combined with gemfibrozil (vs. fenofibrate).
- Combination therapy does not reduce cardiovascular events compared to statin alone in high-risk ASCVD patients (ACCORD study).
- Pemafibrate plus statin reduces triglycerides but does not reduce primary cardiovascular outcomes (PROMINENT trial).
- Fibrates (e.g., fenofibrate) may increase serum creatinine levels, which can complicate monitoring in patients with renal impairment.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Patients must have type 2 diabetes and elevated triglycerides (≥200 mg/dL) with low HDL (≤40 mg/dL) for consideration of fibrate therapy.
- Fibrate monotherapy or statin monotherapy should be optimized before considering combination therapy, with a focus on achieving LDL-C targets for ASCVD prevention.

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Combination therapy with statin and fibrate for cardiovascular risk reduction is no longer recommended.

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Fibrate monotherapy or statin monotherapy remains preferred for triglyceride management unless specific indications exist.
- Consider fenofibrate in select patients with severe hypertriglyceridemia (e.g., triglycerides >500 mg/dL) despite statin therapy, with close monitoring for hepatotoxicity and myopathy.
