# Statin Therapy Intensification in Diabetes
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Statin Therapy Intensification in Diabetes

**Core Recommendations:**
- Statin therapy is the preferred LDL-C lowering and cardioprotective treatment in people with diabetes.
- High-intensity statin therapy achieves ≥50% LDL-C reduction; moderate-intensity achieves 30–49% reduction.
- For primary prevention in diabetes aged ≥40 years, moderate-dose statin is recommended; high-intensity should be considered with additional ASCVD risk factors.
- For secondary prevention in diabetes with ASCVD, high-intensity statin therapy is recommended to achieve LDL-C <55 mg/dL (<1.4 mmol/L).
- If LDL-C goals are not met on maximum tolerated statin, add ezetimibe or PCSK9 inhibitor therapy.
- Statin therapy benefits in diabetes are linearly related to LDL-C reduction without a low threshold for benefit, and effects do not differ by sex.
- For individuals aged >75 years, moderate-intensity statin therapy is recommended, with routine risk-benefit evaluation and potential dose titration.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Baseline ASCVD risk stratification (e.g., presence of ASCVD, age, additional risk factors).
- Assessment of LDL-C levels or focus on LDL-C goal <70 mg/dL (<1.8 mmol/L) in primary prevention if baseline LDL-C is unavailable.
- Evaluation of statin tolerance and use of maximum tolerated dose if high-intensity is not tolerated.
- Discussion of benefits, risks, and costs when considering non-statin agents (ezetimibe or PCSK9 inhibitors).
- Baseline ASCVD risk stratification must account for additional risk factors (e.g., older age, hypertension, dyslipidemia, smoking, CKD, obesity) in primary prevention.
- For individuals <40 years with diabetes and ASCVD risk factors, a discussion of lifetime cardiovascular risk and relative benefits/risks of statin therapy is required.

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Low-dose statin therapy as a primary recommendation in people with diabetes.
- Routine use of low-intensity statin therapy in diabetes without consideration of higher-intensity options.
- Routine use of low-dose statin therapy in people with diabetes who can tolerate higher doses.

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Addition of ezetimibe or PCSK9 inhibitors to high-intensity statin therapy if LDL-C goals are not achieved in primary prevention.
- LDL-C goal of <55 mg/dL (<1.4 mmol/L) for secondary prevention in diabetes with ASCVD.
- Consideration of moderate-intensity statin therapy for individuals <40 years with diabetes and additional ASCVD risk factors.
- Emphasis on lifetime cardiovascular risk discussion for individuals <40 years with diabetes.
- Consideration of moderate-intensity statin therapy for individuals <40 years with diabetes and additional ASCVD risk factors, with emphasis on lifetime cardiovascular risk discussion.
- Addition of ezetimibe or PCSK9 inhibitors to maximum tolerated statin therapy in primary prevention if LDL-C goal <70 mg/dL (<1.8 mmol/L) is not achieved.
