# insulin-related adverse events in hospitals
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Insulin-related adverse events in hospitals

**Core Recommendations:**
- Insulin dosing errors, missed doses, and administration errors (incorrect type/timing) are common causes of adverse events in hospitalized patients.
- Iatrogenic hypoglycemia is often due to improper prescribing of glucose-lowering medications or inadequate management of the first hypoglycemic episode.
- Hypoglycemia risk is elevated in patients with kidney failure, advanced age, comorbidities (heart/liver failure, sepsis, malnutrition), and medication-related factors (e.g., sudden corticosteroid reduction).
- Root cause analysis and aggregation of hypoglycemic episodes are recommended to address systemic issues.
- CGM and AID devices may help mitigate hypoglycemia by providing early alerts to care staff.
- Sudden reduction of corticosteroid dose, reduced oral intake, emesis, or inappropriate timing of short-/rapid-acting insulin doses in relation to meals can precipitate hypoglycemia
- Unexpected interruption of enteral or parenteral feedings or reduced infusion rate of intravenous dextrose may lead to hypoglycemia

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Proactive surveillance of glycemic outliers and an interprofessional, data-driven approach to glycemic management.
- Blood glucose monitoring every 15 minutes during hypoglycemia treatment until stabilization above 70 mg/dL (3.9 mmol/L).
- Patient-specific factors (e.g., kidney failure, comorbidities) must be considered when assessing hypoglycemia risk.
- Root cause evaluation of all hypoglycemic episodes to identify systemic issues.
- Use of fast-acting carbohydrate, intravenous glucose, or glucagon for hypoglycemia treatment based on patient status.
- Blood glucose monitoring every 15 minutes during hypoglycemia treatment until stabilization above 70 mg/dL (3.9 mmol/L) is required for all patients, including those with NPO status receiving intravenous glucose or glucagon

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Fixed A1C targets without consideration of patient-specific factors in older adults.
- Routine use of sliding-scale insulin without proactive glycemic management.
- Delayed or missed blood glucose checks as an acceptable practice.
- Uninterrupted enteral/parenteral feedings without monitoring for hypoglycemia risk.
- Reliance solely on reactive hypoglycemia management without preventive strategies.
- Routine use of sliding-scale insulin without proactive glycemic management or patient-specific adjustments is no longer endorsed

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Implementation of bundled preventive therapies (e.g., CGM, interprofessional glycemic management) to reduce hypoglycemic events by 56–80%.
- Use of CGM to alert care staff of impending hypoglycemia for early intervention.
- Adoption of AID devices to help avoid hypoglycemia in hospitalized patients.
- Mandatory root cause analysis and aggregation of hypoglycemic episodes for systemic improvement.
- Standardized hypoglycemia treatment protocols (15 g fast-acting carbohydrate, IV glucose, or glucagon) with frequent monitoring.
- Use of CGM to alert care staff of impending hypoglycemia for early intervention in real-time
- Adoption of AID devices in hospitalized patients to help avoid hypoglycemia through automated insulin delivery
