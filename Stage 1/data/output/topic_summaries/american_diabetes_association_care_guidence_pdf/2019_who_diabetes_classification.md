# 2019 WHO diabetes classification
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** 2019 WHO diabetes classification

**Core Recommendations:**
- Classifies diabetes into distinct types: Type 1 Diabetes, Prediabetes and Type 2 Diabetes, Diabetes Induced by Systemic Anti-Cancer Therapy, Pancreatic Diabetes or Diabetes in the Context of Disease of the Exocrine Pancreas, Posttransplantation Diabetes Mellitus, Monogenic Diabetes Syndromes, and Gestational Diabetes Mellitus.
- Emphasizes the use of diagnostic tests to differentiate between diabetes types.
- Encourages tailored management strategies based on the specific diabetes classification.
- Encourages the use of C-peptide testing and autoantibody assays to differentiate between Type 1 Diabetes and other forms of diabetes.
- Recommends genetic testing for suspected monogenic diabetes syndromes when clinically indicated.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Diagnostic tests must be performed to confirm diabetes classification.
- Patient history and clinical presentation must be considered for accurate classification.
- C-peptide levels must be measured in patients with unclear diabetes classification to distinguish between insulin-deficient and insulin-resistant states.
- Autoantibody testing (e.g., GAD65, IA-2, ZnT8) is required for confirming autoimmune Type 1 Diabetes in ambiguous cases.

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Inclusion of Diabetes Induced by Systemic Anti-Cancer Therapy as a distinct classification.
- Recognition of Pancreatic Diabetes or Diabetes in the Context of Disease of the Exocrine Pancreas as a separate category.
- Formal classification of Posttransplantation Diabetes Mellitus.
- Acknowledgment of Monogenic Diabetes Syndromes as a distinct entity.
- Updated criteria for Gestational Diabetes Mellitus.
- Inclusion of 'Diabetes Induced by Systemic Anti-Cancer Therapy' as a distinct category, recognizing chemotherapy-induced hyperglycemia as a separate entity.
- Formal classification of 'Posttransplantation Diabetes Mellitus' as a distinct type, acknowledging its unique pathophysiology post-organ transplant.
