# monogenic diabetes diagnosis
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Monogenic diabetes diagnosis

**Core Recommendations:**
- Consider monogenic diabetes in children and adults diagnosed in early adulthood with atypical features (e.g., negative diabetes-associated autoantibodies, no obesity, strong family history)
- Genetic testing is recommended for individuals diagnosed with diabetes under 6 months of age, regardless of current age
- Diagnose MODY in individuals with hyperglycemia onset before age 25, impaired insulin secretion, and autosomal dominant inheritance
- Consult a diabetes genetics specialist for interpretation of genetic mutations and further evaluation
- Provide genetic counseling to affected individuals and family members regarding inheritance patterns and treatment implications
- Consider monogenic diabetes in individuals with transient neonatal diabetes (e.g., due to 6q24 overexpression) who may be treatable with medications other than insulin
- Consider monogenic diabetes in individuals with INS gene mutations causing permanent neonatal diabetes, where insulin therapy is preferred

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Diabetes diagnosed within the first 6 months of life (neonatal diabetes)
- Stable, mild fasting hyperglycemia (100–150 mg/dL [5.6–8.5 mmol/L]) or A1C between 5.6 and 7.6% (38–60 mmol/mol)
- Negative diabetes-associated autoantibodies and lack of typical type 1 or type 2 diabetes features
- Strong family history of diabetes with atypical presentation
- Absence of obesity or metabolic features inconsistent with type 2 diabetes
- Presence of autoantibodies does not definitively exclude monogenic diabetes, as rare cases of monogenic diabetes with positive autoantibodies have been reported

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Routine insulin therapy for all neonatal diabetes cases without genetic testing
- Assuming all early-onset diabetes is autoimmune type 1 diabetes
- Fixed treatment plans for MODY without genetic confirmation
- Ignoring family screening for monogenic diabetes
- Delaying genetic testing in suspected monogenic diabetes cases

**Updated Recommendations:**
- High-dose sulfonylurea therapy for KATP-related neonatal diabetes instead of insulin
- Genetic testing via next-generation sequencing for suspected monogenic diabetes
- Personalized therapy based on MODY subtype (e.g., no therapy for GCK-MODY, sulfonylureas for HNF1A/HNF4A-MODY)
- Genetic counseling for affected individuals and family members
- Screening for extrapancreatic complications in diagnosed MODY cases
- Genetic screening via next-generation sequencing is increasingly recommended as a cost-effective first-line approach for suspected monogenic diabetes
