# resistance exercise benefits
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Resistance exercise benefits in diabetes care

**Core Recommendations:**
- Adults with diabetes are encouraged to participate in 2–3 sessions/week of resistance exercise on non-consecutive days.
- Resistance training of any intensity is recommended to improve strength, balance, and activities of daily living.
- Older adults with diabetes are encouraged to participate in flexibility and balance training 2–3 times/week.
- Resistance exercise improves insulin sensitivity, glucose levels, and cardiometabolic health.
- People with diabetes should monitor blood glucose levels during and after exercise, including understanding prolonged effects based on intensity and duration.
- Resistance training can improve bone mineral density alongside glycemia, strength, and cardiometabolic health.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Pre-exercise glucose levels should be considered (<90 mg/dL may require adjustments in insulin or carbohydrate intake).
- Medication adjustments (e.g., insulin dose reduction) should be individualized based on exercise intensity, duration, and timing.
- Patients should be educated on using CGM or blood glucose monitoring during and after exercise.
- Exercise plans should account for diabetes-related complications (e.g., retinopathy, neuropathy) as outlined in relevant sections.
- Carbohydrate intake and medication adjustments around exercise should be tailored to individual glycemic responses.
- Medication adjustments around exercise should consider the use of insulin pumps or reduced pre-exercise insulin dosage as part of individualized glycemic management.

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Routine preventive measures for hypoglycemia are not usually advised in people with diabetes not treated with insulin or insulin secretagogues.

**Updated Recommendations:**
- High-intensity resistance exercise may raise glucose levels in some individuals, especially if pre-exercise glucose is elevated.
- Use of AID (Automated Insulin Delivery) technology around exercise is recommended for better glycemic management.
- Flexibility and balance activities (e.g., yoga, tai chi) are now emphasized for older adults with diabetes to improve A1C, strength, and balance.
- Physical activity is associated with lower risk of diabetes-related complications, even at lower activity levels.
- High-intensity interval training (HIIT) may raise glucose levels in some individuals, particularly if pre-exercise glucose is elevated, and requires tailored management.
