# diabetes technology integration
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Diabetes technology integration (CGM, AID, CSII, connected pens)

**Core Recommendations:**
- Diabetes devices should be offered to people with diabetes based on individualized needs, circumstances, preferences, and skill level.
- Initial and ongoing education/training must be provided for CGM, AID, and CSII users and caregivers, including data utilization for therapy adjustments.
- Healthcare professionals must stay updated on available technologies and seek additional support when needed.
- Reasonable accommodations for device use should be provided in school and work settings, including time to respond to glucose alerts.
- Standardized reports (e.g., single-page CGM summaries) must be utilized for all diabetes devices.
- Training on CGM may be self-initiated by users with company-provided materials (e.g., OTC CGM brands), though underresourced, younger, or older individuals may require more repetition and in-person support.
- Healthcare teams must assess the need for additional education periodically, particularly if treatment goals are not being met.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Individualized selection of devices based on patient needs, preferences, and caregiver capabilities.
- Initial and ongoing education/training for users and caregivers on device use and data interpretation.
- No prerequisites (e.g., C-peptide level, islet autoantibodies, or insulin duration) for initiating CSII or AID.
- Schools and workplaces must support device use with trained staff and backup treatment plans.
- Healthcare teams must have periodic education to keep up with rapidly evolving technology.
- Backup treatment plans must be included in the student's diabetes medical management plan (DMMP) for potential device failure.
- Healthcare professionals should be aware of device updates and how to access resources needed for their use, including periodic education to stay informed.

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Fixed one-size-fits-all approaches to diabetes technology use.
- Requiring C-peptide levels, islet autoantibodies, or insulin duration before initiating CSII or AID.
- Limiting device access based on age or A1C levels.
- Assuming all users can self-initiate complex devices without training.
- Relying solely on device availability without education and support.

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Early initiation of CGM, CSII, and AID at diagnosis based on patient/caregiver needs and preferences.
- Mandated standardized device reports (e.g., weekly CGM summaries) for clinical decision-making.
- Defined competencies (basic to advanced) for healthcare team members using diabetes technologies.
- OTC CGM brands enabling self-initiation with company-provided training materials.
- ADA’s diabetes technology guide (diabetes.org/living-with-diabetes/treatment-care/diabetes-technology-guide) for device selection.
- Defined competencies (basic, fundamental, intermediate, advanced) for healthcare team members specific to their roles in diabetes technology use.
- Instructions for device use must be outlined in the student's diabetes medical management plan (DMMP) with school support.
