# anxiety management interventions
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Anxiety management interventions in diabetes

**Core Recommendations:**
- Implement collaborative care models for adults with type 2 diabetes, depression, and anxiety to achieve clinically significant anxiety reduction.
- Utilize Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT) to reduce health anxiety, depressive symptoms, and diabetes distress in type 2 diabetes.
- Deploy diabetes technologies (real-time CGM, sensor-augmented pumps, AID) to reduce fear of hypoglycemia in type 1 diabetes independent of frequency reduction.
- Apply CBT-based interventions for young adults with type 1 diabetes to reduce fear of hypoglycemia and improve self-management behaviors.
- Ensure specialized behavioral interventions are delivered by qualified professionals as a positive adjunct to diabetes technology.
- Address injection-related anxiety in diabetes care plans using targeted behavioral interventions.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Patient must have type 2 diabetes with comorbid depression and anxiety for collaborative care protocols.
- Patient must have type 1 diabetes for technology-based fear of hypoglycemia interventions.
- Interventions must be delivered by qualified professionals.
- Evidence supports use in adults and young adults with specific anxiety profiles.
- Interventions should target injection-related anxiety, fear of hypoglycemia, or general anxiety associated with diabetes.
- Patient must have clinically significant anxiety symptoms (e.g., health anxiety, fear of hypoglycemia, or general anxiety) to qualify for CBT or collaborative care interventions.

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Collaborative care models for anxiety management in type 2 diabetes.
- CBT as a primary intervention for health anxiety and diabetes distress.
- Real-time CGM, sensor-augmented pumps, and AID for reducing fear of hypoglycemia.
- CBT-based interventions specifically for young adults with type 1 diabetes.
- Integration of specialized behavioral interventions with diabetes technology.
- Integration of obsessive-compulsive disorder (OCD) symptom screening in diabetes care for patients with injection-related anxiety.
