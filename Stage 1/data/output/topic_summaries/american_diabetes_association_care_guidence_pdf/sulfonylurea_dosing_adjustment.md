# sulfonylurea dosing adjustment
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Sulfonylurea dosing adjustment

**Core Recommendations:**
- Reduce dose if glucose levels are within individualized goal range and no hypoglycemia or hyperglycemia is present at baseline for new-generation sulfonylureas (glimepiride, gliclazide).
- Replace older-generation sulfonylureas (glyburide) with newer-generation agents or reduce dose by 50%.
- Administer sulfonylureas at main mealtime to minimize hypoglycemia risk.
- Split twice-daily doses between two meals to optimize glycemic control.
- Consider dose reduction of newer-generation sulfonylureas (glimepiride, gliclazide) in patients with renal impairment due to prolonged drug half-life and increased hypoglycemia risk.
- Adjust sulfonylurea dosing based on glycemic variability patterns, not just A1C, to minimize hypoglycemia risk in patients with fluctuating glucose levels.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Patient must be on sulfonylurea therapy (new or older generation).
- Baseline glucose levels must be assessed to determine individualized targets.
- Absence of hypoglycemia or hyperglycemia at baseline is required for dose reduction.
- Patient must be able to adhere to meal timing for optimal dosing.
- Assess renal function (eGFR) before initiating or adjusting sulfonylurea therapy, with dose reduction or avoidance in patients with eGFR <60 mL/min/1.73m².
- Evaluate patient’s meal timing consistency and carbohydrate intake patterns prior to sulfonylurea dosing adjustments to optimize timing and dose.

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Use of older-generation sulfonylureas (glyburide) as first-line therapy due to higher hypoglycemia risk.
- Fixed-dose sulfonylurea regimens without adjustment based on glycemic response.
- Use of glyburide in patients with chronic kidney disease (CKD) due to accumulation of active metabolites and elevated hypoglycemia risk.
- Fixed-dose sulfonylurea regimens without consideration of patient-specific glycemic targets or hypoglycemia risk assessment.

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Prefer newer-generation sulfonylureas (glimepiride, gliclazide) over older agents (glyburide).
- Individualized dose reduction based on glycemic control and hypoglycemia risk.
- Meal-based timing adjustments for sulfonylurea administration.
- Incorporate continuous glucose monitoring (CGM) data to guide sulfonylurea dose adjustments, particularly in patients with hypoglycemia unawareness or erratic glucose patterns.
- Prefer once-daily dosing of newer-generation sulfonylureas (e.g., glimepiride) over twice-daily regimens to reduce hypoglycemia risk in patients with adherence challenges.
