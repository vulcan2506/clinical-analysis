# exercise-based glycemic management
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Exercise-based glycemic management in diabetes

**Core Recommendations:**
- Structured exercise interventions of at least 8 weeks lower A1C by 0.66% in type 2 diabetes, even without BMI change
- Combining aerobic and resistance exercise may provide greater glycemic benefits than either mode alone in type 2 diabetes
- Aerobic exercise reduces glycemia acutely in type 1 diabetes compared to high-intensity interval or resistance exercise
- All exercise modes (aerobic, high-intensity interval, resistance) improve 24-hour time in range in type 1 diabetes
- Daily step counts of ~7,000–10,000 steps/day may improve time in range and reduce insulin requirements in type 1 diabetes
- Regular moderate-intensity physical activity prior to and during pregnancy reduces the odds of developing gestational diabetes mellitus by nearly 40% in individuals at risk

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Type 2 diabetes diagnosis for structured exercise intervention recommendations
- Type 1 diabetes diagnosis with consideration of exercise type, intensity, and duration to prevent glycemic variability
- Childbearing potential or pregnancy for preconception and prenatal exercise recommendations
- Assessment of hypoglycemia risk in type 1 diabetes prior to exercise recommendations
- Tolerance assessment for moderate-intensity physical activity in pregnancy
- Assessment of tolerance for moderate-intensity physical activity in individuals of childbearing potential or during pregnancy

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Recommendations for fixed exercise prescriptions without individualized glycemic response consideration in type 1 diabetes
- Exclusive reliance on aerobic or resistance exercise alone for optimal glycemic control in type 2 diabetes
- Prescribing exercise without accounting for step count or daily activity levels in type 1 diabetes

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Integration of combined aerobic and resistance training for enhanced glycemic benefits in type 2 diabetes
- Aerobic exercise prioritization for acute glycemic reduction in type 1 diabetes
- Step count targets (~7,000–10,000 steps/day) as adjunctive glycemic management in type 1 diabetes
- Preconception and prenatal moderate-intensity physical activity recommendations for individuals with preexisting diabetes or at risk for gestational diabetes
- Individualized exercise planning in type 1 diabetes to mitigate hypoglycemia and hyperglycemia risks
- Acute and long-term prenatal exercise positively impacts glycemia, with a larger effect noted in individuals with preexisting diabetes
