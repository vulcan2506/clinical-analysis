# diabetes distress screening
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Diabetes distress screening and management

**Core Recommendations:**
- Screen for diabetes distress at least annually in people with diabetes, caregivers, and family members, and repeat screening when treatment goals are not met, at transitional times, or in the presence of diabetes complications
- Address diabetes distress by initiating conversations about feelings and beliefs in a respectful, sensitive, and direct manner, validating emotional aspects, and linking emotions to self-management behaviors
- Use diabetes-specific validated measures appropriate for each person or population (e.g., age and diabetes type) for routine monitoring of diabetes distress
- Introduce brief coping strategies (e.g., values affirmation, mindful breathing, cognitive reframing) and highlight past successes to reinforce resilience
- Refer to qualified behavioral health professionals, ideally with diabetes expertise, for further assessment and treatment if distress is not adequately addressed during medical appointments
- Counseling about expected diabetes distress during new diagnoses, treatment changes, or life stressors
- Use of digital interventions (e.g., smartphone apps, video visits) to reduce diabetes distress

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Diabetes distress screening is required at least annually, during transitions, or when treatment goals are not met
- Screening should be performed using validated, diabetes-specific measures tailored to age and diabetes type
- Referral to behavioral health professionals is required if distress is not adequately addressed during routine care
- Interventions should be tailored to the individual's life stage and level of diabetes progression
- Screening should include caregivers and family members of people with diabetes
- Assessment of intentional omission or underdosing of insulin/noninsulin medication to cause weight loss before addressing distress
- Evaluation of disordered eating behaviors or disrupted eating patterns prior to standard distress screening

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Routine use of non-diabetes-specific screening tools for diabetes distress
- Assuming diabetes distress is equivalent to depression or anxiety without further assessment
- Ignoring diabetes distress in routine care despite its high prevalence and impact on outcomes

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Integration of brief coping strategies (e.g., values affirmation, mindful breathing) into routine diabetes care
- Use of digital interventions (e.g., smartphone apps, video visits) to reduce diabetes distress
- Counseling about expected diabetes distress during new diagnoses, treatment changes, or life stressors
- Evidence supporting DSMES, CBT, mindfulness-based therapies, and motivational interviewing for reducing diabetes distress
- AID systems may reduce diabetes distress in adults with type 1 diabetes and caregivers of children with type 1 diabetes
- Use of AID systems to reduce diabetes distress in adults with type 1 diabetes and caregivers of children with type 1 diabetes
- Integration of mindfulness-based therapies and mindful self-compassion training into diabetes care
