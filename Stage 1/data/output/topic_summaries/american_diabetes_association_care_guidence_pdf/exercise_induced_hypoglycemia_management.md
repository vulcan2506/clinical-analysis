# exercise-induced hypoglycemia management
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** exercise-induced hypoglycemia management in children/adolescents with type 1 diabetes

**Core Recommendations:**
- Adjust insulin doses (prandial or basal) or carbohydrate intake before/after exercise to prevent hypoglycemia
- Use CGM with frequent glucose monitoring before, during, and after exercise for safety
- Postpone intense activity if marked hyperglycemia (glucose ≥350 mg/dL) with ketones or β-hydroxybutyrate ≥0.6 mmol/L
- For pump users without AID, lower basal rates by ~10–50% or suspend for 1–2 hours during exercise
- Use AID systems with conservative glucose goals or increased targets to prevent hypoglycemia during exercise
- Postpone intense activity if moderate to large urine ketones are present (in addition to hyperglycemia ≥350 mg/dL and β-hydroxybutyrate >1.5 mmol/L)
- Consider additional carbohydrate intake during and/or after exercise based on duration and intensity of physical activity (e.g., 10–15 g for low- to moderate-intensity aerobic activities of 30–60 minutes if fasting)

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Patient must be on insulin or insulin secretagogues
- CGM availability for real-time glucose monitoring
- Individualized glucose targets based on insulin plan and exercise type/intensity/duration
- Assessment of β-hydroxybutyrate levels if hyperglycemia is present before exercise
- Healthy eating pattern and physical activity encouraged for children/adolescents with obesity
- Assessment of urine ketones in addition to β-hydroxybutyrate levels when marked hyperglycemia (≥350 mg/dL) is present before exercise
- Monitoring of weight status and cardiovascular risk factors in children and adolescents with type 1 diabetes and obesity

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Fixed carbohydrate intake without adjustment for exercise intensity/duration
- Routine avoidance of exercise due to hypoglycemia risk without individualized strategies
- Non-adjustment of insulin doses for pre- or post-exercise periods
- Ignoring nocturnal hypoglycemia risk in prolonged or high-intensity exercise
- Using hyperglycemia thresholds without considering ketones or β-hydroxybutyrate levels

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Use of AID systems with device-specific conservative settings for exercise
- Post-exercise basal rate reduction (~20%) to mitigate delayed hypoglycemia
- Carbohydrate intake timing optimization for AID users to prevent rebound hypoglycemia
- Individualized glucose targets (126–180 mg/dL) before activity based on exercise parameters
- Monitoring weight status and promoting healthy eating/physical activity in pediatric T1D with obesity
- Device-specific conservative settings for AID systems to prevent hypoglycemia during exercise
