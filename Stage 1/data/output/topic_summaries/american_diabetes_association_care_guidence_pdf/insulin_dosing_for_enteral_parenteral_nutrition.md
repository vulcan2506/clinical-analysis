# insulin dosing for enteral/parenteral nutrition
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** insulin dosing for enteral/parenteral nutrition

**Core Recommendations:**
- Insulin orders must cover basal, prandial, and correctional needs for individuals on enteral/parenteral nutrition.
- Basal insulin must continue for type 1 diabetes even if feedings are discontinued.
- Insulin dose for nutritional component calculated as 1 unit per 10–15 g carbohydrate in enteral/parenteral formulations.
- Rapid-acting insulin every 4h, regular insulin every 6h, or NPH insulin every 8–12h are options for nutritional coverage during continuous tube feeds.
- For bolus feedings, administer ~1 unit rapid-acting insulin per 10–15g carbohydrate subcutaneously before each feeding.
- For continuous peripheral or central parenteral nutrition, human regular insulin may be added to the total parenteral nutrition solution at a starting dose of 1 unit per 10 g dextrose, adjusted daily based on glycemic response.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Individuals must be receiving enteral or parenteral nutrition requiring insulin.
- Commercially available enteral nutrition preparations contain variable carbohydrates and infusion rates.
- Continuous enteral/parenteral nutrition results in a continuous postprandial state.
- Correctional insulin should be administered subcutaneously every 6h (regular) or every 4h (rapid-acting).
- If enteral nutrition is interrupted, intravenous dextrose may be given for the duration of active insulin time.
- Automated self-adjusting insulin algorithms require continuous glucose monitoring (CGM) or frequent point-of-care glucose checks to ensure safety and efficacy in hospitalized individuals on enteral/parenteral nutrition.

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Covering all insulin needs using basal insulin only (risk of hypoglycemia).
- Using only basal insulin for type 1 diabetes without prandial/correctional coverage.

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Automated self-adjusting insulin algorithms are safe and effective for hyperglycemia control in hospitalized individuals on enteral/parenteral nutrition.
- Adding human regular insulin to parenteral nutrition solution (1 unit per 10g dextrose) if >20 units correctional insulin required in 24h.
- Using NPH insulin for nocturnal tube feeding coverage.
- Subcutaneous insulin glargine achieves equivalent glycemic management to adding insulin to parenteral nutrition bags.
- Target blood glucose <140 mg/dL (7.8 mmol/L) increases hypoglycemia risk in continuous enteral/parenteral nutrition.
- Adding human regular insulin to parenteral nutrition solution is preferred over subcutaneous correctional insulin when >20 units of correctional insulin are required in 24 hours to reduce injection burden and hypoglycemia risk if nutrition is interrupted.
