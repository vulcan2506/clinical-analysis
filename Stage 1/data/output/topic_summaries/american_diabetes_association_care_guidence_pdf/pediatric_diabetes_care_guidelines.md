# pediatric diabetes care guidelines
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Pediatric diabetes care guidelines (Type 1 and Type 2)

**Core Recommendations:**
- Child and family-centered care with ongoing reassessment of self-care transfer and training for daycare/school personnel
- Comprehensive nutrition education at diagnosis and annually, tailored to growth, eating patterns, and risk factors
- ≥60 min/day moderate-to-vigorous physical activity with bone/muscle strengthening ≥3 times/week
- Routine psychosocial screening (e.g., food insecurity, diabetes distress, depression, anxiety) starting at age 7–8 years
- Use of CGM and AID systems for Type 1 diabetes management with school support for diabetes technology
- Education on strategies to mitigate hypoglycemia risk and minimize hyperglycemia associated with exercise in children and adolescents with type 1 diabetes or those using insulin
- Confidential time for adolescents with providers at developmentally appropriate ages during clinical visits

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Interprofessional pediatric diabetes team including physician, CDCES, dietitian, and behavioral health specialist
- Developmentally appropriate DSMES for child/adolescent and family at diagnosis and routinely thereafter
- Individualized A1C goals based on hypoglycemia risk, technology access, and nonglycemic factors
- Screening for microalbuminuria, retinopathy, and neuropathy with updated timing/frequency per SEARCH/TODAY/T1D Exchange evidence
- Assessment of social determinants of health (e.g., food insecurity, housing stability) and family dynamics
- Interprofessional team must include a behavioral health specialist or social worker for children and adolescents with type 2 diabetes to address comorbidities such as obesity, dyslipidemia, hypertension, and microvascular complications
- Initial therapy for new-onset diabetes in children and adolescents with overweight or obesity should address hyperglycemia and metabolic derangements irrespective of ultimate diabetes type, with adjustment after metabolic compensation and islet autoantibody results become available

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Fixed numeric A1C targets without individualized consideration
- Premature transfer of self-management tasks to children/adolescents without ongoing reassessment
- Reliance solely on adult diabetes care models for pediatric management
- Exclusion of behavioral health professionals from routine diabetes care teams
- Limited or non-inclusive nutrition education without cultural/financial considerations

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Expanded pharmacotherapy options (GLP-1 RAs, SGLT2 inhibitors, dual GIP/GLP-1 RA) with pediatric FDA approvals
- Inclusion of AI-based retinal screening tools and updated lipid screening protocols
- Structured transition programs from pediatric to adult care with digital tools for continuity
- Emphasis on ≥60 min/day physical activity and strategies to prevent exercise-related hypoglycemia/hyperglycemia
- Metabolic/bariatric surgery outcomes with ≥10-year follow-up data for pediatric Type 2 diabetes
- Figure 14.1 provides an approach to the initial treatment of new-onset diabetes in children and adolescents with overweight or obesity with clinical suspicion of type 2 diabetes
- Referrals to behavioral health and social work experts for complex social/environmental contexts surrounding pediatric diabetes management
