# dual antiplatelet therapy in diabetes
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Dual antiplatelet therapy in diabetes

**Core Recommendations:**
- Combination dual antiplatelet therapy with aspirin and a P2Y12 receptor antagonist is indicated after acute coronary syndromes and coronary revascularization with stenting.
- In people with diabetes and prior MI (1–3 years before), adding ticagrelor to aspirin significantly reduces the risk of recurrent ischemic events.
- Early aspirin discontinuation compared with continued dual antiplatelet therapy after coronary stenting may reduce bleeding risk without increasing mortality or ischemic events in people with diabetes.
- Combination therapy with aspirin plus low-dose rivaroxaban may be considered for people with stable coronary and/or PAD to prevent major adverse limb and cardiovascular complications.
- Shared decision-making should be used to determine an individually appropriate treatment approach for dual antiplatelet or antiplatelet plus anticoagulant strategies.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Acute coronary syndromes or coronary revascularization with stenting
- History of percutaneous coronary intervention (for prasugrel use)
- Diabetes with prior MI (1–3 years before) for ticagrelor + aspirin benefit
- Stable coronary artery disease or PAD for aspirin + rivaroxaban consideration
- Interprofessional team approach including cardiovascular or neurological specialist
- High-risk transient ischemic attack or minor stroke for short-term dual antiplatelet therapy indication
- Established coronary artery disease and/or PAD for aspirin plus low-dose rivaroxaban consideration

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Prolonged dual antiplatelet therapy without bleeding risk assessment
- Fixed-dose or prolonged aspirin therapy post-stenting without early discontinuation consideration
- Monotherapy with aspirin alone for secondary prevention in high-risk diabetes patients

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Short-term dual antiplatelet therapy after high-risk transient ischemic attack and minor stroke
- Aspirin plus low-dose rivaroxaban (2.5 mg twice daily) for stable coronary and/or PAD in diabetes
- Ticagrelor or clopidogrel for dual antiplatelet therapy if no PCI was performed
- Prasugrel as an option for dual antiplatelet therapy if PCI was performed
- Net clinical benefit assessment favoring ticagrelor in diabetes with prior PCI
