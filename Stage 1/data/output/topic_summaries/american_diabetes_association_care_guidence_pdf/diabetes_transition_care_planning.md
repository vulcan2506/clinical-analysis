# diabetes transition care planning
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Diabetes transition care planning

**Core Recommendations:**
- Implement transition preparation programs beginning in early adolescence and at least 1 year before anticipated transfer.
- Provide interprofessional support and resources for adolescents, young adults, and families prior to and during the transfer process.
- Engage in shared decision-making for the timing of transfer to an adult diabetes specialist without an age-specific cutoff.
- Utilize transition coordinators and technology to support communication and address young adult needs.
- Include a multidisciplinary team including CDEs, nurses, behavioral health professionals, dietitians, and social workers.
- Provide resources to enhance social and peer support during the transition process
- Include clinicians certified as diabetes care and education specialists (CDEs) in the transition team

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Patient must be an adolescent or young adult with type 1 or type 2 diabetes.
- Transfer must be planned collaboratively between the young person, caregivers, and pediatric specialists.
- Assessment of readiness, preferences, and concerns regarding self-management responsibilities.
- Recognition of the critical period of emerging adulthood involving major life transitions.
- Availability of resources to enhance social and peer support during the transition.
- Assessment of readiness, preferences, and concerns regarding self-management responsibilities must include evaluation of financial preparedness for healthcare costs post-transfer (e.g., insurance coverage, prescription costs)
- Prior to transfer, ensure the young person has demonstrated ability to schedule and attend medical appointments independently

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Relying solely on abrupt transfer without comprehensive planning beginning in early adolescence.
- Using a fixed age cutoff to determine transfer to adult diabetes care.
- Fragmented health care delivery lacking coordinated planning between pediatric and adult teams.
- Excluding behavioral health professionals and social workers from the transition team.
- Failing to address the shift in responsibility for self-management from parents to the young person.
- Assuming all adolescents are ready for transfer at a fixed age (e.g., 18 years) without individualized readiness assessment

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Use of transition coordinators to facilitate the transfer process.
- Implementation of technology to support communication with young adults.
- Adoption of comprehensive transition tools developed by the Endocrine Society, ADA, and other organizations.
- Inclusion of emerging adults in the decision-making process regarding transfer timing.
- Focus on addressing psychosocial, emotional, and behavioral challenges specific to the transition period.
- Use of transition tools developed by the Endocrine Society, ADA, and other organizations to standardize transition planning
- Implementation of interventions to promote attendance at follow-up appointments and reduce hospitalizations during the transition period
