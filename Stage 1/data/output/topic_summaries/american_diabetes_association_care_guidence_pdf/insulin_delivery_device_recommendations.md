# insulin delivery device recommendations
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Insulin delivery device recommendations

**Core Recommendations:**
- Insulin pens are preferred for people with insulin-requiring diabetes on multiple daily injections (MDI), though syringes may be used based on individual preference, insulin type, cost, and self-management capabilities.
- Insulin pens or injection aids are recommended for people with dexterity issues, vision impairment, or when shared decision-making supports accurate dosing.
- Connected insulin pens are recommended for people with diabetes on multiple daily insulin injections to facilitate dose tracking and data sharing.
- FDA-approved insulin dose calculators/decision support systems may assist in calculating insulin doses.
- Automated insulin delivery (AID) systems are the preferred insulin delivery method over MDI, CSII, and sensor-augmented pumps in people with type 1 diabetes and select adults with type 2 diabetes.
- Insulin pens may allow people with vision impairment or dexterity issues to dose insulin accurately, and insulin injection aids are also available to assist with these issues.
- Connected insulin pens or pen caps can be programmed to calculate insulin doses, synced with select CGM systems, and provide downloadable data reports for real-time insulin dosing and retrospective review by clinicians.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Patient must be on multiple daily insulin injections (MDI) for pen or syringe recommendations.
- Consideration of patient dexterity, vision, cost, insulin type, and self-management capabilities for device selection.
- Shared decision-making should guide the choice of insulin delivery device, especially for those with dexterity or vision impairments.
- Patient must be using CSII or AID to ensure continued access across third-party payors.
- Use of FDA-approved insulin dose calculators requires professional input and education for initial dosing and ongoing adjustments.
- Insulin pens, once started, can be kept in use for variable durations based on the type of insulin, usually for 28 days, ranging from 14 to 56 days.
- Needle length considerations: shorter needles (4–5 mm) are recommended to lower the risk of intramuscular injection with erratic absorption and possibly lipohypertrophy.

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Routine use of inhaled insulin as a primary insulin delivery method (unless preferred by the patient).
- Reliance solely on vial and syringe for insulin delivery without considering pen alternatives.
- Non-FDA-approved insulin dose calculators or decision support systems.
- Routine use of inhaled technosphere insulin as a primary insulin delivery method (unless preferred by the patient).

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Connected insulin pens for real-time insulin dose logging and data sharing with clinicians.
- Insulin injection aids for people with dexterity or vision impairments to facilitate accurate dosing.
- AID systems as the preferred insulin delivery method over MDI, CSII, and sensor-augmented pumps in type 1 diabetes and select type 2 diabetes.
- FDA-approved insulin dose calculators/decision support systems for dose calculation assistance.
- Continued access to CSII and AID systems across third-party payors for eligible patients.
- Insulin pen caps placed on existing insulin pens may assist with calculating insulin doses and provide a memory function.
- AID systems are the preferred insulin delivery method over MDI, CSII, and sensor-augmented pumps in people with type 1 diabetes, adults with type 2 diabetes, children and adolescents with type 2 diabetes, and those with other forms of insulin-deficient diabetes.
