# insulin deintensification strategy
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Insulin deintensification strategy in older adults

**Core Recommendations:**
- Simplify insulin administration plans by reducing complexity (e.g., fewer administration times, fewer blood glucose checks, and eliminating calculations like sliding-scale insulin or insulin-carbohydrate ratios).
- Deintensify or deprescribe insulin therapy by decreasing dose/frequency or discontinuing treatment entirely when severe/recurrent hypoglycemia occurs, regardless of A1C.
- Individualize glycemic goals based on health status, cognitive/functional capacity, and social circumstances (e.g., A1C <7.0–7.5% for healthy individuals, <8.0% for complex/intermediate cases).
- Use CGM in PALTC settings to monitor hypoglycemia and hyperglycemia, despite staff training requirements, as it provides prompt data for insulin dose adjustments.
- Assess mobility, mentation, medications, and patient preferences to tailor glucose-lowering agents and devices (e.g., CGM, insulin pumps) in post-acute and long-term care settings.
- Incorporate diabetes education/training for staff in post-acute and long-term care settings to improve management of older adults with diabetes, including CGM devices, insulin pumps, and advanced insulin delivery systems (Recommendation 13.16).
- Assess mobility, mentation, medications, and management preferences to tailor glycemic goals and glucose-lowering agents/devices in PALTC settings (Recommendation 13.17).

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Severe or recurrent hypoglycemia on insulin or sulfonylureas, regardless of A1C.
- Wide glucose excursions or significant glycemic variability.
- Cognitive or functional decline, polypharmacy, or loss of care partners.
- Residence in PALTC settings (e.g., nursing homes, skilled nursing facilities) where staff may lack familiarity with advanced diabetes technologies.
- Acute illness, weight loss, anorexia, or short-term cognitive/physical decline in community-dwelling individuals receiving short-term care.
- Staff training in diabetes detection, identification of complications, glycemic monitoring, individualized glycemic goal determination, medication and nutritional considerations, oral care, foot assessment, wound and infection concerns, and institutional quality assessment in PALTC settings.
- Development of facility-specific policies and procedures for prevention, recognition, and management of hypoglycemia and symptomatic hyperglycemia in PALTC facilities.

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Reliance on fixed numeric A1C targets in older adults without individualized consideration.
- Complex insulin regimens requiring frequent calculations (e.g., sliding-scale insulin, insulin-carbohydrate ratios) in high-risk older adults.
- Uncritical continuation of prehospitalization treatment plans if they increased in complexity during hospitalization.
- Assumption that all older adults can safely manage advanced insulin delivery systems (e.g., pumps, CGM) without staff training.
- Use of sulfonylureas in older adults with high hypoglycemia risk without deintensification.
- Reliance on fixed numeric A1C targets in community-dwelling individuals receiving short-term care in skilled nursing facilities without individualized glucose goals (e.g., glucose 100–200 mg/dL preferred).

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Real-time CGM use in PALTC facilities to guide insulin dosing, shown to be safe and effective compared to point-of-care BGM.
- Education and training for PALTC staff on diabetes management, CGM devices, insulin pumps, and hypoglycemia recognition.
- Development of facility-specific policies for hypoglycemia and hyperglycemia management in PALTC settings.
- Prioritization of individualized glycemic goals (e.g., glucose 100–200 mg/dL in skilled nursing facilities) over rigid A1C targets.
- Integration of care partner assessments and social circumstances (e.g., loss of support) into deintensification decisions.
- Real-time CGM use in PALTC facilities for up to 60 days shown to be safe and effective in guiding insulin doses compared with point-of-care BGM in a recent RCT (162).
- High prevalence of hypoglycemia in PALTC facilities among older adults using insulin or sulfonylureas, necessitating CGM for prompt detection despite staff training challenges.
