# diabetic neuropathy classification and presentation
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Diabetic neuropathy classification and presentation

**Core Recommendations:**
- Diabetic neuropathy is a diagnosis of exclusion; nondiabetic neuropathies may coexist and require evaluation.
- Up to 50% of diabetic peripheral neuropathy (DPN) cases may be asymptomatic, necessitating routine screening.
- Symptoms vary by fiber involvement: small-fiber dysfunction causes pain/dysesthesia; large-fiber dysfunction causes numbness, balance issues, and loss of protective sensation (LOPS).
- Autonomic neuropathy symptoms include orthostatic dizziness, syncope, erectile dysfunction, sweating changes, resting tachycardia, and orthostatic hypotension.
- Annual screening for DPN and autonomic neuropathy is recommended starting at diagnosis for type 2 diabetes and 5 years post-diagnosis for type 1 diabetes.
- Impaired counterregulatory responses to hypoglycemia in type 1 and type 2 diabetes can lead to impaired hypoglycemia awareness, which is not directly linked to autonomic neuropathy but should be considered in autonomic assessment.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Diagnosis of type 2 diabetes (screening at diagnosis) or type 1 diabetes (screening 5 years post-diagnosis).
- Annual reassessment for DPN and autonomic neuropathy in all people with diabetes.
- Use of validated tools: 10-g monofilament, 128-Hz tuning fork (vibration), pinprick/temperature testing, and symptom questionnaires.
- Consideration of non-diabetic causes of neuropathy (e.g., vitamin B12 deficiency, toxins, medications) in diagnostic workup.
- Referral to specialist (neurologist) for atypical presentations (acute/subacute, asymmetric, motor involvement).
- Assessment for autonomic neuropathy should be prioritized in individuals with evidence of other microvascular complications, particularly kidney disease and diabetic peripheral neuropathy.

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Routine use of electrophysiological testing or MRI for typical DPN presentations.
- Fixed reliance on numeric A1C targets for neuropathy risk stratification.
- Exclusive focus on symptomatic DPN without screening asymptomatic cases.

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Emphasis on 10-g monofilament testing for identifying feet at risk for ulceration/amputation.
- Inclusion of Ipswich touch test and cardiovascular autonomic testing for early CAN detection.
- Recognition of CAN as an independent mortality risk factor requiring proactive screening.
- Consideration of sudomotor dysfunction and hypoglycemia unawareness in autonomic neuropathy assessment.
- Integration of patient-reported outcomes (e.g., erectile dysfunction, constipation) in autonomic neuropathy screening.
- Advanced CAN may be associated with resting tachycardia (>100 bpm) and orthostatic hypotension (a fall in systolic or diastolic blood pressure by >20 mmHg or >10 mmHg, respectively, upon standing without an appropriate increase in heart rate).
- Early CAN may be completely asymptomatic and detected only by decreased heart rate variability with deep breathing.
