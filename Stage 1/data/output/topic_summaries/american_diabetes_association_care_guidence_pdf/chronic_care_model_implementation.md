# Chronic Care Model implementation
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Chronic Care Model implementation

**Core Recommendations:**
- Transition from reactive to proactive care delivery using a team-based approach
- Utilize clinical information systems and registries for person-specific and population-based support
- Engage collaborative interprofessional teams including primary care clinicians, CDCES, and RDNs
- Prioritize timely modification of behavior change interventions and pharmacologic therapy to avoid therapeutic inertia
- Assess and address social determinants of health and barriers to safe diabetes management
- Engage endocrinology teams with advanced diabetes management expertise for cases of high clinical complexity, multimorbidity, or difficult-to-manage hyperglycemia/hypoglycemia
- Develop individualized diabetes medical management plans in collaboration with school professionals and caregivers for pediatric patients

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Care team must include primary care clinician, CDCES, RDN, and other specialists as appropriate
- Programs should include four or more core CCM elements to maximize A1C reduction benefits
- Interventions must be tailored to subpopulations such as adults on intensive insulin or children/adolescents
- Health systems must foster a quality-oriented culture and utilize value-based payment models
- School professionals and caregivers must be engaged for pediatric patients to ensure safe management
- Care teams for adults on intensive insulin therapy must include clinicians experienced in advanced diabetes management, including technology use
- Pediatric care teams must include school professionals, behavioral health professionals (as needed), and parent(s)/caregiver(s)

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Fragmented delivery systems lacking clinical information capabilities
- Reactive care delivery models without coordinated longitudinal planning
- Case management and clinician education alone without integrated team approaches
- Low-risk Alternative Payment Models with limited financial risk assumption
- Interventions that do not address health-related social needs or community resources

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Patient-Centered Medical Home (PCMH) model for comprehensive primary care
- Accountable Care Organizations (ACOs) supporting CCM implementation
- Accountable Health Communities Model for identifying health-related social needs
- High-risk Alternative Payment Models (e.g., capitated payment) for improved care processes
- Integrated use of technology and advanced diabetes management expertise for complex cases
- Accountable Health Communities Model for addressing health-related social needs (with noted limitations in resource availability)
