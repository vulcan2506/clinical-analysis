# hospital glucocorticoid-induced hyperglycemia management
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Glucocorticoid-induced hyperglycemia management in hospitalized patients

**Core Recommendations:**
- Consider glucocorticoid type and duration of action when determining insulin treatment
- Monitor typical glycemic patterns (normal/mild fasting hyperglycemia, increasing afternoon hyperglycemia peaking in evening) using CGM for patients on daily prednisone/prednisolone
- Administer NPH insulin concomitantly with intermediate-acting steroids (e.g., prednisone) to match steroid-induced hyperglycemic response
- Use long-acting basal insulin for long-acting glucocorticoids (e.g., dexamethasone) or multidose/continuous glucocorticoid use
- Adjust insulin doses (basal, prandial, correction) by 40–60% or more for higher steroid doses, with daily monitoring and adjustments
- Administer NPH insulin in addition to basal-bolus insulin or oral glucose-lowering medications for patients on intermediate-acting steroids (e.g., prednisone) depending on prior diabetes medication history
- Use long-acting basal insulin for long-acting glucocorticoids (e.g., dexamethasone) or multidose/continuous glucocorticoid therapy to manage fasting blood glucose levels

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Glucocorticoid therapy must be ongoing or recently initiated
- Patient must be hospitalized and receiving glucocorticoids
- CGM or POC blood glucose monitoring must be available
- Prior diabetes medication history must be reviewed to determine insulin regimen
- Patient must have documented hyperglycemia or be at risk for glucocorticoid-induced hyperglycemia
- Prior diabetes medication history must be reviewed to determine whether NPH insulin should be added to basal-bolus insulin or oral agents for intermediate-acting steroids

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Fixed insulin dosing without adjustment for glucocorticoid type/duration
- Routine use of sliding-scale insulin alone without basal/bolus components
- Ignoring glycemic patterns specific to glucocorticoid timing
- Using only prandial insulin without basal insulin for long-acting glucocorticoids
- Not accounting for increased insulin requirements with higher steroid doses

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Use of CGM to track glucocorticoid-specific glycemic patterns
- Concomitant NPH insulin administration with intermediate-acting steroids
- Increased insulin dose adjustments (40–60% or more) for higher steroid doses
- Daily insulin dose adjustments based on glycemia and anticipated glucocorticoid changes
- Emphasis on balancing time in range (70–180 mg/dL) with hypoglycemia risk
- Increasing the ratio of insulin to steroids is associated with improved time in range (70–180 mg/dL) but may increase hypoglycemia risk
