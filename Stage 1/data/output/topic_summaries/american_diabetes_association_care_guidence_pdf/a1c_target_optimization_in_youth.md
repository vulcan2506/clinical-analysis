# A1C target optimization in youth
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** A1C target optimization in youth with type 1 diabetes

**Core Recommendations:**
- Set A1C goal of <7% (<53 mmol/mol) for most children and adolescents with type 1 diabetes
- Encourage goal of <6.5% (<48 mmol/mol) in those who can achieve it without adverse effects, especially with access to CGM and AID systems
- Monitor glucose multiple times daily using CGM whenever possible; finger-stick glucose monitoring remains essential if CGM is inaccessible or inaccurate
- Provide initial and ongoing education and behavioral support for CGM use to patients, parents, and caregivers
- Avoid fixed numeric A1C targets in young children (<6 years) due to hypoglycemia risk and inability to recognize/manage hypoglycemia
- Use CGM soon after type 1 diabetes diagnosis to improve A1C outcomes
- Offer behavioral support to improve ongoing CGM use in youth and caregivers

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Type 1 diabetes diagnosis in children or adolescents
- Access to CGM or blood glucose monitoring capability
- Consideration of patient-specific factors such as age, hypoglycemia awareness, and phase of diabetes (e.g., honeymoon phase)
- Availability of diabetes education and support systems for CGM use
- Exclusion of severe hypoglycemia risk in young children (<6 years) or those unable to manage hypoglycemia
- Consider residual β-cell function (honeymoon phase) when setting A1C targets
- Evaluate patient age and hypoglycemia awareness (especially in <6 years) before setting lower A1C goals

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Fixed numeric A1C targets without consideration of patient-specific factors
- Reliance solely on finger-stick glucose monitoring without CGM when available
- Use of older CGM data standards requiring 14 days of data for reliable glycemic assessment
- Avoidance of lower A1C goals (<6.5%) due to perceived hypoglycemia risk without evidence-based assessment
- Ignoring the honeymoon phase as a window for achieving lower A1C goals
- Reliance on 14 days of CGM data as the sole standard for glycemic assessment

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Encouragement of A1C <6.5% (<48 mmol/mol) in eligible youth with type 1 diabetes using modern AID systems and CGM
- Use of ≥7 days of CGM data for reliable glycemic assessment in children and adolescents
- Emphasis on CGM Time in Range (TIR) metrics for glycemic assessment alongside A1C
- Recognition of the honeymoon phase as an opportunity to achieve lower A1C goals
- Awareness of the relationship between higher CGM TIR and improved neurocognitive outcomes in youth
- ≥7 days of CGM data may be sufficient for reliable glycemic assessment in children and adolescents
- Higher CGM TIR is associated with better structural brain development and neurocognitive outcomes in youth
