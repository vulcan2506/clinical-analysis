# diabetic neuropathy management
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Diabetic neuropathy management

**Core Recommendations:**
- Optimize glucose, weight, blood pressure, and lipid management to prevent or slow progression of diabetic neuropathy.
- Assess and treat pain related to diabetic peripheral neuropathy and autonomic neuropathy symptoms to improve quality of life.
- Use gabapentinoids, SNRIs, tricyclic antidepressants, or sodium channel blockers as initial pharmacologic treatments for neuropathic pain.
- Avoid opioids (including tramadol and tapentadol) for neuropathic pain treatment except in rare circumstances due to adverse events.
- Combine pharmacologic agents for neuropathic pain if monotherapy is ineffective.
- Concurrent treatment of sleep and mood disorders alongside neuropathic pain management due to their increased frequency in individuals with diabetic peripheral neuropathy.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Type 1 diabetes for prevention/delay of neuropathy development.
- Type 2 diabetes for slowing progression of neuropathy.
- Patient-specific factors (e.g., life expectancy, cognitive function, hypoglycemia risk) for individualized A1C targets.
- Diagnosis of diabetic peripheral neuropathy or autonomic neuropathy symptoms.
- Prior optimization of glycemic control and cardiovascular risk factors.
- Diagnosis of autonomic neuropathy symptoms (e.g., cardiovascular autonomic neuropathy) for targeted management.

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Opioids (including tramadol and tapentadol) for routine neuropathic pain treatment due to adverse events and lack of long-term efficacy.
- Fixed numeric A1C targets for older adults without consideration of patient-specific factors.
- Conventional lipid-lowering pharmacotherapy (e.g., statins, fibrates) for treating or preventing DPN.
- Routine use of GLP-1 RA agents for DPN management due to controversial and limited evidence.
- Use of lidocaine patches for widespread neuropathic pain.
- Lidocaine patches for widespread neuropathic pain due to limited efficacy.

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Intensive glucose and blood pressure management to reduce risk of CAN in type 2 diabetes.
- Combination therapy with gabapentinoids, SNRIs, TCAs, or sodium channel blockers for neuropathic pain.
- Topical capsaicin (8% patch) for localized neuropathic pain in patients contraindicated for oral therapy.
- Early glycemic management in type 1 diabetes to delay or prevent DPN and CAN.
- Lifestyle interventions (e.g., exercise, weight loss) to improve neuropathy symptoms and outcomes.
- Topical capsaicin (8% patch) for localized neuropathic pain in patients contraindicated for or preferring non-oral therapy.
- Metabolic surgery as a potential intervention for improving neuropathy outcomes in obese individuals with type 2 diabetes (evidence from observational studies).
