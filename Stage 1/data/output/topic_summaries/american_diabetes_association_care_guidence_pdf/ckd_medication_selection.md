# CKD medication selection
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** CKD medication selection in type 2 diabetes

**Core Recommendations:**
- Preferred first-line agents for glucose management in CKD are GLP-1 RAs and SGLT2 inhibitors due to their effectiveness, safety with reduced eGFR, and benefits on CKD progression and CVD risk.
- SGLT2 inhibitors are recommended for individuals with eGFR ≥20 mL/min/1.73 m² to reduce CKD progression and cardiovascular events, with benefits observed even at eGFR as low as 20 mL/min/1.73 m².
- GLP-1 RAs (e.g., semaglutide, liraglutide, dulaglutide) are effective for glucose lowering and cardiovascular risk reduction, with semaglutide specifically recommended as a first-line agent for CKD due to demonstrated kidney and CVD benefits.
- Metformin is contraindicated if eGFR <30 mL/min/1.73 m²; dose reduction required if eGFR <45 mL/min/1.73 m²; temporarily discontinued before iodinated contrast imaging if eGFR 30–60 mL/min/1.73 m².
- Insulin and sulfonylureas require close monitoring and dose adjustment in CKD due to high hypoglycemia risk, with education on hypoglycemia prevention emphasized.
- Tirzepatide (dual GIP/GLP-1 RA) may reduce albuminuria without adverse eGFR changes, though evidence in CKD is limited (only 5% of SURMOUNT-2 participants had eGFR <60 mL/min/1.73 m²).
- SGLT2 inhibitors are recommended to be continued if tolerated until an individual starts dialysis, regardless of eGFR decline.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- eGFR ≥20 mL/min/1.73 m² for initiation of SGLT2 inhibitors.
- eGFR ≥30 mL/min/1.73 m² for initiation of metformin (contraindicated if <30).
- Albuminuria or documented eGFR loss for high-risk CKD progression to justify SGLT2 inhibitor use.
- Monitoring of eGFR and hypoglycemia risk in patients on insulin or sulfonylureas.
- Consideration of patient-specific factors (CVD risk, weight, comorbidities, preferences, cost) when selecting glucose-lowering medications.
- SGLT2 inhibitor initiation requires eGFR ≥20 mL/min/1.73 m², with evidence of efficacy in trials including EMPA-KIDNEY (eGFR ≥20), DAPA-CKD (eGFR >25), and CREDENCE (eGFR >30).
- Albuminuria ≥100 mg/g is a requirement for FLOW trial enrollment (semaglutide kidney outcomes study).

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Lixisenatide and exenatide (GLP-1 RAs requiring renal elimination) should be avoided in individuals with eGFR ≤30 mL/min/1.73 m² or creatinine clearance ≤30 mL/min.
- Fixed numeric A1C targets for older adults with CKD (now individualized based on life expectancy, cognitive function, and hypoglycemia risk).
- Use of metformin in patients with eGFR <30 mL/min/1.73 m² (contraindicated).
- Reliance on serum creatinine alone for metformin dosing guidance (replaced by eGFR-based criteria).
- Insulin glargine, liraglutide, sitagliptin, and glimepiride as agents preventing CKD development in metformin monotherapy (no documented benefit).

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Semaglutide recommended as a first-line GLP-1 RA for CKD due to demonstrated benefits on CVD, mortality, and kidney outcomes in the FLOW trial.
- FLOW trial demonstrated semaglutide reduces major kidney events (HR 0.76) and cardiovascular death in CKD with type 2 diabetes, supporting its first-line recommendation.
- EMPA-KIDNEY trial showed empagliflozin efficacy in CKD with eGFR as low as 20 mL/min/1.73 m², expanding SGLT2 inhibitor use to this threshold.
- SURMOUNT-2 trial suggests tirzepatide reduces albuminuria, though CKD-specific outcomes remain unproven.
