# diabetes-associated cognitive decline
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Diabetes-associated cognitive decline and dementia risk

**Core Recommendations:**
- Diabetes significantly increases risk of all-cause dementia (43%), Alzheimer's dementia (43%), and vascular dementia (91%)
- Diabetes-related dementia is distinct: slow progression, no typical Alzheimer/vascular neuroimaging findings, high A1C, long diabetes duration, insulin use, frailty, and sarcopenia/dynapenia
- Higher A1C correlates with lower cognitive function; intensive glycemic control (<6.0–6.5%) shows no cognitive benefit vs standard control
- Severe hypoglycemia is linked to cognitive decline and dementia; recurrent episodes increase psychomotor/mental function decline risk
- Cognitive capacity affects diabetes self-management (attention, memory, executive function) and requires routine assessment
- Episodes of severe hypoglycemia are independently associated with cognitive decline and are a risk factor for accelerated decline
- Early-onset type 1 diabetes is associated with long-term deficits in intellectual abilities, especially when accompanied by repeated episodes of severe hypoglycemia

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Presence of diabetes or prediabetes for risk stratification
- Documented cognitive impairment or dementia diagnosis
- Assessment of hypoglycemia history and A1C levels
- Evaluation of self-management ability and need for caregiver support
- Consideration of developmental stage for children/adolescents
- Assessment of developmental stage for children/adolescents to determine self-management capacity and need for caregiver support
- Evaluation of diabetes-specific factors (e.g., younger age at diagnosis, longer disease duration, recurrent DKA, microvascular complications) in type 1 diabetes for cognitive decline risk stratification

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Intensive glycemic management (A1C <6.0–6.5%) for improving cognitive outcomes
- Fixed numeric A1C targets without individualized adjustment
- Reliance on standard neuroimaging alone for diabetes-related dementia diagnosis
- Unmonitored complex treatment plans for individuals with cognitive decline
- Assumption of consistent cognitive decline trajectory
- Reliance on fixed A1C targets (e.g., <6.0–6.5%) without individualized adjustment for cognitive outcomes in type 2 diabetes

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Recognition of diabetes-related dementia as a distinct clinical entity
- Routine cognitive capacity assessment in diabetes care, including executive function evaluation
- Introduction of lay care professionals for daily monitoring in cognitively impaired patients
- GLP-1 receptor agonists associated with reduced dementia risk in meta-analysis
- Exercise as a potential nonpharmacologic treatment for cognitive impairment in older adults with type 2 diabetes
- GLP-1 receptor agonists are associated with a statistically significant reduction in dementia risk compared to control therapy in meta-analysis
- Exercise is a potential nonpharmacologic treatment pathway for cognitive impairment in older adults with type 2 diabetes
