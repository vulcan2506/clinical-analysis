# metformin for diabetes prevention
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Metformin for Diabetes Prevention and Monitoring

**Core Recommendations:**
- Consider metformin for adults at high risk of type 2 diabetes, particularly those aged 25–59 with BMI ≥35 kg/m², higher fasting plasma glucose (≥110 mg/dL), or higher A1C (≥6.0%).
- Consider metformin to prevent hyperglycemia in high-risk individuals treated with PI3Kα inhibitors (e.g., alpelisib) or high-dose glucocorticoids.
- Consider periodic assessment of vitamin B12 levels in individuals receiving long-term metformin therapy, especially those with anemia or peripheral neuropathy.
- Monitor vitamin B12 annually in patients taking metformin for more than 4 years or those with risk factors like vegan diets or prior gastric surgery.
- Balance the benefits of glucose lowering against potential adverse effects, treatment burden, and cost, as no pharmacologic agent is FDA-approved solely for diabetes prevention.
- Consider metformin to prevent hyperglycemia in individuals receiving high-dose and/or long-duration glucocorticoid therapy, particularly those with additional risk factors for diabetes (e.g., higher glucocorticoid doses, longer treatment duration).
- Consider metformin for individuals at highest risk of hyperglycemia with PI3Kα inhibitor therapy (e.g., age ≥70 years, BMI ≥30 kg/m², concurrent glucocorticoid use, or baseline hyperglycemia [A1C ≥5.7% or fasting plasma glucose ≥100 mg/dL]).

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Patient must be an adult at high risk of type 2 diabetes, typified by Diabetes Prevention Program criteria.
- Specific high-risk characteristics include age 25–59 years, BMI ≥35 kg/m², fasting plasma glucose ≥110 mg/dL, or A1C ≥6.0%.
- History of gestational diabetes mellitus (GDM) is a qualifying condition for consideration.
- For PI3Kα inhibitor users, patients should be high-risk (e.g., older, obese, concurrent glucocorticoid use, baseline hyperglycemia).
- Vitamin B12 monitoring is required for long-term therapy (>4 years) or presence of deficiency risk factors.
- Metformin should be started 1 week prior to initiating PI3Kα inhibitor therapy (e.g., alpelisib) to prevent hyperglycemia and continued for the duration of treatment.

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Valsartan for the prevention of diabetes (not recommended due to lack of cardiovascular outcome benefit).
- Testosterone therapy for the prevention of type 2 diabetes in men with hypogonadism (not recommended based on TRAVERSE study).
- Widespread vitamin D therapy for adults with high-risk prediabetes (benefit-to-risk ratio remains uncertain).
- Pharmacologic treatments other than metformin for the sole purpose of preventing type 2 diabetes (lack of long-term data).
- Fixed numeric A1C targets for older adults (now individualized based on patient-specific factors).
- Vitamin D therapy for the prevention of type 2 diabetes in adults with high-risk prediabetes (benefit-to-risk ratio remains uncertain due to lack of statistically significant outcomes and potential adverse effects like hypercalcemia).

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Consideration of metformin for preventing hyperglycemia in individuals receiving PI3Kα inhibitors (e.g., alpelisib).
- Consideration of metformin for preventing hyperglycemia in individuals treated with high-dose glucocorticoids.
- Recommendation for annual vitamin B12 monitoring in long-term metformin users (>4 years) or those with specific risk factors.
- Recognition of metformin as the only pharmacologic therapy with robust efficacy and safety data for diabetes prevention.
- Use of GLP-1-based therapies for weight management in individuals with overweight or obesity as an essential component of diabetes prevention.
- Metformin is as effective as lifestyle modification in reducing diabetes risk in participants with BMI ≥35 kg/m² and in younger individuals aged 25–44 years.
- Metformin and intensive lifestyle modification led to an equivalent 50% reduction in diabetes risk in individuals with a history of gestational diabetes mellitus (GDM).
