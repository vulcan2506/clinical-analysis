# health literacy interventions in diabetes
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Health literacy and numeracy interventions in diabetes care

**Core Recommendations:**
- Healthcare professionals and diabetes care specialists should provide easy-to-understand information and reduce unnecessary complexity in care plans.
- Interventions addressing low health literacy in diabetes populations improve A1C, diabetes knowledge, and self-management behaviors.
- Health numeracy is essential for tasks like interpreting glucose readings, decoding food labels, and understanding medication doses.
- Standardized screening measures should be used to assess health literacy and numeracy if treatment decision understanding appears limited.
- Adjunctive education and support are indicated if limited health literacy and numeracy are barriers to optimal care decisions.
- Healthcare professionals should assess and address both health literacy and numeracy barriers simultaneously when developing care plans for patients with diabetes.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Individuals with diabetes or prediabetes who require numeric tasks (e.g., interpreting glucose readings, medication dosing).
- Patients demonstrating limited understanding of treatment decisions or self-management concepts.
- Populations with low health literacy or numeracy identified through screening.
- Diabetes technologies users (e.g., insulin pumps) requiring applied and interpretive health numeracy skills.
- Patients with prediabetes or diabetes who perform numeric tasks (e.g., interpreting glucose readings, medication dosing) must be evaluated for both health literacy and numeracy deficits before finalizing treatment plans.

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Use of standardized screening measures to assess health literacy and numeracy barriers in diabetes care.
- Integration of health numeracy training for patients using diabetes technologies like insulin pumps.
- Emphasis on reducing unnecessary complexity in care plans to accommodate low health literacy.
- Adjunctive education and support strategies tailored to patients with identified health literacy or numeracy barriers.
- Primary numeric skills, applied health numeracy, and interpretive health numeracy should be explicitly integrated into health literacy interventions for patients using diabetes technologies like insulin pumps.
