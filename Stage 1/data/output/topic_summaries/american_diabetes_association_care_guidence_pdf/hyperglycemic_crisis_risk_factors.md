# hyperglycemic crisis risk factors
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Hyperglycemic crisis risk factors and prevention

**Core Recommendations:**
- Individuals with type 1 diabetes/absolute insulin deficiency are at higher risk for hyperglycemic crises.
- Younger age (especially 10–14 years) and adolescence are associated with increased DKA risk.
- Prior history of hyperglycemic crises significantly increases recurrence risk.
- Psychosocial challenges (e.g., disordered eating, transition periods) contribute to DKA risk.
- Missed or inadequate insulin doses are a primary trigger for DKA.
- Female sex is associated with increased DKA risk.
- Designation as part of an underrepresented group (by race, ethnicity, or migration background) is associated with higher DKA risk.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Hemodynamically and cognitively intact individuals may treat mild DKA with oral hydration, frequent monitoring, and subcutaneous insulin if able to tolerate.
- Individuals with suspected HHS require immediate inpatient evaluation due to greater volume depletion and acute illness triggers.
- Screening for social determinants of health (e.g., insulin access, CGM availability) is required for individuals with prior DKA/HHS.
- Structured diabetes self-management education and support (including problem-solving) is recommended to prevent recurrence.
- Multisystemic behavioral interventions are often necessary for those with recurrent DKA.
- Individuals with suspected HHS require immediate inpatient evaluation due to greater volume depletion and acute illness triggers, even if hemodynamically and cognitively intact.

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Use of insulin pump therapy is not associated with increased DKA risk in the current era (previously debated).

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Access to CGM is associated with decreased risk of DKA recurrence.
- Psychological interventions, peer support, and behavioral family systems therapy are effective in reducing DKA admissions.
- Screening for social determinants of health (e.g., inadequate insulin access) and referral to community services are recommended.
- Individualized A1C targets and self-management strategies are emphasized for high-risk groups (e.g., adolescents).
- Access to CGM may decrease risk of DKA recurrence.
