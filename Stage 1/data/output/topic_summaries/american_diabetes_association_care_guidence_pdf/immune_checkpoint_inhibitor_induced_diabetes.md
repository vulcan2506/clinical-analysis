# immune checkpoint inhibitor-induced diabetes
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Immune checkpoint inhibitor-induced diabetes + PI3K/mTOR inhibitor-induced hyperglycemia + glucocorticoid-induced hyperglycemia

**Core Recommendations:**
- ICI-induced diabetes presents as rapid-onset, severe hyperglycemia or DKA, requiring lifelong insulin therapy due to irreversible β-cell destruction.
- PI3K inhibitor-induced hyperglycemia occurs within 2 weeks, with risk factors including preexisting dysglycemia, BMI >25 kg/m², and age >65 years; metformin is first-line, with pioglitazone or SGLT2 inhibitors as alternatives.
- mTOR inhibitor-induced hyperglycemia is managed with metformin as first-line, pioglitazone as second-line, and insulin reserved for refractory cases.
- Glucocorticoid-induced hyperglycemia timing and extent vary by dose, duration, and route; insulin is most frequently used, with sulfonylureas/meglitinides for type 2 diabetes or no prior diagnosis.
- ICI treatment should not be discontinued for severe hyperglycemia, as β-cell destruction is irreversible.
- ICI-induced diabetes is most common after exposure to PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors (e.g., nivolumab, pembrolizumab, durvalumab, avelumab) and less commonly with CTLA-4 inhibitors (e.g., ipilimumab).
- Hyperglycemia due to ICIs can occur at any time after initiation, ranging from 1 week to up to 12 months after the first dose.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- ICI-induced diabetes requires confirmation of insulinopenia for prandial insulin initiation.
- PI3K inhibitor-induced hyperglycemia risk factors include preexisting dysglycemia, BMI >25 kg/m², and age >65 years.
- Glucocorticoid-induced hyperglycemia management must account for timing and extent of glucose elevations, not just morning monitoring.
- PI3K inhibitor-induced hyperglycemia management prioritizes metformin, with pioglitazone or SGLT2 inhibitors as alternatives due to potential ketoacidosis risk.
- mTOR inhibitor-induced hyperglycemia management prioritizes metformin, with pioglitazone as second-line and insulin reserved for refractory cases.
- Confirmation of insulinopenia is required before initiating prandial insulin for ICI-induced diabetes.

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Sulfonylureas and insulin as first-line for PI3K inhibitor-induced hyperglycemia due to potential reactivation of PI3K pathway.
- GLP-1 RAs for PI3K inhibitor-induced hyperglycemia due to uncertain effects on PI3K inhibitor efficacy and potential nausea/vomiting.
- Fixed A1C targets for glucocorticoid-induced hyperglycemia without considering timing and extent of glucose elevations.
- Discontinuation of ICI therapy for severe hyperglycemia, as β-cell destruction is irreversible.
- Routine morning glucose monitoring alone for glucocorticoid-induced hyperglycemia without accounting for afternoon/evening peaks.
- Sulfonylureas and insulin as first-line agents for PI3K inhibitor-induced hyperglycemia due to potential reactivation of the PI3K pathway.

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Lifelong insulin therapy is generally required for ICI-induced diabetes due to irreversible β-cell destruction.
- Basal insulin initiation should be considered for ICI-induced hyperglycemia with blood glucose >250 mg/dL.
- Metformin is first-line for PI3K and mTOR inhibitor-induced hyperglycemia due to efficacy and safety.
- Pioglitazone may be considered as second-line for PI3K or mTOR inhibitor-induced hyperglycemia.
- Insulin is the most frequently used agent for glucocorticoid-induced hyperglycemia, with sulfonylureas/meglitinides for type 2 diabetes or no prior diagnosis.
- Pioglitazone may be considered as monotherapy or in combination with metformin for PI3K inhibitor-induced hyperglycemia, though its slow onset of action may limit effectiveness.
- GLP-1 RAs are not recommended for PI3K inhibitor-induced hyperglycemia due to uncertain effects on PI3K inhibitor efficacy and potential adverse effects (e.g., nausea, vomiting).
