# lipid management in pregnancy
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Lipid Management in Pregnancy

**Core Recommendations:**
- Discontinue lipid-lowering therapies during gestation for most pregnant individuals.
- Review and weigh risks of discontinuing lipid-lowering therapy in high-risk individuals (e.g., familial hypercholesterolemia or preexisting ASCVD).
- Consider initiating statin therapy during pregnancy for high-risk individuals.
- Use contraception for individuals of childbearing potential on lipid-lowering medications with unknown or known risks during pregnancy.
- Provide preconception counseling for individuals with diabetes and childbearing potential regarding lipid-lowering medications.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Evaluate for secondary causes of hypertriglyceridemia in individuals with fasting triglyceride levels ≥500 mg/dL.
- Address lifestyle factors (obesity, metabolic syndrome) and secondary factors (diabetes, chronic liver/kidney disease, hypothyroidism) in hypertriglyceridemia.
- Maximize statin therapy before considering additional agents for elevated triglycerides (150–499 mg/dL).
- Address nutritional and lifestyle changes (weight loss, alcohol abstinence) for hypertriglyceridemia management.

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Routine continuation of lipid-lowering therapies during pregnancy without risk-benefit assessment.
- Use of lipid-lowering therapies (other than bile acid sequestrants) with limited safety data during pregnancy.
- Use of fibrates, niacin, or n-3 fatty acid supplements (other than icosapent ethyl) as add-on therapy to statins for cardiovascular risk reduction.

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Consideration of statin therapy during pregnancy for high-risk individuals (e.g., familial hypercholesterolemia or ASCVD).
- Meta-analysis evidence suggesting pravastatin may reduce preeclampsia, premature birth, and NICU admissions.
- Recommendation to use contraception for individuals on lipid-lowering medications with unknown or known pregnancy risks.
- Addition of icosapent ethyl to statin therapy for individuals with ASCVD or risk factors and elevated triglycerides (150–499 mg/dL) to reduce cardiovascular risk.
- Consideration of icosapent ethyl addition to statin therapy in individuals with ASCVD or cardiovascular risk factors and triglycerides 150–499 mg/dL to reduce cardiovascular events, supported by REDUCE-IT trial data (25% relative risk reduction).
