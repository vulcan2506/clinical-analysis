# obesity pharmacotherapy guidelines
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Obesity pharmacotherapy in diabetes care

**Core Recommendations:**
- Initiate obesity pharmacotherapy at the lowest dose with gradual stepwise uptitration based on tolerability and response.
- Evaluate and adjust diabetes medications (e.g., reduce sulfonylureas or insulin) to mitigate hypoglycemia risk when adding GLP-1 RAs or dual GIP/GLP-1 RAs.
- Assess effectiveness and safety of obesity medications monthly for the first 3 months and quarterly thereafter.
- Continue long-term pharmacotherapy for individuals achieving >5% weight loss after 3 months, unless contraindicated or poorly tolerated.
- Use shared decision-making to determine long-term weight management strategies, including dose adjustments or intermittent therapy.
- Discontinue sulfonylureas or reduce insulin dosing (e.g., reduce bolus by 10–20%, basal ~10% if A1C <7.5%) when initiating GLP-1 RAs or dual GIP/GLP-1 RAs to mitigate hypoglycemia risk.
- Evaluate and adjust diabetes medications to balance achievement of weight loss goals, health benefits, and tolerability; treatment dose may be less than the maximum approved dose.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- BMI ≥30 kg/m² or ≥27 kg/m² with one or more obesity-associated comorbid conditions (e.g., type 2 diabetes, hypertension, dyslipidemia).
- Prior to initiation, evaluate patient comorbidities, treatment burden, cost, and accessibility.
- Contraindicated in pregnancy, active conception attempts, or nursing; counsel individuals of childbearing potential on contraception.
- Monitor for nutritional deficiencies, particularly vitamin D, in individuals receiving GLP-1 RAs.
- Adjust diabetes medications (e.g., sulfonylureas, insulin) to avoid hypoglycemia when initiating GLP-1 RAs or dual GIP/GLP-1 RAs.
- Evaluate for gallbladder disease if cholelithiasis or cholecystitis is suspected; avoid use in at-risk individuals when prescribing GLP-1 RAs or dual GIP/GLP-1 RAs.
- Monitor for diabetic retinopathy in individuals at high risk (older individuals and those with longer duration of type 2 diabetes [≥10 years]) when using GLP-1 RAs.

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Phentermine and other older adrenergic agents approved only for short-term treatment.
- Fixed numeric A1C targets without individualized consideration of patient-specific factors.
- Discontinuation of obesity pharmacotherapy without a structured plan for weight maintenance or alternative strategies.

**Updated Recommendations:**
- GLP-1 RAs (e.g., liraglutide, semaglutide) and dual GIP/GLP-1 RAs (e.g., tirzepatide) preferred for weight loss and glycemic improvement in type 2 diabetes.
- Setmelanotide approved for rare genetic mutations causing severe hyperphagia and extreme obesity (e.g., leptin receptor deficiency).
- Semaglutide 2.4 mg and tirzepatide demonstrated significant weight loss and A1C reduction in clinical trials (STEP 2, SURMOUNT-2).
- Tirzepatide may reduce efficacy of oral hormonal contraceptives; switch to nonoral or barrier methods for 4 weeks post-initiation and post-dose escalation.
- Gradual, flexible titration of semaglutide improves medication-taking behavior and reduces adverse events without compromising efficacy.
- Setmelanotide is approved for rare genetic mutations causing severe hyperphagia and extreme obesity, such as leptin receptor deficiency and proopiomelanocortin deficiency.
- Orlistat may cause malabsorption of fat-soluble vitamins (A, D, E, K) and certain medications (e.g., cyclosporine, thyroid hormone, anticonvulsants); monitor renal function and discontinue if oxalate nephropathy occurs.
