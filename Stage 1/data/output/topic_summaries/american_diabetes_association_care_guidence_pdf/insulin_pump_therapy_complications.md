# insulin pump therapy complications
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** insulin pump therapy complications and AID system considerations

**Core Recommendations:**
- AID systems are preferred over nonautomated pumps and MDI in type 1 diabetes due to improved glycemic outcomes and reduced hypoglycemia risk.
- Insulin pump therapy can be initiated at diagnosis or at any point in diabetes management, with no age restrictions for adults, adolescents, or children.
- Complications include infusion set issues (dislodgement, occlusion) leading to ketosis or DKA, requiring early recognition and management.
- Pump-related skin issues include lipohypertrophy, lipoatrophy, and site infections.
- Discontinuation of pump therapy is uncommon but may occur due to cost, wearability, insurance loss, psychosocial factors, or suboptimal glycemic outcomes.
- Adoption of AID and pump therapy shows geographical variations related to healthcare professional preference or center characteristics.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Assessment of patient readiness and technical understanding for safe pump or AID system use.
- Support from a diabetes care team to guide device selection based on individual needs and preferences.
- Access to resources like Diabetes Wise, Diabetes Wise Pro, or PANTHER Program for device comparison.
- Consideration of socioeconomic factors, as pump therapy is more common in higher-income groups with private insurance.
- Monitoring for infusion set complications and skin issues to prevent ketosis, DKA, or infections.
- Assessment of readiness of the person with diabetes and their caregivers, if applicable, is required before initiation.

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Nonintegrated or standard insulin pumps without automation (AID systems are now preferred).
- Fixed A1C targets without individualized consideration of life expectancy, cognitive function, or hypoglycemia risk.
- Historical small, short-duration studies comparing MDI with insulin pump therapy without modern AID system data.

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Three AID systems approved for use in type 2 diabetes, expanding beyond type 1 diabetes.
- Early initiation of AID or pump therapy at diagnosis or any point in diabetes management.
- Geographical and socioeconomic disparities in pump therapy adoption, highlighting the need for equitable access.
- Use of CGM alongside insulin pump therapy to enhance glycemic control and safety.
- Focus on psychosocial factors (e.g., anxiety, depression) as reasons for pump therapy discontinuation.
- Three AID systems were recently approved for use by people with type 2 diabetes.
