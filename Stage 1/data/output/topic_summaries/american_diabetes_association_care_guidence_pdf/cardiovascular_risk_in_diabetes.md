# Cardiovascular Risk in Diabetes
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Cardiovascular Risk Management in Diabetes

**Core Recommendations:**
- Intensive lifestyle intervention focusing on weight loss through decreased caloric intake and increased physical activity is considered for improving glucose management, fitness, and some ASCVD risk factors.
- Individuals at increased ASCVD risk should receive statin therapy, ACE inhibitor or ARB therapy if hypertensive, and possibly aspirin unless contraindicated.
- ACE inhibitors or ARB therapy have clear cardiovascular benefits in people with diabetes, including reduced mortality, MI, and stroke.
- β-Blockers should be used in individuals with active angina, HFrEF, or for 3 years after MI with preserved left ventricular function.
- People with type 2 diabetes and CKD should be considered for treatment with finerenone to reduce cardiovascular outcomes and CKD progression.
- Consideration of SGLT2 inhibitors (e.g., empagliflozin, dapagliflozin) in patients with type 2 diabetes and established cardiovascular disease or high cardiovascular risk to reduce heart failure hospitalization and cardiovascular death.
- Consideration of GLP-1 receptor agonists (e.g., semaglutide, liraglutide) in patients with type 2 diabetes and established cardiovascular disease or high cardiovascular risk to reduce major adverse cardiovascular events.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Clear cardiovascular benefits exist for ACE inhibitor or ARB therapy in people with diabetes.
- Individuals at increased ASCVD risk should receive statin therapy unless contraindicated.
- Blood pressure targets in patients with diabetes and hypertension should be individualized, with a general target of <130/80 mmHg in those at high cardiovascular risk, as per recent guidelines.
- Lipid testing should include non-HDL cholesterol and apolipoprotein B in addition to LDL cholesterol for more comprehensive cardiovascular risk assessment in patients with diabetes.
- Assessment of kidney function (e.g., estimated glomerular filtration rate [eGFR] and albuminuria) is required to guide the use of ACE inhibitors, ARBs, finerenone, and SGLT2 inhibitors in patients with diabetes.
- Evaluation for heart failure with preserved ejection fraction (HFpEF) in patients with diabetes and symptoms of heart failure, even with preserved left ventricular ejection fraction.
- Consideration of dual antiplatelet therapy (e.g., aspirin plus P2Y12 inhibitor) in patients with diabetes and recent acute coronary syndrome or high-risk coronary artery disease.

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Consideration of finerenone for patients with type 2 diabetes and CKD to reduce cardiovascular outcomes and CKD progression.
- Use of β-blockers in individuals with active angina or HFrEF and for 3 years after MI in those with preserved left ventricular function.
- Empagliflozin and other SGLT2 inhibitors are now recommended for patients with type 2 diabetes and heart failure with reduced ejection fraction (HFrEF) to reduce hospitalization and cardiovascular death.
