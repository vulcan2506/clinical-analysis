# insulin therapy indications
*Source: ICMR Type 2 Diabetes.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Insulin therapy indications and types

**Therapeutic Actions:**
- Insulin is the mainstay of therapy in type 1 diabetes and indicated for type 2 diabetes patients with significant symptomatic hyperglycemia, weight loss, polyuria, polydipsia, or polyphagia.
- Insulin is required for patients with fasting plasma glucose > 270 mg/dl, HbA1c > 9%, severe infections, or presence of ketosis.
- Insulin is indicated for patients not responding to optimal doses of oral anti-hyperglycaemic agents, acute hyperglycemia, DKA/HHS/lactic acidosis, and stressful situations like MI, stroke, or trauma.
- Insulin is recommended for pregnancy, lactation, peri-operative states, and patients intolerant or contraindicated to oral hypoglycaemic agents.
- Insulin is indicated for hepatic and renal failure, renal transplantation, and patients on steroids.

**Prerequisites:**
- Patient must have type 1 diabetes or type 2 diabetes with specific clinical markers such as symptomatic hyperglycemia or failure of oral agents.
- Diagnostic criteria include fasting plasma glucose > 270 mg/dl or HbA1c > 9%.
- Presence of acute conditions like DKA, HHS, lactic acidosis, or severe infections.
- Patient must be intolerant or contraindicated to oral anti-hyperglycaemic agents.
- Clinical context includes pregnancy, lactation, peri-operative state, or specific comorbidities like renal failure.
- Patient must have failed optimal doses of oral anti-hyperglycaemic agents alone or in combination before insulin is indicated for non-acute type 2 diabetes cases.
- Oral agents can be used in renal transplantation patients, distinguishing them from other renal failure cases where insulin is indicated.
