# compounded diabetes medication guidance
*Source: ICMR Type 2 Diabetes.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** compounded diabetes medication guidance

**Therapeutic Actions:**
- Physicians should be aware of herb-drug interactions when patients use indigenous diabetes medications
- Herbal products like Pterocarpus marsupium, Gymnema sylvestre, Mormordica chirantica, and Eugenia jambolana may have blood glucose-lowering effects
- There is a common misconception that all herbal drugs are safe and non-toxic, which is not necessarily true
- Physicians should assess the potential for contamination or adulteration in indigenous diabetes medications, as these may pose additional safety risks beyond herb-drug interactions
- Clinicians should document the use of indigenous diabetes medications in patient records to monitor for adverse effects or interactions with conventional therapies

**Prerequisites:**
- Physicians must evaluate the safety and efficacy of indigenous diabetes medications in patient management
- Careful assessment of herb-drug interactions is required before recommending or continuing indigenous medications
- Physicians must verify the source and preparation method of indigenous diabetes medications to ensure quality and consistency, as variability in formulations can affect efficacy and safety
- A thorough patient history should include inquiry about the use of indigenous diabetes medications, even if not disclosed voluntarily, to prevent unrecognized interactions
