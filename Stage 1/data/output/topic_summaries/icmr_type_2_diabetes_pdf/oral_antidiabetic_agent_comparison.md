# oral antidiabetic agent comparison
*Source: ICMR Type 2 Diabetes.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** oral antidiabetic agent comparison and selection

**Therapeutic Actions:**
- Select oral anti-hyperglycaemic agents based on their mechanism of action targeting gut glucose absorption, peripheral glucose uptake, hepatic glucose output, pancreatic hormone secretion, incretin effects, or renal glucose handling
- Prefer newer sulfonylureas (Glimepiride, Gliclazide) over older agents due to updated clinical profiles
- Monitor for continued drug efficacy and development of side-effects during therapy
- Consider insulin initiation early in patients with marked hyperglycemia, severe symptoms, acute complications, or long-standing undiagnosed diabetes with complications
- Anticipate reduced efficacy of oral agents over time and plan for poly-pharmacy or timely insulin introduction

**Prerequisites:**
- Metformin is the preferred first-line oral agent unless contraindicated
- If metformin is contraindicated, monotherapy can be initiated with any other class of oral agents
- Assess patient-specific factors such as hypoglycemia risk, weight impact, cost, and comorbidities when selecting agents
- Evaluate renal function and hepatic status before prescribing agents metabolized or excreted via these routes
- Initiate lifestyle modification alongside pharmacotherapy at diagnosis
- Initiate pharmacotherapy alongside lifestyle modification for the majority of patients at the time of diagnosis
- Consider continuous insulin therapy if initial assessment reveals complications such as diabetic retinopathy or nephropathy, indicating long-standing undiagnosed diabetes
