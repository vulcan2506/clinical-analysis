# diabetes complications research
*Source: ICMR Type 2 Diabetes.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Diabetes complications research and management

**Therapeutic Actions:**
- Acute complications (hypo-/hyperglycaemic emergencies) require immediate glucose monitoring and tailored treatment based on consciousness level.
- Chronic complications (microvascular, macrovascular, diabetic foot) necessitate early, proactive screening even in prediabetes due to late-stage symptom onset.
- Comprehensive initial assessment includes detailed history, examination, and preliminary tests, with additional investigations for high-risk patients.
- Referral to specialists (e.g., retinal specialist) is mandatory for organ-specific complications or high-risk conditions.
- Management emphasizes patient compliance, medication review, and addressing comorbidities (e.g., hypertension, kidney disease).
- Recurrent hypoglycaemia requires checking for hypothyroidism in addition to renal function
- DKA is characterized by metabolic acidosis with abdominal pain, breathlessness, vomiting, altered sensorium, and dehydration

**Prerequisites:**
- Patient must have confirmed diabetes diagnosis or prediabetes for early complication screening.
- Glycemic control history (HbA1C, blood glucose) and medication compliance must be documented.
- High-risk patients (e.g., with hypertension, kidney disease) require additional investigations.
- Consciousness level and glucose monitoring availability dictate hypoglycaemia treatment approach.
- Organ-specific symptoms or abnormal preliminary tests trigger specialist referral.
- Documentation of duration of diabetes, medication doses, and compliance with medication and lifestyle measures during initial assessment
- Assessment of tuberculosis history as part of comprehensive initial evaluation for high-risk patients
