# diabetes monitoring protocols
*Source: ICMR Type 2 Diabetes.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Diabetes monitoring protocols

**Therapeutic Actions:**
- Monitoring and follow-up protocols for people with diabetes are structured into self-monitoring with blood glucose (SMBG) and annual check-ups.
- Self-monitoring with blood glucose (SMBG) using glucose monitors is a key component of diabetes management.
- Annual check-ups are a critical part of ongoing diabetes care and follow-up.
- Continuous Glucose Monitoring (CGM) is recommended as an alternative or adjunct to traditional SMBG for people with diabetes, particularly those on intensive insulin therapy or with hypoglycemia unawareness.
- HbA1c testing should be performed at least twice per year for people with stable glycemic control and quarterly for those with unstable glycemia or recent treatment changes.
- Urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio (UACR) testing is required annually to screen for diabetic kidney disease in people with type 1 diabetes for ≥5 years or type 2 diabetes at diagnosis.
- Dilated eye examination by an ophthalmologist or optometrist is mandated annually to screen for diabetic retinopathy, with more frequent monitoring for those with pre-existing retinopathy.

**Prerequisites:**
- People with diabetes must be actively monitored and followed up according to structured protocols.
- Access to a glucose monitor is required for self-monitoring of blood glucose (SMBG).
- CGM use requires patient education on device application, data interpretation, and troubleshooting to ensure accurate and actionable glucose data.
- HbA1c testing requires standardized laboratory methods (e.g., NGSP-certified assays) to ensure comparability across clinical settings.
- Annual check-ups must include a comprehensive foot examination to assess for peripheral neuropathy and peripheral arterial disease, with referral to podiatry if abnormalities are detected.
