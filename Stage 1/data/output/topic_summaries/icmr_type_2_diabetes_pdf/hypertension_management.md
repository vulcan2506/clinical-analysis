# hypertension management
*Source: ICMR Type 2 Diabetes.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Hypertension management in diabetes

**Therapeutic Actions:**
- Blood pressure should be checked on every visit and kept under control.
- Initial attempt to control BP should be made using non-pharmacological measures (weight reduction, dietary salt reduction, stress management).
- Drug therapy should be initiated if BP remains >140/90 mm Hg after non-pharmacological measures.
- Monotherapy should be started with ACE inhibitors/ARB, calcium channel blockers, or selective beta blockers/diuretics as third-line.
- If BP remains >140/90 mm Hg after 1 month of monotherapy, add a second drug from a different class (avoid combining ACEI and ARB).
- Recheck blood pressure after 1 week if initial BP >140/90 mm Hg before initiating drug therapy

**Prerequisites:**
- BP >140/90 mm Hg triggers treatment initiation.
- Non-pharmacological measures should be attempted before drug therapy.
- If BP >180/110 mm Hg or end organ damage is present, initiate two drugs and refer to a specialist.
- For diabetic renal disease, lower BP targets are recommended.
- Recheck BP after 1 week if initial BP >140/90 mm Hg.
- Evidence of end organ damage (e.g., retinopathy, nephropathy, or cardiovascular disease) necessitates immediate dual therapy and specialist referral
