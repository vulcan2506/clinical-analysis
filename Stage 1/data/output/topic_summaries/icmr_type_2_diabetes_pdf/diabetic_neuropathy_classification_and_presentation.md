# diabetic neuropathy classification and presentation
*Source: ICMR Type 2 Diabetes.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Diabetic neuropathy classification and presentation

**Therapeutic Actions:**
- Sensory neuropathy presents with paresthesiae (tingling, numbness, pain) or loss of sensation in touch, pain, temperature, and vibration
- Neuropathy can cause foot deformities, dry plantar skin, and neuropathic foot ulcers prone to infection, cellulitis, and osteomyelitis, often requiring partial foot amputation
- Motor weakness in small foot muscles leads to inability to grip footwear, arch alteration, and pressure on the first metatarsal and heel
- Diabetic amyotrophy is indicated by quadriceps wasting, absent knee jerk, and bilateral hip muscle weakness
- Cranial motor neuropathy may involve 3rd, 6th, and rarely 7th nerves; radiculopathy and cranial neuropathies are reversible, while sensorimotor symmetrical polyneuropathy is progressive and irreversible
- Autonomic neuropathy may present with resting tachycardia, fixed heart rate, or exercise intolerance due to cardiac autonomic dysfunction

**Prerequisites:**
- Assess for autonomic neuropathy symptoms such as orthostatic hypotension, gastroparesis, diarrhea/constipation, impotence, cystopathy, and gustatory sweating
- Exclude other causes of neuropathy (e.g., nutritional deficiency, hypothyroidism, spinal disorders, paraneoplastic syndrome, drug-induced, alcohol abuse, uremic neuropathies)
- Perform foot examination for sensation, motor power assessment, and tendon jerks
- Measure blood pressure in sitting and standing positions and conduct ECG for heart rate variability to diagnose autonomic neuropathy
- Perform 10-g monofilament testing for protective sensation in feet as part of standardized foot examination
