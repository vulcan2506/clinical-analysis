# insulin delivery device recommendations
*Source: ICMR Type 2 Diabetes.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Insulin delivery devices and injection techniques

**Therapeutic Actions:**
- Pen-like devices and insulin cartridges deliver insulin subcutaneously through a needle and are easy to use
- Insulin pumps with integrated algorithms can automatically stop insulin release 30 minutes before hypoglycemia onset
- Injections should be given into subcutaneous tissue at a 90° angle, or 45° angle with skin pinching for thin individuals or children
- Insulin injection sites include abdomen, thighs, arms, and buttocks, with abdomen and thighs being most convenient for self-injection
- Insulin analogues can be administered with or after a meal, whereas short-acting/premixed insulin requires a 30-minute interval before meals
- Used needles must be disposed of in a bio-safe manner to prevent sharps injuries and environmental contamination
- Exercise increases the rate of insulin absorption from injection sites, requiring potential dose adjustments or timing modifications

**Prerequisites:**
- Insulin pump use is restricted to diabetes specialists with relevant experience
- Pump algorithm is indicated for patients with brittle diabetes, multiple dose injections, and recurrent hypoglycemia episodes
- Insulin initiation can be performed on an outpatient basis without hospitalization
- Site rotation within one area is recommended to prevent lipohypertrophy or lipoatrophy
- Patient characteristics such as body habitus (thin individuals or children) determine the need for skin pinching and 45° injection angle
- Insulin pump use requires diabetes specialist oversight with relevant experience
- Site selection must consider variable absorption rates between anatomical locations (abdomen > arms > thighs > buttocks)
