# peripheral neuropathy management
*Source: ICMR Type 2 Diabetes.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** peripheral neuropathy management

**Therapeutic Actions:**
- Refer atypical cases to a neurologist for further investigations like nerve conduction studies
- Tight glycemic control is essential for peripheral neuropathy management
- First-line medications for painful peripheral neuropathy include antiepileptics (gabapentin, pregabalin), tricyclic antidepressants (TCAs) (amitriptyline, imipramine), and SNRIs (venlafaxine, duloxetine)
- Use maximum dose of first-line drugs or combine two drugs for pain relief in painful neuropathy
- Second-line drugs like opioids (tramadol) are advised for resistant painful neuropathies
- Refer males with sexual dysfunction to specialists for appropriate therapy

**Prerequisites:**
- Vitamin B12 assay (if possible) and TSH testing for evaluation
- Paraproteinemia testing (if possible) for atypical cases
- Nerve conduction studies for atypical cases referred to a neurologist
- Vibration perception threshold (VPT) examination using a biothesiometer for assessing sensory loss
- Manual palpation of dorsalis pedis and posterior tibial pulses and ABI assessment if peripheral pulsations are absent or clinical suspicion of ischemia
- Record vibration perception results as absent, reduced, or present when using tuning fork assessment
- Assess VPT using a biothesiometer with the patient in supine position (with or without eyes closed)
