# iron transport and regulation mechanisms
*Source: Iron_metabolism.pdf*

**Metabolic Pathway:** transferrin receptor mediated iron uptake and ferritin storage regulation

**Iron Handling Characteristics:**
- Transferrin receptors bind iron-loaded transferrin to facilitate cellular iron uptake via endocytosis.
- Ferritin acts as the primary intracellular iron storage protein, regulating iron availability for metabolic processes.
- Iron regulatory proteins control the expression of transferrin receptors and ferritin based on cellular iron status.
- Ferric reductase activity is essential for reducing dietary ferric iron to absorbable ferrous iron.
- Iron homeostasis is maintained through the coordinated regulation of absorption, transport, and storage mechanisms.
- Ferritin's iron storage capacity is regulated by iron-dependent nucleation and mineralization processes within its nanocage structure.
- Iron regulatory proteins (IRPs) bind to iron-responsive elements (IREs) in the 5' or 3' untranslated regions of target mRNAs to post-transcriptionally control iron metabolism genes.

**Metabolic Prerequisites:**
- Availability of transferrin-bound iron for receptor binding and subsequent endocytosis.
- Functional ferric reductase enzymes to convert dietary iron to the ferrous state.
- Proper expression and activity of iron regulatory proteins to modulate receptor and storage protein levels.
- Intact cellular machinery for endosomal acidification and iron release from transferrin.
- Adequate oxygen levels for the function of iron-dependent enzymes and metabolic pathways.
- Adequate intracellular copper levels for the function of ceruloplasmin, which oxidizes ferrous iron to ferric iron for incorporation into transferrin.
- Functional IRE/IRP system for post-transcriptional regulation of transferrin receptor, ferritin, and DMT1 expression.
