# iron biological functions
*Source: Iron_metabolism.pdf*

**Metabolic Pathway:** iron biological functions and regulation

**Iron Handling Characteristics:**
- Acts as a co-factor in hundreds of proteins and enzymes, including hemeproteins for oxygen transport and iron-sulphur enzymes for electron transfer.
- Facilitates DNA synthesis via ribonucleotide reductase and supports growth; deficiency leads to cell death.
- Regulates absorption via hepcidin and IRP/IRE mechanisms, balancing entry and storage to prevent anemia or overload.
- Stores iron safely in ferritin and hemosiderin forms within hepatocytes and macrophages to detoxify excess metal.
- Transports iron systemically via transferrin, utilizing receptor-mediated endocytosis for cellular uptake.
- Heme-iron absorption occurs via a distinct, more efficient pathway involving intact metalloporphyrin uptake via heme receptors and vesicular transport, with Fe+2 release mediated by microsomal heme-oxygenase.

**Metabolic Prerequisites:**
- Availability of reducing agents (ascorbate, histidine) to mobilize dietary ferric iron in the stomach.
- Presence of Dcytb (duodenal cytochrome b) to reduce Fe+3 to Fe+2 for DMT-1 transport.
- Functional hephaestin and ferroportin for basolateral iron export and oxidation.
- Intact IRE/IRP regulatory machinery to modulate ferritin and transferrin receptor expression.
- Adequate levels of transferrin and transferrin receptors for systemic delivery and cellular uptake.
- Presence of hephaestin, a multicopper ferroxidase, as an accessory protein for ferroportin-mediated basolateral iron export to enable transferrin binding and prevent endothelial damage.
