# atherosclerosis pathogenesis and LDL-C causality
*Source: Lipid Disorders.pdf*

**Recommendation Topic:** atherosclerosis pathogenesis and LDL-C causality

**Clinical Recommendations:**
- Atherosclerosis is caused by progressive deposition of LDL-C and other ApoB-containing lipoproteins in the artery wall, triggering inflammatory reactions leading to plaque formation and progression.
- LDL-C is a direct cause of ASCVD, not merely a risk factor; lowering plasma LDL-C is the primary focus for preventing atherosclerotic CV events.
- Clinical benefit of LDL-C lowering depends on achieved reduction; higher-risk individuals require more intense LDL-C lowering to achieve the same residual risk as lower-risk individuals.
- Risk scores (SCORE2, SCORE2-OP) are endorsed for estimating 10-year risk of fatal/non-fatal MI, stroke, or CV death in persons aged 40–89 without known CVD.
- Risk is a continuum; SCORE2/SCORE2-OP thresholds reflect arbitrary cut-offs based on clinical trial evidence.

**Eligibility Criteria:**
- Estimated absolute risk of acute CV events must guide intensity of LDL-C lowering.
- SCORE2/SCORE2-OP risk algorithms use non-HDL-cholesterol (total cholesterol minus HDL-C) as input.
- Risk modifiers (e.g., plaque burden, biomarkers) may reclassify individuals to higher risk categories for treatment decisions.
- Untreated LDL-C ≥4.9 mmol/L (≥190 mg/dL) indicates at least high total CV risk.
- Risk assessment should consider age and sex, as younger individuals may achieve greater absolute risk reduction.
- Untreated LDL-C levels ≥4.9 mmol/L (≥190 mg/dL) automatically classify individuals as at least high total CV risk regardless of other risk factors.
- Risk assessment for young patients with diabetes (T1DM <35 years; T2DM <50 years) without other risk factors and DM duration <10 years places them in the moderate risk category.
