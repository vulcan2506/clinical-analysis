# Obesity-Related Lipid and Cardiovascular Risk Management
*Source: Lipid Disorders.pdf*

**Recommendation Topic:** Lp(a) Risk Stratification and Management in Obesity-Related Dyslipidemia

**Clinical Recommendations:**
- Lp(a) levels should be measured at least once in every adult's lifetime, particularly in younger patients with FH, premature ASCVD, or family history of premature ASCVD/high Lp(a).
- Lp(a) levels >50 mg/dL (≥105 nmol/L) are considered a CV risk-enhancing factor, with higher levels associated with greater risk.
- Lp(a) measurement should be repeated after menopause if pre-menopausal levels were borderline.
- Risk from high Lp(a) increases slightly at levels >30 mg/dL (62 nmol/L) and becomes clinically relevant above 50 mg/dL (105 nmol/L).
- Lp(a) levels may be underestimated or overestimated due to assay variability, so molar units (nmol/L) are preferred for measurement.

**Eligibility Criteria:**
- Lp(a) measurement should be considered at the first lipid profile or the next one if previously performed.
- Lp(a) levels should be considered in individuals at moderate risk or near treatment decision thresholds to refine CV risk estimation.
- Lp(a) levels should be interpreted alongside other risk factors and absolute CV risk for clinical decision-making.
- Lp(a) levels should be measured at least once in every adult's lifetime, either at the first lipid profile or at the next one if lipid profiles have previously been performed.
