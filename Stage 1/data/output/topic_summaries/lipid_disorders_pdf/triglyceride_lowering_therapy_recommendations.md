# triglyceride-lowering therapy recommendations
*Source: Lipid Disorders.pdf*

**Recommendation Topic:** Triglyceride-Lowering Therapy Recommendations

**Clinical Recommendations:**
- Statins remain the first-line pharmacological therapy for reducing CVD risk in high-risk patients with hypertriglyceridaemia.
- Fibrates (e.g., fenofibrate, bezafibrate) have moderate triglyceride-lowering effects but lack evidence for reducing MACE or total mortality in statin-treated patients.
- High-dose icosapent ethyl is recommended for high-risk or very high-risk patients with elevated triglycerides (135–499 mg/dL) despite statin therapy to lower CVD events.
- Volanesorsen is considered for patients with severe hypertriglyceridaemia due to familial chylomicronemia syndrome (FCS) unresponsive to diet and other therapies.
- New RNA-based therapies targeting ApoC-III are under investigation for significant triglyceride reduction.
- Fibrates are not indicated to lower serum cholesterol or LDL-C levels, even in patients with atherogenic dyslipidaemia.

**Eligibility Criteria:**
- Triglyceride levels should be measured in fasting state (135–499 mg/dL range for icosapent ethyl consideration).
- Patients must be on maximally tolerated statin therapy before considering adjunctive triglyceride-lowering agents.
- Genetic confirmation of FCS is required for volanesorsen use in Europe.
- Genetically confirmed familial chylomicronemia syndrome (FCS) is required for volanesorsen use in Europe.
