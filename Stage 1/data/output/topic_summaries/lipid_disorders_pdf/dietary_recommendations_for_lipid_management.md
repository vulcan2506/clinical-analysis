# dietary recommendations for lipid management
*Source: Lipid Disorders.pdf*

**Recommendation Topic:** dietary recommendations for lipid management in CKD

**Clinical Recommendations:**
- Healthy diet defined as low in saturated fat with focus on wholegrain products, vegetables, fruit, and fish
- Dietary supplements (e.g., red yeast rice, PUFAs, phytosterols) not recommended for reducing CV risk without documented safety and LDL-C-lowering efficacy
- Phytosterols at doses up to 2 grams/day may reduce LDL-C by ~10% but lack CV outcome benefits
- Red yeast rice preparations with ≥3 mg/day monacolins prohibited in EU due to safety concerns
- Rosuvastatin 5 mg daily outperforms dietary supplements in LDL-C reduction in short-term trials
- High-dose, purified icosapent ethyl is an exception for use in hypertriglyceridemia despite general lack of evidence for PUFAs in LDL-C lowering or CV risk reduction

**Eligibility Criteria:**
- Dietary pattern must prioritize wholegrain products, vegetables, fruit, and fish
- Phytosterol use requires doses ≤2 grams/day to avoid adverse effects
- Red yeast rice supplements must comply with EU monacolin dose restrictions (<3 mg/day)
- Phytosterol use must be limited to doses ≤2 grams/day to avoid adverse effects and ensure LDL-C reduction (~10%)
