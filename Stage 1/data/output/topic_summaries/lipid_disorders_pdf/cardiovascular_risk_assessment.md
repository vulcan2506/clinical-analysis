# cardiovascular risk assessment
*Source: Lipid Disorders.pdf*

**Recommendation Topic:** cardiovascular risk assessment and stratification

**Clinical Recommendations:**
- Categorizes patients into specific cardiovascular risk groups based on clinical variables
- Utilizes standardized tables to define recommendation classes and evidence levels for risk management
- Utilizes pooled cohort equations (e.g., ACC/AHA Pooled Cohort Risk Assessment Equations) for 10-year atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease (ASCVD) risk calculation
- Incorporates lifetime risk assessment for younger patients (<40 years) or those with low 10-year risk but high-risk features
- Recommends risk enhancers (e.g., family history of premature ASCVD, coronary artery calcium [CAC] scoring, lipoprotein(a), apolipoprotein B) to refine risk stratification in intermediate-risk patients
- Employs coronary artery calcium (CAC) scoring ≥100 Agatston units or ≥75th percentile for age/sex/race as a risk modifier to intensify statin therapy in borderline/intermediate-risk patients
- Considers social determinants of health (e.g., socioeconomic status, access to care) as contextual factors in risk assessment

**Eligibility Criteria:**
- Patient must be evaluated against defined cardiovascular risk categories
- Clinical variables must be assessed to determine risk stratification
- 10-year ASCVD risk must be calculated using validated tools (e.g., ACC/AHA Pooled Cohort Equations) for patients aged 40–75 years without known ASCVD
- Coronary artery calcium (CAC) scoring is recommended for patients in whom risk-based treatment decisions are uncertain (e.g., borderline/intermediate risk)
- Lipoprotein(a) measurement is recommended at least once in adults to assess risk, particularly in those with family history of premature ASCVD or personal history of ASCVD events despite low LDL-C
- Apolipoprotein B (ApoB) or non-HDL-C should be used as secondary lipid measures when triglycerides are ≥175 mg/dL or in patients with metabolic syndrome/diabetes
- Risk enhancers (e.g., chronic kidney disease, inflammatory conditions, metabolic syndrome) must be systematically evaluated in patients with intermediate risk to guide intensification of therapy
