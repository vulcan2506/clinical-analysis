**Clinical Recommendation:** Autoimmune Disease and ASCVD Risk Analysis

**Key Clinical Actions:**
- Autoimmune diseases significantly increase ASCVD risk, with systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE) and rheumatoid arthritis (RA) showing elevated cardiovascular event rates.
- Coronary arterial calcification is more prevalent and severe in autoimmune diseases like SLE and RA compared to general populations.
- General cardiovascular risk scores (e.g., ASCVD) are often used for autoimmune patients, though disease-specific scores may not outperform them.
- Novel imaging techniques are recommended to assess cardiac manifestations in systemic inflammatory diseases.
- Statin therapy reduces mortality and morbidity in autoimmune patients with hyperlipidemia, particularly in SLE.
- Chronic systemic inflammation and endothelial dysfunction drive atherosclerosis in autoimmune diseases, leading to underestimated risk by traditional lipid scores.
- Statin therapy provides mortality and morbidity benefits in autoimmune patients independent of lipid-lowering effects due to pleiotropic anti-inflammatory properties.

**Patient Preparation / Diagnostic Criteria:**
- Use general ASCVD risk scores for autoimmune patients when disease-specific scores are unavailable or not superior.
- Consider advanced imaging (e.g., coronary calcification assessment) for high-risk autoimmune patients.
- Monitor lipid levels and cardiovascular risk factors closely in autoimmune disease patients due to elevated baseline risk.
- Clinicians should apply a clinical judgment 'risk multiplier' for active inflammation and comorbidities when using general risk scores.
- Statin therapy should be considered in autoimmune patients with active disease even if LDL-C levels are below traditional treatment cutoffs.
