**Clinical Recommendation:** Hypertriglyceridemia Management & Risk Stratification

**Key Clinical Actions:**
- Lifestyle management (low sugar/saturated fat, alcohol reduction, exercise, weight loss) is first-line for adults with persistently elevated TG ≥150 mg/dL after secondary cause evaluation.
- Intensification of LDL-C-lowering therapy is recommended for adults with clinical ASCVD, LDL-C ≥55 mg/dL, and TG ≥150 to 999 mg/dL on maximally tolerated statin.
- Olezarsen is recommended as an adjunct to diet for adults with FCS and fasting TG ≥1000 mg/dL to lower TG and reduce pancreatitis risk.
- IPE may be reasonable for adults ≥50 years with ASCVD or diabetes and TG ≥150 to 499 mg/dL to lower ASCVD risk.
- Statins remain first-line therapy for patients with TG <1000 mg/dL to reduce ASCVD risk, as fibrates do not provide incremental ASCVD benefit when added to statins.
- For patients with familial chylomicronemia syndrome (FCS), adoption of a very-low-fat diet (<10% to 15% of daily calories, or <20 g fat/day) is recommended to suppress chylomicron production.
- In patients with moderate hypertriglyceridemia and no clinical ASCVD or diabetes, statins should be first-line therapy, with the degree of TG reduction proportional to baseline TG levels (average 20% to 40% reduction).

**Patient Preparation / Diagnostic Criteria:**
- Evaluation and management of secondary causes (e.g., diabetes, hypothyroidism, medications) before initiating pharmacotherapy.
- Confirmation of persistent hypertriglyceridemia (fasting TG ≥150 mg/dL) after a minimum of 4 to 12 weeks of lifestyle management.
- Assessment of patient age, presence of clinical ASCVD, diabetes status, and calculated 10-year ASCVD risk.
- Use of maximally tolerated statin therapy as the baseline for evaluating need for additional lipid-lowering agents.
- Consideration of panel-based genetic testing for rare variants in patients with severe hypertriglyceridemia (TG ≥500 mg/dL).
- For patients with TG ≥500 mg/dL, a thorough reassessment for secondary causes should be performed, including consideration of panel-based genetic testing to exclude monogenic causes (e.g., FCS).
- In patients with persistently elevated TG ≥150 mg/dL, lifestyle management must include a minimum of 4 to 12 weeks before pharmacotherapy initiation, with confirmation of persistent hypertriglyceridemia on a stable, maximally tolerated dose of statin if indicated.
