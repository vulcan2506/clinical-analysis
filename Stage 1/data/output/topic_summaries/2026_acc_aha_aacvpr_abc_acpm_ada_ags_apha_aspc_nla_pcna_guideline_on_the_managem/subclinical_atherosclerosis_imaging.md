# Subclinical Atherosclerosis Imaging
*Source: 2026 ACC-AHA-AACVPR-ABC-ACPM-ADA 
 AGS-APhA-ASPC-NLA-PCNA Guideline on
the Management of Dyslipidemia.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Subclinical Atherosclerosis Imaging with CAC Scoring

**Key Clinical Actions:**
- CAC scoring is recommended for adults at intermediate or select borderline risk with uncertain LLT decisions to guide therapy initiation or deferral.
- CAC score of 0 AU in intermediate-risk adults without higher-risk conditions may justify deferring LLT and reassessing in 3–7 years.
- CAC score >0 AU, particularly ≥100 AU or ≥75th percentile, warrants LLT initiation to reduce ASCVD risk.
- CAC testing refines treatment goals in intermediate/high-risk adults when LLT intensity is uncertain.
- CAC scoring is generally recommended for men ≥40 years and women ≥45 years without known ASCVD.
- Emotional distress or anxiety after receiving CAC results should be discussed with patients prior to ordering the test.

**Patient Preparation / Diagnostic Criteria:**
- CAC testing is performed using non-contrast-gated cardiac CT or estimated from noncardiac CT scans.
- CAC score interpretation requires categorization (absent, minimal, mild, moderate, severe, extensive) for clinical decision-making.
- Assessment of subclinical atherosclerosis is prioritized in intermediate-risk adults where risk reclassification is most impactful.
- CAC testing is performed using non-contrast-gated cardiac CT, though CAC burden can also be estimated from noncardiac CT scans.
