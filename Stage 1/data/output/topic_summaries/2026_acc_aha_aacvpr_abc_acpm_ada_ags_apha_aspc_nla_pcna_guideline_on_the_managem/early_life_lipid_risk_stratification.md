# Early-Life Lipid Risk Stratification
*Source: 2026 ACC-AHA-AACVPR-ABC-ACPM-ADA 
 AGS-APhA-ASPC-NLA-PCNA Guideline on
the Management of Dyslipidemia.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Early-Life Lipid Risk Stratification

**Key Clinical Actions:**
- Long-term cardiovascular risk is significantly increased by hyperlipidemia in early adulthood.
- Cumulative LDL-C exposure during young adulthood and middle age is strongly associated with cardiovascular event risk.
- ApoB concentrations across early adulthood predict rates of change in atherogenic lipid exposure.
- Young adults (20–44 years) have suboptimal prevalence, treatment, and control of cardiovascular risk factors.
- Familial hypercholesterolemia phenotype in early life substantially elevates long-term atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease risk.
- Cumulative LDL-C exposure during young adulthood and middle age is a stronger predictor of cardiovascular events than single-point LDL-C measurements in older age.
- ApoB concentrations in early adulthood are predictive of future atherogenic lipid trajectory changes, independent of LDL-C levels.

**Patient Preparation / Diagnostic Criteria:**
- Assessment of cumulative LDL-C exposure during early adulthood and middle age for risk prediction.
- Evaluation of apoB concentrations as a predictor for lipid trajectory changes.
- Screening for familial hypercholesterolemia phenotype in young adults to stratify long-term risk.
- Use of lifetime risk models incorporating cumulative LDL-C exposure to guide early intervention in young adults.
- Screening for familial hypercholesterolemia in young adults should include genetic testing or Dutch Lipid Clinic Network criteria when phenotype is ambiguous.
- ApoB measurement should be prioritized over LDL-C in young adults for risk stratification due to its stronger association with atherogenic lipid trajectories.
- Risk prediction tools for young adults should integrate cumulative LDL-C exposure metrics rather than relying solely on single-point measurements.
