**Clinical Recommendation:** Statin Therapy in Older Adults: Shared Decision-Making & Deprescribing

**Key Clinical Actions:**
- Decisions to initiate, continue, or deprescribe lipid-lowering therapy (LLT) in older adults must balance ASCVD event prevention with risks of multimorbidity, frailty, polypharmacy, and functional decline.
- Clinician–patient discussions about LLT should be revisited over time, include caregivers and specialists, and align with patient priorities (e.g., pill burden vs. high cardiovascular risk).
- Time-to-benefit from primary prevention statins is ~2.5 years in adults up to age 75, but evidence for adults ≥75 years is limited.
- A CAC score of 0 in older adults suggests potential benefits of LLT are unlikely to outweigh risks.
- For patients with life expectancy <1 year, deprescribing statins may be reasonable and associated with improved QOL.
- CAC-guided reclassification of older adults with intermediate ASCVD risk (e.g., CAC ≤10) to lower-risk categories, potentially avoiding LLT when appropriate.
- Consideration of patient preferences for functional status preservation over ASCVD event prevention in late life.

**Patient Preparation / Diagnostic Criteria:**
- Shared decision-making must incorporate patient preferences, life expectancy, frailty, and competing risks.
- CAC scoring may guide personalized LLT decisions in older adults with intermediate ASCVD risk.
- Use clinical decision tools (e.g., ePrognosis, eFrailty) to estimate life expectancy and frailty.
- RCT data for adults >75 years are limited; decisions should be individualized.
- Clinician–patient discussions should address functional status preservation and quality of life.
- Use of clinical decision tools (e.g., ePrognosis, eFrailty) to estimate life expectancy and frailty in LLT decisions.
- Limiting LLT to older adults with CAC scores >10 for personalized shared decision-making in intermediate ASCVD risk.
