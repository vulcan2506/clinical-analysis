# LDL-C Lowering Evidence Synthesis
*Source: 2026 ACC-AHA-AACVPR-ABC-ACPM-ADA 
 AGS-APhA-ASPC-NLA-PCNA Guideline on
the Management of Dyslipidemia.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Intensive LDL-C Lowering and Nonstatin Therapies

**Key Clinical Actions:**
- Intensive LDL-C lowering (e.g., high-intensity statins) reduces major cardiovascular events in high-risk patients.
- PCSK9 inhibitors (e.g., evolocumab) are recommended for patients with familial hypercholesterolemia or ASCVD not achieving LDL-C goals on maximally tolerated statins.
- Bempedoic acid is an alternative for statin-intolerant patients to lower LDL-C when combined with ezetimibe.
- Icosapent ethyl reduces cardiovascular risk in patients with hypertriglyceridemia on statin therapy.
- APOC3 inhibition (e.g., volanesorsen) is considered for familial chylomicronemia syndrome.
- Evolocumab is specifically recommended for homozygous familial hypercholesterolemia patients not achieving LDL-C goals on maximally tolerated therapy.

**Patient Preparation / Diagnostic Criteria:**
- Maximally tolerated statin therapy must be attempted before adding nonstatin therapies.
- Persistent hypertriglyceridemia (e.g., triglycerides ≥150 mg/dL) requires risk stratification and potential intensification of therapy.
- LDL-C targets are individualized based on ASCVD risk (e.g., <70 mg/dL for very high-risk patients).
- Nonstatin therapies (e.g., PCSK9 inhibitors, bempedoic acid) should be considered only after documented intolerance or inadequate response to maximally tolerated statin therapy, with documented LDL-C levels and ASCVD risk assessment.
