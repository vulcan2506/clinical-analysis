# HoFH Treatment Guideline
*Source: 2026 ACC-AHA-AACVPR-ABC-ACPM-ADA 
 AGS-APhA-ASPC-NLA-PCNA Guideline on
the Management of Dyslipidemia.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** HoFH Treatment Guideline

**Key Clinical Actions:**
- HoFH patients exhibit severe LDL receptor abnormalities and blunted response to traditional LDL-C-lowering therapies (statins, ezetimibe, bempedoic acid, PCSK9 mAbs, inclisiran).
- Combination therapy with statins, ezetimibe, and PCSK9 mAbs is typically initiated, but advanced therapies (lomitapide, evinacumab) are often required due to receptor-independent mechanisms.
- Referral to lipid specialists for advanced therapies (lomitapide, evinacumab) and consideration of lipoprotein apheresis for suboptimal responders.
- Aggressive LDL-C-lowering therapy is warranted immediately upon diagnosis due to extreme ASCVD risk.
- Maximally tolerated statin therapy is recommended, as residual LDL receptor activity may still confer benefit.
- HoFH patients exhibit cholesterol deposition in tendons, cutaneous tissues, coronary arteries, aortic valve and root, and all major vascular beds from birth
- Premature ASCVD events are commonly seen in HoFH patients, with first major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE) reported at median ages of 37 and 25 years in high-income versus lower-income countries, respectively

**Patient Preparation / Diagnostic Criteria:**
- Genetic confirmation of HoFH (pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants in LDLR, APOB, PCSK9, ARH, or LDLRAP1).
- Baseline LDL-C levels and cardiovascular risk stratification before initiating therapy.
- Regular monitoring of liver function and adherence to low-fat diet for lomitapide therapy.
- Ongoing cardiology follow-up due to high ASCVD risk.
- Family cascade screening to identify affected relatives.
- Genetic confirmation of HoFH requires identification of biallelic pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants in LDLR, APOB, PCSK9, ARH, or LDLRAP1 (inherited from both parents, often with one parent having HeFH)
- Family cascade screening is necessary to identify affected relatives, as both parents often have HeFH
