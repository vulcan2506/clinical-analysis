**Clinical Recommendation:** Lp(a) Measurement and Risk Stratification

**Key Clinical Actions:**
- Lp(a) is an LDL-like particle with robust associations to ASCVD and calcific aortic valve disease, independent of LDL-C and other risk factors.
- A single Lp(a) measurement is generally sufficient due to genetic determination and stability over time, except in secondary causes (e.g., kidney, liver, thyroid disease, pregnancy, menopause, or certain medications).
- Lp(a) concentrations are highest in individuals of African or South Asian ancestry, but relative ASCVD risk is similar across ancestries.
- Cascade testing in first-degree family members is recommended for early identification of elevated Lp(a), especially with a family history of premature ASCVD or aortic stenosis.
- Fasting is not required for Lp(a) testing.
- Lp(a) concentrations are minimally modified by lifestyle factors, making a single measurement generally sufficient for risk assessment.

**Patient Preparation / Diagnostic Criteria:**
- Lp(a) should be measured using assays calibrated in molar units (nmol/L) and isoform-insensitive to ensure accuracy.
- Laboratories must report the assay name and calibration units (mg/dL or nmol/L) for consistency.
- Assays should be traceable to official reference standards (e.g., NIST) for reliability.
- Repeat testing may be considered for Lp(a) concentrations in threshold ranges (e.g., ≥125 nmol/L or 50 mg/dL).
- Laboratories should report the assay name and calibration units (mg/dL or nmol/L) for consistency and comparability.
