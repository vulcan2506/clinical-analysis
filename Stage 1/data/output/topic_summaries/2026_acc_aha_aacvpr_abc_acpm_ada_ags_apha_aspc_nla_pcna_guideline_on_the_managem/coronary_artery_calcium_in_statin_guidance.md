# Coronary Artery Calcium in Statin Guidance
*Source: 2026 ACC-AHA-AACVPR-ABC-ACPM-ADA 
 AGS-APhA-ASPC-NLA-PCNA Guideline on

**Clinical Recommendation:** Coronary Artery Calcium (CAC) in Statin Guidance

**Key Clinical Actions:**
- CAC scoring is used to refine statin therapy decisions in intermediate-risk patients by providing incremental risk stratification beyond traditional risk factors.
- A CAC score of 0 identifies individuals at low near-term but not long-term risk, warranting less aggressive statin therapy in the short term.
- CAC progression over time enhances risk prediction for coronary and cardiovascular events, informing treatment adjustments.
- CAC scoring improves risk assessment in adults with a family history of premature coronary heart disease, aiding personalized statin initiation.
- Absence of CAC (score = 0) in asymptomatic individuals with diabetes may not guarantee low long-term risk, requiring ongoing risk reassessment.
- CAC scoring is particularly useful in patients with metabolic syndrome or insulin resistance to refine statin therapy decisions beyond traditional risk factors.
- CAC progression >15% annually is associated with a significantly higher risk of future cardiovascular events, warranting more intensive statin therapy or additional risk factor modification.

**Patient Preparation / Diagnostic Criteria:**
- Intermediate-risk patients for statin therapy should undergo CAC scoring to guide treatment decisions.
- CAC scoring should be performed using standardized imaging protocols (e.g., ECG-gated CT).
- Repeat CAC scoring is recommended at intervals of 3–5 years to assess progression and refine risk stratification.
- CAC scoring should be performed in patients with a 10-year ASCVD risk of 5-20% (intermediate risk) to guide statin therapy decisions, as per ACC/AHA 2026 guidelines.
- CAC scoring is recommended in patients with a family history of premature CHD to personalize statin initiation, even if traditional risk scores are low.
- Repeat CAC scoring is recommended at 3–5-year intervals in patients with CAC >100 or CAC progression >15% annually to monitor risk and adjust therapy.
- CAC scoring should be interpreted in the context of other imaging findings (e.g., carotid plaque, coronary CT angiography) to refine risk stratification.
