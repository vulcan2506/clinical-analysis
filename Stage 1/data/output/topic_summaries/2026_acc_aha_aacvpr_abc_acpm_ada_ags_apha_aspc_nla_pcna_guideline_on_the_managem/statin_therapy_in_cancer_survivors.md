# Statin Therapy in Cancer Survivors
*Source: 2026 ACC-AHA-AACVPR-ABC-ACPM-ADA 
 AGS-APhA-ASPC-NLA-PCNA Guideline on
the Management of Dyslipidemia.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Statin Therapy in Cancer Survivors + Chemotherapy-Induced Cardiomyopathy Protection

**Key Clinical Actions:**
- CVD is the second most common cause of death among cancer survivors, with elevated ASCVD risk persisting long-term.
- Statin therapy is associated with lower overall and cancer-related mortality in cancer survivors without increasing cancer recurrence risk.
- Cancer survivors are more likely to discontinue statin therapy despite increased ASCVD risk, requiring clinician attention.
- Statins may protect against chemotherapy-induced cardiomyopathy (e.g., anthracyclines, trastuzumab) and enhance some anticancer treatments.
- Statins do not increase cancer recurrence risk and may reduce cancer-related mortality in observational studies.

**Patient Preparation / Diagnostic Criteria:**
- Cancer survivors should follow primary prevention ASCVD guidelines unless limited life expectancy or drug-drug interactions (DDI) exist.
- Statin therapy should be considered in cancer survivors at borderline or higher ASCVD risk when initiation is uncertain.
- For secondary prevention of ASCVD in cancer survivors, statin therapy is strongly recommended.
- Life expectancy of ≥2.5 years is a general threshold for statin benefit in primary prevention.
- Active cancer is not a contraindication to statin therapy unless DDIs or very limited life expectancy are present.
- Among 2-year survivors of cancer with no metastatic disease, patients with lung and colorectal cancer are particularly vulnerable to statin nonadherence.
