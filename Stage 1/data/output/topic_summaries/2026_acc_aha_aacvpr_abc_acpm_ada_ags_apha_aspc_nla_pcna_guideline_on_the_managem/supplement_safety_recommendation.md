# Supplement Safety Recommendation
*Source: 2026 ACC-AHA-AACVPR-ABC-ACPM-ADA 
 AGS-APhA-ASPC-NLA-PCNA Guideline on
the Management of Dyslipidemia.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Supplement Safety Recommendation

**Key Clinical Actions:**
- Dietary supplements are not regulated as strictly as FDA-approved lipid-lowering therapies (LLT) and may lack safety, efficacy, and manufacturing quality assurances.
- Fish oil supplements are not recommended for lipid lowering or ASCVD due to lack of clinical benefit and potential adverse effects (e.g., increased LDL-C and atrial fibrillation risk).
- Agents like berberine, garlic/onion, turmeric, and red yeast rice may modestly reduce TC and LDL-C, but evidence is inconsistent and results vary across trials.
- Cinnamon showed no significant cholesterol-lowering effects in trials.
- Supplements should not replace FDA-approved LLT without clinical justification.

**Patient Preparation / Diagnostic Criteria:**
- Patients without established ASCVD with LDL-C 70–189 mg/dL and 10-year ASCVD risk 5%–20% should be evaluated for statin therapy (e.g., rosuvastatin 5 mg daily) rather than relying on supplements.
- Patients must be adults without established ASCVD with LDL-C levels between 70 and 189 mg/dL and a 10-year ASCVD risk (PCE) of 5% to 20% to be eligible for the comparison between statin therapy and dietary supplements in the SPORT trial context.
