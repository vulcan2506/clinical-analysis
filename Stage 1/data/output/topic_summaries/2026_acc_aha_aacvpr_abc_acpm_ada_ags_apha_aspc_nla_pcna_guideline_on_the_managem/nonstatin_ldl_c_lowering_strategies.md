# Nonstatin LDL-C Lowering Strategies
*Source: 2026 ACC-AHA-AACVPR-ABC-ACPM-ADA 
 AGS-APhA-ASPC-NLA-PCNA Guideline on
the Management of Dyslipidemia.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Nonstatin LDL-C Lowering Strategies

**Key Clinical Actions:**
- Nonstatin medications may be used in addition to statin therapy to further lower LDL-C or as an alternative for patients with statin intolerance.
- Ezetimibe lowers LDL-C by a mean of 18% (25% incremental reduction when added to statin therapy) with low side effects.
- PCSK9 inhibitors (mAbs) lower LDL-C by 45% to 64%, reduce ASCVD events in very-high-risk patients, and are well tolerated.
- Inclisiran lowers LDL-C by 48% to 52% and is well tolerated with ongoing cardiovascular outcome trials.
- Bempedoic acid lowers LDL-C by 21% to 24% as monotherapy or 17% to 18% with maximally tolerated statin.

**Patient Preparation / Diagnostic Criteria:**
- Nonstatins should be considered when statin therapy alone does not achieve LDL-C targets.
- TG-lowering medications are used for severe hypertriglyceridemia (≥500 mg/dL) to reduce pancreatitis risk.
- Non-HDL-C and apoB are preferred therapeutic targets in adults with hypertriglyceridemia.
