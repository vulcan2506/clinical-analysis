# Statin Intensity Classification
*Source: 2026 ACC-AHA-AACVPR-ABC-ACPM-ADA 
 AGS-APhA-ASPC-NLA-PCNA Guideline on
the Management of Dyslipidemia.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Statin Intensity Classification + LDL-C Reduction Goals

**Key Clinical Actions:**
- Statins are categorized by expected LDL-C reduction: high-intensity (≥50%), moderate-intensity (30–49%), and low-intensity (<30%)
- Therapy goals include both expected percentage LDL-C reduction and absolute achieved LDL-C levels
- Initial statin dosing should be lower in populations prone to side effects (e.g., East Asian ancestry)
- Statin selection considers pharmacokinetic heterogeneity and drug–drug interaction risks
- Simvastatin 80 mg initiation/titration is not recommended due to increased myopathy risk
- Expected percentage LDL-C reductions for statins are estimated using median reductions from the VOYAGER database (atorvastatin, rosuvastatin, simvastatin) and FDA-approved product labeling for other statins (fluvastatin, lovastatin, pitavastatin, pravastatin)
- Individual responses to statin therapy vary in clinical practice due to high heterogeneity in LDL-C-lowering medication responses

**Patient Preparation / Diagnostic Criteria:**
- Statin therapy is recommended when benefits of reducing ASCVD events outweigh risks
- Dose-specific statin choices must account for FDA-approved labeling and RCT evidence
- Clinician tools (e.g., ACC Statin Intolerance Tool) should be used to manage drug–drug interactions
- Statin selection must consider FDA-approved product labeling for LDL-C reduction estimates in adults with hyperlipidemia, primary hypercholesterolemia, and mixed dyslipidemia
- RCTs demonstrating reduction in major ASCVD events should guide statin selection and dosing
