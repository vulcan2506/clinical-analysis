# Coronary Artery Calcium Prognostic Evidence
*Source: 2026 ACC-AHA-AACVPR-ABC-ACPM-ADA 
 AGS-APhA-ASPC-NLA-PCNA Guideline on
the Management of Dyslipidemia.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Coronary Artery Calcium (CAC) Prognostic Evidence

**Key Clinical Actions:**
- CAC scores ≥1,000 are associated with significantly increased long-term all-cause and cause-specific mortality in asymptomatic patients.
- Very high CAC (≥1000) correlates with elevated cardiovascular disease events, non-cardiovascular outcomes, and mortality.
- CAC scoring provides prognostic insights equivalent to secondary prevention thresholds in select populations.
- Statin therapy initiation is recommended for asymptomatic individuals with elevated CAC scores, particularly those with positive family history of premature CAD.
- CAC progression over time predicts higher risk of coronary and cardiovascular events.
- CAC scoring is most informative in intermediate-risk patients (e.g., 5–20% 10-year ASCVD risk) to refine risk stratification.

**Patient Preparation / Diagnostic Criteria:**
- CAC scoring requires dedicated non-contrast cardiac CT imaging for accurate quantification.
- Patient preparation includes avoidance of caffeine and beta-blockers may be used to optimize heart rate for imaging quality.
- CAC scoring is most informative in intermediate-risk patients (e.g., 5–20% 10-year ASCVD risk) to refine risk stratification.
- Routine use of CAC scoring in low-risk patients (e.g., <5% 10-year ASCVD risk) is not recommended due to limited incremental value.
- Empiric treatment with statins + vitamin C/E in asymptomatic adults with elevated CAC scores without risk-based thresholds is no longer endorsed.
