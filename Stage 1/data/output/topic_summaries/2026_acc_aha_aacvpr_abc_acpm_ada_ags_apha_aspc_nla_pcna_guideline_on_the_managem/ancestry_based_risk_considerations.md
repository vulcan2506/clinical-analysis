**Clinical Recommendation:** Ancestry-Based Risk Considerations in CVD Risk Stratification

**Key Clinical Actions:**
- Race and ethnicity are social constructs; risk assessment should focus on modifiable factors (e.g., lifestyle, environment) rather than biological ancestry.
- South Asian ancestry is recognized as a high-risk demographic group and considered a risk-enhancer.
- Asian populations exhibit increased prevalence of diabetes at lower BMI and waist circumferences.
- Filipino American individuals show elevated CKM syndrome risk factors (hypertension, hyperlipidemia, obesity).
- Disaggregated data are essential for Asian, Native Hawaiian, Pacific Islander, and Hispanic/Latino groups due to heterogeneity in CVD risks.
- Urban and Western acculturation are associated with increased cholesterol levels in individuals of Asian Indian ancestry and obesity in adults of Filipino ancestry.

**Patient Preparation / Diagnostic Criteria:**
- Risk assessment should use individualized tools (e.g., PREVENT-ASCVD equations) that exclude race/ethnicity as a variable.
- Lifestyle recommendations must be tailored to cultural preferences for diet, exercise, and weight management to maximize adherence.
