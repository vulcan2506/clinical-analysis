# Pediatric Lipid Management Guidelines
*Source: 2026 ACC-AHA-AACVPR-ABC-ACPM-ADA 
 AGS-APhA-ASPC-NLA-PCNA Guideline on

**Clinical Recommendation:** Pediatric Lipid Management & Pharmacotherapy

**Key Clinical Actions:**
- Lifestyle management is recommended for all children and adolescents with lipid abnormalities to improve LDL-C, TG, and non-HDL-C.
- Statin therapy is recommended for children ≥8 years with LDL-C persistently ≥160 mg/dL consistent with FH who do not respond to 3–6 months of lifestyle management.
- Panel-based genetic testing for pathogenic/likely pathogenic rare variants for FH is useful to guide diagnosis, cascade testing, and treatment.
- Aggressive lipid-lowering therapy (LLT) is required for HoFH at the time of diagnosis, often in infancy, including specialized evaluation by pediatric cardiologists.
- Statins and other LLTs may be considered at younger ages (e.g., 6 years) or lower LDL-C cutpoints in the presence of high-risk conditions like diabetes, CKD, or elevated Lp(a).
- Lifestyle management should be maintained durably in subspecialty care, as it can be durably maintained and has unmeasured health benefits beyond lipid reduction (e.g., obesity, diabetes, cancer prevention).

**Patient Preparation / Diagnostic Criteria:**
- Patient must be ≥8 years of age for standard statin initiation in FH.
- LDL-C level must be persistently ≥160 mg/dL (4.1 mmol/L) for ≥3 to 6 months.
- Inadequate response to 3 to 6 months of lifestyle management is required before pharmacotherapy.
- Clinical presentation consistent with Familial Hypercholesterolemia (FH) is required for specific genetic testing and aggressive LLT.
- Diagnosis of Homozygous FH (HoFH) requires clinical or genetic confirmation for specialized management protocols.
- Children with high-risk conditions (e.g., diabetes, CKD, childhood cancer treatment) may consider statins or other LLTs at younger ages (e.g., 6 years) or lower LDL-C cutpoints, even without meeting the standard ≥160 mg/dL threshold.
