# CAC Risk Assessment
*Source: 2026 ACC-AHA-AACVPR-ABC-ACPM-ADA 
 AGS-APhA-ASPC-NLA-PCNA Guideline on
the Management of Dyslipidemia.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** CAC Risk Assessment for ASCVD Risk Stratification

**Key Clinical Actions:**
- CAC score improves risk assessment and reclassifies intermediate-risk adults into higher/lower-risk categories.
- CAC=0 may justify deferring statin therapy in intermediate-risk adults, except in FH, severe hypercholesterolemia, diabetes, tobacco use, or strong family history of premature ASCVD.
- CAC ≥100 or ≥75th percentile in intermediate-risk adults warrants statin initiation due to high ASCVD event rates.
- Graded CAC risk: CAC ≥300 mimics event rates of treated ASCVD; CAC ≥1000 doubles risk vs. CAC 400–999.
- CCTA provides additional prognostic value beyond CAC, detecting noncalcified plaque and high-risk features.
- CAC testing is particularly useful for risk stratification in borderline-risk adults and younger adults with multiple risk factors.
- CAC=0 in adults with diabetes is associated with low 5-year event rates but risk increases substantially thereafter.

**Patient Preparation / Diagnostic Criteria:**
- CAC scoring requires gated cardiac CT to minimize motion artifact.
- CAC=0 in smokers may not fully mitigate risk; statin therapy should not be withheld.
- Carotid plaque presence warrants statin initiation even with CAC=0.
- CAC percentiles require age/sex/race-adjusted reference values (e.g., MESA or CAC-tools.com).
- CAC percentiles must be calculated using age, sex, and race-adjusted reference values (e.g., MESA or CAC-tools.com).
- CAC scoring on non-cardiac CTs has a potential limitation of a false-negative rate reported as high as 24%.
