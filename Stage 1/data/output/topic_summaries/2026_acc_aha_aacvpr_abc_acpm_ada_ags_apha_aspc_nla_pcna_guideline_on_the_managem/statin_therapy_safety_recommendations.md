# Statin Therapy Safety Recommendations
*Source: 2026 ACC-AHA-AACVPR-ABC-ACPM-ADA 
 AGS-APhA-ASPC-NLA-PCNA Guideline on
the Management of Dyslipidemia.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Statin Therapy Safety Recommendations

**Key Clinical Actions:**
- Individualized clinician-patient discussion prior to initiating lipid-lowering medication to review benefits and risks.
- Continuation of statin therapy in adults with elevated diabetes risk or new-onset diabetes, emphasizing adherence and lifestyle management.
- Statin therapy is reasonable in adults at elevated ASCVD risk with chronic, stable liver disease (including metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease).
- Management of statin-attributed muscle symptoms (SAMS) is prioritized, including dose adjustment or discontinuation based on severity.
- Statin-associated autoimmune myopathy requires statin cessation and avoidance of all potential myotoxic pharmacotherapies.
- Monitor hepatic transaminase levels before and after initiating ezetimibe combined with statin therapy is explicitly tied to the risk of persistent elevations (>3 times ULN) when added to a statin.

**Patient Preparation / Diagnostic Criteria:**
- Patient priorities, functional status, multimorbidity, frailty, polypharmacy, and life expectancy must be considered in benefit-risk discussions for older adults.
- Life expectancy of at least 2.5 years is required to consider initiating moderate-intensity statin therapy in adults aged >75 years.
- Coronary artery calcium (CAC) measurement may be used to reclassify risk in adults aged >75 years with uncertain LLT decisions.
- Monitor hepatic transaminase levels before and after initiating ezetimibe combined with statin therapy.
- Avoid statins during lactation; pregnancy is not a contraindication but statins should be avoided.
- Life expectancy of at least 2.5 years is required to consider initiating moderate-intensity statin therapy in adults aged >75 years, with CAC measurement (scores of 1–10 or zero) used to reclassify risk in uncertain cases.
