# Severe Hypercholesterolemia Management
*Source: 2026 ACC-AHA-AACVPR-ABC-ACPM-ADA 
 AGS-APhA-ASPC-NLA-PCNA Guideline on
the Management of Dyslipidemia.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Severe Hypercholesterolemia Management (LDL-C ≥190 mg/dL)

**Key Clinical Actions:**
- Exclude and address secondary causes of dyslipidemia in adults with LDL-C ≥190 mg/dL
- Prescribe maximally tolerated statin therapy to lower LDL-C and reduce ASCVD risk
- Use add-on LDL-C-lowering therapies (e.g., ezetimibe, PCSK9 inhibitors) in high-risk patients based on ASCVD risk, severity, and patient preference
- Employ progressively lower LDL-C treatment goals for higher absolute ASCVD risk
- Consider lipoprotein apheresis for select individuals unable to tolerate or access optimal drug therapy
- Consider simple carbohydrate-restricted eating patterns, such as the ketogenic diet, as adjunct lifestyle therapy for LDL-C reduction in adults with severe hypercholesterolemia

**Patient Preparation / Diagnostic Criteria:**
- Exclusion of secondary causes of dyslipidemia before initiating therapy
- Confirmation of LDL-C ≥190 mg/dL (or non-HDL-C >220 mg/dL or apoB >140 mg/dL) for diagnosis
- Cascade screening and panel-based genetic testing for pathogenic FH variants if FH is suspected
- Lifestyle modifications (e.g., carbohydrate-restricted eating patterns) as adjunct therapy
- Monitoring of apoB with a goal of <55 mg/dL to minimize residual risk
- Evaluate for secondary causes of dyslipidemia (e.g., hypothyroidism, nephrotic syndrome, obstructive liver disease, medication-induced dyslipidemia) before initiating lipid-lowering therapy in adults with severe hypercholesterolemia
