# Statin Drug Interaction
*Source: 2026 ACC-AHA-AACVPR-ABC-ACPM-ADA 
 AGS-APhA-ASPC-NLA-PCNA Guideline on
the Management of Dyslipidemia.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Statin Drug Interaction (CYP450 & Transporter-Mediated)

**Key Clinical Actions:**
- Statins are frequently coprescribed with other cardiovascular medications and are susceptible to drug-drug interactions (DDI).
- Most statin-related DDI are mediated through CYP3A4, CYP2C9, and drug transporters (e.g., P-glycoprotein, OATP, BCRP).
- Lovastatin and simvastatin exhibit the highest number of clinically significant DDI due to significant CYP3A4 metabolism.
- Atorvastatin is metabolized by CYP3A4 to a lesser extent than lovastatin/simvastatin.
- Pravastatin does not undergo CYP450 metabolism, reducing DDI risk.
- Fluvastatin undergoes significant metabolism through CYP2C9, which is a key pathway for its drug interactions.
- Pitavastatin and rosuvastatin are less dependent on CYP3A4 metabolism, reducing their interaction potential with CYP3A4 inhibitors/inducers.

**Patient Preparation / Diagnostic Criteria:**
- Assess CYP3A4 and CYP2C9 activity when prescribing statins to avoid adverse interactions.
- Monitor for therapeutic efficacy or adverse effects when statins are coprescribed with CYP3A4 or CYP2C9 inhibitors/inducers.
- Assess CYP2C9 activity when prescribing fluvastatin, pitavastatin, or rosuvastatin to avoid adverse interactions.
