# Statin Therapy in Familial Hypercholesterolemia

 AGS-APhA-ASPC-NLA-PCNA Guideline on

**Clinical Recommendation:** Statin Therapy in Familial Hypercholesterolemia

**Key Clinical Actions:**
- Statin therapy is the first-line treatment for patients with familial hypercholesterolemia (FH) to reduce LDL-C and cardiovascular risk.
- Intensification of lipid-lowering therapy (e.g., high-intensity statins, ezetimibe, or PCSK9 inhibitors) is recommended for patients with FH who do not achieve LDL-C goals.
- Coronary artery calcium (CAC) scoring is used to further stratify cardiovascular risk in FH patients receiving standard lipid-lowering therapy.
- Genetic testing for FH is recommended in patients with severe hypercholesterolemia to confirm diagnosis and guide management.
- Dietary modifications and lifestyle changes are adjunctive measures but are insufficient alone for LDL-C reduction in FH.
- Consideration of non-statin therapies (e.g., ezetimibe, PCSK9 inhibitors) in patients with heterozygous FH who have achieved LDL-C goals but have residual inflammatory or atherosclerotic risk (e.g., elevated lipoprotein(a) or hs-CRP).
- Integration of dietary patterns (e.g., Mediterranean diet) as adjunctive therapy to statins in FH, with evidence suggesting reduced progression of atherosclerosis when combined with lipid-lowering treatment.

**Patient Preparation / Diagnostic Criteria:**
- Confirmation of FH diagnosis via clinical criteria (e.g., Dutch Lipid Clinic Network criteria) or genetic testing.
- Baseline LDL-C measurement and regular monitoring to assess treatment response and adherence.
- Assessment of cardiovascular risk using tools such as the SAFEHEART registry or ASCVD risk calculators.
- Evaluation of statin intolerance or contraindications before initiating or intensifying therapy.
- Consideration of non-statin therapies (e.g., ezetimibe, PCSK9 inhibitors) in patients unable to tolerate or achieve goals with statins.
- Documentation of lipoprotein(a) levels at baseline and periodically in FH patients, as elevated levels (>50 mg/dL) may warrant more aggressive lipid-lowering therapy or additional risk stratification.
