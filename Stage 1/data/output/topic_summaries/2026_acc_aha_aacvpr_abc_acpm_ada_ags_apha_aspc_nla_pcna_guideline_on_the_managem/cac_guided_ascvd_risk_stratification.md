# CAC-Guided ASCVD Risk Stratification
*Source: 2026 ACC-AHA-AACVPR-ABC-ACPM-ADA 
 AGS-APhA-ASPC-NLA-PCNA Guideline on
the Management of Dyslipidemia.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** CAC-Guided ASCVD Risk Stratification

**Key Clinical Actions:**
- CAC scores ≥300 indicate ASCVD risk comparable to established treated ASCVD, warranting aggressive LDL-C lowering (goal <70 mg/dL or <55 mg/dL if ≥300).
- CAC scores ≥100 stratify intermediate-risk patients for statin therapy, reducing MACE and plaque progression.
- CAC scores 1–99 AU or <75th percentile guide LDL-C-lowering therapy with a 30% reduction goal and LDL-C <100 mg/dL.
- CAC score=0 in adults does not benefit from statin therapy over 10 years.
- Visual CAC assessment on nongated chest CT scans correlates with quantitative scores, enabling risk stratification.
- CAC score ≥400 in individuals without ASCVD derived greater benefit from statin therapy with reduction in MACE events compared with those not treated with statins.
- CAC scoring in younger adults (e.g., CARDIA study) identifies elevated long-term CHD risk even with low scores (1–19 or 20–99).

**Patient Preparation / Diagnostic Criteria:**
- Electrocardiogram-gated CT is preferred for formal CAC scoring.
- CAC scoring should be age-, sex-, and race-standardized (percentile-based).
- Family history of premature CAD and high CAC scores may warrant earlier statin initiation.
- Presence of CAC, regardless of race and ethnicity, is a strong predictor of CHD.
