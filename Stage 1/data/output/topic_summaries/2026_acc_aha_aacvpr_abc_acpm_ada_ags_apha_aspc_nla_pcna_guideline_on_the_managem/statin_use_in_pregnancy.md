# Statin Use in Pregnancy
*Source: 2026 ACC-AHA-AACVPR-ABC-ACPM-ADA 
 AGS-APhA-ASPC-NLA-PCNA Guideline on
the Management of Dyslipidemia.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Statin Use in Pregnancy + Lactation

**Key Clinical Actions:**
- Statin use is generally not recommended during pregnancy or lactation due to concerns for teratogenicity and adverse pregnancy outcomes.
- Recent studies show conflicting results regarding statin safety in pregnancy, with some evidence of increased risks (preterm birth, low birth weight, preeclampsia/eclampsia) but no significant increase in congenital anomalies.
- Bile acid sequestrants are considered safe during pregnancy and lactation due to lack of systemic absorption, though they may interfere with fat-soluble vitamin absorption.
- Ezetimibe should be avoided during pregnancy and lactation due to insufficient fetal risk data.
- Triglyceride levels increase 2- to 4-fold during the third trimester, and severe gestational hypertriglyceridemia may lead to acute pancreatitis.
- Statin use may be considered in high-risk individuals (e.g., ASCVD or FH) after shared decision-making, weighing potential risks against benefits.

**Patient Preparation / Diagnostic Criteria:**
- For patients with HoFH, lipoprotein-apheresis may be used during pregnancy for LDL-C lowering if statin therapy is deferred.
- TG-lowering medications can be considered for severe hypertriglyceridemia (TG ≥500 mg/dL) during pregnancy after excluding secondary causes.
- Secondary causes of hypertriglyceridemia must be excluded before initiating TG-lowering therapy in pregnancy.
- TG-lowering medications should only be used for persistently severe hypertriglyceridemia (TG ≥500 mg/dL) after excluding secondary causes of hypertriglyceridemia.
