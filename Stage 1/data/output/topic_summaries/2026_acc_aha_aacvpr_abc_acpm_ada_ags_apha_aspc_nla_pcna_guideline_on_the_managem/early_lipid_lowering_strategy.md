# Early Lipid-Lowering Strategy
*Source: 2026 ACC-AHA-AACVPR-ABC-ACPM-ADA 
 AGS-APhA-ASPC-NLA-PCNA Guideline on
the Management of Dyslipidemia.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Early Lipid-Lowering Strategy + Risk Stratification

**Key Clinical Actions:**
- Treat dyslipidemia earlier to reduce lifelong risk of prolonged exposure to atherogenic lipoproteins
- Use PREVENT™ equations instead of Pooled Cohort Equations (PCE) for 10- and 30-year risk assessment in adults aged 30 to 79 years
- Consider LDL-lowering therapy in adults with 10-year PREVENT-ASCVD risk of 3% to <5% (borderline) and should be considered for 5% to <10% (intermediate) after clinician-patient discussion
- Use LDL-C and non-HDL-C treatment goals to guide lipid-lowering therapy (LLT)
- Measure ApoB to improve risk assessment and guide therapy, particularly in those with elevated triglycerides, diabetes, or low achieved LDL-C
- Early consideration of pharmacotherapy in youth with familial hypercholesterolemia (FH) and in young adults with LDL-C ≥160 mg/dL or strong family history of premature ASCVD
- Use of the 'CPR' Model (Calculate, Personalize, Reclassify) for risk stratification in primary prevention

**Patient Preparation / Diagnostic Criteria:**
- Health behavior counseling to support lifestyle optimization should start in youth
- Use coronary artery calcium (CAC) scoring in men ≥40 years and women ≥45 years to improve risk assessment and guide LDL-C goals
- Measure Lp(a) at least once to identify higher-risk individuals
- LDL-lowering therapy is recommended for primary prevention in adults aged 40 to 75 years with diabetes, chronic kidney disease stage 3 or 4, or HIV, regardless of LDL-C level
- After age 75 years, LDL-C-lowering pharmacotherapy can be considered in conjunction with lifestyle interventions to reduce ASCVD risk
- Lp(a) measurement thresholds: ≥125 nmol/L (50 mg/dL) considered risk-enhancing (1.4-fold increased risk), ≥250 nmol/L (100 mg/dL) associated with ≥2-fold higher risk
- CAC scoring should consider both absolute CAC amount and standardized percentile (age, sex, race) for prognostic importance
