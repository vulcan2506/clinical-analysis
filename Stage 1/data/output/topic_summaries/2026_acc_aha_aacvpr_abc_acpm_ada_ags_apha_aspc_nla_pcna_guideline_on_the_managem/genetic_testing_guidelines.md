**Clinical Recommendation:** Genetic Testing for Familial Hypercholesterolemia (FH)

**Key Clinical Actions:**
- Panel-based genetic testing identifies variants in LDLR, APOB, or PCSK9 causing FH, improving detection rates even in individuals without severely elevated LDL-C.
- Genetic testing facilitates cascade screening to identify relatives with FH, though LDL-C testing alone may miss some cases.
- Presence of an FH-causing variant increases ASCVD risk beyond LDL-C levels alone, guiding intensified lipid-lowering therapy.
- Genetic testing is recommended for adults with severe hypercholesterolemia (LDL-C ≥190 mg/dL or apoB ≥140 mg/dL) due to higher prevalence of pathogenic variants (~2.5%).
- Diagnostic yield is higher in individuals with clinical suspicion of FH, family history of premature CAD, or founder population descent.
- Genetic testing for FH may identify rare autosomal recessive forms of FH or conditions that mimic FH (e.g., sitosterolemia, lysosomal acid lipase deficiency).
- Observational studies and prospective evaluations show improved LDL-C treatment rates in individuals with FH after return of genetic test results.

**Patient Preparation / Diagnostic Criteria:**
- Genetic testing should be considered in adults with LDL-C ≥190 mg/dL or apoB ≥140 mg/dL to identify pathogenic FH variants.
- Clinical suspicion of FH (e.g., family history, early-onset hypercholesterolemia) warrants genetic testing even at lower LDL-C thresholds.
- Cascade screening via genetic testing is prioritized for relatives of FH patients to identify at-risk individuals.
- Secondary causes of hypercholesterolemia must be excluded before attributing elevated LDL-C to genetic FH.
- Lipid testing (LDL-C, apoB) remains essential for cascade screening in individuals without access to genetic testing.
- Secondary causes of hypercholesterolemia must be excluded before attributing elevated LDL-C to genetic FH, and this includes using tools like the North American Familial Chylomicronemia Syndrome scoring tool to assess for FCS.
