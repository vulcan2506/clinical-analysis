# Lipid-Lowering Therapy Framework
*Source: 2026 ACC-AHA-AACVPR-ABC-ACPM-ADA 
 AGS-APhA-ASPC-NLA-PCNA Guideline on
the Management of Dyslipidemia.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Lipid-Lowering Therapy Framework + Nonstatin Medications Evolution

**Key Clinical Actions:**
- Statin therapy is the cornerstone of LDL-C lowering and ASCVD risk reduction due to proven safety, tolerability, and efficacy in cardiovascular outcome trials.
- Nonstatin LDL-C-lowering medications have evolved from oral agents with modest effects to more potent subcutaneous options (e.g., PCSK9 inhibitors, lomitapide, evinacumab).
- TG-lowering medications (e.g., fibrates, niacin) are reserved for severe hypertriglyceridemia or pancreatitis risk but are not recommended as add-on therapy to statins for ASCVD risk reduction due to lack of proven event reduction.
- Icosapent ethyl (IPE) is the only primary TG-lowering medication with demonstrated ASCVD event risk reduction when combined with statin therapy in high-risk patients with moderate TG elevations.
- Medication selection is guided by mechanism of action, dosing frequency, expected lipid-lowering efficacy, and patient-specific factors (e.g., HoFH, comorbidities).

**Patient Preparation / Diagnostic Criteria:**
- Achieving sufficient LDL-C lowering is a prerequisite before considering TG-lowering therapy for ASCVD risk reduction.
- Use of nonstatin therapies (e.g., PCSK9 inhibitors, lomitapide) requires adherence to restricted programs or monitoring due to adverse effects (e.g., hepatotoxicity, drug interactions).
- Vitamin E, linoleic acid, alpha-linolenic acid, EPA, and DHA supplements are required for patients on lomitapide to mitigate fat-soluble vitamin malabsorption.
- Initiation of immediate-release niacin must start at a low dose and slowly titrate up to an effective dose to minimize side effects.
- Concomitant ethanol intake must be avoided when using lomitapide to prevent exacerbation of hepatic steatosis and hepatotoxicity.
