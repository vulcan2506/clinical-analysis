# Geriatric Lipid Management Principles
*Source: 2026 ACC-AHA-AACVPR-ABC-ACPM-ADA 
 AGS-APhA-ASPC-NLA-PCNA Guideline on
the Management of Dyslipidemia.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Geriatric Lipid Management Principles

**Key Clinical Actions:**
- Prioritize individualized decision-making over rigid risk thresholds for older adults with multiple chronic conditions.
- Evaluate the time to benefit versus life expectancy before initiating or continuing statin therapy.
- Consider deprescribing statins in patients with advanced, life-limiting illness where benefits do not outweigh burdens.
- Utilize coronary artery calcium (CAC) scoring to refine risk stratification and guide statin allocation in elderly populations.
- Recognize that statin therapy provides cardiovascular benefit in older adults (≥75 years) but requires careful assessment of frailty and competing risks.
- Incorporate ezetimibe as an alternative or adjunct to statins in older adults with statin intolerance or contraindications, particularly in those with elevated CAC scores or high-risk features.

**Patient Preparation / Diagnostic Criteria:**
- Assessment of patient life expectancy and functional status to determine appropriateness of long-term therapy.
- Evaluation of competing risks and multimorbidity before initiating lipid-lowering therapy.
- Consideration of patient preferences and goals of care in shared decision-making processes.
- Use of validated risk calculators adjusted for age when estimating 10-year ASCVD risk.
- Review of current medication list to identify potential drug-drug interactions in polypharmacy scenarios.
- Assess renal function and hepatic enzymes prior to initiating lipid-lowering therapy in older adults, with dose adjustments for renal impairment and monitoring for drug-induced liver injury.
