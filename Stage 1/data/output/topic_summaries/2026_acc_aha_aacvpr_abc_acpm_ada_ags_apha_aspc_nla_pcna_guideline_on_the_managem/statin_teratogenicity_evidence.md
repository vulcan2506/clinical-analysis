**Clinical Recommendation:** Statin Teratogenicity Evidence and Pregnancy Management

**Key Clinical Actions:**
- Statin exposure in the first trimester is associated with central nervous system and limb anomalies in case reports.
- Animal studies (rats, rabbits) demonstrate developmental toxicity with atorvastatin exposure.
- Systematic reviews and meta-analyses report adverse perinatal outcomes following maternal statin exposure.
- Mevalonate supplementation in pregnant rats suppresses teratogenicity of HMG-CoA reductase inhibitors.
- FDA removed the strongest warning against statin use during pregnancy but still advises most pregnant patients to stop statins.
- Mevalonate supplementation in pregnant rats suppresses teratogenicity of HMG-CoA reductase inhibitors, suggesting a mechanistic basis for statin teratogenicity.

**Patient Preparation / Diagnostic Criteria:**
- Pregnancy testing before initiating statin therapy in women of reproductive potential.
- Discontinuation of statins in most pregnant patients due to teratogenic risk.
- Use of LDL apheresis for managing pregnancies in patients with homozygous familial hypercholesterolemia.
- Monitoring of lipid levels and adverse outcomes in pregnant patients with severe hypertriglyceridemia.
- Consideration of alternative lipid-lowering therapies (e.g., colesevelam) with lower teratogenic risk.
- Consideration of lipoprotein apheresis in homozygous familial hypercholesterolemia pregnancies even if LDL-C levels are not optimally controlled with apheresis alone.
