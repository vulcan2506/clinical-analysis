# Lp(a) Risk Management Guidance
*Source: 2026 ACC-AHA-AACVPR-ABC-ACPM-ADA 
 AGS-APhA-ASPC-NLA-PCNA Guideline on
the Management of Dyslipidemia.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Lp(a) Risk Management Guidance

**Key Clinical Actions:**
- Individuals with elevated Lp(a) should intensify modifiable risk factor management early, including lipids, blood pressure, and glycemic control.
- Lifestyle changes aligned with AHA's Life's Essential 8 (e.g., smoking cessation, healthful diet, activity) are recommended to lower ASCVD risk.
- PCSK9 inhibitors (mAbs) are considered for high-risk patients with elevated Lp(a) not achieving LDL-C goals on maximal statin therapy.
- Lp(a) testing is recommended for patients with ASCVD to guide risk stratification and treatment intensification.
- Statins do not lower Lp(a) and may modestly increase it, but their cardiovascular benefits justify continued use.
- Lp(a) testing should be prioritized in patients with a family history of premature ASCVD or known genetic dyslipidemias (e.g., FH) to guide early risk stratification and management.

**Patient Preparation / Diagnostic Criteria:**
- Lp(a) testing should be performed in patients with ASCVD or high-risk individuals to guide management.
- Treatment intensification should align with overall risk, incorporating both traditional risk factors and Lp(a)-related risk.
- PCSK9 inhibitors should be considered in high-risk patients with elevated Lp(a) not achieving LDL-C goals on maximal statin therapy.
- Lp(a) testing should be performed using an isoform-independent assay to ensure accurate quantification, as Lp(a) levels can vary significantly depending on the assay method.
