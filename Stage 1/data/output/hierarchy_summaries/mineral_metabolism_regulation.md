# Mineral Metabolism & Regulation

Iron homeostasis maintains systemic and cellular iron balance through tightly regulated transport, storage, and utilization mechanisms, ensuring adequate supply for metabolic processes while preventing toxicity from excess iron.

**Key Features:**
- Heme and non-heme iron utilization: Iron serves as a co-factor in hemeproteins (e.g., hemoglobin) for oxygen transport and in iron-sulfur enzymes for electron transfer in metabolic pathways.
- Transferrin receptor-mediated endocytosis: Cellular iron uptake occurs via transferrin receptors binding iron-loaded transferrin, enabling endocytosis and intracellular iron release.
- Ferritin-mediated iron storage and regulation: Ferritin stores excess intracellular iron in a bioavailable but non-toxic form and releases it as needed, with expression regulated by iron regulatory proteins (IRPs).

**Notable Details:**
- Iron is essential for DNA synthesis via ribonucleotide reductase, and deficiency can lead to cell death due to impaired replication.
- Transferrin receptors are upregulated in iron-deficient states to enhance cellular iron uptake.
- Ferritin stores iron in a bioavailable but non-toxic form, with its expression regulated by iron regulatory proteins (IRPs) in response to intracellular iron levels.
- Iron-sulfur clusters in enzymes (e.g., aconitase) are critical for mitochondrial electron transport and metabolic regulation.
- Iron regulatory proteins (IRPs) modulate the expression of ferritin and transferrin receptors in response to intracellular iron levels.
- Heme iron is more readily absorbed than non-heme iron, which is influenced by dietary factors and inhibitors like phytates.
- Iron overload can lead to oxidative stress and cellular damage due to the generation of reactive oxygen species.
- Systemic iron balance is maintained through regulated absorption in the duodenum, recycling of senescent red blood cells, and controlled release from storage sites.

**Metabolic Pathway:** iron biological functions and regulation

**Iron Handling Characteristics:**
- Acts as a co-factor in hundreds of proteins and enzymes, including hemeproteins for oxygen transport and iron-sulphur enzymes for electron transfer.
- Facilitates DNA synthesis via ribonucleotide reductase and supports growth; deficiency leads to cell death.
- Regulates absorption via hepcidin and IRP/IRE mechanisms, balancing entry and storage to prevent anemia or overload.
- Stores iron safely in ferritin and hemosiderin forms within hepatocytes and macrophages to detoxify excess metal.
- Transports iron systemically via transferrin, utilizing receptor-mediated endocytosis for cellular uptake.
- Heme-iron absorption occurs via a distinct, more efficient pathway involving intact metalloporphyrin uptake via heme receptors and vesicular transport, with Fe+2 release mediated by microsomal heme-oxygenase.

**Metabolic Prerequisites:**
- Availability of reducing agents (ascorbate, histidine) to mobilize dietary ferric iron in the stomach.
- Presence of Dcytb (duodenal cytochrome b) to reduce Fe+3 to Fe+2 for DMT-1 transport.
- Functional hephaestin and ferroportin for basolateral iron export and oxidation.
- Intact IRE/IRP regulatory machinery to modulate ferritin and transferrin receptor expression.
- Adequate levels of transferrin and transferrin receptors for systemic delivery and cellular uptake.
- Presence of hephaestin, a multicopper ferroxidase, as an accessory protein for ferroportin-mediated basolateral iron export to enable transferrin binding and prevent endothelial damage.

**Metabolic Pathway:** transferrin receptor mediated iron uptake and ferritin storage regulation

**Iron Handling Characteristics:**
- Transferrin receptors bind iron-loaded transferrin to facilitate cellular iron uptake via endocytosis.
- Ferritin acts as the primary intracellular iron storage protein, regulating iron availability for metabolic processes.
- Iron regulatory proteins control the expression of transferrin receptors and ferritin based on cellular iron status.
- Ferric reductase activity is essential for reducing dietary ferric iron to absorbable ferrous iron.
- Iron homeostasis is maintained through the coordinated regulation of absorption, transport, and storage mechanisms.
- Ferritin's iron storage capacity is regulated by iron-dependent nucleation and mineralization processes within its nanocage structure.
- Iron regulatory proteins (IRPs) bind to iron-responsive elements (IREs) in the 5' or 3' untranslated regions of target mRNAs to post-transcriptionally control iron metabolism genes.

**Metabolic Prerequisites:**
- Availability of transferrin-bound iron for receptor binding and subsequent endocytosis.
- Functional ferric reductase enzymes to convert dietary iron to the ferrous state.
- Proper expression and activity of iron regulatory proteins to modulate receptor and storage protein levels.
- Intact cellular machinery for endosomal acidification and iron release from transferrin.
- Adequate oxygen levels for the function of iron-dependent enzymes and metabolic pathways.
- Adequate intracellular copper levels for the function of ceruloplasmin, which oxidizes ferrous iron to ferric iron for incorporation into transferrin.
- Functional IRE/IRP system for post-transcriptional regulation of transferrin receptor, ferritin, and DMT1 expression.
