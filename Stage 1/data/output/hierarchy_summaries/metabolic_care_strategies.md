# Metabolic Care Strategies

Metabolic Care Strategies encompass tailored approaches to manage diabetes and metabolic health through fasting protocols, balancing safety, education, and physiological optimization across religious and non-religious contexts.

**Key Features:**
- Religious fasting protocols for T1DM management with structured risk assessment
- Intermittent fasting strategies (alternate-day, 5:2) for metabolic health
- Time-restricted eating with fixed daily meal windows (e.g., 16:8)

**Notable Details:**
- T1DM patients face very high risks during religious fasting (hypoglycemia, ketoacidosis, dehydration)
- Prafasting consultation 6-8 weeks prior is mandatory for T1DM patients
- International Diabetes Federation and Diabetes and Ramadan International Alliance risk assessment tools generate fasting safety scores
- Alternate-day fasting alternates 24-hour fasting with ad libitum eating
- 5:2 fasting restricts intake to 500–600 kcal on two non-consecutive days weekly
- Time-restricted eating typically uses 8–12 hour windows (e.g., 16:8 method)
- Medical supervision is required for individuals with diabetes or eating disorders
- Hydration and electrolyte balance are critical during all fasting periods

**Clinical Recommendation:** religious fasting protocols in T1DM management

**Core Management Principles:**
- Patients with T1DM are at very high risk during religious fasting due to hypoglycemia, hyperglycemia, ketoacidosis, dehydration, and thrombosis risks.
- Structured education and risk stratification are mandatory, including consultation 6-8 weeks prior to fasting.
- Self-Monitoring of Blood Glucose (SMBG) must be performed multiple times daily, including post-iftar/suhoor and during symptoms.
- Dietary advice includes balanced meals with 40-50% low glycemic index carbohydrates, 20-30% protein, and <35% fat, divided equally between iftar and suhoor.
- Fasting must be broken immediately if blood glucose <70 mg/dL, >300 mg/dL, or symptoms of hypoglycemia/hyperglycemia/dehydration occur.
- Altered sleep patterns with decreased REM and increased NREM sleep during fasting decrease insulin sensitivity, worsening glycemic control in T1DM.

**Clinical Prerequisites:**
- Consultation with physician 6-8 weeks prior for risk stratification and quantification.
- Patients with recurrent hypoglycemia, hypoglycemia unawareness, poor control, brittle diabetes, or non-compliance must avoid fasting.
- Insulin adjustments for basal-bolus regimen: reduce long/intermediate-acting insulin by 15-40% (once daily) or 50% (twice daily), and reduce premeal insulin by 25-50% at suhoor.
- Insulin pump users must reduce basal rate by 20-40% in the last 3-4 hours of fasting and increase by 0-30% in the first 2 hours post-iftar.
- Target pre-iftar/pre-suhoor and post-iftar/post-suhoor blood glucose is 90-130 mg/dL, with self-titration of insulin in 2-unit increments.
- Patients 'unwilling' or 'unable' to monitor and manage their blood glucose levels must be strongly advised to avoid fasting.


### religious fasting risk assessment

religious fasting risk assessment and management. Use the International Diabetes Federation and Diabetes and Ramadan International Alliance comprehensive prefasting risk assessment to generate a risk score for safety of religious fasting.

### Source 1: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf

**Clinical Recommendation:** religious fasting risk assessment and management

**Core Recommendations:**
- Use the International Diabetes Federation and Diabetes and Ramadan International Alliance comprehensive prefasting risk assessment to generate a risk score for safety of religious fasting.
- Provide fasting-focused education to minimize risks, including glucose monitoring timing and avoidance of intense physical activity during the last few hours of fasting.
- Assess and optimize treatment plan, dose, and timing for people with diabetes well in advance of religious fasting to reduce risk of hypoglycemia, dehydration, hyperglycemia, and/or ketoacidosis.
- Recommend individualized fluid adjustment and meal advice with emphasis on higher fiber intake, complex carbohydrates, and sustained daily fluid intake.
- Use diabetes technologies (e.g., flash glucose monitoring, CGM, insulin pumps) to enhance safety during fasting, especially in high-risk groups.
- Provide culturally sensitive education on breaking fast meals, emphasizing replacement of added sugars with complex carbohydrates and higher fiber intake to minimize postprandial hyperglycemia
- Recommend avoiding intense physical activity during the last few hours of fasting due to increased hypoglycemia risk (~50% of events occur then)

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Pregnancy with any type of diabetes is considered high risk (score >6.0) and fasting is probably unsafe.
- Impaired cognitive function or advanced frailty increases risk score to high (score >6.0).
- Risk assessment must be completed well in advance of planned fasting to allow for treatment optimization.
- Healthcare professionals should inquire about any religious fasting plans for people with diabetes and provide culturally sensitive, individualized support.
- Risk score categories: low (0–3.0), moderate (3.5–6.0), high (>6.0).
- Risk assessment must account for the number of fasting days (e.g., Ramadan’s 29–30 days vs. Yom Kippur’s ~25 hours) as a modifiable risk factor
- Healthcare professionals must proactively inquire about religious fasting plans during routine visits to enable anticipatory guidance and treatment optimization

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Fixed numeric A1C targets for people with diabetes planning to fast are no longer recommended.
- Routine initiation of SGLT2 inhibitors close to the start of fasting days is discouraged due to risk of excessive thirst.
- Use of sulfonylureas or insulin secretagogues without risk assessment and dose adjustment is discouraged during fasting.
- Overindulgence in high-carbohydrate, high-sugar, and high-fat foods after breaking fast without individualized meal advice.

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Adoption of the International Diabetes Federation and Diabetes and Ramadan International Alliance risk calculator for prefasting assessment.
- Emphasis on increased frequency and strategic timing of glucose monitoring during fasting, particularly in the last few hours.
- Recommendation to prioritize diabetes medications with low hypoglycemia risk (e.g., SGLT2 inhibitors, metformin) during long fasting periods.
- Use of automated insulin delivery (AID) systems and CGM to support high-risk individuals during fasting.
- Culturally sensitive, individualized treatment plans that account for the type of fast, diabetes type, and patient-specific factors.
- Adoption of the International Diabetes Federation and Diabetes and Ramadan International Alliance risk calculator, validated by multiple clinical studies across different countries, for prefasting risk assessment
- Recommendation to prioritize diabetes medications with low hypoglycemia risk (e.g., SGLT2 inhibitors, metformin) for individuals fasting for long hours and multiple consecutive days



## Edge Cases & Standalone Topics

Nonreligious fasting strategies focus on structured eating patterns for metabolic health, weight management, and glucose control without religious or spiritual intent. These approaches emphasize time-based or caloric restriction to optimize physiological outcomes.

**Key Features:**
- Intermittent fasting — alternating between fasting and non-fasting periods (e.g., alternate-day fasting, 5:2 pattern)
- Time-restricted eating — consuming all meals within a fixed daily window (e.g., 8–15 hours)

**Notable Details:**
- Alternate-day fasting alternates between 24-hour fasting and ad libitum eating days
- 5:2 fasting restricts intake to 500–600 kcal on two non-consecutive days per week
- Time-restricted eating typically uses 8–12 hour windows (e.g., 16:8 method)
- May improve insulin sensitivity and reduce HbA1c in prediabetic individuals
- Requires medical supervision for individuals with diabetes or eating disorders
- Hydration and electrolyte balance are critical during fasting periods
- Evidence suggests benefits for weight loss and cardiometabolic health
- Adherence varies; behavioral support may enhance long-term success

**Clinical Recommendation:** nonreligious fasting strategies (intermittent fasting/time-restricted eating)

**Core Recommendations:**
- Primary forms include intermittent fasting or time-restricted eating for weight and glucose management
- Intermittent fasting includes alternate-day fasting, 5:2 eating pattern, and time-restricted eating (8–15 h windows)
- Produces mild to moderate weight loss (3–8%) over 8–12 weeks, comparable to continuous calorie restriction
- Time-restricted eating is adaptable to any eating pattern and safe for adults with type 1 or type 2 diabetes
- Principles of healthy eating still apply during non-fasting periods
- Alternate-day fasting involves energy restriction of 500–600 calories on alternate days
- The 5:2 eating pattern involves energy restriction of 500–600 calories on two consecutive or nonconsecutive days with usual intake the other five days

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- People with diabetes taking insulin and/or secretagogues must be medically monitored during fasting periods
- Water intake is a key distinction between nonreligious and religious fasting practices

**Updated Recommendations:**
- 2024 systematic review and meta-analysis found intermittent energy restriction produces small but significant reductions in waist circumference and fat-free mass but is not superior to continuous energy restriction
