# Guideline Development & Management

These features collectively define a rigorous, transparent, and multidisciplinary framework for developing, managing, and updating clinical guidelines, ensuring evidence-based decision-making while mitigating bias and promoting adaptability to diverse clinical contexts.

**Key Features:**
- Multidisciplinary Guideline Writing Committee Composition — Ensures diverse expertise and patient representation from multiple professional organizations
- Guideline Task Force Member Affiliations — Includes global representation from academic, clinical, and research institutions across various specialties
- Guideline Approval and Dissemination Process — Requires multi-committee endorsement and copublication in a leading cardiology journal
- Guideline Update Prioritization Process — Involves continuous surveillance and structured surveys to assess evidence for updates
- Topic Selection Process — Uses predefined criteria and stakeholder input to identify and recommend new guideline topics
- GRADE methodology for evidence appraisal and recommendation grading
- Evidence-to-Decision (EtD) frameworks for transparent, consistent decision-making
- Scope definition and audience targeting for guideline applicability
- Integration of cost/value analysis in guideline development (e.g., 2025 AHA/ACC updates)
- Shared decision-making emphasis with patients/caregivers (e.g., ESC framework)
- Guideline Development Policy - RWI Management ensures unbiased document creation by disclosing writing committee members' relationships with industry
- Guideline Iteration Update highlights practice-changing recommendations since the last iteration
- Guideline Peer Review Process involves a committee appointed by the Joint Committee, including nominees from ACC, AHA, and collaborators
- Evidence-Based Guideline Development conducts an extensive literature review from October 2024 to December 2024

**Notable Details:**
- Guideline Writing Committee includes patient advocates alongside specialists (e.g., cardiologists, pharmacists, dietitians)
- Task force members hold dual roles in clinical practice and academic/research institutions across multiple countries
- Approval process involves four distinct committees (ACC and AHA) with final endorsement between October and December 2025
- Copublication occurs in the Journal of the American College of Cardiology
- GRADE system rates evidence certainty as high, moderate, low, or very low
- 2025 AHA/ACC updates incorporate cost/value methodology (update from 2014 statement)
- RWI (Relationships with Industry) disclosures are required for both writing committee members (Appendix 1) and peer reviewers (Appendix 2)
- Evidence review includes MEDLINE (PubMed), EMBASE, Cochrane Library, and AHRQ databases, with English-language human subject research prioritized

- Guidelines not immediately updatable may face retirement (temporary or permanent).

### ESC guideline development and implementation framework

Guidelines assist health professionals in proposing the best diagnostic or therapeutic approach for individual patients, emphasizing shared care decisions with patients or caregivers.

### Source 1: Lipid Disorders.pdf

**Recommendation Topic:** ESC guideline development and implementation framework

**Clinical Recommendations:**
- Guidelines assist health professionals in proposing the best diagnostic or therapeutic approach for individual patients, emphasizing shared care decisions with patients or caregivers.
- Guideline topics are selected for updating after annual expert review of new evidence by the ESC Clinical Practice Guidelines (CPG) Committee.
- Interim Focused Updates are created when new evidence could influence clinical practice before the next full guideline update.
- Recommendations are based on major randomized trials, systematic reviews, meta-analyses, and other evidence types (e.g., diagnostic accuracy studies, prognostic models).
- Recommendations are scored using predefined criteria for strength (Class I, IIa, IIb, III) and level of evidence (A, B, C).

**Eligibility Criteria:**
- Recommendations require at least 75% agreement among voting Task Force members to be approved.
- Declaration of interest forms must be provided and reviewed for all Task Force members to manage conflicts of interest.
- Funding for guideline development must be derived without healthcare industry involvement.
- Peer review must include multiple rounds by topic experts, ESC National Cardiac Societies, and relevant ESC Subspecialty Communities.
- Off-label medication use requires sufficient evidence, ethical consideration, patient consent, and compliance with country-specific regulations.
- Systematic literature searches are conducted in cases of controversy or uncertainty to ensure all key studies are considered for recommendations.


### Methodology Standards

GRADE Methodology + Evidence-to-Decision (EtD) Frameworks. Guidelines must use the GRADE system to rate evidence certainty (high, moderate, low, very low) and strength of recommendations.. EtD frameworks must be used to ensure systematic, transparent, and consistent decision-making in guideline development.

### Source 1: Endocrine_Society_Guideline_Methodology_links.pdf

**Guideline Development Component:** GRADE Methodology + Evidence-to-Decision (EtD) Frameworks

**Process Requirements:**
- Guidelines must use the GRADE system to rate evidence certainty (high, moderate, low, very low) and strength of recommendations.
- EtD frameworks must be used to ensure systematic, transparent, and consistent decision-making in guideline development.
- Risk of bias in nonrandomized studies must be assessed using tools like ROBINS-I to rate certainty of evidence.
- Standardized wording should be applied to improve clarity and efficiency in GRADE EtD frameworks.
- Guideline panels should avoid informal recommendations and use actionable statements based on a taxonomy framework.
- Guideline panels must use standardized wording for recommendations to improve clarity and efficiency in GRADE EtD frameworks (e.g., 'We recommend...' vs. 'We suggest...').
- Guideline development must include explicit consideration of equity in EtD frameworks to address potential disparities in care.

**Mandatory Standards:**
- Guideline panels must include multidisciplinary experts and, where applicable, patient representatives.
- Evidence appraisal must follow GRADE criteria, including downgrading for risk of bias, inconsistency, indirectness, imprecision, and publication bias.
- EtD frameworks must address key factors such as benefits, harms, values, resource use, equity, and feasibility.
- Certainty of evidence must be explicitly rated for each outcome using GRADEpro or equivalent tools.
- Recommendations must be linked to the underlying evidence and certainty ratings.
- Evidence appraisal must explicitly address indirectness by evaluating the applicability of evidence to the target population and setting.
- Guideline panels must document the rationale for any deviations from standard GRADE methodology in the evidence-to-decision process.

**Notable Details:**
- Funding exclusivity: No industry, government, or external entity financial support allowed for guideline development.
