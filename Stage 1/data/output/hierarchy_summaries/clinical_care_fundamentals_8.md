# Clinical Care Fundamentals (8)

This topic highlights critical safety and regulatory considerations for using gadolinium-based contrast media (GBCM) in pediatric patients, emphasizing age-based restrictions, heightened risks in neonates/infants, and the need for alternative imaging strategies due to immature renal function and potential long-term deposition effects.

**Key Features:**
- Age-based restrictions on gadolinium use in pediatric patients (FDA: <2 years, EMA: <1 year)
- Heightened caution in neonates and infants due to immature renal function and GFR measurement limitations

**Notable Details:**
- Gadolinium-based contrast media are contraindicated in children <2 years per FDA guidance
- EMA recommends avoiding GBCM in children <1 year due to higher risk of nephrogenic systemic fibrosis (NSF)
- Chronic kidney disease (CKD) in pediatric patients increases the risk of gadolinium retention and toxicity
- Accurate GFR measurement is challenging in neonates and infants, complicating risk assessment
- Gadolinium deposition in the brain and other tissues has been observed in pediatric patients, though clinical significance remains unclear
- Alternative imaging modalities (e.g., ultrasound, non-contrast MRI sequences) should be considered when feasible
- Dose reduction and prolonged intervals between contrast-enhanced studies are recommended in pediatric patients
- Shared decision-making with parents/guardians is essential when GBCM use is unavoidable in high-risk pediatric populations

**Clinical Recommendation:** pediatric gadolinium safety in CKD

**Clinical Actions:**
- Avoid gadolinium-based contrast media in children <2 years (FDA) or <1 year (EMA) due to licensing and safety concerns.
- Exercise caution in neonates and infants due to immature kidney function and inability to accurately measure GFR.
- Consider all other imaging modalities before using gadolinium-based contrast media in high-risk pediatric populations.
- Avoid high-risk gadolinium agents in very young children (e.g., neonates <4 weeks) based on expert suggestion.
- Pregnant women are considered at potential risk for NSF due to fetal kidney immaturity.
- Consider cost implications of nonlinear-chelated gadolinium preparations in lower-income and lower-middle-income countries when evaluating imaging options.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- GFR <30 mL/min/1.73m² is a general contraindication for gadolinium use.
- Accurate GFR measurement is required before gadolinium administration in neonates and infants.
