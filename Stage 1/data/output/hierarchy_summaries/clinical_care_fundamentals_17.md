# Clinical Care Fundamentals (17)

Protocols designed to mitigate biological and chemical hazards in laboratory environments by enforcing universal biosafety precautions for all specimens, ensuring consistent safety measures from handling to disposal.

**Key Features:**
- Universal biosafety assumption: Treat all specimens as potentially positive for infectious agents (e.g., HIV, hepatitis B/C)
- Mandatory PPE use: Wear gloves, lab coats, and safety glasses during all procedural steps
- Secure disposal: Autoclave residual sample material after analysis

**Notable Details:**
- HIV, hepatitis B, and hepatitis C are explicitly listed as target pathogens for biosafety precautions
- Autoclaving is the specified method for decontaminating residual samples
- PPE requirements apply to every stage of the method, not just high-risk steps

### Source 1: electrolytes.pdf

**Test Method:** biosafety and chemical handling protocol

**Performance Characteristics:**
- Treat all specimens as potentially positive for infectious agents (e.g., HIV, hepatitis B, hepatitis C).
- Wear protective gloves, laboratory coats, and safety glasses during all steps of the method.
- Discard residual sample material by autoclaving after analysis.
- Place disposable items (pipette tips, vials, gloves, etc.) in biohazard autoclave bags and keep sealed until autoclaved.
- Wipe work surfaces with 10% bleach solution after work is finished.

**Pre-analytical Requirements:**
- Hepatitis B vaccination series recommended for analysts working with whole blood and/or serum.
- Handle acids and bases with extreme care due to caustic and toxic properties.
- Use organic solvents only in well-ventilated areas or under a chemical fume hood.
- Ensure MSDSs for all chemicals are readily accessible as hard copies in the lab.
