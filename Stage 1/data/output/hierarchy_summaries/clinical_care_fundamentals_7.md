# Clinical Care Fundamentals (7)

This topic delineates the structured phases of perioperative care, emphasizing the temporal and functional boundaries of pre-operative, intra-operative, and post-operative segments, with a focus on patients with Type 1 Diabetes Mellitus (T1DM) due to their specialized glycemic management needs.

**Key Features:**
- Peri-operative period definition: Spans from admission to the pre-operative area until discharge from the post-anesthesia care unit (PACU)
- Phase segmentation: Divided into pre-operative, intra-operative, and post-operative segments

**Notable Details:**
- Peri-operative care in T1DM requires specialized management due to insulin dependence and glycemic control needs
- The PACU discharge marks the end of the peri-operative period, not hospital discharge
- Pre-operative phase includes admission to the pre-operative area, not just the surgical suite
- Intra-operative phase covers the entire surgical procedure under anesthesia
- Post-operative phase begins immediately after anesthesia and continues until PACU discharge

**Core Management Principles:**
- The peri-operative period spans from admission to the pre-operative area until discharge from the post-anesthesia care unit (PACU).
- The peri-operative period is divided into pre-operative, intra-operative, and post-operative segments.
- The pre-operative segment includes the time from admission to the pre-operative area until transfer to the operating suite.
- The intra-operative segment ends with transfer to the PACU.
- The post-operative segment ends with discharge from the PACU.

**Clinical Prerequisites:**
- A systematic approach to assessing the quality of care throughout the spectrum of elective surgical procedures.
- Metabolic optimization must begin at admission to the pre-operative area, not just on the day of surgery, to prevent hyperglycemia and ketosis risks associated with surgical stress.
