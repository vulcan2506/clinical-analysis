# Clinical Care Fundamentals (5)

The polyol pathway represents a hyperglycemia-driven metabolic disruption in diabetes mellitus, particularly Type 1, where excess glucose is diverted into alternative biochemical routes, leading to cellular dysfunction through osmotic stress and enzymatic imbalances.

**Key Features:**
- Polyol pathway activation via aldose reductase and sorbitol dehydrogenase
- Intracellular sorbitol and fructose accumulation causing osmotic stress

**Notable Details:**
- Reduced Na+/K+/ATPase activity due to osmotic imbalance
- Efflux of osmolytes (myoinositol, taurine, adenosine) exacerbating cellular dysfunction
- Hyperglycemia-induced shunting of glucose into the polyol pathway in T1DM
- Osmotic stress from polyol accumulation disrupting cellular homeostasis

**Clinical Recommendation:** polyol pathway metabolic disruption in T1DM

**Core Management Principles:**
- High intracellular glucose shunts into the polyol pathway, increasing sorbitol and fructose production via aldose reductase and sorbitol dehydrogenase.
- Accumulation of sorbitol and fructose causes osmotic stress, leading to efflux of osmolytes (myoinositol, taurine, adenosine) and reduced Na+/K+/ATPase activity.
- NADPH depletion during polyol pathway activity reduces glutathione regeneration, increasing oxidative stress.
- Advanced glycation end (AGE) products form intracellularly and extracellularly, impairing neurotropic support, mitochondrial function, and activating inflammatory pathways via RAGE and NF-kB.
- Increased ROS production from disrupted oxidative phosphorylation is the primary driver of metabolic disruptions in the polyol pathway.
- Increased extracellular AGE formation activates RAGE, triggering NF-kB-mediated inflammatory responses that further exacerbate polyol pathway disruptions.

**Clinical Prerequisites:**
- Monitoring for early signs of polyol pathway-related complications (e.g., neuropathy) in T1DM patients.
- Assessing oxidative stress markers and antioxidant status in high-risk patients.
- Evaluating mitochondrial function and ROS levels in patients with uncontrolled hyperglycemia.
- Routine evaluation of mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation efficiency in T1DM patients with persistent hyperglycemia to assess ROS production.
