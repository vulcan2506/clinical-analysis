# Clinical Laboratory Systems

Clinical Laboratory Systems under 'Electrolyte Testing Systems' and 'Hematologic Diagnostic Markers' focus on ensuring accurate, reliable, and clinically validated measurements for electrolyte balance and immune cell function, respectively. These systems integrate rigorous quality control, calibration, and diagnostic protocols to support clinical decision-making and regulatory compliance.

**Key Features:**
- Electrolyte Testing Systems with Ion-Selective Electrode (ISE) methods for sodium, potassium, and chloride in urine and other specimens, emphasizing regulatory adherence and performance validation.
- 24-month quality control monitoring for CBC and urine electrolytes to ensure analyzer accuracy and compliance with specified stability and calibration standards.
- Urine electrolyte QC data analysis and reporting with Lot, N, Mean, SD, and CV for Sodium, Potassium, and Chloride to track reagent lot performance.
- Leukocyte differential analysis quantifying percentages and absolute counts of neutrophils, lymphocytes, monocytes, eosinophils, and basophils to assess immune status.
- Leukocyte disorder diagnostic markers identifying shifts in cell morphology and counts, such as neutrophilia with left shift or reactive lymphocytes, to distinguish bacterial from viral infections.
- Electrode calibration standards (ISE Reference Electrolyte, ISE Diluent Gen2, ISE Internal Standard Gen2) with specified stability and internal standard correction for drift.
- Instrument troubleshooting protocol requiring hardware checks, recalibration, and escalation to Roche for unresolved issues to maintain system reliability.
- Clinical applications of electrolyte analytes (e.g., Sodium for aldosteronism, diabetes insipidus, dehydration) to guide diagnostic and therapeutic decisions.
- Electrolyte assay performance validation (Roche ISE Plus) with bias thresholds for Sodium (<5%), Chloride (<7%), and Potassium (<14%) to ensure measurement accuracy.
- Hematologic diagnostic markers providing quantitative and qualitative insights into immune cell populations and their functional states for infection and disorder differentiation.

**Notable Details:**
- Urine electrolyte QC data must include Lot, N (sample size), Start Date, End Date, Mean, SD, and CV for each reagent lot to ensure traceability and performance tracking.
- Electrode stability varies: Na+/K+/Cl- electrodes stable for 2 months or 9000 tests; Reference electrode stable for 6 months, requiring scheduled replacement to maintain accuracy.
- Bias thresholds for Roche ISE Plus assay: Sodium <5% (12.4-198.7 mmol/L), Chloride <7% (24.8-198.7 mmol/L), Potassium <14% (3.68-99.4 mmol/L) to validate assay performance.
- Calibration standards include an internal standard solution measured during calibration and between samples to correct drift, ensuring consistent measurement accuracy.
- Urine QC pool performance data must track Mean, SD, and CV for Sodium, Potassium, and Chloride in urine collections to monitor long-term stability and reagent lot performance.

- Instrument troubleshooting protocol requiring hardware checks, recalibration, and escalation to Roche for unresolved issues

## Hematologic Diagnostic Markers

Hematologic diagnostic markers in leukocyte analysis provide quantitative and qualitative insights into immune cell populations and their functional states, aiding in the differentiation of infections, inflammatory responses, and hematologic disorders.

**Key Features:**
- Leukocyte differential analysis quantifies percentages and absolute counts of neutrophils, lymphocytes, monocytes, eosinophils, and basophils to assess immune status.
- Leukocyte disorder diagnostic markers identify shifts in cell morphology and counts, such as neutrophilia with left shift or reactive lymphocytes, to distinguish bacterial from viral infections.

**Notable Details:**
- Neutrophils typically comprise 50-70% of leukocytes and are primary responders to bacterial infections.
- Lymphocytes account for 20-40% of leukocytes and include T cells, B cells, and NK cells for adaptive immunity.
- Neutrophilia with left shift indicates bacterial infections and increased release of immature neutrophils from the bone marrow.
- Toxic granulation, Döhle bodies, and cytoplasmic vacuolation in neutrophils suggest severe infections.
- Lymphocytosis with reactive lymphocytes (increased cytoplasm, irregular nuclei) is indicative of viral infections.
- Monocytes (2-8% of leukocytes) differentiate into macrophages and dendritic cells for phagocytosis and antigen presentation.
- Eosinophils (1-4%) are involved in allergic responses and parasitic infections.
- Basophils (<1%) release histamine and heparin in allergic and inflammatory reactions.

## Adrenal Crisis Management

Adrenal crisis management focuses on addressing life-threatening hypotension and metabolic acidosis resulting from cortisol and aldosterone deficiency in adrenal insufficiency, with primary Addison's disease exacerbating these complications due to elevated ACTH levels.

**Key Features:**
- Hypotension in adrenal insufficiency stems from cortisol deficiency impairing vascular tone and aldosterone deficiency causing sodium loss and volume depletion
- Metabolic acidosis arises from aldosterone deficiency leading to hyperkalemia and impaired renal hydrogen ion excretion

**Notable Details:**

- Electrolyte correction (potassium, sodium) is prioritized alongside glucocorticoid administration
- Acidosis typically resolves with volume resuscitation and mineralocorticoid replacement rather than direct bicarbonate therapy
- Delayed treatment of adrenal crisis can lead to refractory shock and multiorgan failure

## Electrolyte Homeostasis

Electrolyte homeostasis in the body is primarily governed by sodium and chloride, which together regulate osmolality, water distribution, and cellular function across extracellular and intracellular spaces.

**Key Features:**

- Chloride intake is primarily through dietary sodium chloride (NaCl).
- Chloride distribution is ~55% in extracellular space, ~30% in bones, and ~15% in cells.

### Source 1: Sodium and Cloride .pdf

**Electrolyte Parameter:** metabolic alkalosis mechanisms and diagnostic interpretation

**Pathophysiology and Clinical Associations:**
- Increase in HCO3 concentration due to conditions preventing renal excretion or loss of gastric juice (H and Cl ions)
- ECFV decreased in gastrointestinal diseases such as vomiting
- ECFV increased in Cushing's disease and hyperaldosteronism
- Diuretics cause reduction in Na reabsorption leading to hypochloraemia
- Hyperaldosteronism and Cushing's disease present with low Cl that cannot be corrected by common salt
- Hypochloremia in metabolic alkalosis may also result from chronic respiratory acidosis compensation (e.g., in chronic obstructive pulmonary disease) due to renal bicarbonate retention

**Clinical Prerequisites:**
- Assessment of gastrointestinal disease status (e.g., vomiting history)
- Evaluation of adrenal function (Cushing's disease, hyperaldosteronism)
- Measurement of ECFV status (decreased vs increased)
- Review of diuretic usage and renal sodium reabsorption
- Correlation of chloride levels with extent of hypokalaemia
- Assessment of respiratory function and acid-base status to rule out compensatory respiratory acidosis contributing to metabolic alkalosis


### reportable ranges and dilution protocol

Reportable ranges for Na⁺ are 80-180 mmol/L in Serum/Plasma and 20-250 mmol/L in Urine. Reportable ranges for K⁺ are 1.5-10.0 mmol/L in Serum/Plasma and 3-100 mmol/L in Urine. Reportable ranges for Cl⁻ are 45-140 mmol/L in Serum/Plasma and 20-250 mmol/L in Urine

**Test Method:** reportable ranges and dilution protocol

**Performance Characteristics:**
- Reportable ranges for Na⁺ are 80-180 mmol/L in Serum/Plasma and 20-250 mmol/L in Urine
- Reportable ranges for K⁺ are 1.5-10.0 mmol/L in Serum/Plasma and 3-100 mmol/L in Urine

- Manual dilution requires using deionized water as the diluent
- Final results must be multiplied by the appropriate dilution factor after re-assaying
- Manual dilution must be performed using a 1:10 ratio (specimen:deionized water) unless otherwise specified by the manufacturer's instructions for the assay

**Pre-analytical Requirements:**
- Specimen type must be identified as either Serum/Plasma or Urine
- Manual dilution is only performed if the specimen falls outside the specified reportable ranges
- Deionized water must be used for all manual dilutions
- The specimen must be re-assayed after dilution before calculating the final result
- The appropriate dilution factor must be applied to the re-assayed result
- The dilution factor must be documented in the laboratory information system (LIS) along with the original and diluted results
