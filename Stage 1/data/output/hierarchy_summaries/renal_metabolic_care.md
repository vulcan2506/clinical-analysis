# Renal & Metabolic Care

This collection integrates standardized kidney function assessment with evidence-based chronic kidney disease (CKD) management strategies, emphasizing precision, equity, and patient-centered care across diverse clinical contexts and populations.

**Key Features:**
- GFR evaluation methodology prioritizes eGFRcr initially, with eGFRcr-cys for higher accuracy or when eGFRcr is unreliable
- Dialysis initiation criteria focus on symptoms, quality of life, GFR, and lab abnormalities rather than arbitrary eGFR thresholds
- Albuminuria measurement standardization favors urine ACR (first morning void) over total protein for initial testing and confirms orthostatic proteinuria
- CKD diagnostic protocols require repeat measurements to confirm incidental findings in at-risk individuals tested with both urine albumin and GFR assessment
- Transgender GFR estimation incorporates shared decision-making considering muscle mass, sex hormones, and gender identity, reporting eGFR alongside filtration markers
- eGFR marker optimization acknowledges limitations of SCr (muscle mass) and cystatin C (steroid/thyroid/cancer effects) and neither is perfect
- eGFR variation analysis framework uses intraindividual variation (~5%) and RCV ranges (14–20%) to guide cautious interpretation of small changes
- eGFR equation standardization mandates race-neutral, region-consistent equations and validation of pediatric equations
- Urine sample reporting standards specify ACR reporting in untimed samples, storage conditions (fresh or 4°C for up to 7 days), and avoidance of freezing at -20°C
- Point-of-care kidney function testing protocols implement creatinine/albumin POCT with strict preanalytical, analytical, and postanalytical quality controls where lab access is limited
- Pediatric GFR estimation equations prioritize CKiD U25 2021 eGFRcr, EKFC, or CKD-EPI40, with preference for CKiD U25 in children under 25 years
- Protein intake guidelines tailor recommendations to CKD stage and dialysis status, balancing nutritional needs and disease progression risks
- KDIGO 2022 Diabetes-CKD guideline serves as the primary reference for integrated diabetes and kidney disease management
- CKD-MBD management relies on serial assessments of phosphate, calcium, and PTH to mitigate vascular calcification risks
- Uremic symptom assessment uses validated tools to monitor appetite, fatigue, and quality of life in progressive CKD

**Notable Details:**
- eGFRcr-cys is inaccurate in catabolic states, advanced cirrhosis/heart failure, high-dose steroids, or frailty
- Intraindividual eGFR biological variation is ~5.3% (creatinine/cystatin C) or 5.0% (combined); RCV ranges 14–20%
- Protein intake should be 0.8 g/kg/day for CKD stage G3+, 1.0–1.2 g/kg/day for dialysis patients, and avoided above 1.3 g/kg/day due to risks of albuminuria and CVD mortality

## Kidney Function Assessment

It emphasizes accuracy, equity, and practical implementation in GFR estimation, albuminuria measurement, and CKD diagnosis.

**Clinical Actions:**

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**

- Cystatin C measurements should be available when evaluating GFR to support the use of eGFRcr-cys.
- Healthcare providers must understand the value and limitations of both eGFR and mGFR as well as factors influencing SCr and cystatin C measurements.


### dialysis initiation criteria

dialysis initiation criteria and preemptive planning. Initiate dialysis based on a composite assessment of symptoms, signs, QoL, preferences, GFR, and laboratory abnormalities rather than arbitrary eGFR thresholds.

### Source 1: Chronic Kidney Disease.pdf

**Clinical Recommendation:** dialysis initiation criteria and preemptive planning

**Clinical Actions:**
- Initiate dialysis based on a composite assessment of symptoms, signs, QoL, preferences, GFR, and laboratory abnormalities rather than arbitrary eGFR thresholds.
- Initiate dialysis if specific clinical situations are evident, such as uremic symptoms, inability to control volume status, or progressive nutritional/cognitive deterioration.
- Avoid early initiation of dialysis at higher GFR levels as data shows no survival or cardiac benefit compared to late start.
- Plan for preemptive kidney transplantation and/or dialysis access when GFR is <15–20 ml/min/1.73 m² or risk of KRT is >40% over 2 years.
- In children, initiate dialysis for poor growth refractory to optimized nutrition and medical management in addition to adult indications.
- Initiate dialysis in the GFR range between 5 and 10 ml/min per 1.73 m² when specific clinical indications are present, though this is not invariably the case.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Presence of one or more specific clinical indications (e.g., neurological signs, pericarditis, medically resistant acid-base/electrolyte abnormalities, intractable pruritus, serositis).
- Inability to control volume status or blood pressure despite management.
- Progressive deterioration in nutritional status refractory to dietary intervention, or cognitive impairment.
- Evidence of progressive and irreversible CKD in children.
- Multidisciplinary discussion involving the patient (if age appropriate), caregivers, and healthcare providers for decision-making.
- Multidisciplinary discussion involving the child (if age appropriate), caregivers, and healthcare providers for decision-making in pediatric cases.

### Source 1: Chronic Kidney Disease.pdf

**Clinical Recommendation:** albuminuria measurement standardization in CKD

**Clinical Actions:**
- Use urine ACR as the preferred initial test for albuminuria (ACR ≥30 mg/g or ≥3 mg/mmol) over total protein measurement due to higher sensitivity and specificity.
- Prefer first morning void urine samples for initial testing to reduce variability and confirm orthostatic proteinuria.
- Confirm positive reagent strip results with quantitative laboratory measurement of ACR or PCR.
- Report ACR to one decimal place (mg/mmol) or whole numbers (mg/g) to standardize interpretation.
- Use immunological assays for urine albumin quantification with analytical CV <15% and traceability to international standards.
- Use both urine PCR and ACR for initial testing in children to account for higher prevalence of tubular proteinuria, with ACR prioritized for diabetic screening and glomerular damage characterization.
- Confirm positive reagent strip results with quantitative laboratory measurement of ACR or PCR, and express results as a ratio to urine creatinine even if initial semiquantitative tests are positive.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- First morning void urine samples are preferred for initial testing in adults and children.
- Quantitative laboratory measurement of ACR or PCR is required to confirm positive reagent strip results.
- Urine albumin assays must have analytical CV <15% and be traceable to international standards.
- Laboratories should participate in external quality assessment schemes for urine albumin, creatinine, and ACR.
- Storage of urine samples for albumin measurement should be at 4°C for up to 7 days; avoid freezing at -20°C.
- Laboratories must ensure urine albumin assays are traceable to international standards using certified reference materials, despite current lack of standardization in commercially available assays.
- For neonates and infants, interpret proteinuria using age-specific thresholds (e.g., PCR <500 mg/g for infants 6 months to 2 years; ACR <30 mg/g for children >2 years).

### eGFR marker optimization rationale

eGFR marker optimization rationale (cystatin C vs SCr). Creatinine (SCr) may be misleading at extremes of body habitus, spinal cord injuries, or sarcopenia due to its link to muscle mass. Cystatin C is impacted by steroid use, thyroid disease, and cancer. Neither SCr nor cystatin C alone is a perfect marker for estimating GFR clearance

### Source 1: Chronic Kidney Disease.pdf

**Clinical Recommendation:** eGFR marker optimization rationale (cystatin C vs SCr)

**Clinical Actions:**
- Creatinine (SCr) may be misleading at extremes of body habitus, spinal cord injuries, or sarcopenia due to its link to muscle mass
- Cystatin C is impacted by steroid use, thyroid disease, and cancer
- Neither SCr nor cystatin C alone is a perfect marker for estimating GFR clearance
- Combination of SCr and cystatin C (eGFRcr-cys) provides more accurate GFR estimates compared to measured values
- Risk gradients for outcomes are stronger for eGFRcys and eGFRcr-cys compared to eGFRcr
- Very low SCr levels (<45 ml/min/1.73m²) in frail patients may reflect poor health status (e.g., sarcopenia) rather than true kidney dysfunction, leading to underestimation of risk

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Use cystatin C or combined eGFRcr-cys for more accurate prognosis in CKD
- Consider biological factors (e.g., muscle mass, steroid use) when interpreting GFR estimates
- Use eGFRcys or eGFRcr-cys for risk stratification in CKD when SCr-based eGFR (eGFRcr) is likely to be inaccurate due to extremes of body habitus, spinal cord injuries, or sarcopenia

**Clinical Recommendation:** eGFR variation analysis framework

**Clinical Actions:**
- Intraindividual biological variation in eGFR is similar across CKD-EPI equations (creatinine: 5.3%, cystatin C: 5.3%, creatinine-cystatin C: 5.0%).

### eGFRcr-cys limitations

eGFRcr-cys limitations and mGFR recommendations. eGFRcr-cys is inaccurate in catabolic states (e.g., serious infections, inflammatory states, high cell turnover in cancers), advanced cirrhosis or heart failure, use of high-dose steroids, or frail patients.

### Source 1: Chronic Kidney Disease.pdf

**Clinical Recommendation:** eGFRcr-cys limitations and mGFR recommendations

**Clinical Actions:**
- eGFRcr-cys is inaccurate in catabolic states (e.g., serious infections, inflammatory states, high cell turnover in cancers), advanced cirrhosis or heart failure, use of high-dose steroids, or frail patients.
- eGFRcr-cys may be insufficiently reliable in settings requiring high accuracy, such as simultaneous kidney transplant with other solid organ transplants, kidney donor candidacy, or drug dosing with narrow therapeutic index or serious toxicity.
- Plasma clearance protocols are inaccurate in people with extensive edema or ascites; urinary clearance protocols are recommended instead.
- Imaging of nuclear tracers is less accurate than eGFR and not recommended for GFR measurement.
- Decisions to measure GFR should be made by nephrologists and other physicians using a framework to determine required accuracy for specific clinical decisions.
- Earlier blood sampling is most accurate for better-preserved GFRs in mGFR protocols.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- mGFR should be performed using standardized methods with standard operating procedures.
- External quality assessment (EQA) should be used for assays of exogenous markers in mGFR.
- GFR centers, directed by a nephrologist or laboratory director, are recommended to ensure high-quality results and increase utilization.
- Special considerations for clearance methods are required for populations such as later sampling time for low GFR or urinary clearance for edematous patients.
- National kidney societies should collaborate with payers to support reimbursement for mGFR procedures.
- GFR centers should be directed by a nephrologist champion or laboratory director to ensure high-quality results and increase utilization.

**Clinical Recommendation:** eGFR accuracy limitations and complementary testing

**Clinical Actions:**
- eGFR has inherent error compared to mGFR, with 10% of estimates having ≥30% error even in high-accuracy populations (P50 ≥90%)
- Non-GFR determinants (e.g., diet, muscle mass, tubular secretion) significantly impact creatinine-based eGFR accuracy
- Cystatin C-based eGFR is less affected by non-GFR determinants but has its own confounders (e.g., adiposity, thyroid dysfunction, inflammation)
- Combined eGFRcr-cys improves accuracy over either marker alone due to complementary non-GFR determinants
- Discordance between eGFRcr and eGFRcys (>15 ml/min/1.73m² or ≥20%) warrants further evaluation (e.g., mGFR or cystatin C monitoring)

- Non-GFR determinants for cystatin C (e.g., adiposity, smoking, thyroid dysfunction, glucocorticoid excess, inflammation) are less well understood than those for creatinine

- Physicians must understand eGFR/mGFR limitations to select appropriate GFR assessment methods for clinical decisions
- mGFR is advised when accuracy within 15% is required (e.g., critical treatment decisions)
- eGFR is acceptable if accuracy within 30% is sufficient and non-GFR determinants are stable
- For eGFRcr-cys, non-GFR determinants must be considered; eGFRcys alone may be preferable in cases of extreme creatinine variability (e.g., muscle loss)
- Measured urinary creatinine clearance (CrCl) should be supervised if used, as it is prone to collection errors
- For optimal eGFR application, conditions should ideally simulate morning fasting or moderate protein intake, though this may be challenging to implement clinically
- If measured urinary creatinine clearance (CrCl) is used, it should be supervised to minimize collection errors, as unsupervised collections have a mean bias of 25% and high variability (e.g., 25% of participants had CrCl ≥18% lower or ≥23% higher than mGFR)

**Clinical Recommendation:** CKD monitoring in diabetes standards

**Core Recommendations:**
- Assess kidney function annually with random urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio (UACR) and estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) in people with type 1 diabetes with duration ≥5 years and all people with type 2 diabetes regardless of treatment.
- Confirm moderately or severely elevated albuminuria (≥30 mg/g creatinine) with two of three abnormal UACR specimens collected within 3–6 months due to high biological variability (>20%).
- Use the 2021 CKD-EPI creatinine equation to calculate eGFR, with persistent eGFR <60 mL/min/1.73 m² and/or UACR >30 mg/g creatinine considered abnormal.
- Monitor UACR and eGFR frequency based on CKD progression risk, cardiovascular disease risk, and complications (e.g., 1–4 times per year for high-risk patients).
- Use cystatin C-based eGFR or combined creatinine-cystatin C equations in patients with altered creatinine generation (e.g., amputation, frailty, low muscle mass).

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Type 1 diabetes with duration ≥5 years or any type 2 diabetes diagnosis.
- Persistent elevation of urinary albumin excretion, eGFR <60 mL/min/1.73 m², or other kidney damage manifestations for CKD diagnosis.
- Steady-state serum creatinine for accurate eGFR calculation.
- Exclusion of transient UACR elevations due to exercise, infection, fever, heart failure, hyperglycemia, menstruation, or hypertension.
- Use of accredited laboratory for UACR measurements to avoid false positives/negatives.

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Semiquantitative or qualitative dipstick screening for albuminuria without simultaneous urine creatinine measurement.
- Timed or 24-hour urine collections for albuminuria assessment due to burden and preference for spot UACR.
- Use of outdated eGFR equations (pre-2021 CKD-EPI creatinine equation).
- Relying on single UACR measurements for diagnosis of albuminuria without confirmation.
- Ignoring biological variability in UACR measurements (>20%) when interpreting results.

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Recommendation for combined creatinine-cystatin C eGFR equations for improved accuracy in specific patient populations.
- Emphasis on tracking UACR trends over time rather than isolated measurements.
- Frequency of monitoring adjusted based on CKD progression risk and complications (e.g., cardiovascular disease, anemia).
- Use of cystatin C in patients with altered creatinine generation (e.g., amputation, frailty).
- Endorsement of UACR confirmation with two of three abnormal specimens within 3–6 months.

**Clinical Recommendation:** chronic kidney disease management in T1DM

**Core Management Principles:**
- Nondiabetic renal disease (NDRD) is rare in T1DM (2–3% incidence), requiring kidney biopsy if hematuria, rapidly deteriorating renal function, sudden gross proteinuria, short diabetes duration, or absence of retinopathy is present.
- Diabetic nephropathy is rare after 30 years of diabetes without prior end-organ involvement.
- Patients with T1DM are at increased risk for renal artery stenosis (presenting as difficult-to-control hypertension, flash pulmonary edema, or progressive renal dysfunction) and papillary necrosis (with hematuria, flank pain, and fever).

- Ambulatory blood pressure monitoring is recommended to assess hypertension in T1DM patients with renal disease, as it provides a more accurate assessment than clinic measurements.

## Grading Methodologies

These features collectively describe structured methodologies for grading clinical recommendations and evidence certainty, primarily using the GRADE framework to balance benefits, harms, and evidence quality in guideline development.

**Key Features:**
- Recommendation Strength Criteria: Determines recommendation strength (Strong vs Conditional) based on the balance of benefits and harms, with higher certainty of evidence generally warranting stronger recommendations.
- Evidence Certainty Grading Framework (GRADE): Assesses certainty of evidence for critical outcomes, starting with 'High' for RCTs and adjusting based on study limitations, inconsistency, indirectness, imprecision, or publication bias.
- Evidence Certainty Assessment: Uses GRADE methodology to determine certainty levels for each outcome via systematic review, with initial certainty based on study design (e.g., randomized trials → high; observational studies → low).
- Graded Clinical Recommendation Framework: Indicates strength of recommendation (Level 1 or 2) and certainty of evidence (A, B, C, or D), with Level 1 implying most patients would want the action and supporting policy development.

**Notable Details:**
- Initial certainty grade for RCTs is 'High' (Grade A) in the GRADE framework.
- Strong recommendation (Level 1) is assigned when desirable consequences clearly outweigh undesirable consequences.
- Certainty of evidence is adjusted downward based on study limitations, inconsistency, indirectness, imprecision, or publication bias.
- Graded recommendations use Level 1 ('We recommend') or Level 2 ('We suggest') to indicate strength.
- Certainty of evidence is shown as A, B, C, or D in the graded clinical recommendation framework.
- GRADE Evidence Certainty Assessment integrates with the EtD (Evidence-to-Decision) framework for guideline development.
- Exceptions exist where low-certainty evidence may still warrant a strong recommendation.
- Level 1 recommendations support policy development due to broad patient desirability.

## Contrast-Induced Nephropathy

Contrast-induced nephropathy encompasses the renal risks associated with arterial and venous administration of contrast media, with a focus on contrast-associated acute kidney injury (CA-AKI) in patients with varying renal function, particularly those with chronic kidney disease (CKD). The overarching theme emphasizes risk stratification, prevention strategies, and the nuanced differences between arterial and venous contrast exposure.

**Key Features:**
- Arterial contrast nephrotoxicity: Higher risk of CA-AKI due to patient comorbidities or intrinsic nephrotoxicity compared to venous administration.
- Contrast media nephrotoxicity: Risk assessment and management in CKD patients, prioritizing indication-based imaging and individualized risk-benefit evaluation.

**Notable Details:**
- Use 'contrast-associated AKI (CA-AKI)' instead of 'contrast-induced AKI' due to weak causal links.
- Arterial administration of contrast media carries a higher risk of CA-AKI than venous administration.
- Consider general population indications for imaging studies alongside individual risk-benefit assessments in CKD patients.
- Contrast media nephrotoxicity risk is elevated in patients with pre-existing chronic kidney disease (CKD).
- Prevention strategies should be tailored to patient-specific factors, including comorbidities and renal function.
- Arterial contrast nephrotoxicity is influenced by both the route of administration and patient-specific risk factors.
- Risk assessment for contrast media nephrotoxicity should integrate patient history, renal function, and imaging necessity.

- Known risk factors for CA-AKI include advanced age, heart failure, proteinuria, and hyperglycemia, which should be considered when assessing patient risk before contrast administration.

**Clinical Actions:**

**Clinical Recommendation:** actionable genetic conditions in CKD

**Clinical Actions:**
- Identify glomerular disease due to mutations in Alport genes (COL4A3/4/5)
- Screen for conditions at risk for recurrence after kidney transplantation (e.g., aHUS, primary hyperoxaluria, APRT deficiency)
- Perform specific screening for extrarenal manifestations (e.g., intracranial aneurysms in ADPKD, renal cell carcinoma in FLCN)
- Incorporate genetic testing for reproductive counseling (e.g., prenatal/preimplantation diagnosis)
- Perform genetic testing for conditions with high recurrence risk post-transplant (e.g., CFH/CFI/C3 mutations in aHUS) to guide preemptive transplant strategies

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Genetic testing must be supported by evidence-based recommendations for clinical action
- Collaboration between nephrologists, geneticists, and genetic counselors for optimal implementation
- Standardized protocols for genetic testing and interpretation within a health system
- Shared decision-making with patients regarding the utility and risks of genetic testing
- Standardized operating protocols for kidney biopsy must include ultrasound guidance to minimize complications
