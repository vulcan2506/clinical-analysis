# Cardiovascular Risk Management

These guidelines collectively emphasize a precision-medicine approach to cardiovascular risk management, integrating advanced lipid-lowering therapies, genetic and biomarker-based risk stratification, and individualized care pathways across diverse patient populations, including special considerations for diabetes, pregnancy, and high-risk conditions.

**Key Features:**
- 2026 Dyslipidemia Guideline Update: Intensified LDL-C and non-HDL-C targets (<55 mg/dL and <85 mg/dL) for severe hypercholesterolemia with ASCVD using ezetimibe, PCSK9 mAb, or bempedoic acid
- Statin Therapy Intensity and LDL-C Goals by ASCVD Risk Stratification: Higher baseline risk correlates with greater net benefit; shared decision-making is critical near 3% 10-year event risk threshold
- Lp(a) Integration in Cardiovascular Risk Assessment: Elevated Lp(a) ≥50 mg/dL triggers statin intensification or PCSK9 inhibitor consideration in high-risk patients
- Statin Therapy Intensification in Diabetes: Moderate- to high-intensity statins recommended for adults 40–75 with diabetes without ASCVD to achieve ≥30–49% LDL-C reduction and LDL-C <100 mg/dL
- Familial Hypercholesterolemia (FH) Treatment: Genetic sequencing and validated risk scores (e.g., Montreal-FH-SCORE) for diagnosis and risk stratification
- Lipid Management in Pregnancy: Statin discontinuation in most pregnancies, with hydrophilic statins (e.g., pravastatin) considered only in select cases
- Obesity-Related Lipid Management: Non-HDL-C and apoB prioritized over LDL-C for atherogenic risk assessment in obesity
- Pediatric Lipid Management: Lifestyle interventions first-line; statins recommended for children ≥8 years with persistent LDL-C ≥160 mg/dL
- Hypertriglyceridemia Management: Lifestyle modifications (diet, exercise, weight loss) as first-line for TG ≥150 mg/dL after secondary causes ruled out
- Genetic Testing for FH: Panel-based testing for LDLR, APOB, or PCSK9 variants to improve detection and enable cascade screening
- Statin Intolerance Management: Addresses patient concerns, excludes secondary causes, and considers switching statins or adding nonstatin therapies if intolerance occurs
- Lipid-Lowering Therapy in CKD: Moderate-intensity statins ± ezetimibe recommended for adults with CKD stage 3+ and LDL-C 70–189 mg/dL
- Primary Prevention Guideline: Health behavior counseling and individualized lipid-lowering therapy discussions for adults aged 30–59 with low ASCVD risk
- Dietary Recommendations: Plant-based Mediterranean-style diet emphasized for lipid management, including in CKD
- Physical Activity Recommendations: ≥150 minutes/week moderate-to-vigorous aerobic exercise plus resistance training 2 days/week

This collection of guidelines and recommendations focuses on evidence-based strategies for lipid and cardiovascular risk management across diverse patient populations, emphasizing individualized care, advanced therapies, and integration of novel biomarkers and genetic testing.

- Pediatric Lipid Management: Lifestyle interventions first-line; statins recommended for children ≥8 years with persistent LDL-C ≥160 mg/dL
- Hypertriglyceridemia Management: Lifestyle modifications (diet, exercise, weight loss) as first-line for TG ≥150 mg/dL after secondary causes ruled out
- Genetic Testing for FH: Panel-based testing for LDLR, APOB, or PCSK9 variants to improve detection and enable cascade screening

**Notable Details:**
- Lp(a) measurement recommended at least once in every adult's lifetime for risk stratification
- Statin therapy should be stopped 1–2 months before pregnancy or immediately upon discovery in non-high-risk hypercholesterolemia
- Non-HDL-C and apoB are more accurate than LDL-C for assessing atherogenic risk in obesity
- Genetic testing for FH improves detection even in individuals without severely elevated LDL-C
- Routine CAD screening in asymptomatic individuals does not improve outcomes and is not recommended

Severe Hypercholesterolemia + Subclinical Coronary Atherosclerosis Management. In adults with severe hypercholesterolemia (LDL-C ≥190 mg/dL) and clinical ASCVD, addition of ezetimibe, PCSK9 mAb, or bempedoic acid is recommended to achieve LDL-C <55 mg/dL and non-HDL-C <85 mg/dL.

**Clinical Recommendation:** Severe Hypercholesterolemia + Subclinical Coronary Atherosclerosis Management

**Key Clinical Actions:**
- In adults with severe hypercholesterolemia (LDL-C ≥190 mg/dL) and clinical ASCVD, addition of ezetimibe, PCSK9 mAb, or bempedoic acid is recommended to achieve LDL-C <55 mg/dL and non-HDL-C <85 mg/dL.
- In adults with severe hypercholesterolemia (LDL-C ≥190 mg/dL) without ASCVD but with HeFH, risk factors, or coronary calcification, addition of ezetimibe, PCSK9 mAb, and/or bempedoic acid is recommended to achieve LDL-C <70 mg/dL and non-HDL-C <100 mg/dL.
- In adults with CAC score ≥300–999 AU, statin therapy is recommended as first-line to achieve ≥50% LDL-C lowering and LDL-C <70 mg/dL.
- In adults with CAC score ≥100–299 AU or ≥75th percentile, LDL-C lowering therapies (statin first-line) are recommended to achieve LDL-C <70 mg/dL.
- In adults with severe hypercholesterolemia (LDL-C ≥190 mg/dL) and HeFH without ASCVD, consideration of genetic testing for cascade screening of first-degree relatives is recommended to identify additional affected individuals.
- For adults with subclinical coronary atherosclerosis (CAC score ≥100 AU), incorporation of coronary artery calcium (CAC) scoring into shared decision-making for statin initiation is recommended, particularly in intermediate-risk patients.

**Patient Preparation / Diagnostic Criteria:**
- Maximally tolerated statin therapy must be initiated before adding ezetimibe, PCSK9 mAb, or bempedoic acid.
- Clinical or genetic confirmation of heterozygous familial hypercholesterolemia (HeFH) is required for intensified therapy in severe hypercholesterolemia without ASCVD.
- Genetic confirmation of heterozygous familial hypercholesterolemia (HeFH) is required for intensified therapy in severe hypercholesterolemia without ASCVD, with preference for cascade testing of first-degree relatives to identify additional cases.

**Key Clinical Actions:**
- Management has evolved from statin monotherapy to combination therapy targeting multiple lipid metabolism pathways.
- Individualized clinician-patient discussions must precede dyslipidemia medication initiation, addressing benefits, risks, side effects, and patient preferences.
- Statin therapy slightly increases new-onset diabetes risk in predisposed individuals (e.g., BMI ≥30 kg/m², fasting glucose ≥100 mg/dL), but benefits outweigh risks.
- Comprehensive assessment of medication-associated symptoms and adherence is required during follow-up encounters.
- Length of exposure to elevated LDL-C and Lp(a) should be factored into treatment decisions due to residual risk even with intensive LDL-C lowering.
- Combination therapy with agents targeting complementary lipid metabolism pathways (e.g., statin + ezetimibe, PCSK9 inhibitors, or bempedoic acid) is now standard for high/very high cardiovascular risk patients to address residual risk.
- Clinician-patient discussions must reaffirm benefits and address medication adherence during follow-up encounters to optimize ASCVD prevention.

**Patient Preparation / Diagnostic Criteria:**
- Patient-centered care must include education on statin-associated diabetes risk before initiation, especially in high-risk individuals.
- Potential drug-drug interactions (DDIs) with statins, particularly CYP3A4-metabolized statins (atorvastatin, lovastatin, simvastatin), must be evaluated prior to initiation.
- Monitoring of hepatic transaminases is required for medications like niacin, fibrates, and omega-3 fatty acids.
- Referral to a lipid specialist is recommended for patients with familial lipid disorders, very high lipoprotein levels, or intolerance to lipid-lowering agents.
- Genetic testing may be considered to confirm complex inherited lipid disorders.
- Evaluation of statin drug-drug interactions (DDIs) with cardiovascular medications is mandatory prior to statin initiation, with dose modifications or avoidance as needed to minimize adverse events.
- Monitoring of hepatic transaminases is required for patients with hepatic impairment receiving prescription omega-3 fatty acids (icosapent ethyl).

### Source 3: Chronic Kidney Disease.pdf

**Clinical Recommendation:** cardiovascular risk assessment in CKD

**Clinical Actions:**
- People with CKD are at increased risk of CVD, including structural heart disease, heart failure, and sudden death, with risk increasing progressively as eGFR declines.
- Diagnosis of cardiac disease in CKD is complex; standard tests (e.g., exercise electrocardiography, contrast imaging) may be limited due to CKD-related factors.
- Troponin assays maintain diagnostic accuracy in CKD but may require higher assay-specific cutoff levels; trends in troponin concentration over time should be monitored via serial measurements.
- Estimate 10-year cardiovascular risk using validated tools (e.g., CKD-patched SCORE or AHA PREVENT™ equations) to guide statin therapy initiation.
- Statin-based regimens should maximize absolute LDL cholesterol reduction to achieve the largest treatment benefits, with intensive regimens (e.g., atorvastatin 20 mg, rosuvastatin 10 mg) safe in CKD.
- CKD-MBD (mineral and bone disorder) accelerates vascular calcification, contributing to both intimal (atherosclerotic plaque) and medial (vascular stiffness) calcification, which worsens cardiovascular risk in CKD.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- eGFR <60 mL/min/1.73m² (GFR categories G3a–G5) for statin or statin/ezetimibe combination in adults ≥50 years.
- eGFR ≥60 mL/min/1.73m² (GFR categories G1–G2) for statin therapy in adults ≥50 years.
- Statin therapy suggested for adults aged 18–49 with CKD and known coronary disease, diabetes, prior ischemic stroke, or 10-year coronary risk >10%.
- Consider statin therapy in adults aged 18–49 with CKD and 10-year coronary risk <10% if deemed appropriate.
- PCSK-9 inhibitors may be added to maximal tolerated statin therapy for high coronary risk in CKD (eGFR ≥20 mL/min/1.73m²).
- For adults with CKD and total cholesterol >7.5 mmol/l (290 mg/dl) with personal or family history of premature ischemic heart disease (event before age 60), consider familial hypercholesterolemia and specialist referral.

**Clinical Recommendation:** Lipid Management in Pregnancy

**Key Clinical Actions:**
- Stop statin therapy 1 to 2 months before attempting pregnancy or as soon as pregnancy is discovered in persons with hypercholesterolemia not at high ASCVD risk.
- Consider hydrophilic statins (e.g., pravastatin) if statin continuation is deemed beneficial after individualized risk-benefit discussion.

- Perform lipoprotein apheresis in pregnant or lactating individuals with HoFH to lower LDL-C and reduce ASCVD risk.

**Patient Preparation / Diagnostic Criteria:**
- Individualized benefit-risk discussion before continuing statin therapy in pregnant individuals with FH or history of ASCVD.
- Avoid bile acid sequestrants when TG levels ≥300 mg/dL due to potential worsening of bloating and constipation.
- Monitor and manage severe hypertriglyceridemia (TG ≥500 mg/dL) with adjunctive therapies to prevent pancreatitis.
- Engage in clinician-patient discussion regarding statin use, fetal risks, and ASCVD progression risks in high-risk pregnancies.
- Engage in clinician-patient discussion regarding statin use, fetal risks, and ASCVD progression risks in pregnant individuals at very high ASCVD risk (e.g., FH with additional risk factors).

**Key Clinical Actions:**
- Obesity worsens CKM risk factors, inflammation, and adiposopathy ('sick fat').
- Non-HDL-C and apoB levels more accurately reflect atherogenic risk than LDL-C in obesity.

- Elevated triglycerides (TG) and reduced HDL-C are typical lipid profile abnormalities in obesity, alongside increased small, cholesterol-depleted LDL particles.

**Patient Preparation / Diagnostic Criteria:**
- Tailored lifestyle counseling and support for weight reduction based on comorbidities, dietary preferences, and accessibility.
- Consultation with a Registered Dietitian Nutritionist (RDN) for sustainable dietary interventions.
- Comprehensive lifestyle approach including physical activity for weight reduction and metabolic/cardiovascular outcomes.
- Consultation with a Registered Dietitian Nutritionist (RDN) for tailored dietary interventions to ensure sustainability and effectiveness.

**Key Clinical Actions:**
- Utilize genetic sequencing and comprehensive variant screening to diagnose FH, moving beyond limited-variant screening.
- Apply validated risk scores (e.g., Montreal-FH-SCORE, SAFEHEART) to predict cardiovascular events and mortality in FH patients.
- Initiate advanced therapies like lomitapide, mipomersen, or incisirian for homozygous FH when statins/ezetimibe are insufficient.
- Implement cascade screening and genotype-based recall strategies for family members of diagnosed FH patients.
- Recognize that severe hypercholesterolemia and FH genotypes independently predict coronary heart disease risk.
- Utilize cholesterol-year-score (CYS) as part of the Montreal-FH-SCORE to quantify cumulative LDL-C exposure over time for refined risk stratification in FH patients.
- Incorporate lipoprotein(a) [Lp(a)] measurement alongside LDL-C and genetic testing to refine cardiovascular risk prediction in FH, as elevated Lp(a) independently increases ASCVD risk in this population.

**Patient Preparation / Diagnostic Criteria:**
- Genetic testing or comprehensive variant screening to confirm diagnosis and distinguish from polygenic hypercholesterolemia.
- Assessment of clinical signs and family history to support genetic diagnosis in severe hypercholesterolemia.
- Evaluation of ethnic inequalities and access to diagnostic coding for accurate prevalence estimation.
- Long-term follow-up data collection for real-world outcomes in FH cohorts.
- Consideration of statin intolerance or contraindications before initiating non-statin advanced therapies.
- Mandatory documentation of statin intolerance or contraindications in medical records prior to initiating non-statin advanced therapies (e.g., lomitapide, mipomersen, or PCSK9 inhibitors) to ensure appropriate therapeutic escalation.
- Require integration of FH-specific risk scores (e.g., Montreal-FH-SCORE, SAFEHEART) into electronic health records (EHR) for automated risk calculation and clinical decision support.

- Lifestyle management is recommended for all children and adolescents with lipid abnormalities to improve LDL-C, TG, and non-HDL-C.

- Patient must be ≥8 years of age for standard statin initiation in FH.
- LDL-C level must be persistently ≥160 mg/dL (4.1 mmol/L) for ≥3 to 6 months.
- Inadequate response to 3 to 6 months of lifestyle management is required before pharmacotherapy.

**Core Recommendations:**

**Clinical Recommendation:** Evidence-Based Recommendation Framework (Class of Recommendation + Level of Evidence)

**Key Clinical Actions:**
- Class 1 (STRONG) recommendations indicate benefit clearly outweighs risk, using phrases like 'is recommended' or 'should be performed'.
- Class 2a (MODERATE) recommendations suggest benefit likely outweighs risk, using phrases like 'is reasonable' or 'can be useful'.

## Cardiovascular Risk Assessment in Diabetes

Cardiovascular risk assessment in diabetes integrates traditional risk factors with advanced imaging tools like Coronary Artery Calcium (CAC) scoring to refine risk stratification, guide statin therapy, and improve outcomes across diverse patient populations, including type 1 diabetes (T1DM) and pediatric cases.

**Key Features:**
- CAC-guided ASCVD risk stratification refines intermediate-risk adults into higher or lower categories using CAC scores, improving statin initiation decisions
- CAC screening in T1DM identifies high-risk individuals with CAC >400 correlating strongly with clinical CAD
- Pediatric diabetes cardiovascular risk management includes lipid screening protocols starting at age ≥2 years in T1DM and annually in T2DM
- CAC-informed statin initiation stratifies therapy intensity based on incidental coronary atherosclerosis severity on noncardiac CT scans

**Notable Details:**
- CAC score of 0 may justify deferring statin therapy in intermediate-risk adults except in diabetes, FH, or strong family history of premature ASCVD
- CAC scores ≥300 indicate ASCVD risk comparable to treated ASCVD, warranting LDL-C goals <70 mg/dL or <55 mg/dL
- CAC scores ≥1000 are associated with significantly increased long-term all-cause and cause-specific mortality in asymptomatic patients
- CAC prevalence in T1DM increases with age (11% in <38 years, 88% in 50–55 years) and is higher in males
- CAC scoring provides incremental risk stratification beyond traditional risk factors in intermediate-risk patients
- Pediatric lipid screening in T1DM starts after glycemia improvement and age ≥2 years, then every 3 years if LDL ≤100 mg/dL
- CAC severity (e.g., ≥1000) indicates extensive multivessel and diffuse extra-coronary calcification
- CAC score ≥100 stratifies intermediate-risk patients for statin therapy, reducing MACE and plaque progression

### Source 1: 2026 ACC-AHA-AACVPR-ABC-ACPM-ADA AGS-APhA-ASPC-NLA-PCNA Guideline on the Management of Dyslipidemia.pdf

**Clinical Recommendation:** Fibrate and Niacin Therapy in Hypertriglyceridemia

**Key Clinical Actions:**
- Fibrate therapy is recommended for patients with persistent hypertriglyceridemia to reduce cardiovascular risk.
- Combination lipid therapy with fibrates is considered in specific high-risk populations.
- Extended-release niacin with laropiprant is evaluated for use in high-risk patients despite mixed trial outcomes.
- Niacin is generally not recommended for patients already receiving intensive statin therapy due to lack of benefit.
- Clinical trials like HPS2-THRIVE and AIM-HIGH inform the decision-making for adding niacin to statin regimens.
- Fibrate therapy is specifically recommended for patients with severe hypertriglyceridemia (≥500 mg/dL) to prevent acute pancreatitis, independent of cardiovascular risk reduction.

**Patient Preparation / Diagnostic Criteria:**
- Patients must have persistent hypertriglyceridemia despite standard management.
- Patients should be assessed for cardiovascular risk factors such as type 2 diabetes or established ASCVD.
- Baseline liver and muscle function must be evaluated before initiating niacin or fibrate therapy.
- Baseline and periodic monitoring of liver transaminases is required during fibrate therapy due to risk of hepatotoxicity.

- Utilize Polygenic Risk Scores (PRS) to assess genetic risk of CAD and improve risk prediction beyond clinical factors.
- Recognize that high CAD PRS magnitude is greatest in adults <55 years and effects are additive with family history.
- Apply aggressive lifestyle management and lipid-lowering therapy based on personalized risk estimation including reproductive markers.
- Consider early menarche (<10 years) as a reproductive risk marker in ASCVD risk assessment for primary prevention.

**Patient Preparation / Diagnostic Criteria:**
- Detailed medical history including reproductive and pregnancy history.
- Assessment of conventional risk factors (e.g., hypertension, diabetes).
- Use of applicable 10-year and 30-year risk estimation by PREVENT-ASCVD equations.
- Consideration for subclinical atherosclerosis imaging where applicable.
- Validation of CAD PRS using appropriate ancestry-specific or multi-ancestry models.
- Use multi-ancestry Polygenic Risk Scores (PRS) validated for diverse ancestry groups to assess CAD genetic risk.

**Clinical Recommendation:** Familial Hypercholesterolemia Patient Group Criteria

**Key Clinical Actions:**
- Group A: Treat homozygous FH with LDL-C >500 mg/dL
- Group B: Treat heterozygous FH with LDL-C ≥300 mg/dL
- Group C: Treat heterozygous FH with LDL-C ≥70 mg/dL and documented CAD or PAD
- Aggressive LDL-C lowering goals based on ASCVD status and FH confirmation
- Use of maximally tolerated statins plus ezetimibe, PCSK9 mAbs, or bempedoic acid
- Exclude high saturated fat diet as an etiology for severe hypercholesterolemia before confirming primary hypercholesterolemia diagnosis
- Perform dietary history to identify ketogenic diet-induced severe hypercholesterolemia

**Patient Preparation / Diagnostic Criteria:**
- Clinically diagnosed familial hypercholesterolemia (homozygous or heterozygous)
- Specific LDL-C thresholds (>500, ≥300, or ≥70 mg/dL) depending on genotype
- Documentation of coronary artery disease or peripheral artery disease for Group C
- Exclusion of high saturated fat diet as etiology in severe hypercholesterolemia
- Clinical or genetic confirmation of FH for aggressive LDL-C goals
- Documentation of coronary artery calcification for risk stratification in severe hypercholesterolemia without ASCVD
- Consider additional ASCVD risk factors (e.g., hypertension, diabetes, smoking) for treatment intensification in HeFH

**Clinical Recommendation:** cardiometabolic risk assessment protocols in T1DM

**Core Management Principles:**
- Blood pressure and weight must be measured at each visit.
- Screen for dyslipidemia soon after T1DM diagnosis (once blood glucose is stable) and repeat every 5 years if normal.
- Annual assessment of urinary albumin excretion in patients aged ≥11 years with 2–5 years of T1DM duration.
- Use Indian Academy of Pediatric BMI cut-offs (23 kg/m² for overweight, 27 kg/m² for obesity) in South Asian children due to higher cardiometabolic risk.
- Avoid routine coronary heart disease screening beyond resting ECG; perform detailed assessment only in symptomatic patients or those with ECG abnormalities or multiple risk factors.
- Hypertension in children is defined as systolic or diastolic blood pressure ≥95th centile for age, sex, and height on three or more occasions (per CDC standards)

**Clinical Prerequisites:**
- Blood glucose levels must be stable before dyslipidemia screening.
- BMI cut-offs for South Asians (23 kg/m² overweight, 27 kg/m² obesity) should be applied in Indian children.
- Framingham or Reynolds risk scores may be used for CV risk stratification, though their performance in young South Asians is uncertain.
- Non-fasting lipid profiles are acceptable if fasting is not feasible, but confirmatory fasting tests are required if triglycerides or LDL-C are elevated.
- Detailed CVD assessment is mandatory for patients with CVD symptoms, baseline ECG ischemia, or multiple CV risk factors.
- Blood glucose levels must be stable before dyslipidemia screening (as per ISPAD guidelines)
