# Clinical Care Fundamentals (18)

This topic addresses the clinical and logistical considerations for performing Next-Generation Sequencing (NGS) testing to identify molecular mechanisms of acquired resistance in tumors, with a focus on the feasibility of obtaining tumor biopsy samples for comprehensive analysis.

**Key Features:**
- NGS Testing for Resistance: Uses tumor biopsy samples to detect molecular alterations driving resistance to therapies.
- Surgical or core tumor biopsy: Preferred method for obtaining tissue when feasible to enable comprehensive NGS analysis.

**Notable Details:**
- Feasibility of obtaining tumor biopsy: Critical factor determining the ability to perform NGS testing for resistance mechanisms.
- Acquired resistance: NGS testing aims to identify specific molecular changes that develop in response to prior treatments.
- Tumor biopsy method: Surgical or core biopsy is preferred over fine-needle aspiration for higher tissue yield and quality.
- Comprehensive NGS analysis: Requires sufficient tumor tissue to detect low-frequency resistance mutations.
- Clinical utility: Identifying resistance mechanisms guides subsequent targeted therapy selection.
- Logistical challenges: Biopsy feasibility may be limited by tumor location, patient comorbidities, or prior treatments.
- Sample preservation: Proper handling and storage of biopsy tissue are essential to maintain DNA/RNA integrity for NGS.
- Turnaround time: NGS testing for resistance should be prioritized to inform timely treatment adjustments.
