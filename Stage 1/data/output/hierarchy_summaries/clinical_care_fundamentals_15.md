# Clinical Care Fundamentals (15)

These features collectively highlight the multifactorial risks of injection site abscesses, driven by procedural lapses, microbial contamination, and host-specific vulnerabilities, particularly in patients with type 1 diabetes mellitus (T1DM) or those using continuous subcutaneous insulin infusion (CSII).

**Key Features:**
- Poor hygiene at injection site
- Repeated use of the same needle
- Repeated injections at the same site
- Use of contaminated insulin
- Failure to change catheter at recommended intervals in CSII users
- Consideration of atypical mycobacteria (e.g., Mycobacterium chelonae and fortuitum) as potential causative agents

**Notable Details:**
- T1DM patients are at higher risk due to frequent injections and potential immune dysfunction
- CSII users must adhere to catheter change intervals (typically every 2–3 days) to prevent biofilm formation
- Atypical mycobacteria (e.g., Mycobacterium chelonae) are slow-growing and require prolonged culture incubation
- Contaminated insulin vials or pens can introduce bacterial or fungal pathogens directly into subcutaneous tissue
- Repeated injections at the same site increase local tissue trauma and microbial colonization risk
- Biofilm formation on CSII catheters can shield bacteria from immune clearance and antibiotics
- Immune dysfunction in T1DM may impair local inflammatory responses, delaying abscess detection
- Contaminated insulin may introduce pathogens like Staphylococcus aureus or Pseudomonas aeruginosa
