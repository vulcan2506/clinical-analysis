# Clinical Care Fundamentals (23)

This topic examines the physiological relationship between diabetes and auditory dysfunction, highlighting how metabolic disturbances like hyperglycemia and oxidative stress contribute to hearing impairment through vascular and neurological pathways.

**Key Features:**
- Increased prevalence: Hearing impairment is more common in diabetic populations compared to non-diabetic individuals
- Broad frequency impact: Affects both high-frequency and low-to-midfrequency auditory ranges
- Cochlear microangiopathy: Hyperglycemia-induced vascular damage to the cochlea
- Auditory neuropathy: Oxidative stress contributing to nerve-related auditory dysfunction

**Notable Details:**
- Pathophysiological drivers include hyperglycemia and oxidative stress
- Mechanism involves both vascular and neurological pathways
- Cochlear microangiopathy leads to reduced blood flow and oxygenation in the inner ear
- Oxidative stress damages auditory nerve fibers, impairing signal transmission

### diabetes-associated hearing impairment

### Source 1: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf

**Clinical Recommendation:** Diabetes-associated hearing impairment

**Core Recommendations:**
- Hearing impairment is more common in people with diabetes than in those without, affecting both high-frequency and low- to midfrequency ranges.
- Hyperglycemia and oxidative stress contribute to cochlear microangiopathy and auditory neuropathy as proposed pathophysiologic mechanisms.
- Low HDL cholesterol, coronary heart disease, peripheral neuropathy, and general poor health are identified risk factors for hearing impairment in individuals with diabetes.
- Impairment in smell is reported in individuals with diabetes, whereas impairment in taste is not consistently observed.

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Long-term follow-up (>20 years) is required to observe associations between elevated time-weighted mean A1C and increased risk of hearing impairment.

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Every 10% increase in A1C is associated with a 19% higher risk of high-frequency hearing impairment in long-term follow-up.
