    # glycemic management for bone health
    *Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*
    
    **Clinical Recommendation:** Glycemic management and pharmacological treatment for bone health in diabetes
    
    **Core Recommendations:**
    - Advocate for moderate physical activity to enhance muscle health, gait coordination, and balance as part of fracture preventive strategies
    - Ensure adequate daily intake of protein, calcium, and vitamin D, and cessation of smoking for all individuals with diabetes
    - Initiate antiosteoporosis therapy and adequate calcium/vitamin D supplementation as soon as possible for individuals with fragility fractures
    - Consider osteoanabolic therapy (teriparatide, abaloparatide, romosuzumab) for very-high-risk individuals given diabetes is a low-bone-turnover condition
    - Follow osteoanabolic treatments with antiresorptives to preserve BMD gains and prevent rapid loss of newly formed bone
    - Individualize vitamin D supplementation targets based on patient risk (e.g., osteoporosis, antiresorptive therapy) rather than rigid thresholds, with serum levels >30 ng/mL recommended for individuals receiving antiresorptive therapy
    
    **Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
    - Assessment of T-score (≤−2.5) or elevated FRAX score (≥3% for hip fracture or ≥20% for major osteoporotic fracture) for pharmacological treatment consideration
    - Evaluation of additional risk factors such as falls and elevated FRAX score for adults with T-score between −2.0 and −2.5
    - Pretreatment assessment, supplementation, close monitoring, and specialist consultation for individuals with advanced CKD (eGFR <30 mL/min/1.73 m²) receiving denosumab
    - Consideration of self-management abilities and ability to maintain injection schedules (e.g., every 6 months for denosumab) prior to drug initiation
    - Referral to a bone metabolic specialist for individuals on long-term treatment with antiosteoporosis medications, with multiple fragility fractures, or with multiple comorbidities
    
    **Previous Recommendations:**
    - Routine supplementation with vitamin D (with or without calcium) for the primary prevention of fractures in community-dwelling postmenopausal women and men aged 60 years or older
    - Prescription of romosuzumab in individuals who experienced a myocardial infarction or a stroke within the past year
    - Use of T-score alone without considering additional risk factors for fracture in type 2 diabetes, as T-score underestimates fracture risk in this population
    
    **Updated Recommendations:**
    - Strong consideration of pharmacological treatment for adults with diabetes with a T-score between −2.0 and −2.5 in the presence of additional risk factors for fracture
    - Use of osteoanabolic agents (teriparatide, abaloparatide, romosuzumab) for very-high-risk individuals due to the low-bone-turnover nature of diabetes
    - Initiation of antiosteoporosis therapy during an inpatient stay for individuals with fragility fractures to reduce care delays
    - Recognition that antiosteoporosis treatment reduces the risk of subsequent low-trauma fracture, especially in the first 1–2 years after a fracture
    - Individualized selection of bisphosphonate therapy (oral or intravenous) for individuals with poor medication-taking behavior or gaps in access to medical care
    - Initiate antiosteoporosis therapy during an inpatient stay for individuals with fragility fractures to reduce care delays
