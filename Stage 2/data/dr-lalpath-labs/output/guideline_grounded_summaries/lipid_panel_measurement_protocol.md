    # ApoB Measurement Recommendation
    *Source: 2026 ACC-AHA-AACVPR-ABC-ACPM-ADA AGS-APhA-ASPC-NLA-PCNA Guideline on the Management of Dyslipidemia.pdf*
    
    **Clinical Recommendation:** ApoB Measurement for ASCVD Risk Stratification and Therapeutic Guidance
    
    **Key Clinical Actions:**
    - ApoB is reasonable to measure in adults on lipid-lowering therapy (LLT) with ASCVD, CKM syndrome, type 2 diabetes, or elevated triglycerides (TG) to guide therapeutic intensification when LDL-C and/or non-HDL-C goals are achieved.
    - ApoB may be reasonable in untreated adults to enhance ASCVD risk assessment, guide LLT initiation, and characterize inherited lipid disorders.
    - ApoB directly quantifies atherogenic lipoprotein particle number (including LDL, VLDL, and Lp(a)), providing a more accurate measure of residual risk than LDL-C.
    - ApoB is particularly useful in populations with discordance between LDL-C and apoB (e.g., CKM syndrome, diabetes, or elevated TG), where LDL-C may appear at goal while apoB remains elevated.
    - ApoB measurement is standardized, unaffected by fasting status, and supports decisions on nonstatin therapies (e.g., ezetimibe, PCSK9 inhibitors, bempedoic acid, or inclisiran).
    
    **Patient Preparation / Diagnostic Criteria:**
    - Adults on LLT with ASCVD, CKM syndrome, type 2 diabetes, or elevated TG (TG ≥150 mg/dL) for therapeutic intensification guidance.
    - Untreated adults for enhanced ASCVD risk assessment or inherited lipid disorder characterization.
    - Use of standardized apoB measurement methods (not affected by fasting status).
    - Use of the Martin/Hopkins method to estimate LDL-C is preferred over the Friedewald equation to reduce discordance with apoB levels.

    # Lipid Measurement Guideline Update
    *Source: 2026 ACC-AHA-AACVPR-ABC-ACPM-ADA AGS-APhA-ASPC-NLA-PCNA Guideline on the Management of Dyslipidemia.pdf*
    
    **Clinical Recommendation:** Lipid Measurement Guideline Update: LDL-C Estimation + ApoB/Lp(a) Assessment + CAC Imaging
    
    **Key Clinical Actions:**
    - Use Martin/Hopkins or Sampson/NIH equations over Friedewald for LDL-C estimation in adults and children with standard lipid profiles.
    - Prefer nonfasting lipid profiles for most adults due to minimal prognostic differences vs fasting, except in TG ≥400 mg/dL or genetic dyslipidemia.

    - CAC scoring recommended for adults at intermediate or borderline risk to refine LLT intensity decisions.
    - Fasting lipid profile required for TG ≥200 mg/dL in individuals with known or suspected disorders in triglyceride metabolism or if further evaluation of hypertriglyceridemia is warranted.

    # Diabetes-Specific Lipid Management Guideline
    *Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*
    
    **Clinical Recommendation:** Diabetes-Specific Lipid Management: Lifestyle + Pharmacotherapy
    
    **Core Recommendations:**
    - Recommend Mediterranean or DASH eating patterns, reduce saturated/trans fats, increase n-3 fatty acids, soluble fiber, plant stanols/sterols, and physical activity for lipid and ASCVD risk reduction in diabetes.
    - Intensify lifestyle therapy and optimize glycemic control for triglycerides ≥150 mg/dL and/or low HDL (<40 mg/dL men, <50 mg/dL women).
    - Obtain lipid profiles at diagnosis, initial evaluation, annually (or more frequently if indicated), and 4–12 weeks after statin initiation/dose change.
    - For diabetes aged 40–75 without ASCVD: moderate-intensity statin + lifestyle therapy.
    - For diabetes with ASCVD: high-intensity statin + lifestyle therapy targeting LDL reduction ≥50% and LDL <55 mg/dL; add ezetimibe/PCSK9 inhibitor if goals unmet.
    - Recommendation to attempt finding a tolerable statin dose or alternative statin before discontinuing therapy due to side effects, including evidence for benefit from extremely low, less-than-daily statin doses.
    
    **Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
    - Lipid profile required at statin initiation and 4–12 weeks post-dose change for monitoring response.
    - Tailor nutrition interventions to age, pharmacologic treatment, lipid levels, and medical conditions.
    - Annual lipid monitoring in adults with diabetes not on statins; more frequent in <40 years or longer diabetes duration.
    - Statin therapy continuation in adults >75 years already on treatment; consider initiation after risk-benefit discussion.
    - Immediate lipid panel before statin initiation to establish baseline.
    - Lipid profile should include total cholesterol, LDL cholesterol, HDL cholesterol, and triglycerides at all specified monitoring intervals.
    
    **Previous Recommendations:**
    - Routine avoidance of lipid-lowering therapy in adults >75 years without prior ASCVD (now individualized).
    - Assuming statin intolerance without attempting dose adjustment or alternative statin.
    - Discontinuing lipid-lowering therapy solely due to childbearing potential without weighing risks/benefits.
    - Routine avoidance of lipid-lowering therapy in adults >75 years without prior ASCVD (now individualized with discussion of benefits and risks).
    
    **Updated Recommendations:**
    - Bempedoic acid recommended for statin-intolerant diabetes to reduce cardiovascular events.
    - PCSK9 inhibitor or ezetimibe addition to high-intensity statin for diabetes with ASCVD if LDL goals unmet.
    - Inclisiran siRNA considered as alternative for statin-intolerant diabetes with ASCVD.
    - Annual lipid monitoring in diabetes not on statins (previously less frequent).
    - LDL goal <55 mg/dL for diabetes with ASCVD on high-intensity statin therapy.
    - Consideration of inclisiran siRNA as an alternative cholesterol-lowering therapy for people with diabetes and ASCVD intolerant to statin therapy.

    # Lipid Monitoring and Treatment Response Guideline
    *Source: 2026 ACC-AHA-AACVPR-ABC-ACPM-ADA AGS-APhA-ASPC-NLA-PCNA Guideline on the Management of Dyslipidemia.pdf*
    
    **Clinical Recommendation:** Lipid Monitoring and Treatment Response
    
    **Key Clinical Actions:**
    - Perform lipid profile 4 to 12 weeks after initiation or dose adjustment of lipid-lowering therapy (LLT)
    - Repeat lipid profiles every 6 to 12 months thereafter to assess efficacy and adherence
    - Individualize frequency based on ASCVD risk, LDL-C reduction needs, and medication stability
    - Assess response as percentage reduction in LDL-C from baseline and achievement of absolute goals
    - Use nonfasting lipid profiles for most patients unless known hypertriglyceridemia is present

    # Lipoprotein(a) and Statin Therapy Analysis
    *Source: 2026 ACC-AHA-AACVPR-ABC-ACPM-ADA AGS-APhA-ASPC-NLA-PCNA Guideline on the Management of Dyslipidemia.pdf*
    
    **Clinical Recommendation:** Lp(a) Risk Stratification and Therapeutic Response Analysis
    
    **Key Clinical Actions:**
    - Elevated Lp(a) levels are recognized as an independent risk factor for ASCVD and aortic stenosis, influencing risk stratification beyond standard lipid panels.
    - Statin therapy does not significantly reduce Lp(a) levels, necessitating alternative strategies for patients with high Lp(a) and residual risk.
    - PCSK9 inhibitors are recommended to lower Lp(a) levels and reduce major adverse cardiovascular events in high-risk patients.
    - Lp(a) testing is increasingly utilized in clinical practice to identify patients who may benefit from intensified lipid-lowering therapies.
    - Lipoprotein apheresis is considered a therapeutic option for patients with extremely high Lp(a) levels who have failed or cannot tolerate other therapies.
    - Elevated Lp(a) levels are associated with increased risk of myocardial infarction and aortic stenosis, independent of other cardiovascular risk factors.
    - Genetic polymorphisms in the apolipoprotein(a) gene (e.g., LPA rs10455872) may modify the cardiovascular risk conferred by elevated Lp(a) levels.
    
    **Patient Preparation / Diagnostic Criteria:**
    - Measurement of Lp(a) levels is required to assess cardiovascular risk in patients with elevated LDL-C or high ASCVD risk.
    - Baseline Lp(a) levels should be assessed before initiating statin therapy to determine the need for additional lipid-lowering interventions.
    - Genetic polymorphism in the apolipoprotein(a) gene may be considered in conjunction with plasma Lp(a) levels for comprehensive risk assessment.
    - Baseline Lp(a) levels should be measured in patients with a family history of premature ASCVD or aortic stenosis, even in the absence of elevated LDL-C.
    - Lp(a) levels should be reassessed periodically in patients with persistently high levels (>125 nmol/L) to monitor progression and guide therapeutic decisions.

    # Lipoprotein Target Guideline
    *Source: 2026 ACC-AHA-AACVPR-ABC-ACPM-ADA AGS-APhA-ASPC-NLA-PCNA Guideline on the Management of Dyslipidemia.pdf*
    
    **Clinical Recommendation:** Lipoprotein Target Guideline for ASCVD Risk Reduction
    
    **Key Clinical Actions:**
    - Lipoprotein targets (e.g., LDL-C, apoB) are now explicitly stratified by ASCVD risk categories.
    - Coronary artery calcium (CAC) scoring is integrated into risk-based treatment decisions.
    - Agatston units (AU) are used for CAC quantification in risk stratification.
    - HDL-C and triglycerides are considered alongside LDL-C and apoB for comprehensive risk assessment.
    - Non-HDL-C is explicitly included alongside LDL-C and apoB as a secondary target for lipid-lowering therapy in patients with high triglycerides or metabolic syndrome.
    - Lipoprotein(a) [Lp(a)] is recommended for measurement at least once in adults to assess inherited cardiovascular risk, particularly in those with a family history of premature ASCVD or recurrent events despite low LDL-C.
    
    **Patient Preparation / Diagnostic Criteria:**
    - Risk stratification must incorporate ASCVD risk categories to determine lipoprotein targets.
    - CAC scoring is recommended for intermediate-risk patients to refine treatment decisions.
    - Lipid testing must include fasting or non-fasting measurements of LDL-C, apoB, non-HDL-C, and triglycerides, with Lp(a) assessed at least once in all adults.
    - CAC scoring is required for patients with borderline or intermediate ASCVD risk (5–20% 10-year risk) to guide treatment intensification decisions.
