    # diagnostic criteria for diabetes
    *Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*
    
    **Clinical Recommendation:** Diagnostic criteria for diabetes
    
    **Core Recommendations:**
    - Diagnosis requires confirmation via repeat testing in asymptomatic individuals using FPG, 2-h PG (OGTT), or A1C.
    - Random plasma glucose ≥200 mg/dL (11.1 mmol/L) with classic hyperglycemia symptoms or hyperglycemic crisis is sufficient for diagnosis without repeat testing.
    - A1C testing is used to assess chronicity of hyperglycemia but is not diagnostic alone in asymptomatic individuals.
    - FPG or A1C is preferred for routine screening due to ease of administration, while 2-h PG (OGTT) is more sensitive and recommended for high-risk conditions (e.g., cystic fibrosis-related diabetes).
    - Carbohydrate intake of at least 150g for 3 days prior to OGTT is required to avoid false-positive results.
    
    **Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
    - Classic symptoms of hyperglycemia (polyuria, polydipsia, unexplained weight loss) or hyperglycemic crisis for random plasma glucose diagnosis.
    - Repeat testing required for confirmation in asymptomatic individuals regardless of test used.
    - Nonpregnant individuals for routine screening with FPG or A1C.
    - Mixed eating pattern with ≥150g carbohydrates for 3 days prior to OGTT.
    - Prompt sample processing for glucose testing to avoid falsely low results due to glycolysis.
    
    **Previous Recommendations:**
    - Use of single-test results without confirmation for diagnosis in asymptomatic individuals.
    - Relying solely on A1C for diagnosis in conditions with altered red blood cell turnover (e.g., hemoglobinopathies).
    - Carbohydrate restriction prior to OGTT due to risk of false-positive results.
    - Use of OGTT without standardized carbohydrate intake preparation.
    - Diagnosis based on isolated glucose measurements without clinical correlation.
    
    **Updated Recommendations:**
    - A1C, FPG, or 2-h PG criteria applicable for diagnosing diabetes in children and adolescents, with risk-based screening recommendations.
    - OGTT preferentially recommended for high-risk conditions (e.g., cystic fibrosis-related diabetes, post-transplantation diabetes mellitus).
    - Emphasis on prompt glucose sample processing to prevent glycolysis-related falsely low results.
    - Standardized carbohydrate intake (≥150g for 3 days) prior to OGTT to ensure accurate results.
    - Incorporation of A1C testing to assess chronicity of hyperglycemia in symptomatic individuals.

    # diagnostic criteria for diabetes
    *Source: ICMR Type 2 Diabetes.pdf*
    
    **Clinical Recommendation:** Diagnostic criteria for diabetes and prediabetes
    
    **Therapeutic Actions:**
    - Diabetes diagnosis requires confirmation with a second test if using FPG ≥ 126 mg/dl, 2-hour OGTT ≥ 200 mg/dl, or HbA1c ≥ 6.5%
    - Symptoms of diabetes (e.g., polyuria, polydipsia, weight loss, fatigue) plus casual plasma glucose ≥ 200 mg/dl can confirm diabetes without fasting
    - Prediabetes is defined by IFG (FPG 100-125 mg/dl), IGT (2-hour OGTT 140-199 mg/dl), or HbA1c 5.7-6.4%
    - Random plasma glucose 140-199 mg/dl warrants OGTT for further evaluation
    - Testing requires normal diet (≥200g carbohydrate/day for 3 days), overnight fast (8-10 hours), and standardized OGTT protocol (75g glucose load)
    - More than half of all patients with diabetes may be asymptomatic, requiring laboratory confirmation for diagnosis
    
    **Prerequisites:**
    - Patient must be on a normal diet (≥200g carbohydrate/day) for at least 3 days prior to testing
    - Fasting plasma glucose testing requires an 8-10 hour overnight fast
    - OGTT requires 75g glucose load dissolved in ≥250ml water, consumed within 5 minutes
    - Second blood sample collected exactly 2 hours post-glucose load with patient resting and refraining from smoking/eating
    - Confirmation of diabetes diagnosis with a second test if using FPG, OGTT, or HbA1c criteria
    - Osmotic symptoms (polyuria, polydipsia), weight loss despite polyphagia, tiredness, generalized pruritus, recurrent urogenital infections, or delayed wound healing must be present alongside casual plasma glucose ≥ 200 mg/dl to confirm diabetes without fasting

    # glycemic management guidelines
    *Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*
    
    **Clinical Recommendation:** Comprehensive glycemic management
    
    **Core Recommendations:**
    - Assess glycemic status regularly using A1C, fasting glucose, and continuous glucose monitoring (CGM) where appropriate.
    - Set individualized glycemic goals based on patient-specific factors such as age, comorbidities, and hypoglycemia risk.
    - Prevent and manage hypoglycemia with structured education, glucose monitoring, and treatment protocols.
    - Adjust diabetes management during intercurrent illness to prevent hyperglycemia and ketoacidosis.
    - Diagnose and manage hyperglycemic crises (DKA and HHS) with standardized protocols for fluid resuscitation, insulin therapy, and electrolyte correction.
    
    **Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
    - Baseline glycemic assessment including A1C, fasting glucose, and patient history.
    - Evaluation of patient comorbidities, life expectancy, and hypoglycemia risk before setting glycemic goals.
    - Patient education on hypoglycemia recognition, prevention, and treatment.
    - Sick-day management plan for patients with intercurrent illness.
    - Access to emergency protocols for hyperglycemic crises management.
    
    **Previous Recommendations:**
    - Fixed A1C targets without consideration of patient-specific factors.
    - Routine use of urine glucose testing as the primary method for glycemic monitoring.
    - One-size-fits-all glycemic management without individualized adjustments.
    - Delayed treatment of hyperglycemic crises without standardized protocols.
    - Exclusive reliance on fasting glucose for glycemic assessment without CGM or postprandial glucose monitoring.
    
    **Updated Recommendations:**
    - Incorporation of CGM data for real-time glycemic trend analysis and hypoglycemia prevention.
    - Use of time-in-range (TIR) metrics as a complementary goal to A1C for glycemic assessment.
    - Structured hypoglycemia risk stratification and prevention strategies.
    - Standardized sick-day management protocols for diabetes patients during illness.
    - Evidence-based protocols for hyperglycemic crises management with emphasis on early intervention.
