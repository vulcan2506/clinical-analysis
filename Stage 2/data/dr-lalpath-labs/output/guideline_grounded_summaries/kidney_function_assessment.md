    # diabetic nephropathy monitoring
    *Source: ICMR_Guidelines_for_Management_of_Type_1_Diabetes.pdf*
    
    **Clinical Recommendation:** diabetic nephropathy monitoring and management
    
    **Core Management Principles:**
    - Focus on glycemic control to prevent progression from normoalbuminuria to microalbuminuria (primary prevention) and from microalbuminuria to overt nephropathy (secondary prevention)
    - Use RAAS blockade (ACEi/ARB) for secondary prevention in T2DM with hypertension or microalbuminuria, but not in normotensive, normoalbuminuric T1DM patients
    - Implement multimodal interventions including optimal blood glucose control, antihypertensive therapy, lipid-lowering drugs, and dietary protein restriction (0.6–0.8 g/kg/day)
    - Screen annually for albuminuria and renal function in patients with diabetes duration ≥5 years, considering non-albuminuric phenotypes
    - Target blood pressure <140/90 mmHg (or <130/80 mmHg in high cardiovascular risk) with RAAS blockade for renoprotection
    - Consider ACE gene polymorphism (II, ID, DD genotypes) to guide RAAS blockade intensity in T1DM, with DD genotype warranting more aggressive therapy
    
    **Clinical Prerequisites:**
    - Annual screening for albuminuria and renal function in patients with diabetes duration ≥5 years
    - Optimal blood glucose control to preserve GFR and reduce hyperfiltration injury
    - Use of RAAS blockade in T2DM with hypertension or microalbuminuria, but not in normotensive, normoalbuminuric T1DM patients
    - Dietary protein restriction (0.6–0.8 g/kg/day) for patients with progressive nephropathy
    - Lipid-lowering therapy (statins) for diabetic patients with CKD stages 1–4
    - Target systolic blood pressure <140 mmHg and diastolic <90 mmHg (or <130/80 mmHg in high cardiovascular risk) in T1DM for renoprotection
    - Use RAAS blockade in T1DM patients with microalbuminuria to delay progression to overt nephropathy and CKD
