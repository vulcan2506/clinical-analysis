    # Statin Therapy Guidance
    *Source: 2026 ACC-AHA-AACVPR-ABC-ACPM-ADA AGS-APhA-ASPC-NLA-PCNA Guideline on the Management of Dyslipidemia.pdf*
    
    **Clinical Recommendation:** Statin Therapy Intensity and LDL-C Goals by ASCVD Risk Stratification
    
    **Key Clinical Actions:**
    - Higher baseline predicted risk correlates with greater net benefit from statin therapy; shared decision-making is essential near 3% 10-year event risk threshold.
    - In primary prevention with intermediate risk (5% to <10% by PREVENT-ASCVD), initiate at least moderate-intensity statin for ≥30% LDL-C lowering; high-intensity statins (≥50% LDL-C reduction) are reasonable.
    - For borderline (3% to <5%) or intermediate (5% to <10%) risk, treat to LDL-C <100 mg/dL and non-HDL-C <130 mg/dL.
    - In high-risk primary prevention (≥10% 10-year risk), high-intensity statins are recommended first-line with goal LDL-C <70 mg/dL and non-HDL-C <100 mg/dL.
    - In ASCVD patients not at very high risk, consider LDL-C goal <55 mg/dL and non-HDL-C <85 mg/dL due to incremental event reduction at lower LDL-C levels.
    - Assessment of potential drug–drug interactions (DDI) with other cardiovascular medications before initiating statin therapy to minimize adverse events.
    
    **Patient Preparation / Diagnostic Criteria:**
    - Baseline 10-year ASCVD risk estimation using PREVENT-ASCVD equations for treatment decisions.
    - Follow-up LDL-C testing to assess adherence and adequacy of statin therapy effect.
    - Review of concomitant medications for potential drug–drug interactions before initiating statin therapy.
    - Patient-clinician discussion of expected net benefit, cost-effectiveness, and risk profile before statin initiation.
    - Assessment of statin intolerance and consideration of nonstatin therapies (e.g., ezetimibe, PCSK9 inhibitors) if LDL-C goals are not met.
    - Assessment of potential drug–drug interactions with other cardiovascular medications to minimize adverse events and optimize therapy.

    # Risk Enhancer Consideration
    *Source: 2026 ACC-AHA-AACVPR-ABC-ACPM-ADA AGS-APhA-ASPC-NLA-PCNA Guideline on the Management of Dyslipidemia.pdf*
    
    **Clinical Recommendation:** Risk Enhancer Consideration for ASCVD Risk Stratification
    
    **Key Clinical Actions:**
    - Consider risk enhancers qualitatively in patients at borderline risk during benefit-risk discussions for lipid-lowering therapy (LLT) initiation.
    - Use risk enhancers to personalize decisions about LLT initiation and therapy intensity, especially in adults at borderline risk.
    - Incorporate patient characteristics, diagnoses, and laboratory values associated with increased ASCVD risk, even if not included in PREVENT-ASCVD equations.
    - Use the presence of ≥1 risk enhancers to identify individuals at borderline risk who may benefit more from LLT.
    - Facilitate clinician-patient awareness of risk enhancers to improve adoption of LLT recommendations when hesitation exists.
    
    **Patient Preparation / Diagnostic Criteria:**
    - Patients must be evaluated for borderline risk using PREVENT-ASCVD equations before considering risk enhancers.
    - Risk enhancers should be assessed in the context of a benefit-risk discussion regarding LLT initiation.
    - Exclude patients with secondary causes of elevated hsCRP (e.g., postmenopausal hormone therapy, hepatic dysfunction, uncontrolled hypertension, diabetes, uncontrolled hypothyroidism, active cancer, or inflammatory conditions) before using hsCRP as a risk enhancer.

    # Dyslipidemia Treatment Guideline
    *Source: 2026 ACC-AHA-AACVPR-ABC-ACPM-ADA AGS-APhA-ASPC-NLA-PCNA Guideline on the Management of Dyslipidemia.pdf*
    
    **Clinical Recommendation:** Dyslipidemia Treatment Guideline: Comprehensive Management and Safety
    
    **Key Clinical Actions:**
    - Management has evolved from statin monotherapy to combination therapy targeting multiple lipid metabolism pathways.
    - Individualized clinician-patient discussions must precede dyslipidemia medication initiation, addressing benefits, risks, side effects, and patient preferences.
    - Statin therapy slightly increases new-onset diabetes risk in predisposed individuals (e.g., BMI ≥30 kg/m², fasting glucose ≥100 mg/dL), but benefits outweigh risks.
    - Comprehensive assessment of medication-associated symptoms and adherence is required during follow-up encounters.
    - Length of exposure to elevated LDL-C and Lp(a) should be factored into treatment decisions due to residual risk even with intensive LDL-C lowering.
    - Combination therapy with agents targeting complementary lipid metabolism pathways (e.g., statin + ezetimibe, PCSK9 inhibitors, or bempedoic acid) is now standard for high/very high cardiovascular risk patients to address residual risk.
    - Clinician-patient discussions must reaffirm benefits and address medication adherence during follow-up encounters to optimize ASCVD prevention.
    
    **Patient Preparation / Diagnostic Criteria:**
    - Patient-centered care must include education on statin-associated diabetes risk before initiation, especially in high-risk individuals.
    - Potential drug-drug interactions (DDIs) with statins, particularly CYP3A4-metabolized statins (atorvastatin, lovastatin, simvastatin), must be evaluated prior to initiation.
    - Monitoring of hepatic transaminases is required for medications like niacin, fibrates, and omega-3 fatty acids.
    - Referral to a lipid specialist is recommended for patients with familial lipid disorders, very high lipoprotein levels, or intolerance to lipid-lowering agents.
    - Genetic testing may be considered to confirm complex inherited lipid disorders.
    - Evaluation of statin drug-drug interactions (DDIs) with cardiovascular medications is mandatory prior to statin initiation, with dose modifications or avoidance as needed to minimize adverse events.
    - Monitoring of hepatic transaminases is required for patients with hepatic impairment receiving prescription omega-3 fatty acids (icosapent ethyl).
