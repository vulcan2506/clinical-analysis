# Laboratory Result Validation Protocol
*Source: WM17S.pdf*

**Significance Level:** INFORMATIONAL

**Clinical Recommendation:** Laboratory Result Validation Protocol

**Clinical Interpretation:**
- All results pertain to the submitted specimen and depend on sample quality.
- Laboratory investigations are tools to facilitate diagnosis and require clinical correlation by the referring physician.
- Test results may show interlaboratory variations and require further testing for exact values at additional cost.
- If results are alarming or unexpected, immediate contact with Customer Care is advised for remedial action.
- Test results are not valid for medico-legal purposes.
- Report delivery may be delayed due to unforeseen circumstances.
- *Guideline context/considerations:* Post-analytical validation involves evaluating automated results for plausibility and clinical correlation, investigating instrument flags for abnormal cell populations or technical problems, and performing morphological correlation via blood smear examination to confirm automated results and identify abnormalities not detected by analyzers.
- Clinical guidelines suggest that post-analytical result validation should include immediate notification for critical values indicating life-threatening conditions.
- *Guideline context/considerations:* age-stratified and gender-specific reference ranges may improve diagnostic accuracy.

**Key Clinical Actions:**
- All results pertain to the submitted specimen and depend on sample quality.
- Laboratory investigations are tools to facilitate diagnosis and require clinical correlation by the referring physician.
- Test results may show interlaboratory variations and require further testing for exact values at additional cost.
- If results are alarming or unexpected, immediate contact with Customer Care is advised for remedial action.
- Test results are not valid for medico-legal purposes.
- Report delivery may be delayed due to unforeseen circumstances.
- *Guideline-derived actions:* Adhere to established critical value notification protocols for life-threatening conditions.
- *Guideline-derived actions:* Document all post-analytical findings, corrections, and actions taken in the laboratory information system (LIS) for audit trails.
- *Guideline-derived actions:* Perform morphological correlation via blood smear examination to confirm automated results and identify abnormal cell morphology not detected by analyzers.
- *Guideline-derived actions:* Utilize statistical quality control methods, such as Levey-Jennings charts and WestGuard rules, and participate in external quality assessment programs to verify analytical accuracy.

**Patient Preparation / Diagnostic Criteria:**
- Submit requests within 72 hours post reporting.
- Further testing may be required for derivation of exact values.
- Sample must be drawn from an accredited source (e.g., CAP/ISO-certified laboratory).
- *Guideline context/considerations:* Validation requires verification of patient identification and sample integrity (e.g., hemolysis, clotting, or insufficient volume).
- *Guideline context/considerations:* Critical value limits may include specific thresholds (e.g., WBC < 2.0 × 10^9/L or > 30.0 × 10^9/L, Hb < 70 g/L, platelets < 50 × 10^9/L).
- *Guideline context/considerations:* Accuracy may be improved through the use of age-stratified and gender-specific reference ranges.
- Guideline context/considerations: proper sample handling and patient preparation for CBC testing are essential for accurate results.
