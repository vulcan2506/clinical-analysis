# Vitamin B12 Reference Range
*Source: WM17S.pdf*

**Patient Result:** 200.00 pg/mL (ref: 211.00 - 911.00 pg/mL)

**Clinical Interpretation:** The patient's result (200.00 pg/mL) is below the lower limit of the reference range (211.00 pg/mL), indicating possible Vitamin B12 deficiency requiring further clinical evaluation (e.g., methylmalonic acid, homocysteine, or clinical assessment for deficiency symptoms).
- Clinical guidelines suggest that Vitamin B12 is necessary for making red blood cells, DNA, maintaining nerve cell health, and releasing energy from food, and deficiency symptoms include anaemia, fatigue, sore/red tongue, muscle weakness, nerve damage, depression, and memory loss (*Dietary advice for Vitamin B12 and folate deficiency.pdf*).

**Significance Level:** HIGH

**Clinical Recommendation:** Vitamin B12 Reference Range
- Clinical guidelines suggest that reduced stomach acid (e.g., in older adults or due to acid-suppressing medications) impairs B12 absorption, and individuals with pernicious anaemia require lifelong injections for treatment (*Dietary advice for Vitamin B12 and folate deficiency.pdf*).

**Key Clinical Actions:**
- Vitamin B12 (Cyanocobalamin) is measured in pg/mL with a laboratory reference range of 211.00 - 911.00 pg/mL.
- Clinical guidelines suggest that intestinal surgeries or digestive disorders (e.g., Crohn's disease, coeliac disease) increase deficiency risk (*Dietary advice for Vitamin B12 and folate deficiency.pdf*).

**Patient Preparation / Method:**
- Clinical guidelines suggest that patients on acid-suppressing medications may need fortified foods or supplements for adequate B12 intake (*Dietary advice for Vitamin B12 and folate deficiency.pdf*).
- Clinical guidelines suggest that individuals with pernicious anaemia require lifelong B12 injections for treatment (*Dietary advice for Vitamin B12 and folate deficiency.pdf*).
- Clinical guidelines suggest that patients with suspected pernicious anaemia should undergo testing for intrinsic factor antibodies or undergo a Schilling test to confirm diagnosis (*Dietary advice for Vitamin B12 and folate deficiency.pdf*).

**Diagnostic Criteria:**
- Clinical guidelines suggest that Vitamin B12 deficiency can be evaluated through clinical assessment for deficiency symptoms and laboratory tests such as methylmalonic acid and homocysteine (*Dietary advice for Vitamin B12 and folate deficiency.pdf*).
