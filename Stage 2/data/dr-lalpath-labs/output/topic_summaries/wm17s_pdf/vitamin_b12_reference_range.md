# Vitamin B12 Reference Range
*Source: WM17S.pdf*

**Label:** Vitamin B12 Reference Range

**Patient Result:** 200.00 pg/mL (ref: 211.00 - 911.00 pg/mL)

**Clinical Interpretation:** Patient's value (200.00 pg/mL) is below the lower limit of the reference range (211.00 pg/mL), indicating possible deficiency requiring further clinical evaluation (e.g., methylmalonic acid or homocysteine testing) per guideline recommendations.
*Clinical guidelines suggest that levels below the reference range may reflect impaired red blood cell production, nerve cell health, or energy metabolism, with symptoms including anaemia, fatigue, neurological issues, or cognitive changes.*

**Significance Level:** HIGH

**Clinical Recommendation:** Vitamin B12 Reference Range

**Key Clinical Actions:**
- Consider testing methylmalonic acid (MMA) or homocysteine to confirm deficiency, as these markers rise when B12-dependent pathways are impaired.
- Evaluate for risk factors such as reduced stomach acid (e.g., due to age or acid-suppressing medications), intestinal disorders (e.g., Crohn’s disease), or pernicious anaemia.
- Assess dietary intake, particularly in vegans or those with limited animal product consumption, as fortified foods or supplements may be needed.
- *Guideline context: Pernicious anaemia destroys stomach cells, preventing B12 absorption and requiring lifelong injections.*

**Patient Preparation:**
- If acid-suppressing medications are used, discuss fortified food options or supplements with a clinician.
- Individuals with suspected pernicious anaemia may require intrinsic factor antibody testing or further evaluation.
- *Guideline context: Patients on acid-suppressing medications may need fortified foods or supplements for adequate B12 intake. Individuals with pernicious anaemia require lifelong B12 injections for treatment.*
- *Guideline context: Vegans should aim to eat foods fortified with vitamin B12 at least 3 times a day. If fortified foods are inadequate, take a vitamin B12 supplement of 10 micrograms per day as recommended by the Vegan Society.*

**Diagnostic Criteria:**
- Deficiency is typically suspected when serum B12 is below the reference range, but functional markers (MMA, homocysteine) can help confirm clinical significance.
