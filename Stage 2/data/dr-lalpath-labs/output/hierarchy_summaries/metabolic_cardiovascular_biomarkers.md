# Metabolic & Cardiovascular Biomarkers

These guidelines collectively address the interplay between metabolic biomarkers, renal function, and cardiometabolic risk, emphasizing standardized measurement, physiological thresholds, and contextual factors that influence clinical interpretation across vitamin D/B12 status, kidney function, and thyroid hormone dynamics.

**Key Features:**
- Vitamin D (25(OH)D) assay quantifies D2/D3 metabolites with >75 nmol/L required for optimal calcium absorption
- Serum creatinine testing for kidney function using standardized methods (Modified Jaffe or Kinetic) in dyslipidemia risk stratification
- TSH circadian rhythm variability (peak 2-4 a.m., trough 6-10 p.m.) causing up to 50% fluctuation in levels

**Notable Details:**
- 25(OH)D levels are influenced by sunlight exposure, latitude, skin pigmentation, sunscreen use, and hepatic function
- Optimal calcium absorption requires vitamin D 25(OH) levels exceeding 75 nmol/L
- Pregnancy or steroid therapy can significantly alter thyroid hormone binding protein levels
- Total T3/T4 measurements may require adjustment for binding protein variations
- Serum creatinine is the primary biomarker for kidney function evaluation in dyslipidemia management
- Kidney function assessment is integrated into cardiovascular risk stratification for dyslipidemia
- TSH testing timing should account for circadian rhythm to avoid misinterpretation
- The assay measures both D2 (Ergocalciferol) and D3 (Cholecalciferol) metabolites of vitamin D

# point-of-care kidney function testing
*Source: Chronic Kidney Disease.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** point-of-care kidney function testing for creatinine and albumin

**Clinical Actions:**
- POCT may be used for creatinine and urine albumin measurement where access to a laboratory is limited or to facilitate the clinical pathway
- Ensure pre-analytical, analytical, and post-analytical quality criteria are met for POCT devices
- Generate an estimate of GFR using the equation consistent with the region when using POCT for creatinine
- POCT ACR devices should produce a positive result in 85% of people with significant albuminuria (ACR ≥30 mg/g or ≥3 mg/mmol)
- POCT devices for albuminuria testing should also analyze creatinine to produce an ACR when feasible

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Access to a laboratory is limited or point-of-care testing facilitates the clinical pathway
- Pre-analytical, analytical, and post-analytical quality criteria must be met
- Use region-consistent GFR estimation equation with POCT creatinine
- POCT ACR devices must meet performance criteria for detecting significant albuminuria
- External quality assessment must be included in the quality criteria for POCT devices

# hypertension and kidney function management
*Source: American Diabetes Association Care Guidence.pdf*

**Clinical Recommendation:** Hypertension and kidney function management in diabetes

**Core Recommendations:**
- Frequency of kidney function assessment clarified
- Systolic blood pressure goals emphasized
- Monitoring for medication intolerance in nonpregnant individuals with diabetes and hypertension
- Monitoring frequency for eGFR drops, serum potassium changes, and hypokalemia with ACE inhibitors, ARBs, or MRAs
- Potassium level monitoring after initiation of nonsteroidal mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists (nsMRAs)

**Patient Eligibility Criteria:**
- Urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio ≥100 mg/g
- eGFR 30–90 mL/1.73 m²
- On a renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system (RAS) inhibitor
- Type 2 diabetes diagnosis
- Non-dialysis status for SGLT2 inhibitor use

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Fixed numeric A1C targets for older adults (implied by individualized targets in broader guidelines)

**Updated Recommendations:**
- Simultaneous initiation of SGLT2 inhibitor and nsMRA can be considered in eligible patients
- SGLT2 inhibitor use in non-dialysis individuals to reduce CKD progression and cardiovascular risk
- Guidance on GLP-1-based therapy in individuals on dialysis to reduce cardiovascular risk
- Reordered RAS inhibitor dose placement in kidney outcome trials figure
- Clarified guidance on kidney-protective medications in pregnant or sexually active individuals of childbearing potential
- Guidance on initiation or continuation of GLP-1-based therapy in individuals on dialysis to reduce cardiovascular risk (Recommendation 11.11b)
- Clarified guidance on kidney-protective medications that are potentially harmful in pregnant and sexually active individuals of childbearing potential (Recommendation 11.10)


### Edge Cases & Standalone Topics

These guidelines address nuanced considerations in thyroid function testing, emphasizing circadian variations, binding protein influences, and clinical contexts that may alter test interpretation.

**Key Features:**
- TSH circadian rhythm variability with peak (2-4 a.m.) and trough (6-10 p.m.) phases causing up to 50% fluctuation
- Total T3/T4 levels are influenced by thyroid hormone binding protein concentrations

**Notable Details:**
- Pregnancy or steroid therapy can significantly alter thyroid hormone binding protein levels
- TSH testing timing should account for circadian rhythm to avoid misinterpretation
- Total T3/T4 measurements may require adjustment for binding protein variations
- Up to 50% variation in TSH levels occurs due to circadian rhythm alone
